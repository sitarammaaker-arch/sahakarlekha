# How to do monthly reconciliation in a cooperative society? Checklist for cash book, bank statement and Trial Balance

For example, suppose in the year-end audit the auditor asks, "Who did the bank reconciliation in July, and when did the Trial Balance tally?" The secretary has no answer, because from July till now every month's accounts were put off with "we'll see later". Now twelve months of errors are opening up together: some bank cheques never cleared, there is a ₹2,000 difference between the member register and share capital, and one account in the Trial Balance is sitting on a reverse balance.

Cleaning up twelve months together is the hardest job; a small clean-up every month is the easiest. **Monthly reconciliation (month-end close)** is exactly that habit: at the end of every month, doing a few fixed matches and "closing" the accounts, so that the next month starts on a clean page. This article gives its full order: which match when, with what and in what order, and what to check before closing.

> 📘 **In short:** **Seven matches** are enough to close the month: **(1)** all entries recorded and approved; **(2)** cash book vs physical cash; **(3)** BRS of every bank account; **(4)** sub-registers vs control account (member shares, loans, suppliers, assets, stock); **(5)** Trial Balance Dr = Cr, last month's Closing = this month's Opening, and a check for reverse balances; **(6)** reports and signatures; **(7)** locking the period. As per Section 48(1) of the Haryana Act, statements are made at the intervals the Registrar fixes; I did not find any separate statutory rule for monthly reconciliation, so this is **good practice**, and if there is an order of the Registrar or a higher body, that comes first.

Three parts of this subject are already covered in depth in separate articles: the full BRS method in [bank reconciliation (BRS)](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide), the difference between cash and bank book in [cash book and bank book](/blog/cash-book-vs-bank-book), and Trial Balance errors in [Trial Balance](/blog/trial-balance-explained). This article ties those three into **one order for one month**, and adds what they leave out, that is, the sub-register match and the framework for closing the month.

## The order of closing the month: at a glance

The order matters. Until the entries are complete, matching has no meaning; until the matches are done, the Trial Balance cannot be trusted.

| Order | Match | Between what | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Completeness of entries | Receipts/bills/slips vs vouchers in the books | No entry missed or pending |
| 2 | Cash | Cash Book vs count of the safe | Difference zero or with reason |
| 3 | Bank | Bank Book vs bank statement | BRS, difference understood |
| 4 | Sub-registers | Member/loan/supplier/asset/stock register vs control account in the books | Difference zero |
| 5 | Trial Balance | Total Dr vs total Cr; Opening vs last Closing | Balanced and reasonable |
| 6 | Reports | Pack for the meeting | Review and signatures |
| 7 | Lock | Next step | No entries in the closed period |

## Match 1: all entries recorded and approved

First make sure that no transaction of the month is outside the books. Every other match rests on this.

- [ ] **Receipt books:** in the serial numbers of the whole month's receipts, no gap, torn or blank receipt; an entry in the books for every receipt.
- [ ] **Bills and invoices:** a purchase/expense entry for all the bills that came in the month, and vouchers for the payments made.
- [ ] **Bank slips and every line of the statement:** interest, bank charges, ECS/NEFT receipts, cheque returns, which are often missed in the society's books.
- [ ] **Voucher serial numbers:** no number missed or repeated.
- [ ] **Pending approvals:** no voucher lying in "pending" status; if the society has an approval system, settle everything before closing the month. (What a voucher is and its parts: [what is a voucher](/blog/what-is-voucher-types-uses-accounting-entry), [voucher narration and documents](/blog/voucher-narration-and-documents).)
- [ ] **Correct date of the month:** correct any transaction posted with next month's date, or last month's in this month.

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant:** SahakarLekha's **Day Book** shows all the vouchers of each day in one place, which makes it easy to catch missed days; in **Voucher Approval**, pending, approved and rejected vouchers show in separate tabs. The serial check of receipts vs books, and seeing which bill has not been entered yet, remains your job.

## Match 2: cash book vs physical cash

This is the oldest and most direct match: *is there as much cash in the safe as there should be in the books?*

- [ ] On the **last date** of the month, match the physical count of the safe (notes and coins separately) and the Cash Book balance, keeping both side by side.
- [ ] If there is a difference, **find the reason** the same day, and if it is not found, record it in writing as a cash shortage/excess and tell the chairman/secretary.
- [ ] A separate count of **petty cash** and matching of its bills.
- [ ] **Cash Book Closing = next month's Opening:** check that it has carried to the next month on its own.
- [ ] On no day should the **cash balance in the Cash Book be reversed (Cr)**. This is impossible; it means a payment was recorded without cash.

The full checklist of cash handling is in [cash handling and verification](/blog/cash-handling-and-verification), and from the treasurer's point of view in [the treasurer's accounting work](/blog/treasurer-accounting-work-checklist).

> 💡 **For the treasurer/accountant:** SahakarLekha's **Cash Book** gives the cash balance for every date automatically from the recorded vouchers, so you only have to match it with the count of the safe. Counting and deciding the reason for a difference is your job.

## Match 3: bank statement vs bank book

For every bank account, separately, up to the last date of the month. The full method and a full example are in the [BRS guide](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide); here, only what to check **from the month-end close point of view**:

- [ ] **Statement balance** and **Bank Book balance**, both on the same last date.
- [ ] Look for **only three sources** of difference: cheques issued but not yet cleared, cheques deposited but not yet credited, and the bank's entry that is not in your books (interest, charges, returns).
- [ ] Whatever "unexplained difference" remains **after the BRS**, find it first, then move ahead.
- [ ] **Old uncleared cheques:** make a list of cheques several months old and write the reason.
- [ ] **Signature on the BRS:** who made it, who checked it, and when. This is the first thing asked for in the audit.

One important difference: **the BRS is made account-wise**. If the society has three bank accounts, then three BRS.

> 💡 **For the accountant/treasurer:** SahakarLekha's **Bank Reconciliation** page works out and shows the difference when you enter the bank statement balance, and lets you save a **sign-off record** of the reconciliation (saved reconciliations show as an audit record); it does not save if the financial year is locked. Deciding the reason for every item of difference is your decision. Step-by-step help: [How to do Bank Reconciliation (Help)](/help/bank-reconciliation).

## Match 4: sub-register vs control account

This is the match most societies skip, and it surprises the most in the audit. Some accounts in the books are **control accounts**, that is, holding the total amount; behind them are **sub-registers**, that is, member-wise or party-wise details. The totals of the two should be equal.

| Control account (books) | Sub-register | What to match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Share capital | Member register/share register | Total of members' paid-up shares = share capital account in the books |
| Member loans | Loan register | Total of member-wise outstanding loans = loan account in the books |
| Suppliers (liability) | Supplier statements of account | Each supplier's balance = his balance in the books, and with his own statement |
| Customer/member receivables | Outstanding register | Total of party-wise dues = receivable account in the books |
| Fixed assets | Asset register | Cost of assets in the register = fixed assets account in the books |
| Stock | Stock register/count | Register quantity×value = stock in the books (at year-end, with the physical count) |

A hypothetical monthly reconciliation sheet *(amounts only to explain)*:

| Control account | Books | Sub-register | Difference | Reason / action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Share capital | ₹8,40,000 | ₹8,42,000 | ₹2,000 | One member's share receipt went into the register, but the voucher went to "admission fee" → correct by journal |
| Member loans | ₹21,00,000 | ₹21,00,000 | ₹0 | Matched |
| Suppliers | ₹1,64,000 | ₹1,64,000 | ₹0 | Matched |
| Fixed assets | ₹6,50,000 | ₹6,50,000 | ₹0 | Matched |

The strength of this sheet is that the ₹2,000 difference in the first row **is not caught by the Trial Balance**: in the TB, Dr = Cr stay equal, because an entry posted to the wrong account is also balanced on both sides. That is why a tallied TB is not proof that "everything is fine". (This limit of the TB: [Trial Balance](/blog/trial-balance-explained).)

For member shares: [member and share register](/blog/member-and-share-accounting). For loans: [loan and interest accounting](/blog/loan-and-interest-accounting). For stock: [inventory and stock management](/blog/inventory-and-stock-management).

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant:** In SahakarLekha some of these matches are already built in. In the **Share Register**, a **"Drift"** mark appears against a member whose record is running different from the control in the books. On the **Asset Register**, a matching message shows between the cost in the asset register and the fixed assets books. And in **Profit Distribution**, if member shares and the share capital account in the books differ, the distribution entry is not made until the difference is resolved. I did not find such a ready page for matching loans, suppliers and stock, so do their monthly match from your own registers using the reconciliation sheet method above. The **Ledger Hygiene** page checks the chart of accounts (orphan voucher references, unused or duplicate-named accounts and so on), but it is only for viewing and corrections are made on Ledger Heads.

## Match 5: Trial Balance

When the four matches above are done, then make the Trial Balance. (This is the order: matches first, then TB. If you make the TB first and then do the matches, you will have to make the TB again after the corrections.)

- [ ] **Total Dr = total Cr** (in the Closing columns). If not, find it first, then move ahead. (How to find: [when the Trial Balance does not tally](/blog/trial-balance-explained).)
- [ ] **Opening = last month's Closing:** on every account, especially cash and bank.
- [ ] **Check for reverse balances** *(general accounting sense; every society's situation can be different)*:
  - Cash balance is **Cr** → impossible, some payment recorded without cash.
  - Bank balance is **Cr**, but there is no overdraft facility → something is wrong.
  - Member loan balance is **Cr** → more recovery posted, or to the wrong member.
  - Supplier balance is **Dr** → advance payment, or in the wrong account.
  - Stock balance **Cr or negative** → more sales recorded than stock.
  - An income account on a **Dr** balance, or an expense on **Cr** → a correction or a mistake.
- [ ] **Suspense/temporary accounts should be zero:** if any entry was put in a "decide later" account, bring it to the right account by the end of the month. (On suspense: [Trial Balance](/blog/trial-balance-explained).)
- [ ] **Comparison with last month:** if any account has suddenly gone up or down many times, find out why.

> 💡 **For the accountant:** SahakarLekha's **Trial Balance** shows each account's Opening, the period's Dr and Cr, and Closing, and when total Closing Dr = Cr it gives the message **"Trial Balance is Balanced"**. Recognising a reverse balance and deciding the next step is your job. (How to read it: [View Trial Balance (Help)](/help/view-trial-balance).)

## Matches 6 and 7: reports, signatures and lock

When everything has matched, put the month's picture before the managing committee:

- [ ] **Report pack:** cash/bank balance, BRS, Trial Balance, Receipts & Payments, list of dues. (Which report when: [MIS reports for management](/blog/mis-reports-for-management); how to read them: [how to read financial reports](/blog/how-to-read-financial-reports).)
- [ ] **Three signatures on the reconciliation sheet:** the one who made it, the one who checked it and the one who approved it. A match nobody checked is half a match.
- [ ] **List of unresolved differences,** with the responsible person and last date against each.
- [ ] **Locking the period:** after the month is closed, stopping new entries in its dates or changes to old ones, so that the closed accounts do not quietly change later. (Year-end locking: [year-end and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).)

> 💡 **For the administrator/secretary:** In SahakarLekha, from **Society Setup** the administrator can set a **period-lock date**; entries up to that date are closed, and entries in the earlier period can be made only after removing the lock. This is a month-level tool, separate from the financial-year lock. When to apply the lock and who has the right to remove it is the committee's decision.

## What the law says, what the practice is

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Balance Sheet, profit and loss, trading account and other statements | Section 48(1) | The society should make these statements **at the intervals** the Registrar fixes from time to time |
| Time limit for the Balance Sheet | Rule 79 | Within 3 months of the end of the cooperative year |
| Form of the books of account | Rule 78 | In the form and manner told by the Registrar |
| Auditor's access, verification of cash | Section 95(2),(3) | Open access to books, vouchers, stock; verification of cash |
| Incomplete accounts | Section 95(6),(7) | If incomplete at the time of audit, the Registrar can get them written up; cost from the society or the responsible person |

**What was not found in the law:** in the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989 I did not find **any separate rule for bank reconciliation, Trial Balance or cash count every month**. Section 48(1) leaves the frequency of statements to the **Registrar's order**; if your Registrar, central bank or higher body has given an order for monthly or quarterly statements, that comes first. In this article, the seven matches and their order are **good accounting practice**, not legal directions.

The checklist in this article does not replace it; it is for understanding it and for your own records. (Background: [e-PACS and day-end](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).)

## A short hypothetical example

*(Hypothetical: not of any real society.)* Suppose a society did not close its months all year, assuming "everything is fine". At year-end, when they sat down to make the bank BRS, they found missing entries of cheques, interest and charges across five account-months; they found a difference of ₹2,000 in member shares and ₹30,000 in the loan register; and the Trial Balance tallied only because someone had set two mistakes against each other.

If the same society had done just seven small matches every month, every difference would have been caught in that same month, when the reason was remembered and the papers were at hand. **Month-end close is not a big job; it is an order of twelve small jobs.**

## Common mistakes

1. **Starting matches before the entries are complete.** The matches have to be repeated again and again.
2. **Assuming everything is fine because the TB tallied.** The TB does not catch an entry posted to the wrong account.
3. **Making the BRS only at year-end.** Five or six months of mistakes open up together.
4. **Skipping the sub-register match.** Differences in shares, loans and suppliers show up the latest.
5. **Looking only at the Cash Book without counting the cash.**
6. **Letting entries lie in the suspense account.**
7. **No signatures on the reconciliation sheet.** No answer to "who did it" in the audit.
8. **Changes in the dates of a closed month.** Without a lock, the closed accounts quietly change.
9. **Leaving an unresolved difference saying "it's small".** Small differences add up to big ones.
10. **Running your own system instead of the prescribed portal's procedure in a PACS.**

## Three things to remember

- 🔁 Seven matches to close the month, in a fixed order: entries complete → cash → bank → sub-register vs control account → Trial Balance → reports and signatures → lock. Matches first, Trial Balance after.
- ⚠️ A tallied Trial Balance is not proof that "everything is fine"; matching the sub-registers of shares, loans, suppliers, assets and stock with the control account is the check the TB does not do.
- ⚖️ Section 48(1) leaves the frequency of statements to the Registrar's order and I did not find a separate rule for monthly reconciliation; it is good practice. In a PACS, the prescribed portal and the higher body's instruction are final.

## Frequently asked questions

**How to do monthly reconciliation in a cooperative society?** — At the end of the month, do seven matches in order: completeness of entries, cash book vs physical cash, BRS of every bank account, sub-register vs control account, Trial Balance, signatures on the reports, and locking the period.
**Why is a BRS necessary every month?** — Because missed bank entries (interest, charges, cheque returns) and stuck cheques pile up over time and make catching them difficult. The BRS is made account-wise, on the last date of the month.
**If the Trial Balance tallies, is everything correct?** — No. The TB only sees that total Dr and total Cr are equal; it does not catch an entry posted to the wrong account, a missed entry, or equal and opposite mistakes.
**What is sub-register vs control account?** — Some accounts in the books (like share capital, member loans) are control accounts holding the total amount, and behind them are member-wise registers. The totals of both should be equal, and this is the most skipped part of monthly reconciliation.
**Is making a Trial Balance every month compulsory under the law?** — In the text of the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989 that I read, I did not find a separate rule for a monthly TB or BRS. As per Section 48(1), statements are made at the intervals the Registrar fixes; see your Registrar's/higher body's order.
**Who should do the monthly reconciliation?** — Usually the accountant or secretary makes it, the treasurer checks cash and bank, and the chairman or managing committee approves it. The exact arrangement depends on the bye-laws and the society's practice.
**Which reverse balances should be checked at month-end?** — Cr balance of cash, Cr balance of bank without an overdraft, Cr balance of member loans, Dr balance of suppliers, negative balance of stock, and income/expense accounts on the opposite side.
**What is the benefit of locking the period?** — New entries or changes in the dates of a closed month are stopped, so that the reconciled accounts do not quietly change later. The committee should decide when to lock and who removes it.
**What if a difference in the match is not found?** — Record it in a written list as an "unexplained difference", with the responsible person and date, and tell the managing committee. Do not hide it or cover it in some account.
**How to do monthly reconciliation in a PACS?** — As per your prescribed portal (e-PACS) and the higher body's instructions; this article is a supplement, not an alternative.

## In the end

Month-end close is the best insurance for the accounts. A society whose cash, bank, sub-registers and Trial Balance match each other at the end of every month has a year-end audit full of reconciliation sheets, not questions. And a secretary or treasurer who has a signed reconciliation sheet for every month does not have to search for the answer to the auditor's "who did it, when was it done".
