# How should a dairy cooperative society keep accounts of milk procurement and farmer payments?

For example, suppose that after the payment of a ten-day cycle, a member comes to the secretary and says, "Why did I get less money this time?" The secretary has three things in front of him: a bundle of slips, the collection register, and the notebook of cattle-feed advances. On top of that, the milk union's payment has not yet fully come into the society's account.

In a dairy society, dozens of members pour milk every day in two shifts. Each shift's quantity, fat and SNF are different, and every ten days or every month each member's account has to be settled, after subtracting deductions. If even one link in this chain is loose, both the member's trust and the society's money are at stake. This article ties that chain together in order: collection, rate, slip, end-of-cycle settlement, deductions, payment, dispatch to the union and reconciliation, and records for the audit.

> 📘 **In short:** Milk procurement accounting runs in five steps: **(1)** in every shift, record member-wise quantity, fat, SNF and the quality decision (accepted / accepted with deduction / rejected); **(2)** fix the rate from the union's or society's fat×SNF rate table; **(3)** at cycle-end, work out each member's **gross = total amount of accepted milk** and **net = gross − deductions**; **(4)** make one balanced entry for it: **Dr Milk Purchase, Cr Milk Payment Payable and the deduction accounts**; **(5)** pay, bring the payable account to zero, and reconcile with the union's bill. I did not find any fixed rule in the law on the rate table or the payment cycle; these depend on the union, the bye-laws and the society's policy.

The basics of a dairy society, that is, what fat/SNF is, what a cycle is, and where the society's income comes from, are in [Dairy cooperative society: accounting basics](/blog/dairy-cooperative-accounting-basics). This article is the **how-to part** that follows it: the same basics, but with every step, every entry and every checklist.

## The full cycle at a glance

| Stage | When | What happens | Record |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Collection** | Daily, every shift | Weighing and testing the member's milk | Collection register, slip to member |
| **2. Rate** | Along with collection | Rate from the fat×SNF table | Rate table (with date) |
| **3. Settlement** | Cycle-end (ten-day/monthly) | Each member's gross, deductions, net | Settlement statement |
| **4. Entry** | On approval of the settlement | Entry for purchase and liability | Voucher |
| **5. Payment** | After the cycle | To the member by bank/cash | Payment register |
| **6. Dispatch** | Daily/per shift | Sending milk to the union | Dispatch slip, union's bill |
| **7. Reconciliation** | Every cycle/month | Sent vs the union's payment | Reconciliation statement |

The most important point in this chain: **the payment due to members and the society's receipt from the union are two separate accounts, and their timing can differ.** So keep the purchase liability and the receipt from the union in separate, clean accounts.

## Step 1: Shift collection

The heart of a dairy society is its collection register. In every shift (morning/evening), each member's entry should have these things:

| Column | What to record | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Date and shift | Day and morning/evening | Boundary of the cycle |
| Member code | Permanent, one member one code | Avoids confusion |
| Quantity (litres) | Weight or measure | Basis of payment |
| Fat % and SNF % | Test result | Basis of rate |
| Quality decision | Accepted / accepted with deduction / rejected | Effect on amount |
| Rate and amount | From the rate table, then quantity × rate | Payment |

**The quality decision** is a fine point that often gets no place in the register:

- **Accepted:** the full amount will be paid.
- **Accepted with deduction:** the milk was taken but with a percentage deduction, for example for slight sourness or lower quality; write the deduction percentage and the reason.
- **Rejected:** the milk was not taken (for example it had curdled, or adulteration was found). Its **amount will not be added to the payment**, but its entry must stay in the register, so that the member can be told why it was not taken.

**Each day's work the same day:**

- [ ] Each shift's collection is recorded in that same shift, and at shift-end the total litres are added up in the register.
- [ ] **Give the member a slip** at the same time: quantity, fat/SNF, rate, amount. The slip is the member's proof, and later the most direct check for "why is my money less".
- [ ] Keep checking the weighing scale and the fat/SNF testing machine from time to time. (How often the machine is calibrated depends on the union's instructions.)
- [ ] The reason for every rejected or deducted entry is written down.

> 💡 **For the secretary/operator:** The biggest pressure in a shift is speed: there is a queue, and every member is in a hurry. On SahakarLekha's **Milk Collection** page, as soon as you fill in the date, shift, member, litres, fat and SNF, the rate comes automatically from the rate table (if there is no table, from the per-point fat rate), the three quality options (accepted / with deduction / rejected) come with a deduction percentage, Enter on the keyboard opens the next field, and a **milk slip** can be printed for every entry. Doing the weighing and testing yourself and deciding on quality remains your job.

## Step 2: How the rate is fixed

In a dairy society the rate usually comes from a **two-axis table**: fat ranges on one side, SNF ranges on the other. The rate at the meeting point of the fat range and SNF range your milk falls in is the rate that applies. A hypothetical example *(rates are only for explanation; the real table will be your union's/society's)*:

| Fat ↓ / SNF → | 8.0–8.5 | 8.5–9.0 | 9.0–9.5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4.0–4.5** | ₹33 | ₹35 | ₹36 |
| **4.5–5.0** | ₹36 | ₹38 | ₹39 |
| **5.0–5.5** | ₹39 | ₹41 | ₹43 |

Member A: 10 litres, fat 4.8, SNF 8.7 → range (4.5–5.0, 8.5–9.0) → ₹38 → 10 × 38 = **₹380**.
Member B: 10 litres, fat 4.2, SNF 8.2 → range (4.0–4.5, 8.0–8.5) → ₹33 → 10 × 33 = **₹330**.

Example with deduction: A's 10 litres were taken with a 10% deduction → ₹380 − 10% = **₹342**.

**Rate-table rules that are often missed:**

- **With a date:** every table must have an effective date. If the union changes the rate, the new table applies from the new date, and **old entries must not be changed.** Milk that came while the previous table was in force keeps that rate.
- **Season:** in many places there are separate tables for the flush and lean seasons. The current season's table should apply.
- **Edge values:** what happens if fat or SNF goes outside the highest or lowest limit of the table? Many charts apply the edge rate to edge values; but this depends on your chart's rule, so confirm it with the union.
- **The table is the union's, not the society's:** the society does not apply higher or lower rates at its own will. Whatever rate is in force, its written basis (the union's letter or a resolution) should be on file.

For rate-table details, the meaning of fat/SNF and payment examples, also see [Dairy society: accounting basics](/blog/dairy-cooperative-accounting-basics).

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant:** The most common rate mistake is using an old table, or changing old entries while applying a new table. On SahakarLekha's **Milk Rate Charts** page, the fat×SNF table is made with an effective date and (optionally) a season; the table in force on the entry's date is picked according to that date, and if the table is empty the system says the rate was "not found" instead of silently applying zero. What the table's rates should be is the union's and society's decision.

## Step 3: End-of-cycle settlement

At the end of every cycle (ten-day, fifteen-day or monthly), each member's account is made. The formula is simple:

> **Gross = total amount of the member's accepted entries in that cycle** (rejected entries are not added)
> **Net payable = gross − total deductions**

One hypothetical member, one ten-day cycle *(all figures are hypothetical)*:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Accepted milk of 20 shifts: 168 litres, average rate ₹42 | ₹7,056.00 |
| (6 litres of milk from one shift rejected, not added) | — |
| **Gross** | **₹7,056.00** |
| (−) Recovery for cattle feed | ₹1,800.00 |
| (−) Recovery for medicine | ₹300.00 |
| **Total deductions** | **₹2,100.00** |
| **Net payable** | **₹4,956.00** |

**Its entry** (on approval, in one balanced voucher):

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milk Purchase (expense/direct purchase) | ₹7,056 | — |
| Milk Payment Payable (liability) | — | ₹4,956 |
| Member Inputs Receivable (feed/medicine credit) | — | ₹2,100 |
| **Total** | **₹7,056** | **₹7,056** |

Total Dr and total Cr are equal: equal to the gross. This means: the society bought milk worth ₹7,056, of which ₹2,100 went to clear the member's credit (the receivable went down), and ₹4,956 is still to be paid to the member (the liability went up). Note that the milk purchase account should stay **separate** and not be mixed into the general purchase account, so that milk purchase shows clearly in the Trading Account.

**Settlement rules:**

- [ ] **Draft before approval:** each member's settlement is first made as a draft, checked, then approved; the entry is made only on approval.
- [ ] Deductions should not be **more than the gross.** If they are, carry the remaining credit to the next cycle in the settlement, so the member is not shown a negative payment.
- [ ] **Give the settlement statement to the member:** the cycle's quantity, average rate, gross, each deduction, net.
- [ ] A cycle's settlement is made only once: a duplicate creates a double liability.

> 💡 **For the accountant:** Working out the ten-day accounts of hundreds of members by hand is the biggest burden and the riskiest job. SahakarLekha's **Farmer Settlement** page adds up the member's accepted milk within the cycle dates to get the gross (leaving out rejected entries), lets you add deductions, shows the net payable and the balance, keeps the settlement in **draft and approved** status, and on approval makes **one balanced entry** like the one above (Dr Milk Purchase; Cr Payment Payable and each deduction's account); if a deduction account is missing or the deduction is larger than the gross, it does not let that entry be made. What to do about deductions is your decision.

## Step 4: Deductions and recovery of inputs

A dairy society does not only take milk; it often also gives things like **cattle feed, fodder, medicine, artificial insemination** on credit, which are recovered from the milk payment. This arrangement is convenient for both the member and the society, but if the accounts are not kept separately, this is where most disputes happen.

**When giving credit:**

| Event | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cattle feed worth ₹1,800 given to a member on credit | Member Inputs Receivable | Feed sales/income |

**At the time of recovery from the milk payment**, the same deduction line: Cr Member Inputs Receivable (shown in the settlement above).

**Rules:**

- [ ] **A receipt for every credit**, with the member's signature or thumb impression, the date and the item.
- [ ] **A separate account and a separate register for credit:** not mixed up with the milk payment. Each member's outstanding inputs should be visible at a glance.
- [ ] **Basis of the deduction:** recovery from the milk payment should be with the member's consent and by the bye-laws/committee's decision. In the text I read, I did not find any direct provision for deductions from the milk payment, so this depends on the **bye-laws and the society's policy.** Also keep in writing in your policy what percentage can be deducted in each cycle.
- [ ] **Age of dues:** which member has not repaid credit for how many cycles, put before the managing committee once a month.

> 💡 **For the secretary:** SahakarLekha's **Dairy Inputs** page records credit for cattle feed, medicine or AI (artificial insemination) member-wise and posts it to the Member Inputs Receivable account; the same outstanding amount appears in **Farmer Settlement** as "Recover". How much credit to give and to whom not to give is the society's decision.

## Step 5: Payment

After the settlement entry, the member's money has gone into the society's **liability** (Milk Payment Payable). Payment reduces it:

| Event | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payment to member by bank | Milk Payment Payable | Bank | ₹4,956 |
| Or cash payment | Milk Payment Payable | Cash | ₹4,956 |

After payment, that member's "Milk Payment Payable" should be zero. Whatever remains is an **outstanding payment**, and its list should be made after every cycle.

**Rules:**

- [ ] **Direct payment into the bank** is better, especially for large amounts: the record is made automatically and cash counting and risk go down. (Methods: [Bank payment methods](/blog/bank-payment-methods-cheque-dd-upi).)
- [ ] If there is **cash payment**, take the member's signature or thumb impression on receipt, and the date in the payment register. (Precautions: [Cash handling and verification](/blog/cash-handling-and-verification).)
- [ ] **Within how many days payment should be made:** I did not find any fixed time limit for this in the law. It depends on the union's arrangement, the bye-laws and the society's policy. Whatever the cycle is (ten-day, monthly), keep it **the same every time** and tell members in advance.
- [ ] **Age-wise list of outstanding member payments:** money that a member has not collected for some reason should not quietly be turned into income; it remains a liability.

## Dispatch to the union and reconciliation

The society's milk goes on to the union. Three separate accounts run here, which should not be mixed up:

1. **Purchase from members** (the steps above)
2. **Sent to the union** (dispatch)
3. **Money received from the union**

**Dispatch entry** *(hypothetical amount)*:

| Event | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Milk sent to the union | Union Receivable (debtor) | Milk Sales, Bulk/Union (income) | ₹1,64,000 |
| Payment received from the union (bank) | Bank | Union Receivable | ₹1,64,000 |

**Shortage, that is, the difference between the quantity taken and sent:** suppose in a cycle the society took **4,200 litres** from members and sent **4,150 litres** to the union. The difference is 50 litres, that is, about 1.2%. Where this difference went should be clear: a measurement difference, spillage, or thrown away due to sourness? The cost of milk the society bought but did not send to the union falls on the society, so **write the shortage on every dispatch**, with the reason, and show its total to the managing committee at cycle-end. I did not find any statutory limit on how much shortage is acceptable; this depends on the union's policy or the society's decision.

**Reconciliation with the union (every cycle or month):**

- [ ] **Quantity:** the society's dispatch (litres) vs the union's receipt.
- [ ] **Quality:** the union laboratory's fat/SNF vs the society's test. If there is a difference, find that same cycle, that same date.
- [ ] **Amount:** the union's bill vs the society's "Union Receivable" entry; if the union has made deductions, their reason.
- [ ] **Receipt of payment:** which amount came into the bank, which is still due. (Monthly bank reconciliation: [BRS](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide).)

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant:** SahakarLekha's **Milk Dispatch** page records each dispatch's quantity, fat/SNF, rate, vehicle number and shortage (in litres), shows the sale amount vs the amount **received** from the union and the **balance** (mode of payment: bank or cash), and posts the dispatch entry to the Union Receivable and union-sales accounts. The **Dairy Registers** page gives the collection, settlement and payment registers in one place. Deciding the reason for shortage and talking to the union about differences is your job.

## Bonus and dividend: the link to year-end

At year-end the dairy society's profit is distributed. **In a dairy society, bonus is often based on the quantity/value of milk supplied** and **dividend is on share capital.** This difference is explained in [Profit distribution](/blog/profit-distribution-and-reserves), and the limits on dividend, that is, in Haryana 10% per year on paid-up share capital as per Rule 72, are in the [Member dividend guide](/blog/cooperative-society-member-dividend-guide).

One special point: the second proviso to Section 28(4) of the Haryana Act says the term of the committee of primary, central and apex milk producers' cooperative societies is **as per their bye-laws**, and the second proviso to Section 30 says the election of their office-bearers is also **as per their bye-laws.** That is, in a dairy society some administrative rules differ from an ordinary society, and checking your own bye-laws matters even more.

> 💡 **For the secretary:** SahakarLekha's **Dairy Distribution** page makes the proposal for bonus (based on milk value/per litre) and dividend (on share capital) and the member-wise distribution, with draft/approved status. The decision on rate and distribution belongs to the general body and the bye-laws.

## A short hypothetical example

*(Hypothetical: not of any real society.)* Suppose that in a small dairy society the daily milk is recorded, but when the rate table changes, entries of old dates are also "corrected" to the new rate. At cycle-end the settlement is made by hand, on paper, and members' deductions are not matched with their separate credit register. At the year-end audit a difference of ₹14,300 turns up between the union's bill and the society's entries, and there is no list of outstanding member payments.

If the same society did three things every cycle: kept the rate table separately with dates, made a single balanced settlement entry, and reconciled with the union's bill every cycle, the same difference would have been caught in the first cycle, when the reason is still remembered.

## Common mistakes

1. **Recording collection late.** The accounts go wrong at cycle-end.
2. **Not writing fat/SNF,** recording only litres. Correct quality-based payment cannot be made.
3. **Changing the rate of old dates** along with the new table.
4. **Not keeping a record of rejected milk.** No answer when the member asks.
5. **Making a settlement twice** or deducting more than the gross.
6. **Not keeping a separate account for input credit.** No proof of the deduction remains.
7. **Not giving the member a slip/settlement statement.** Trust breaks.
8. **Mixing milk purchase into the general purchase account.** Milk purchase does not show separately in the Trading Account.
9. **Not reconciling with the union.** The difference between milk sent and milk paid for stays hidden till year-end.
10. **Running your own system in place of the prescribed software/portal's process.**

## Three things to remember

- 🥛 Milk procurement accounting is a chain of five steps: shift collection, rate from the rate table, end-of-cycle settlement (gross − deductions = net), one balanced entry (Dr Milk Purchase; Cr Payment Payable and deduction accounts), and payment, then reconciliation with the union's bill.
- 🧾 Rejected milk, deductions, the rate table's date and input credit: a separate, written record for each one; do not change old entries to the new rate, and give members the slip and settlement statement.
- ⚖️ The auditor's access (Section 95(3)) is clear in the law; the rate table, payment cycle, basis of deductions and shortage limit come from the union/bye-laws/society's policy, and where there is prescribed software or a portal, it is final.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should a dairy cooperative society keep accounts of milk procurement?** — In every shift, record member-wise quantity, fat, SNF and the quality decision; work out the amount from the rate table; at cycle-end make each member's gross, deductions and net payable; pass its balanced entry (Dr Milk Purchase, Cr Payment Payable and deduction accounts); then pay and bring the payable account to zero.
**How is a farmer's payment worked out?** — Subtracting deductions (such as recovery for cattle feed or medicine) from the total amount of accepted milk (gross) gives the net payable amount; the amount of rejected milk is not added.
**How is the rate fixed from fat and SNF?** — Usually from a two-axis table: the rate at the meeting point of the fat range and the SNF range is the one that applies. The table belongs to the union or the society and should be kept with an effective date.
**What to do with old entries when the rate table changes?** — Do not change them. The new table applies from the new effective date; milk of old dates stays at the old rate.
**What to do if milk is rejected?** — Do not add its amount to the payment, but do keep the entry and the reason in the register, so the member can be told.
**Within how many days should payment be made?** — I did not find any fixed time limit for this in the law. It depends on the union's arrangement, the bye-laws and the society's policy. Whatever cycle is chosen, keep it the same every time.
**How is credit for cattle feed recovered?** — When giving credit, Dr Member Inputs Receivable; in the milk payment settlement, the deduction of the same (Cr Member Inputs Receivable). The basis of the deduction is the bye-laws and the society's policy.
**Why is reconciliation with the union necessary?** — So that differences between the milk the society sent (quantity, quality) and the union's payment are caught in time. I did not find any statutory limit on how much shortage is acceptable.
**What does the auditor look at in a dairy society?** — As per Section 95(3), he has open access to books, vouchers and property. In practice, the matching of the collection register, rate table, settlement statements, payment proof, input credit and union reconciliation is checked.
**What if the milk union has prescribed software?** — Then make the collection, rate and payment entries as per that prescribed system. This article does not replace it; it is for your own records and understanding.

## In the end

A dairy society's trust rests on one question in every member's mind: "Was my milk weighed correctly, was the right rate applied, and did I get the full money?" A society that has the slip of every shift, the settlement statement of every cycle and accounts reconciled with the union is not afraid of this question. And a secretary who has all this in one place does not have to hunt for accounts at the end of the month.
