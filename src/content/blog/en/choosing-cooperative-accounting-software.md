# How to choose accounting software for a society: a practical checklist

There is a lot of accounting software on the market — but a cooperative society has special needs. Choosing the wrong tool wastes both money and effort, and often you have to change again after a year or two. This article explains in detail what a society should look at when choosing software, what to avoid, and how to make the decision in an orderly way.

## A society's needs are different from an ordinary business

A cooperative society is not an ordinary shop or company — it has special things like members, share capital, loans and recovery, audit classification, reserve fund, statutory distribution of profit, and returns to the Registrar. An ordinary business accounting tool does not handle all this — it does not even have a concept like "member" or "share capital". So when a society uses such a tool, it has to manage many things by hand with workarounds, which increases both errors and effort.

> 💡 The very first rule — choose software that is **made for societies**, not for general business. This one point gets half the decision right.

Besides this, a society's audit and compliance needs are also special — Trial Balance, Receipts & Payments, Income & Expenditure, Balance Sheet, and reports in the cooperative format are needed in one click. With a tool that does not give these cooperative-format reports, audit time every year takes heavy effort.

## Check these when choosing

| Criterion | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Cooperative-focused | Handles members/shares/loans/audit |
| Hindi-friendly | Secretary/staff can run it easily |
| Affordable | Not a burden on a small society (good options are even free) |
| Data security | Backup and safe storage |
| Easy | Even a non-expert can use it |
| Cloud (SaaS) | Access from anywhere, automatic updates |
| Cooperative reports | Trial Balance, Income & Expenditure, Balance Sheet, audit format |
| Support/training | Help is available when there is a problem |

> ⚠️ "Expensive means better" is not necessarily true. Fit, ease and security matter more than price.

### What each criterion means

- **Cooperative-focused:** The tool should already have a member register, share accounts, loan recovery, reserve fund and profit distribution — so you do not have to make workarounds later.
- **Hindi-friendly:** A society's secretary or clerk is often not comfortable in English; a Hindi interface makes daily work faster and error-free.
- **Affordable:** A small society's budget is limited; choose a tool whose price is within its pocket — many good options are free or very cheap.
- **Data security:** The society's entire accounts are its most valuable asset; regular backup and safe storage are essential. (See: [Data security and backup](/blog/data-security-and-backup).)
- **Easy:** A tool that takes months to learn is often abandoned; a simple interface decides adoption.
- **Cloud:** In detail below.
- **Cooperative reports:** Ready-format reports for the audit and the Registrar, so there is no year-end rush.
- **Support/training:** Help, guides and videos in Hindi when there is a problem.

## Why cloud (SaaS) is better

Cloud/SaaS software runs in the browser — nothing to install or update, data on a secure server, and access from anywhere. Its concrete benefits:

- **Access from anywhere:** Office, home or mobile — see the accounts wherever there is internet.
- **Automatic updates:** New rules/features keep coming without doing anything; unlike old desktop software, you do not have to buy a new version every year.
- **Automatic backup:** Data stays safe on the server; safe even if the computer breaks down or is stolen.
- **No expensive server:** The society does not need its own server or IT staff.

This is far safer and more flexible than desktop software kept on a single computer or a paper register. In desktop software the data stays on that one machine — if it breaks down, everything is gone; in the cloud there is no such risk. (See: [Why digital accounting](/blog/digital-accounting-for-cooperatives).)

## Look at the cost the right way

The price of software is not just its annual fee — look at the total cost: is there a separate installation fee? A separate fee for every user? Separate money for updates? Training cost? Some "cheap" tools turn out expensive because of these hidden costs. On the other hand, some excellent cooperative-focused tools are available free or for a nominal fee for small societies. So being "free" or "cheap" is not a weakness — just check that it has the necessary features and security.

## Do look at the migration path

The fear that "old data will be lost" often stops change. Good software gives a clear path for **migration from Tally/Excel** — masters, opening balances and history come along, and you do not have to type them again. When choosing software, do ask how the old data will come in. (See: [Migration from Tally/Excel](/blog/migrate-from-tally-or-excel) and [Opening balance guide](/blog/opening-balances-new-society).)

## Paper vs desktop vs cloud

A society usually has three options — the old paper register, desktop software kept on one computer, or cloud (SaaS). See the comparison:

| Aspect | Paper register | Desktop software | Cloud (SaaS) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Only where the register is | Only on that computer | From anywhere |
| Backup | None (destroyed in fire/water) | By hand, often forgotten | Automatic |
| Reports | Made by hand | Are generated | One click, live |
| Updates | — | New version every year | Automatic |
| Error risk | High | Medium | Low (automatic checks) |
| Cost | Looks low, more time | Version + server | Often free/cheap |

This comparison makes it clear that cloud is generally the safest and most convenient — especially for a society that has no separate IT staff. A paper register looks cheap, but it carries the highest effort, errors and risk of destruction.

## Must-have vs nice-to-have

Not every feature is equally necessary. First confirm the **must-haves**, then look at the nice-to-haves:

- **Must-have:** Member/share/loan management, double-entry accounting, Trial Balance and final accounts, audit-format reports, data backup, Hindi.
- **Nice-to-have:** Mobile app, SMS/notifications, GST/TDS reports, multiple branches, dashboard, multi-user roles.

If a must-have feature is missing, the tool is incomplete for the society however attractive it looks. On the other hand, a missing nice-to-have can be added later.

## Who should decide

The software decision should not be made by the secretary or any one person alone — because its effect stays on the whole society for years. The best way is for the managing committee to discuss it: the secretary/accountant explains the needs (they will run it every day), and the managing committee decides on cost and long-term fit. A tool that the daily user finds hard does not last long — so the user's opinion matters most. (See: [Roles of office-bearers](/blog/office-bearers-chairman-secretary-treasurer).)

## After choosing — adoption and training

Choosing the right tool is half the work; the other half is adopting it properly. For this: migrate the old data, give the secretary/clerk basic training (entering vouchers, taking out reports), and at the start, if possible, run both old and new in parallel for a few weeks so that trust builds. A good tool gives guides, videos and support in Hindi, which makes this learning easy. In a few weeks the team becomes comfortable and then work becomes faster than before. (See: [Getting started with SahakarLekha](/blog/getting-started-sahakarlekha).)

## Think ahead — the society will grow

A small society today can be a big one tomorrow — more members, new activities (loans, storage, shop), maybe branches. So choose a tool that can grow with the society: one that can handle more members, more transactions, new features and roles. A tool that fits today but will not handle tomorrow's needs will have to be changed again after a few years — and every change takes time and effort. Choosing the right, scalable tool once saves you from this repeated trouble.

## How to decide — a simple scorecard

Shortlist one or two tools, then give each one 1–5 points on the criteria above and add them up. Along with this:

1. **Take a free trial:** Try it in real use; do not rely only on the brochure.
2. **Do one real task:** Enter one voucher, take out one report — how easy did it feel?
3. **Test the support:** Ask a question and see how quickly, and in which language, the answer comes.
4. **Look at demo data:** Do the cooperative reports really come in a ready format?
5. **Ask other societies:** The experience of those already using it is the most reliable.

This way the decision is based on trying it out, not on emotion or advertising.

## Common mistakes when choosing

- **Choosing an ordinary business tool** → cooperative needs are missed; workarounds later.
- **Looking only at price** → a cheap but unsuitable tool turns out expensive; look at fit first.
- **Not taking a trial** → buying without trying is a risk; always run it first.
- **Not asking about data security** → make clear in advance how backup is done.
- **Not looking at the migration path** → if the old data gets stuck, the change gets stuck.
- **Ignoring language/ease** → a tool the secretary cannot run is useless.

## Warning signs (Red flags)

Some things are a clear signal to avoid a tool: not giving a free trial, not giving a straight answer about data backup, not letting you export your data later (the data becomes a "hostage"), a very complex interface, and no reliable channel of support. If you see any of these, be careful.

## The data is always yours

One thing that is often overlooked — the society's data should **always belong to the society**, not be held hostage by the software company. So when choosing, make sure you can export your complete data at any time — even if you have to change the tool later. A tool that does not let you take your data out traps you, even if it looks free today. A reliable tool stays transparent: your data, under your control, available any time. (See: [Data export and backup](/blog/data-security-and-backup).)

## Frequently asked questions

**Which software is right for a society?** — One that is made for societies, Hindi-friendly, affordable, secure and easy, and gives cooperative reports.
**Will any accounting software do?** — Cooperative needs (members/shares/audit) are special; a general tool does not fit fully.
**Is expensive software better?** — No; fit and ease matter more (good options are even free).
**Is cloud safer or desktop?** — Cloud is generally safer — automatic backup, data safe even if the machine breaks down.
**Will the old data come along?** — A good tool gives migration from Tally/Excel; do ask about this when choosing.
**How to decide?** — Take a free trial, do one real task, test the support, and compare by giving points on the criteria.
**Does a small society also need software?** — Yes; even a simple and free tool makes the accounts accurate, the audit easy and saves time.
**Will I be able to take my data out later?** — With a reliable tool, yes; the data always belongs to the society and can be exported any time — do confirm this when choosing.
**Should the secretary decide alone or the managing committee?** — The managing committee; the secretary/accountant explains the needs and experience, and the committee decides on cost and long-term fit.

## Three things to remember
- 🎯 Choose a tool made for societies (not an ordinary business tool).
- 🔒 Hindi-friendly, affordable, secure, easy — and one that gives cooperative reports.
- ☁️ Do look at cloud + a migration path; take a trial before buying.

## In the end
Choosing the right software is a one-time decision that makes years easier. Choose it not in a hurry but by testing it against the criteria and trying it out — then that tool runs with the society for years and makes every audit and every year-end easier.
