# Can the chairman of a cooperative society decide alone? Powers of the managing committee and the general body

For example, suppose the chairman comes to the office in the morning and tells the secretary, "Order goods worth ₹2 lakh, we will pass the resolution later, don't wait for the meeting." The secretary knows that nothing was discussed in any meeting, but the chairman is the society's most senior office-bearer. If he refuses, the relationship sours; if he agrees, tomorrow the auditor will ask, "Who approved this?"

This question looks simple on the surface, but its answer is split into three layers: **the general body, the managing committee and the chairman.** The powers of all three are different, and when one takes over another's work, it leads to disputes, audit objections and sometimes even the supersession of the committee. This article makes clear who can decide what, what the law says about it, and what the secretary or accountant should do when a "verbal order from the chairman" comes.

> 📘 **In short:** In the text of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 that I read, **the chairman is not given any power to decide alone.** Final authority lies with the **general body** (Section 25(1)); day-to-day management is done collectively by the **elected managing committee**; the chairman is an office-bearer elected from within the committee, who presides over meetings and gives a **casting vote when votes are equal** (Section 20(a)). If the chairman can do some things, those are tasks **delegated by the bye-laws or a committee resolution**, not his own will by virtue of his post. But the Act also has no list of the chairman's powers; check its exact scope in your **bye-laws.**

This article makes no comment on any person; it explains the legal framework of the posts. An at-a-glance comparison of the roles of the three office-bearers is in [Roles of the chairman, secretary and treasurer](/blog/office-bearers-chairman-secretary-treasurer); here the topic is **the power to decide.**

## Three layers: who is above whom

The structure of a cooperative society is not like a company. Here the "owners" are all the members, and they exercise their authority through the general body.

| Layer | Who | Role | Source of authority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **General body** | All members (or representatives fixed in the bye-laws) | The society's **final authority**; at least one meeting a year | Sections 25(1), 25(3) |
| **Managing committee** | Members elected by the general body, 5-year term | Day-to-day management, **collective** decisions | Section 28 |
| **Chairman** | Elected by the committee members from among themselves | Presiding, casting vote on a tie, representation | Sections 30, 20(a) |
| **Secretary / Manager / MD** | Employee or nominee | Carrying out decisions | Section 31(2); bye-laws |

A simple comparison: the general body is the "owner", the managing committee is its "elected management team", and the chairman is the "conductor" of that team's meetings. The team captain does not decide in place of the team; he carries the team's decision forward. (On the committee's role: [Role of the managing committee](/blog/managing-committee-role).)

## Powers of the general body

Section 25(1) says that the **final authority of the society vests in the general body of members.** (If the bye-laws provide for a smaller body of representatives, it exercises as much power as is fixed in the rules or bye-laws.) The subjects of the general body held once a year are listed in Section 25(3):

- Approval of the **programme of activities** for the coming year
- Consideration of the **audit report and annual report**
- Review of the previous year's performance
- Review of the society's position, including the **list of defaulters and their amounts**
- **Creating special funds** and their use
- Consideration of the list of **employees who are relatives of office-bearers**
- When due, **election of the managing committee**
- **Amendment of the bye-laws**
- Any other subject as per the bye-laws

Some other powers come from separate provisions:

- **Maximum credit limit:** as per Rule 24, **only the general body** can fix the maximum credit limit in a meeting.
- **Amendment of bye-laws:** as per Section 10, an amendment is valid only when it is registered; and it must be passed in the general body by a majority of **two-thirds of the members present and voting**, with 15 clear days' prior notice.
- **Right to vote:** as per Section 20, every member has one vote; a member in default of a liability to the society cannot vote.

That is why the general body's record and a proper notice matter a lot. (AGM preparation: [AGM checklist](/blog/agm-preparation-checklist).)

## Powers of the managing committee

Members of the managing committee are **elected** by the general body and the term is **five years**, unless the Registrar removes them earlier (Sections 28(1), 28(4)). One result of this is direct: the committee is **one body**, and its decisions must be made in a meeting, by majority, with minutes.

The text of the law gives some clear signs that authority is *collective*, not of any one person:

- **The example of the MD:** even where a managing director is appointed because of a government shareholding of ₹10 lakh or more, Section 31(2) says he will work **under the supervision and control of the committee**, and he gets the powers given in the bye-laws or **delegated by the committee.** When even the chief executive appointed by the government is under the committee, it is hard to assume the chairman has sole power.
- **Service rules:** as per Rule 29, the committee has to make rules for the recruitment and service conditions of employees, in the light of model rules and with the Registrar's approval. That is, appointments are made by fixed rules, not at the chairman's will.
- **Calling a meeting:** as per Section 26, a requisition to call a meeting must be met within one month; as per Rule 23, meetings are called by the chief executive officer or an officer authorised by him.

And **scrutiny of decisions** is also in the law: under Section 27 the Registrar can **suspend** any resolution of the managing committee (for up to 6 months) if it is **outside** the powers under the Act, Rules or bye-laws or is against the interest of the society/members. That is, the committee too decides within the limits of the law, not of its own will. ([How to write a resolution](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)

## Powers of the chairman: what they are and what they are not

**What is clearly written about the chairman in the Act:**

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How the chairman is chosen | Section 30 | Committee members elect office-bearers from among themselves, **within 60 days** of the election, presided over by a presiding officer appointed by the Registrar |
| Casting vote | Section 20(a) | When votes are equal, the chairman has a **second or casting vote** |
| Term limit | Section 30 (proviso) | A committee member who has been chairman/vice-chairman continuously for **10 years** is not eligible again without a gap of at least **5 years** |
| No-confidence motion | Section 30A | By a **two-thirds** majority of elected members, in a specially called meeting; the chairman leaves office at once; only after **one year** from the election |
| The chairman is an "officer" | Section 2(m) | Chairman, secretary, manager, treasurer etc. are all officers |

**What I did not find in the Act:** any list of the chairman's powers and duties; any power to spend alone up to some amount; any freedom to decide alone on behalf of the managing committee. In the texts of the Act and Rules that I read, I found neither a power for the chairman to decide alone nor a separate section forbidding it; the whole framework stands on **collective** decision-making.

So what can the chairman do? Usually:

- **Preside over the meeting** and conduct the discussion.
- **Casting vote on a tie.**
- **Represent the society** (before the bank, department, federation).
- **Tasks delegated by the bye-laws or a committee resolution**, such as approving payments up to a fixed limit, co-signing cheques, or action in an emergency that must be confirmed at the next meeting.

Be careful about the last point. **Delegation** is legally strong only when it is **in writing**, that is, in the bye-laws or a committee resolution, with a **fixed limit.** In the Rules I found no separate method for delegating the managing committee's powers or any spending limit, so all of that rests on your bye-laws and committee resolutions.

## "Who will decide?" A table

| Subject | Who decides | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual work plan, consideration of audit report | General body | Section 25(3) |
| **Maximum credit limit** | General body | Rule 24 |
| Amendment of bye-laws | General body (2/3 majority, registration) | Section 10 |
| Election of managing committee | General body / members | Sections 25(3), 28 |
| Election of office-bearers | Committee members from among themselves | Section 30 |
| Removing the chairman | 2/3 majority of elected members | Section 30A |
| Recruitment and service conditions of employees | Managing committee, by service rules (Registrar's approval) | Rule 29 |
| Day-to-day management, meeting decisions | Managing committee (majority; chairman's casting vote on a tie) | Section 20(a); bye-laws |
| Rate of dividend (limit 10%, Haryana) | Within the rule's limit; general body/bye-law process | Rule 72; [Dividend guide](/blog/cooperative-society-member-dividend-guide) |
| Spending/purchase up to a fixed amount | Fixed in bye-laws; otherwise the committee | Bye-laws (I did not find a limit in the Rules) |
| Chairman deciding alone | **No separate power found**; only tasks delegated by bye-laws/resolution | Framework of Sections 25(1), 31(2) |

The most important row in this table is the last one, and so is a key question linked to it: on subjects where the bye-laws have no clear limit, a **committee resolution** is the safest route.

## Conflict of interest: the chairman is bound too

Rule 30 places a **conflict of interest** restriction on officers of the society: they cannot hold a direct or indirect interest in contracts, property transactions or other dealings with the society (except some exceptions like loans, investments or housing), and this bar continues for two years after leaving office. This means that in a deal where the chairman or his family has an interest, his role in the decision will be highly doubtful. (Details: [Board Resolution guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)

## The cost of deciding alone: what can happen

If the chairman starts deciding himself in place of the committee, or the committee passes decisions as a formality without a meeting, these routes open in the law:

| What | Who | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suspension/cancellation of a resolution | Registrar, suspension for up to 6 months | Section 27 |
| Removing a committee member | Registrar, after hearing; disqualification for up to 5 years | Section 35 |
| Removing the committee / administrator | Registrar, after hearing | Section 34(1) |
| No-confidence | 2/3 of elected members | Section 30A |
| Member disputes | Arbitration/Registrar route | [Where to take a dispute](/blog/cooperative-society-dispute-resolution-haryana) |

For a **primary agricultural cooperative society (PACS)**, Section 34(1) has a special proviso: the Registrar can remove a PACS committee **only** in these situations: (i) the society has had a **loss for three years** in a row; (ii) **serious financial irregularity or fraud** has been identified; or (iii) there are **judicial directions** or a **permanent lack of quorum.** Rule 28 also gives reasons for a committee member vacating office (or being removed), such as default in a liability for more than three months.

A caution here: these provisions tell what *can happen*, not what *will happen.* Every decision of the Registrar depends on the facts of the case and comes after an opportunity of hearing. This article does not accuse anyone and is not legal advice.

## When a verbal order comes: what the secretary and accountant should do

This is where the point of principle becomes real pressure. The secretary and accountant work with the chairman every day, and "saying no" is not easy. A practical, **polite but firm** way:

1. **Ask, don't refuse:** "All right, when shall we put this in a resolution, at the next meeting or shall we call one today?" In most cases the solution lies in calling a meeting.
2. **For work within the fixed limit** (delegated to the chairman by the bye-laws or an earlier resolution), write the reference: "Under resolution no. 14 dated ___."
3. **For what is beyond the limit,** ask for written instructions and write the full reason on the voucher. If it is an emergency, record in advance that a resolution for **ratification at the next meeting** will be put up. (This is practice; I did not find it written separately in the Act.)
4. **Keep a record of everything:** the date of the order, who gave it, what was ordered, and what you replied. This is not to accuse anyone, but to protect yourself and the society.
5. **Do not make a voucher for a payment beyond the limit without approval.** That is the very purpose of Maker–Checker. ([Internal control](/blog/internal-control-in-cooperatives).)

The secretary too is an "officer" under Section 2(m), and keeping records is his responsibility as well. ([The secretary's work](/blog/secretary-work-meetings-records-accounts-audit).) How accountability is shared depends on the facts of each case; this is not legal advice. If there is ever a situation of conflict, take advice from your ARCS/RCS.

## A separate note for PACS

If your society is a PACS in Haryana, keep two things separate:

- **Bye-laws:** a PACS has its own (model) bye-laws, which may set out in detail the powers of the chairman and the committee and spending limits. Check the exact text in your PACS's registered bye-laws. (Background: [PACS model bye-laws](/blog/pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-explained).)
- **Prescribed portal and higher body:** for work that runs in your PACS on the prescribed portal (e-PACS) and by ARCS/DCCB instructions, that process is final; the points in this article are for understanding it, not a replacement for it. ## Common mistakes

1. **Assuming "he is the chairman, so he will decide".** The law's framework is collective.
2. **Writing the resolution later** and putting an earlier date. This is the most dangerous habit.
3. **Assuming a spending limit without reading the bye-laws.** The limit comes from the bye-laws.
4. **Deciding the general body's powers in the committee** (such as the maximum credit limit, bye-law amendment).
5. **Treating the casting vote on a tie as a power over every decision.** It applies only on a tie.
6. **Ignoring conflict of interest** (Rule 30).
7. **Writing only "decision taken" in the minutes,** without writing the basis or authority.
8. **The secretary accepting every verbal order without a record.**
9. **Getting signatures "by circulation" without calling a meeting,** without checking whether the bye-laws allow it.
10. **In PACS, running your own system in place of the prescribed portal/higher body's instructions.**

## Three things to remember

- 🏛️ Final authority belongs to the general body, day-to-day management to the elected committee, and the chairman's power extends to presiding, a casting vote on a tie and delegated tasks; I did not find any separate power to decide alone in the Act-Rules.
- 📝 Whatever is delegated to the chairman should be in writing, in the bye-laws or a committee resolution, with a fixed limit; emergency action should be ratified at the next meeting.
- 🧾 On a verbal order, the secretary and accountant should politely ask for written instructions or a resolution and keep a record of everything; in PACS, the prescribed portal and the bye-laws are final.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the chairman of a cooperative society decide alone?** — In the text of the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989 that I read, I did not find any power for the chairman to decide alone. Decisions are taken collectively by the managing committee and final authority belongs to the general body. The chairman can only do what has been delegated to him by the bye-laws or a committee resolution.
**What powers does the chairman have?** — Presiding over meetings, a casting vote when votes are equal (Section 20(a)), representing the society, and tasks delegated by the bye-laws/resolution. I did not find a separate list of his powers in the Act.
**Which is higher, the managing committee or the general body?** — The general body. As per Section 25(1), final authority lies in the general body of members; the managing committee is its elected management team.
**What does the general body decide?** — The work plan, consideration of the audit report, the list of defaulters, funds, elections, bye-law amendments (Section 25(3)), and the maximum credit limit (Rule 24).
**Can the chairman be removed?** — Yes. As per Section 30A, if a no-confidence motion is passed by a two-thirds majority of elected members in a specially called meeting, the chairman leaves office at once; the motion can be brought only one year after the election.
**For how many years can someone be chairman?** — As per Section 30, after being chairman/vice-chairman continuously for 10 years, a gap of at least 5 years is required. The committee's term is 5 years (Section 28(4)).
**What should the secretary do if the chairman gives a verbal order?** — Politely ask for a resolution or written instructions, do work within the fixed limit by citing the resolution, and keep a record of the order and the reply. This is practical advice, not a legal opinion.
**What can the Registrar do about the chairman's decision?** — As per Section 27, he can suspend for up to 6 months a resolution of the managing committee that is outside the powers under the Act/Rules/bye-laws or against interest; in serious cases there is also the process of removing the committee or a member (Sections 34, 35).
**When can a PACS committee be removed?** — As per the proviso to Section 34(1), only in three situations: loss for three years in a row, serious financial irregularity/fraud, or judicial directions/permanent lack of quorum, and after a hearing.
**What is the spending limit the chairman can approve alone?** — I did not find any such fixed limit in the Act or Rules. It is fixed by your bye-laws or a committee resolution; check your PACS's/society's registered bye-laws.

## In the end

The right answer to "how much power does the chairman have" is neither "a lot" nor "none at all"; it is: **as much as the bye-laws and the committee have delegated in writing, and for every other decision, the committee and the general body.** This framework does not weaken the chairman; it protects him, and the secretary and accountant too, from questions later. In a society where every decision shows up in a meeting, with a resolution and on record, there are fewer disputes and more trust.
