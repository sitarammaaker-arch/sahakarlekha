# Financial year and accounting period: what time the accounts are for

Every report, every opening balance, every year-end closing is tied to one thing — **the period of time**. Once you understand the **financial year and accounting period** properly, the accounts, the audit and year-on-year comparisons become meaningful; if you don't, income and expenses get posted in the wrong year and the whole account goes astray. This article explains both of these — and along with them confusing income-tax terms like "Previous Year vs Assessment Year" — in simple language, with examples and sources.

## What is a financial year

The financial year is the 12-month period for which the society prepares its accounts. In India it usually runs from **1 April to 31 March of the next year**. This period is the basis of accounting, taxation and compliance — all of them.

- Under the **Companies Act, 2013 (Section 2(41))** too, the financial year of companies normally ends on **31 March** (a new company's first period can be up to 18 months, with RoC permission).
- Cooperative societies also usually run on this same April–March cycle.
- Adopting April–March in India is a long historical tradition, and now both income tax and company law are based on this period — so it has become the standard.

> 💡 Note — the financial year is not the calendar year (January–December); in India it is April–March.

## What is an accounting period

An accounting period is the block of time for which results are measured — usually the whole financial year, but also a month or a quarter for **internal review**.

| Purpose | Period |
| --- | --- |
| Final accounts, audit, returns | The whole financial year |
| Management review / MIS | Month / quarter |
| Cash-bank reconciliation | Monthly |

So the "financial year" is a standard 12-month period, while an "accounting period" can be any fixed block of time for which you take out a report.

**Example:** the same society can give its board a "monthly recovery report" every month (monthly period), a "budget vs actual" every quarter (quarterly period), and "final accounts" at the end of the year (the full financial year) — all three are different accounting periods, but the base is the same financial year.

## In income tax: Previous Year vs Assessment Year

This is where the most confusion happens, so understand it clearly:

- **Previous Year (Section 2(34)/Section 3):** the financial year in which **income is earned** (April–March).
- **Assessment Year (Section 2(9)):** the year **immediately after** it, in which tax on that income is assessed and the return is filed (this also starts on 1 April).

**Example:** income earned from 1 April 2025 – 31 March 2026 → this is **Previous Year 2025-26**, and its **Assessment Year will be 2026-27** (when you file the ITR). (Source: [Income Tax Department — Section 3, previous year](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/Pages/acts/income-tax-act.aspx))

| Income earned (Previous Year) | Tax assessment (Assessment Year) |
| --- | --- |
| 2024-25 (April 24 – March 25) | 2025-26 |
| 2025-26 (April 25 – March 26) | 2026-27 |
| 2026-27 (April 26 – March 27) | 2027-28 |

> 💡 Simple rule: the year you earned = Previous Year; the next year, when you paid tax on it = Assessment Year. (See: [Annual and statutory returns](/blog/annual-statutory-returns).)

## The principles behind the accounting period

The period is not just a date — it rests on some basic accounting principles:

- **Periodicity:** the endless life of a business is divided into small, fixed blocks of time, and the result of each block is measured.
- **Accrual:** income/expenses are recorded in the period they relate to — even if the cash comes in or goes out later.
- **Matching:** the income of a period is matched with the expenses of the same period; only then does the correct profit or loss come out.
- **Going concern:** it is assumed that the institution will keep running in the near future, so assets are shown not at the "closing-down value" of an immediate sale but at normal value, and period-wise measurement becomes meaningful.

**Example:** the money for a sale made in March came in April — so under the accrual principle that sale is income of **the financial year that includes March**, not of April. (See: [Accounting assumptions](/blog/accounting-assumptions-going-concern-prudence).)

## Financial year vs calendar year

- **Calendar year:** 1 January – 31 December (the everyday year).
- **Financial year (India):** 1 April – 31 March (the year of accounts).

So when someone says "this year's accounts", ask — calendar year or financial year? In accounting the base is always the **financial year**.

## Why fixing the period is necessary

When the period is fixed, income and expenses are matched to the right block of time — only then are results and comparisons meaningful:

- **Comparison:** this year vs last year's profit/recovery can be compared properly only when both have the same period.
- **Audit and returns:** only the final accounts of the whole financial year form the basis of audit and returns.
- **Reports:** every report is always tied to a period — "from which date to which date".

**Example:** if a society calls 15 months of figures "one year's profit" and compares it with the previous 12 months, the profit will look inflated — the comparison becomes false. So always keep the period the same and clear.

> ⚠️ Choosing the right financial year at setup is necessary — it affects every later report, the opening balance and the year-end closing.

## The practical effect for a society

The financial year is not just a date — it binds the society's whole year:

- **Opening/closing:** one year's Closing Balance becomes the next year's Opening — so the year must be closed correctly.
- **Comparative reports:** "this year vs last year" in the Balance Sheet and the income and expenditure account shows correctly only when the period is the same.
- **Statutory deadlines:** audit, AGM, income tax/TDS/GST returns — all are counted from this FY.
- **FY lock:** when the year is closed, the period should be locked so that old entries are not changed by mistake.
- **Member dividend/patronage:** decided only on the whole year's surplus.

> 💡 So one wrong FY date at setup can affect the whole year's reports and compliance — keep it right from the start.

## Financial years differ around the world

Every country's financial year is not the same — in many countries it is January–December, in some July–June. India has chosen April–March (a historical tradition). So when comparing with a foreign institution/report, keep the difference in period in mind — otherwise the figures will not match.

## The rhythm of the year — the accounting cycle

A financial year runs like one complete cycle:

1. **April (start):** a new **Opening Balance** from last year's Closing Balance; the new FY set up. (See: [Opening balances for a new society](/blog/opening-balances-new-society).)
2. **Through the year:** daily voucher entries → cash/bank book, ledger; month-end reconciliation (BRS).
3. **Quarterly:** internal review, recovery/budget comparison.
4. **March (end):** final accounts prepared → Trial Balance → profit and loss/income and expenditure → Balance Sheet.
5. **Year-end closing:** audit, settling objections, accounts passed in the AGM, **FY lock**, and the next year's Opening Balance. (See: [Year-end closing and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).)

> 💡 This same cycle repeats every year — so a clean end of one year is a clean start of the next. If a link is missed in between (such as reconciliation not done or FY lock not applied), the next year also starts with the same problem. That is why every step must be completed on time. (See: [New financial year and budget](/blog/new-financial-year-and-budget).)

## The first accounting period (new society)

A new society's first financial year may not be a full 12 months — for example, if registration happened mid-year, the first period can be shorter (or, as per the rules, longer). So entering the correct dates of the first period at setup is necessary, otherwise the very first report will go astray.

## Common confusions

- **"Financial year = January–December"** — no; in India it is April–March.
- **"Previous Year and Assessment Year are the same"** — no; PY is the year of earning, AY the next year (of tax assessment).
- **"Reports only at the end of the year"** — no; by choosing a period you can get reports for a month/quarter too.
- **"Income only when cash comes"** — under the accrual principle income belongs to the period it relates to, not the date cash is received.

## Common mistakes related to the period

- **Entry in the wrong year:** posting March's expense in April — this makes both years' profit wrong. Prevention: decide the period from the transaction date.
- **Mixing calendar and financial year:** saying "this year" and taking January–December. Prevention: in accounting always April–March.
- **Picking up the Opening Balance wrongly:** not bringing last year's Closing correctly. Prevention: the next Opening only after the year-end closing.
- **Not applying the FY lock:** entries of a closed year get changed. Prevention: lock the period as soon as the year is closed.
- **Wrong date for the first period:** not entering a new society's first (short/long) period properly. Prevention: set the first period according to the registration date.

## Three things to remember
- 🗓️ In India the financial year = 1 April to 31 March (not the calendar year).
- 📏 Fixing the period matches income and expenses correctly (accrual + matching).
- ⚙️ Choose the right FY at setup — every report, opening and year-end closing is linked to it.

## Frequently asked questions

**From when to when is the financial year?** — In India usually 1 April to 31 March.
**What is the difference between an accounting period and the financial year?** — The financial year is a standard 12-month period; an accounting period can also be a month/quarter.
**What is the difference between Previous Year and Assessment Year?** — Previous Year is the year in which income was earned; Assessment Year is the next year, when tax on it is assessed/the return is filed.
**Can a report be had for any period?** — Yes; reports are taken out by choosing a period (date-to-date).
**How many months is a new society's first financial year?** — It depends on the registration date — it can be less than a full 12 months or, as per the rules, longer too.
**The money for a March sale came in April — which year's income is it?** — Under the accrual principle it is income of the financial year that includes March.
**Is the financial year April–March in every country?** — No; in many countries it is January–December or July–June. In India it is April–March.
**Why is the FY lock necessary?** — Locking the period when the year is closed means old entries are not changed by mistake, and the final accounts and audit stay safe.
**What is the link between Closing Balance and Opening Balance?** — One financial year's Closing Balance becomes the next year's Opening Balance — so a correct end of the year is a correct start of the next.
**What is the difference between accrual and cash basis?** — Under accrual, income/expenses are recorded in the period they relate to (even if the money comes in or goes out later); under cash basis only on the movement of money. Accrual is better for correct period measurement.
**Can a society choose a financial year other than April–March?** — In India tax and company law are based on April–March, so in practice this is the standard; for any special situation confirm with the Registrar/CA.
**Is a monthly/quarterly report also an "accounting period"?** — Yes; any fixed block of time for which you measure results is an accounting period — the whole financial year is its largest form.

## In the end
A correct understanding of time is the foundation of accounting. Choose the right financial year (April–March) and period, follow accrual and matching, and tie every report to its period — then the whole year's reports and year-on-year comparisons stay reliable. Look at income tax PY/AY in the same rhythm, and the return will not be confusing either. In short — fixing the period is not a technical formality, it is the first condition of every correct figure; get it right at the start, and the whole year stays easy.
