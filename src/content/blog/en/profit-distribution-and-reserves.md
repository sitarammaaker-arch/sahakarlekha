# Distribution of profit: reserve fund, dividend and bonus

After a year's work, when the society makes a profit, the most important question comes up — "What do we do with this profit now?" Distributing it straight among members is the most common and the most costly mistake. Cooperative law says that profit must be distributed in a **fixed order**. This article explains that order and its accounting in simple language.

## First understand: profit is distributed with thought

The profit (net profit) left after purchases and sales, and income and expenses, is divided into different parts according to fixed rules. This distribution is approved by the general body (General Body). **The whole profit never goes into dividend.**

> 💡 To understand how profit is worked out, first see [how to read reports](/blog/how-to-read-financial-reports).

In a cooperative society this "profit" is often also called **surplus**, because the aim is not just to earn profit but to serve the members. Whatever the name, the order of rules for distribution stays the same.

## Where does "net profit" come from?

Before distribution, it is important to understand where the "profit" being talked about came from. What is left after reducing all expenses from the whole year's income is the **net profit** — it comes from the Income & Expenditure account:

> 📘 **Net profit = total income − total expenditure**

This includes all income such as trading profit, interest and commission, and all expenses such as salary, rent, interest paid and depreciation. This final figure is the basis of distribution — so the distribution will be correct only when the Income & Expenditure account has been prepared correctly. (See: [basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics).)

## The correct order of distribution

In the cooperative law of most states, profit is distributed in this order:

1. **Reserve fund** — first of all (legally required).
2. **Other required funds** — cooperative education fund, member welfare/building fund and so on (as the society's rules say).
3. **Dividend** — on members' share capital, up to the fixed rate.
4. **Bonus (patronage bonus)** — according to how much business the member did with the society (purchases/sales).
5. **Balance** — carry it forward to the next year.

> ⚠️ Rates and limits differ according to each state's law and the society's rules (bye-laws). Do confirm the **current rate** that applies to your society from your rules/the cooperative department — this article only explains the method of accounting.

## 1. Reserve fund (first of all)

This is the fund that makes the society strong and helps in difficult times. Usually **at least a quarter of the profit (25%)** must be put into it (the correct rate depends on the law).

Example: profit ₹1,00,000 → reserve fund (25%) = ₹25,000. (Here 25% is only an example rate; see the exact rate that applies to your society in the Rule-74 section below.)

Entry:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profit distribution account | ₹25,000 | — |
| Reserve fund | — | ₹25,000 |

## The actual reserve-fund rate in Haryana: Rule 74
The 25% mentioned above is a general/maximum example — **Rule 74** of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules, 1989 sets the actual limit as follows: **at least 10% (one-tenth) of net profit** must go to the reserve fund; the Registrar can raise it by order, but **not above 25% (one-quarter)**. So the legal range is 10%–25% — confirm the exact rate that applies to your society from the bye-laws/Registrar's order.

> ⚠️ Rule 74(2): the reserve fund is "indivisible" — no member has any fixed share in it (it is different from share capital). Rule 74(3): if this fund is invested/deposited somewhere, it cannot be withdrawn, pledged or used for any other purpose — even for making good a loss, the Registrar's approval is needed first.

### What is the reserve fund actually used for?

The reserve fund is like the society's "emergency savings" — it is not money lying idle:

- **Making good losses:** a loss in a bad year is handled from this (in Haryana, the Registrar's approval is needed first for this too — Rule 74(3)).
- **Credit and stability:** banks/federations trust a society with strong reserves more.
- **Long-term strength:** it keeps adding up every year and makes the society's foundation thicker.

That is why the law keeps it "indivisible" — no member has a separate share in it, and it is completely separate from share capital.

## 2. Other required funds

According to the society's rules, small parts are set aside for the cooperative education fund, welfare fund and so on. Take these out **before** dividend too. Keep a separate account for each fund so that it is clearly visible in the audit.

These funds have their own purposes — the cooperative education fund for training of members/employees and cooperative awareness, the welfare fund for member-interest activities, and the building fund for the society's own structure. Which funds apply, and at what percentage, is decided by your bye-laws and state law — so check your society's list and rates against the bye-laws.

## 3. What is left now: "distributable profit"

Dividend and bonus can be given only from the **remaining** amount:

> 📘 **Distributable profit = total profit − reserve fund − other required funds**

## 4. Dividend (on shares)

Dividend is given on members' **share capital**, up to the fixed rate. First it becomes a liability, then it reduces on payment.

At the time of declaration:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profit distribution account | ₹X | — |
| Dividend payable (liability) | — | ₹X |

On payment, Dividend payable Dr and Cash/Bank Cr. (A correct record of shares is essential — see [member and share register](/blog/member-and-share-accounting).)

## 5. Bonus (patronage bonus)

This is given **according to the member's business** — the more a member bought from or sold to the society, the more they get. It reflects the cooperative principle of "service first, profit later". Its accounting is also like dividend (distribution → liability → payment).

### Dividend vs bonus — keep the difference clear

Both go to members, but the basis is completely different:

| Aspect | Dividend | Patronage bonus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | **Share capital** held | **Business** done with the society (purchases/sales) |
| Who gets more | The one with more shares | The one who did more transactions |
| Principle | Return on capital | Return on service/patronage |

> 🔍 This "patronage bonus" is what sets a cooperative society apart from an ordinary company — here not just capital, but the business done with the society is also rewarded.

## At a glance: example of ₹1,00,000 profit

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Total profit | ₹1,00,000 |
| (−) Reserve fund (25%) | ₹25,000 |
| (−) Cooperative education/welfare fund | ₹3,000 |
| **Distributable profit** | **₹72,000** |
| (−) Dividend (on shares) | ₹50,000 |
| (−) Bonus (patronage bonus) | ₹15,000 |
| Balance (carry forward) | ₹7,000 |

## The full distribution — entry by entry

Now look at the ₹1,00,000 example above in the form of entries. Suppose the general body has approved everything:

| Step | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Reserve fund | Profit distribution | Reserve fund | ₹25,000 |
| 2. Education/welfare fund | Profit distribution | Related fund | ₹3,000 |
| 3. Dividend (declaration) | Profit distribution | Dividend payable | ₹50,000 |
| 4. Bonus (declaration) | Profit distribution | Bonus payable | ₹15,000 |

All four Cr entries increase funds/liabilities, and the "profit distribution account" keeps reducing. The remaining ₹7,000 is carried forward to the next year. Later, when the dividend and bonus are paid, those liabilities reduce and cash/bank goes down.

> 🔍 Note — here the total distribution (25,000 + 3,000 + 50,000 + 15,000 = 93,000) is less than the total profit of ₹1,00,000. This is the correct position; the distribution must never be more than the profit.

## Do not distribute more than you should

> ⚠️ The biggest mistake — distributing **more** than the distributable profit. This reduces the society's capital and is caught directly in the audit. The distribution must never be more than the "distributable profit". (For more such mistakes, see [10 common mistakes](/blog/common-accounting-mistakes).)

## Three things to remember

- 🥇 First reserve, then other funds, then dividend — do not change the order.
- 🧾 A separate account for every fund and for dividend, so that it is clearly visible in the audit.
- ✅ The distribution must be approved by the general body and stay within the limit of "distributable profit".

## Effect on the books after distribution

After the distribution entries, the picture looks like this:

- **Reserve and other funds** → increase on the liabilities/funds side of the Balance Sheet.
- **Dividend/bonus payable** → stays under liabilities until paid; on payment, cash/bank reduces.
- **Remaining profit** → is carried forward to the next year (carry-forward).

So correct distribution is not just following the rules — it also keeps the next year's opening and Balance Sheet correct. (Learn more: [how to read reports](/blog/how-to-read-financial-reports).)

## Common mistakes — and how to avoid them

- **Distributing the whole profit** → first reserve and other funds, only then distributable profit.
- **Changing the order** → reserve → other funds → dividend → bonus → carry-forward.
- **Distributing without the general body's approval** → every distribution must be approved by the general body.
- **Everything in one account** → a separate account for every fund/dividend.
- **Distributing more than the distributable profit** → capital reduces, and an audit objection arises.
- **Using the reserve as you like** → it is indivisible; withdrawal/use needs the Registrar's approval.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can we distribute the whole profit among members?** — No; first take out the reserve and other required funds, then pay dividend/bonus from the remaining amount.
**What is the correct order of distribution?** — Reserve fund → other required funds → dividend → patronage bonus → balance carried forward.
**How much must go into the reserve fund?** — Under Haryana Rule 74, at least 10% of net profit, and up to a maximum of 25% by the Registrar's order; confirm your exact rate from the bye-laws/Registrar's order.
**What is the difference between dividend and bonus?** — Dividend is on share capital, bonus on the business done with the society (purchases/sales).
**Who approves the distribution?** — The general body (General Body), as per the rules/bye-laws.
**Can the reserve fund be used to make good a loss?** — Yes, but under Haryana Rule 74(3), the Registrar's approval is needed first.
**How do we work out distributable profit?** — Total profit − reserve fund − other required funds.
**Where does net profit come from?** — From the Income & Expenditure account: total income − total expenditure; this is the basis of distribution.
**Where does the remaining profit go?** — It is carried forward to the next year (carry-forward), and becomes part of the next year's opening.

## In the end

Distribution of profit is the real identity of cooperation — first make the society strong (reserve), then give members their fair share (dividend and bonus). Correct order, the general body's approval and separate accounts — with these three things the distribution stays clean and audit-ready. And most important: the distribution must never go beyond the limit of "distributable profit".
