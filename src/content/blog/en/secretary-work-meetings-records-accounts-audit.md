# What does a secretary do in a cooperative society? Responsibilities for meetings, records, accounts and audit

As an example, suppose that on a Monday morning three things arrive on the same day. A letter from the Registrar's office: "send the minutes of the last meeting". A member asking why his nominee is not entered in the register. And a notice from the auditor that vouchers and cash will be checked next week. From whom is the answer to all three expected? In most societies — from the secretary.

The secretary is often called "the person who does all the society's work", but that description helps no one. The real question is **what falls on the secretary in daily work, what the law says about it, and which work should show up in which record.** This article is a map of exactly that: meetings and minutes, correspondence and records, oversight of receipts, payments and vouchers, member records, reports, and audit documents.

> 📘 **In short:** The secretary is the executive office-bearer of the society — calling meetings and writing minutes, handling correspondence and registers, keeping an eye on vouchers and the cash and bank accounts, keeping member records up to date, keeping reports ready and organising documents for the audit. **The Haryana Act has no list of the secretary's duties** — the exact scope is set by the bye-laws, RCS orders and service rules; but the law says clear things about meetings, registers, custody of records and audit, which are given below.

An at-a-glance comparison of the roles of the chairman, secretary and treasurer is in a separate article, [Roles of the chairman, secretary and treasurer](/blog/office-bearers-chairman-secretary-treasurer); here we cover only **the depth of the secretary's work**.

## Where the secretary appears in the law — and what is not said

The definition of "officer" in Section 2(m) of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 includes the chairman, vice-chairman, managing director, **secretary**, manager, committee member, treasurer and other such persons who are empowered by the rules or bye-laws to give directions about the business of the society. So in the eyes of the law the secretary is an *officer*, and every lapse of the secretary is the lapse of an accountable post.

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The secretary is an "officer" | Section 2(m) | Secretary, manager, treasurer etc. are all officers |
| Calling a meeting | Section 26(1); Rule 23 | A general meeting or committee meeting must be called within one month of a written demand by the Registrar/members/committee members (the threshold for a demand is in the bye-laws); under Rule 23 meetings are called by the chief executive officer or an officer authorised by him |
| One general meeting a year | Section 25(3) | Once a year: approval of the work plan, consideration of the audit report and annual report, list of defaulters, funds, list of employees who are relatives of office-bearers, elections (when due), amendment of bye-laws etc. |
| Result of not calling a meeting | Section 26(3) | If the general meeting is not held in the prescribed time or a demand is not complied with, the Registrar, after a hearing, can declare committee members disqualified for five years, and if the default is by an office-bearer/employee, can impose a fine of up to ₹10,000 |
| Suspension of a resolution | Section 27 | The Registrar can suspend a resolution of the managing committee (for up to 6 months at most) if it is beyond the powers of the Act/Rules/bye-laws or against the interest of the society/members |
| Member register | Section 40; Rule 22 | Entries in the register about date of membership, shares, nominee etc. have prima facie evidentiary value; Rule 22 gives the columns of the register (name, address, occupation, shares, dates, nominee) |
| List and custody of records | Section 49; Rule 36 | List of books, records and securities, with the custodian's signature; a new list when the custodian changes; a copy to the Registrar |
| Not producing/handing over records | Sections 50, 51, 117(5) | Penalty for wilful failure (imprisonment up to 6 months or a fine up to ₹1,000 or both) |
| Cooperation in the audit | Section 95(3),(4) | The auditor has open access to books, vouchers and cash; every officer must give the information asked for |
| After the audit report | Section 96(2) | Opportunity to explain to the Registrar; then remove the defects in the prescribed time and report the action taken |
| False return | Section 117(3) | Fine of up to ₹5,000 for wilfully giving a false return/information |
| Notice of change of address | Section 119 | Notice of change of address to the Registrar within 30 days |
| Balance Sheet | Rule 79 | Prepared within 3 months of the end of the year |

**What was not found in the law:** In the text of the Act and Rules that I read, there is no itemised list of the secretary's duties, nor a clear rule on the method or time limit for writing minutes. These things come from **the bye-laws, Registrar/RCS orders, PACS/society service rules and instructions of the higher institution**. So read the checklist below as *good practice*; treat something as law only where a section of the law is given.

## 1. Meetings and minutes

Calling a meeting, preparing for it and keeping its record are among the most visible parts of the secretary's work, and this is where the result of a lapse is heaviest (Section 26(3)).

**Before the meeting**

- [ ] **Decide the type of meeting:** annual general meeting (AGM), special general meeting, managing committee meeting or sub-committee meeting — the notice and agenda are different for each.
- [ ] **Notice on time** and in the manner set by the bye-laws (how many days before, by what means — check the bye-laws).
- [ ] **Agenda** in writing: for the AGM, include the points of Section 25(3), that is, the audit report, the list of defaulters, funds and, when due, elections.
- [ ] **If a demand letter arrives** (Section 26(1)), record its date and start counting the one month.
- [ ] For full AGM preparation, see the [AGM preparation checklist](/blog/agm-preparation-checklist).

**On the day of the meeting and after**

- [ ] Record **attendance** (with signatures) and **quorum** — how much the quorum should be is stated in the bye-laws.
- [ ] **Write the minutes right after the meeting**, while things are fresh; with every decision, proposer, seconder and result (unanimous/majority).
- [ ] **At the next meeting, read out the previous minutes**, get them confirmed and take signatures.
- [ ] In a resolution with financial sanction, clearly write **the amount, the head and the authorised person**. (How to write a resolution and when a Certified True Copy is needed: [Board Resolution guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)

> 💡 Because of Section 27, the wording of a resolution matters: a resolution that looks "beyond the powers of the Act/Rules/bye-laws" can be suspended by the Registrar. So in the minutes do not write only "it was decided", but also *under which power*.

> 💡 **For the secretary:** The biggest hassle with meetings is remembering everything: which meeting was held when, its number, what was decided. SahakarLekha's **Meeting Register** keeps, for every meeting — AGM, special general meeting, board or committee — the number, date, time, place, agenda, attendance, resolutions, minutes and status (scheduled/held/adjourned/cancelled) in one place. Writing the minutes remains your work; but later, finding the answer to "what was decided in such-and-such meeting" does not take half a day.

## 2. Correspondence and records

Letters come and go every day from the Registrar, the higher bank, the federation and members. The secretary's job is not only to reply but also to show **which letter came when and what action was taken.**

- [ ] **Inward-outward register:** date of every letter, sender/recipient, subject, and status of action.
- [ ] **Filing system:** department correspondence, bank, audit, members, employees, resolutions — separate files.
- [ ] **Custody list** (Section 49 and Rule 36): which register, book or security is with whom, with the custodian's signature; make it at the start of the cooperative year as per the rule, and a new list when the custodian changes.
- [ ] **Address and registered details:** if the address changes, inform the Registrar within 30 days (Section 119).
- [ ] **Mark letters with deadlines** separately — the last date of the time given by the Registrar (such as a reply to an audit objection) should be in the calendar.
- [ ] **Handover on change of post:** if the secretary changes, all records, keys and passwords with a written handover. Section 117(5) provides a penalty for not doing so.

> 💡 **For the secretary:** Record-keeping means not just files, but proof that "I acted on time". SahakarLekha's **Compliance Calendar** works out the upcoming statutory dates for TDS, GST, income tax, PF/ESI and audit from the society's settings and shows them (for now it is for viewing; it does not track "filed"), so that the date stays on the page, not in the secretary's head. You will have to track the date for replying to a Registrar's letter in your own inward-outward register.

## 3. Oversight of receipts, payments and vouchers

If the treasurer watches over the funds and cash, **the secretary is in charge of the entry of daily transactions and their papers**, especially in societies where the secretary also writes the books. If the roles of the two are clearly divided, the scope for fraud goes down. The full list of the treasurer's part is in [The treasurer's accounting work](/blog/treasurer-accounting-work-checklist).

The daily and monthly list from the secretary's point of view:

- [ ] **A receipt for every receipt of money** in serial order, and a **voucher** for every payment with the supporting document (bill, resolution, invoice). (What to attach: [Voucher narration and documents](/blog/voucher-narration-and-documents).)
- [ ] **The person who makes and the person who approves a voucher should be different** — this one rule helps the most.
- [ ] **Check approval before payment**; a managing committee resolution for payments above the set limit.
- [ ] **TDS/GST**, where applicable, should be recorded in the voucher itself. ([TDS and 26Q](/blog/tds-and-26q-for-societies), [GST](/blog/gst-for-cooperatives).)
- [ ] **At the end of the month**, match the Cash Book, Bank Book and voucher file; check whether the BRS has been made. ([How to do a BRS](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide).)
- [ ] Make a list of entries **without a voucher or without approval** and report it in writing to the chairman/managing committee.

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant:** Most of the time goes into getting a signature on every voucher and finding "which vouchers were left without approval". SahakarLekha's **Voucher Approval** page shows pending, approved and rejected vouchers in separate tabs, so the reviewer can see all pending vouchers in one place and approve or reject them. Method of making vouchers: [Voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide).

## 4. Member records

The member register is the most basic record of the society, and because of Section 40 it also has legal weight: the **date of membership, date of cessation of membership, number of shares and since when, and nominee** entered in the register are treated as prima facie evidence. So if something is not entered in the register, it may be hard to prove in a dispute.

- [ ] **Member register** complete: name, address, occupation, shares, date of becoming a member, date of cessation of membership (the columns of Rule 22).
- [ ] **Nominee** of every member entered and up to date; this is what is used in share transfer on the death of a member.
- [ ] **Share register** matches the member register; the share amount matches the share capital account in the books.
- [ ] **New applications** settled on time; for the membership application process see the [Membership application guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-membership-application-guide).
- [ ] **When members leave/are expelled**, record the date and reason; a voucher for the share refund.
- [ ] **Member-wise account** (shares, deposits, loans, dues) — the list of defaulters for the AGM (Section 25(3)) is made from this.

How to keep every member's account is covered in detail in [Member and share register](/blog/member-and-share-accounting), and members' rights [here](/blog/membership-rights-and-duties). The member-wise list for dividend also rests on this register ([Member dividend guide](/blog/cooperative-society-member-dividend-guide)).

> 💡 **For the secretary:** Making the member register on paper and matching it with the share capital book every time is months of work. In SahakarLekha, **Members** and the **Share Register** are on separate pages, and the **Form 1 member list** (name, member ID, address, shares, number of shares, date of joining, status, nominee) can be taken out as a PDF. Every member's own page (Member 360) shows that member's transactions in one place.

## 5. Keeping reports ready

Reports are part of the secretary's preparation for both meetings and audit. Keeping these reports ready regularly is enough:

| Report | When | To whom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash/bank balance position | Daily/weekly | Chairman, treasurer |
| Trial Balance | Monthly | Managing committee ([Trial Balance](/blog/trial-balance-explained)) |
| Receipts & Payments, Income & Expenditure | Monthly/quarterly | Managing committee |
| List of defaulters/dues | Monthly, and for the AGM | Managing committee, general body (Section 25(3)) |
| Budget versus actual | Quarterly | Managing committee ([Budget analysis](/blog/budget-vs-actual-analysis)) |
| Annual financial statements | Within 3 months of the end of the year | General body, Registrar (Rule 79) |
| Annual return | After the year | Department, tax authorities (annual return) |

Keep a **note** of two or three lines with the report: what changed since last time, which difference is unresolved, what decision is needed. How to read reports is in [How to read financial reports](/blog/how-to-read-financial-reports) and [MIS reports](/blog/mis-reports-for-management).

> ⚠️ In any return or information you give to the Registrar or any authority, wilfully giving wrong information attracts a fine under Section 117(3). So match the figures with the books before sending.

> 💡 **For the secretary:** Before a meeting, most of the time in putting reports together goes into collecting figures. In SahakarLekha the **Trial Balance**, **Receipts & Payments** and **Reports** are built from the same books you fill in every day, so there is no need to add up separate registers before the meeting.

## 6. Organising documents for the audit

Under Section 95(3) the auditor has *open access* to books, papers, vouchers, stock and property, and under Section 95(4) the secretary, manager and every officer will give the auditor the information asked for. So the week before the audit should be only for matching, not for preparation.

**Before the audit**

- [ ] Voucher file month-wise, in serial order and with supporting documents.
- [ ] Bank file: monthly BRS, statements, FDR receipts.
- [ ] Member register, share register, loan register, stock register up to date.
- [ ] **Resolution register** and minutes book, with signatures.
- [ ] Custody list and handover letters.
- [ ] **Compliance letter** on the objections of the previous audit.

**After the audit**

- [ ] Make a list of the irregularities, unrecovered amounts, shortfalls or doubtful assets pointed out in the **audit report** (Section 96(1)).
- [ ] When the Registrar's direction comes, **remove the defects within the time given** and send the **report of action taken** (Section 96(2)).
- [ ] On every objection: what was done, by whom, when, with which document — in writing.

Full audit preparation is in the [Audit preparation checklist](/blog/audit-preparation-checklist), and the audit process in [How a cooperative society audit works](/blog/how-cooperative-society-audit-works). For year-end preparation, see [Year-end and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).

> 💡 **For the secretary:** Audit objections often get scattered across papers and WhatsApp. SahakarLekha's **Audit Register** records every objection with a category (cash, stock, loans, accounts, compliance), keeps the last date for rectification and shows the status (pending/resolved) and the date of resolution; the **Audit Schedules** page works out the audit schedules from the trial balance as per the state and gives them as PDF/Excel. *Replying* to the objection and sending it to the Registrar remains your work.

## PACS secretaries: keep the prescribed portal's procedure separate

If you are the secretary of a PACS in Haryana and your society's work runs on the **prescribed ERP/portal (e-PACS)**, do **not** treat the checklist in this article as a replacement for that portal's procedure.

- Do **daily day-end, Maker–Checker, loan/deposit entries and the reports that go to the department** only according to your prescribed portal and the orders of the higher institution (ARCS/DCCB/HARCO Bank).
- Use this article as a **supplement**: for meetings, minutes, member records, custody lists and audit files, which the secretary has to handle outside the portal too.
- If in your PACS some work is on the prescribed portal and some in your own books, write down the scope of each separately, so that the audit can see which record is where.
- For the secretary's recruitment, service conditions and promotion, see the separate [PACS service rules](/blog/haryana-pacs-cms-service-rules-recruitment-guide), and information on e-PACS [here](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).

Before starting an account on SahakarLekha, check which portal or software is mandatory for your society. Deciding this is a matter for your Registrar's/higher institution's instructions, not this article. Work for which a portal is prescribed should stay on that portal.

## A small imaginary example

*(Imaginary: not of any real society.)* Suppose a society has not held an AGM for the last two years, because "every time there was some obstacle". The last entry in the minutes book is 14 months old, only half the members' nominees are in the register, and there is no written reply to old audit objections. One day a letter from the Registrar arrives.

If the secretary had made a meeting calendar every month, checked the member register every quarter and made a list of objections right after the audit, none of these things would have become so big. This is the essence of this whole article: **the secretary's work is not very big, it is very scattered, and the way to tie it together is a regular calendar and records in one place.**

## Common mistakes

1. **Putting off the AGM.** Section 26(3) opens the way to disqualification of committee members and even a fine on an office-bearer/employee.
2. **Writing minutes later from memory.** Decisions, proposers and results are left incomplete.
3. **Not writing the basis of power in a resolution.** Risk of suspension under Section 27.
4. **Mismatch between the member register and share register.** Causes trouble in dividend, elections and disputes.
5. **Not recording the nominee.** The most common root of disputes on the death of a member.
6. **Entries without a voucher or without approval.** The most common reason for audit objections.
7. **Handover of post without a handover letter.** Falls within Section 117(5).
8. **Treating an audit objection as "just the auditor's view".** Take written action on every objection.
9. **Sending a wrong return.** Section 117(3) provides a fine.
10. **In a PACS, running your own method instead of the prescribed portal's procedure.** The prescribed procedure is final.

## Three things to remember

- 🗓️ The secretary's work is not big, it is scattered: meetings and minutes, correspondence and records, oversight of vouchers, the member register, reports and audit files — tie them all into a regular calendar.
- ⚖️ The law is clear on meetings (Sections 25, 26), registers (Section 40), custody of records (Section 49, Rule 36) and audit (Sections 95, 96); the list of the secretary's duties comes from the bye-laws/RCS orders/service rules.
- 🧭 In a PACS, the prescribed portal's procedure is separate and final; this article is a supplement to it, not an alternative.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does a secretary do in a cooperative society?** — Calls meetings and writes minutes, handles correspondence and registers, oversees vouchers and accounts, member records, reports and audit documents. The exact scope is set by the bye-laws, RCS orders and service rules.
**Are the secretary's duties written in the law?** — In the text of the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989 that I read, no list of the secretary's duties was found. Under Section 2(m) the secretary is an "officer".
**What is the difference between the secretary and the treasurer?** — The secretary is the executive for daily administration, records and entries; the treasurer oversees the funds, cash and bank. In small societies both roles can be with one person.
**How many times a year is the general meeting (AGM) held?** — Under Section 25(3), at least once a year, covering subjects such as the audit report, work plan, list of defaulters, funds and, when due, elections.
**What happens if the AGM is not called?** — Under Section 26(3), the Registrar, after a hearing, can declare committee members disqualified for five years, and if the default is by an office-bearer or employee, can impose a fine of up to ₹10,000.
**When should minutes be written?** — Right after the meeting, and get them confirmed by reading them at the next meeting. I did not find any clear time limit for this in the law; it depends on the bye-laws.
**What is the legal importance of the member register?** — Under Section 40, the date of membership, shares and nominee entered in the register are prima facie evidence. So it is necessary to keep it complete and up to date.
**What is the secretary's responsibility in the audit?** — Under Section 95(4), giving the auditor the information asked for, giving access to vouchers and records, and after the audit report, removing defects on the Registrar's direction and reporting the action taken (Section 96).
**What should the secretary hand over when the post changes?** — All registers, books, keys, passwords and securities, with a written handover letter; under Rule 36, a new list is made when the custodian changes.
**What should PACS secretaries do?** — Do the daily day-end, Maker–Checker and reports that go to the department only according to your prescribed portal (e-PACS) and the orders of the higher institution; this article is a supplement for meetings, records and audit files.

The post of secretary is one of *trust* more than of work: a true record of meetings, the correct rights of members and clean files in the audit. For a secretary who ties all this into a regular calendar, a Registrar's letter and an audit notice are not a burden but an ordinary day.
