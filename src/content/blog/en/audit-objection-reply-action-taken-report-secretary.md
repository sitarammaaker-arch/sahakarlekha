# How to reply to an audit objection in a cooperative society? A sample action taken report for the secretary

For example, suppose the audit report has reached the society. Para 3 says there is a difference of ₹12,400 between the cash book and the bank passbook. Para 6 says loans of ₹1,85,000 given to four members are overdue. Para 9 says a bill is not attached to a payment voucher. The secretary puts the report on the table and wonders: what should the reply say? Will one sentence for all of them, "Noted", be enough? Which para is our mistake and which is only the auditor's misunderstanding? And to whom, in what form, and by what date must the reply go?

The reply to an audit objection is exactly where a society's reputation is made or lost. An objection itself is not bad; **a reply without proof or without a date** is bad. This article gives a simple method from the secretary's point of view: what to do as soon as the report arrives, what kind of reply each para needs, a complete **sample action taken report (ATR)**, and a summary of the sections of the Act that sit at the root of this whole work.

> 📘 **In short:** Under Section 96(2) of the Haryana Act, the Registrar gives the society **an opportunity to explain** the defects pointed out by the auditor, and after that the society **removes the defects and reports the action taken** **within the time and in the manner the Registrar directs**. So a reply has three parts: **para-wise facts, action taken and proof**. The period for replying was **not found** in the text of the Act or Rules we read: it will be set by the Registrar's direction or by the date written on the report.

## What an objection really is

Section 96(1) of the Act says the auditor gives the audit report to both the society and the Registrar, and the report records these things:

- (a) every transaction that appears to be **against the law, the rules or the bye-laws**;
- (b) every amount that **should have been entered in the books but was not**;
- (c) any loss or deficiency that needs **further enquiry**;
- (d) any money or property of the society that appears to have been **embezzled or fraudulently withheld**;
- (e) assets that appear **bad or doubtful**;
- (f) any other matter pointed out by the Registrar.

So every objection falls under one of these six. For your reply, it helps to think of them in three broad classes (this is our practical classification, not the Act's):

| Class | Examples | Shape of the reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Procedural** | Incomplete narration on a voucher, no copy of the receipt, missing signatures | Correction + proof |
| **Money-related** | Mismatched balance, overdue loan, amount not entered in the books | Correction/recovery + dates + amount |
| **Serious** | Shortage, suspected embezzlement, major rule violation | Committee decision, advice from the Registrar's office |

## As soon as the report arrives: seven things on day one

1. **Write down the date of receipt.** On what date the report came, from whom, and in what form. If any direction letter from the Registrar has come, note its date and the reply date written in it separately.
2. **Make a para-wise list.** Each para's number, amount and subject in one table.
3. **Check the facts of each para**, before writing the reply. Take out the books, vouchers, bank statement and registers. A reply based on wrong information costs the most.
4. **Decide the class of each para:** the auditor is right (accept + correct), the facts were incomplete (explanation + documents), or we do not agree (respectful disagreement + proof).
5. **Decide the responsible person** for each para: accountant, cashier, secretary, or a committee member.
6. **Place it before the managing committee meeting**, record its resolution, and pass the outline of the reply in the same meeting. The way to write meeting minutes is in [board meeting minutes](/blog/board-meeting-minutes-format-secretary-sample-checklist).
7. **Keep it ready for the annual general meeting**: under Section 25(3)(b), one task of the annual general body is **considering the audit report**, and under Section 25(3)(d) it also reviews **the list of defaulters and the amounts due**. The way to prepare is in [AGM preparation](/blog/agm-preparation-checklist).

## Three kinds of reply

**1. Accept + correct.** The para is right, the fault is ours. The reply has three things: what was corrected, on what date, and what the proof is (attached). Example: in para 9 there was no bill. Reply: "A copy of the bill was obtained from the supplier on 12-11 and attached to voucher 214 (Annexure-4); written instruction given to the cashier that no payment will be made without a bill in future."

**2. Explanation.** The transaction was correct, but the document did not reach the auditor or the matter was only partly understood. The reply gives the full facts and a copy of the document. Example: explaining the difference in para 3 through the bank reconciliation (BRS): "The difference of ₹12,400 is cheques the society issued on 28-03 and the bank cleared on 02-04; BRS attached."

**3. Disagreement.** The society feels the objection does not hold under the rules. Here too the reply must be **respectful, factual and with proof**, with no comment on anyone's intention. As per the text of Section 96(2), the Registrar gives the society **an opportunity to explain** the defects. This opportunity is the society's platform for disagreement, so put the disagreement in writing and on time, not verbally.

## Sample: action taken report

The sample below is imaginary. Change the names, amounts and dates and use it in your work. First the **covering letter**, then the **para-wise table**.

### Covering letter

> **[Name of society]** · Registration number: [number] · Address: [address]
>
> Letter number: [number] · Date: [date]
>
> To, [Designation and office of the Registrar/Deputy Registrar/concerned officer]
>
> **Subject:** Para-wise explanation and action taken report on the audit report for the year [20XX-XX].
>
> **Reference:** Your letter number [number] dated [date] / audit report dated [date].
>
> Sir, with reference to the above, it is submitted that the managing committee of the society, in its meeting dated [date] (resolution number [number]), considered all [N] paras of the audit report. The para-wise explanation and action taken are given in the attached table, and the related proofs are attached as Annexure-1 to Annexure-[N]. For the paras where action is still in progress, the coming dates are given, and progress will be reported accordingly.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Secretary: ______________   Chairman: ______________
> (Name, designation, seal)
>
> Enclosed: para-wise table (page ___), Annexure-1 to [N], copy of the managing committee resolution.

### Para-wise table

| Para | Objection (in brief) | Amount | Type of reply | Society's reply / action taken | Proof | Responsible | Date | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Difference between cash book and bank passbook | ₹12,400 | Explanation | The difference is cheques issued but not yet cleared; BRS as on 31-03 prepared | Annexure-1 (BRS, bank statement) | Accountant | 05-11 | Settled |
| 6 | Overdue loans of four members | ₹1,85,000 | Accept + correct | Notices issued to all four; ₹60,000 recovered from two members (receipts attached); next instalment date fixed for the balance ₹1,25,000 | Annexure-2 (notices, receipts, recovery list) | Secretary | 20-11 | Partial |
| 9 | No bill on voucher 214 | ₹4,800 | Accept + correct | Copy of bill received from supplier; attached to voucher; written instruction to cashier not to pay without a bill | Annexure-3 (bill, instruction letter) | Cashier | 12-11 | Settled |

**Three columns never to leave blank:** **Proof** (in which annexure), **Date** (when it happened or when it will happen) and **Status** (settled / partial / pending). The "Responsible" column is what ensures this objection does not come back next year.

### If the action is not yet complete

For a "partial" or "pending" para, never write a general sentence like "efforts are on". Write: **what has been done so far** (amount, date), **what remains**, and **the next step and its date**. The para 6 row above follows exactly this: ₹60,000 recovered, ₹1,25,000 remaining, next date. Such a reply looks honest, and in the next report it becomes easy to write "settled" against that same date.

## Sections of the Act that add weight to your reply

Understanding the whole audit framework in the Act keeps your reply on the right track. As per the text we read (amended up to 2007):

- **Section 95(1)-(2):** every society must get its audit done at least once a year; the audit includes valuation of assets and liabilities, examination of the balance sheet, profit and loss account and **overdue loans**, and **verification of cash balance and securities**. That is why points like para 3 (cash/bank) and para 6 (overdue) come up in almost every audit.
- **Section 95(3)-(4):** the auditor has **free access** to the books, papers, vouchers, stock and other property, and the society's members, manager, officers, employees and agents must give all the information asked for. So in your reply, avoid any situation like "the document was not given" or "the information was not provided".
- **Section 95(5):** the auditor can summon any officer or member and call for books and documents; in **serious irregularities** he can also take them into his possession.
- **Section 95(6)-(7):** if the accounts are **not complete** at the time of audit, the Registrar or an authorised person can **get them written up** at the cost of the society or the responsible person, and after a hearing can order costs and compensation. Incomplete books can become not just an objection but a direct financial burden.
- **Section 96(1)-(2):** the subjects of the auditor's report (see above) and the society's duty to explain and correct.
- **Section 25(3)(b) and (d):** consideration of the audit report and the list of defaulters in the general body.
- **Sections 97-98:** the Registrar can also hold an inspection and an inquiry. So audit is not the only route by which defects can come to light.
- **Section 101 (surcharge):** if any office-bearer, committee member or employee made a payment against the rules, caused a loss to the society's property through **misconduct or wilful negligence**, or embezzled or fraudulently withheld money, the Registrar can get an inquiry done and, **after giving an opportunity of hearing**, can order the money to be repaid with interest and costs/compensation. Under the proviso, action can be started only **within five years** of that act.

There is no need to panic after reading Section 101, but it makes clear that for **serious paras** (shortage, suspected embezzlement, payment against the rules), a reply like "noted" or hushing everything up is the biggest risk. On such paras, take a written decision of the managing committee and ask the Registrar's office or your auditor/adviser for the way forward.

> ⚠️ This article summarises sections of the Haryana Act; it is not legal advice. In the **Rules, 1989** we found Rule 77 (audit in the manner the Registrar directs), Rule 78 (the Registrar decides the form of the books), Rule 79 (balance sheet and other statements within **three months** of the close of the financial year) and Rule 80 (audit fee) (from a third-party copy). In these **we did not find any time limit for replying to the audit report**: Section 96(2) leaves the time and manner to "as the Registrar may direct".

## What not to do

- **Overnight changes in the books.** Changing the date or amount of an old voucher after the audit can turn an objection into a suspicion of embezzlement. Always correct through **a correction entry with a new date**, never by erasing the old one.
- **A reply like "noted" or "will be taken care of".** It has no action, no date and no proof.
- **Blaming without proof** ("the auditor doesn't know", "the previous secretary's mistake").
- **Postponing the reply date.** The date will be in your Registrar's direction; note it first of all.
- **Pasting the same reply on every para.** Each para needs its own reply.
- **The secretary alone deciding a serious para.** Such a para needs a resolution of the managing committee.
- **A promise without a date.** Instead of "recovery will be made soon", write "₹X by 20-11".

**What the portal does:**

- In the **Audit Rectification Register** ("Audit Register" in the registers section) each objection is recorded as one row: **audit year** (financial year, e.g. 2026-27), **para number**, **category** (cash, stock, loan, accounts, compliance, other), **description of the objection**, **amount**, **last date for rectification**, **status** (pending / partial / settled), **action taken**, **rectification date** and **remarks**.
- Each objection gets **its own serial number** (e.g. AUD/2026-27/001), counted afresh in each financial year and confirmed by the server's sequence (a temporary number is used when there is no network).
- The page shows **search and a status filter**, counts of pending/partial/settled and the total amount; the whole register can be taken out as a **PDF**.
- The objections recorded for a financial year are formally linked into the **audit certificate** (with para number, category, status and description). If the year in an objection is written only as "2026", the portal warns that it will not reach the certificate and asks you to change it to the correct financial year.
- **Who can fill it:** permission to record objections is with the secretary, society administrator and the auditor/internal auditor/external CA roles. The cashier and viewer cannot fill it.
- When the **financial year is locked**, no new objection can be recorded.
- Cancelled vouchers are not erased: the page reminds you of their count and they can be seen on the vouchers page, which matches the correction-entry method.

**What the portal does not do (know this clearly):**

- **We did not find a facility to upload proof (documents) in this register.** Annexures must be kept on paper or in a separate folder; in the register you can only write their reference ("Annexure-2").
- **The covering letter or action taken report is not made automatically.** The PDF is of the register, not of the letter to be sent to the Registrar; make the letter from the sample above.
- **We did not find in the code any reminder alert or notification for the last date of rectification** for this register. Watch the dates yourself.
- **There is no column for the responsible person.** Write it in "Remarks".
- **Filling the rectification date is not mandatory when you choose "settled".** The portal asks for it but does not stop you, so filling it must come from your own habit.
- The register does not **count the reply deadline**: you have to watch that yourself from the Registrar's direction.

**Practical method:** as soon as the report comes, record each para in the register (para, category, amount, rectification date), write the annexure reference in "Action taken", and change the status as paras get settled. Then take out the register's PDF and use it as the base for the para-wise table above. Financial corrections (like bank reconciliation, or the receipt for recovery of an overdue) will be recorded in your normal vouchers and registers.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing a reply without understanding the para.** Check the facts first, then write.
- **Not attaching proof.** If the reply is right but there is no paper, it will be treated as "verbal".
- **Promises without a date and a responsible person.**
- **Changing old entries after the audit** (instead of a correction entry).
- **Sending the action taken report without a managing committee resolution.**
- **Not mentioning old unsettled paras in the next report.** Unsettled paras come back next year.
- **Forgetting to place the audit report before the general body.**

## Three things to remember
- 📑 Each para gets its own reply: facts, action taken, reference to proof and a date.
- 🏛️ The reply period is set by the Registrar's direction. Note it as soon as the report arrives and get a managing committee resolution.
- 🧾 Always correct through a correction entry with a new date; never by erasing or changing the old record.

## Frequently asked questions

**To whom, and by when, must an audit objection be answered?** — Under Section 96(2), the Registrar gives the society an opportunity to explain and then asks for the defects to be removed and the action taken reported within a set time and manner. The period was not found in the Act and Rules we read; it will be in the Registrar's direction or the report's letter.

**What should an action taken report contain?** — Para-wise: the objection, type of reply, the society's answer and action taken, reference to proof, responsible person, date and status; along with a covering letter and a copy of the managing committee resolution.

**What if we do not agree with the objection?** — Put your disagreement in writing, factually and with proof. The text of Section 96(2) gives the society an opportunity to explain; place the disagreement in that opportunity.

**Is it fine to change old entries after the audit?** — No. Instead of changing, make a correction entry with a new date and write the reason in the narration, so the history stays clean.

**Which paras should be treated as serious?** — Shortage, suspected embezzlement, payment against the rules. In Section 96(1), embezzlement and doubtful assets are separate items, and a surcharge proceeding can arise under Section 101. On such paras, take a written decision of the managing committee and seek advice from the Registrar's office.

**How should objections be placed before the general body?** — Under Section 25(3)(b), the annual general body considers the audit report; keep the para-wise status table and the list of defaulters (Section 25(3)(d)) with it.

**What if the accounts were incomplete and the auditor got them written up himself?** — Under Section 95(6)-(7), the cost of writing them up can fall on the society or the responsible person, and the Registrar can order costs and compensation after a hearing. So always keep the books complete.

**Can objections be recorded in the portal?** — Yes: in the Audit Rectification Register with para, category, amount, date, status and action taken. We did not find a facility to upload proof (attachments) in it.

**Does the portal make the reply letter?** — No. The register's PDF can be taken out, but the letter to the Registrar has to be made from the sample above.

**What if the audit objection comes back next year?** — It means the cause was not fixed. Fix a responsible person, change the working method and give a clear answer on the unsettled paras.

## In the end

A correct reply to an audit objection is made of four things: **facts, action, proof and date**. When all four are in the para-wise table and the managing committee resolution is attached, the reply looks trustworthy to the audit department and next year's report keeps getting shorter.
