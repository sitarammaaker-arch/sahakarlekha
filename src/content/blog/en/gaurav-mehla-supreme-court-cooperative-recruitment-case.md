A cooperative society in Haryana advertised recruitment in 2014, held interviews and made appointments to posts such as clerk-cum-salesman and chowkidar. There was no allegation of fraud against the selected employees personally, no unsuccessful candidate challenged the selection, and no forum found any flaw in the advertisement or the interview. Even so, about 11 years later these employees lost their jobs only because three required government officers were not present at the board meeting where the appointments were approved. In **Gaurav Mehla & Ors. v. State of Haryana & Ors. (2026 INSC 641)**, the Supreme Court reversed this whole outcome and laid down a very important principle — employees should not be punished for the officers' mistakes. This judgment has a direct effect on appointment and service disputes in any cooperative society in Haryana — PACS, CM-PACS or marketing-cum-processing society.

## The full case — from the 2014 appointment to the 2026 judgment

Thanesar Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Society, Kurukshetra advertised recruitment on 11 July 2014 for 3 posts of clerk-cum-salesman and 4 posts of peon-cum-chowkidar — 21 days were given to apply, interviews were held on 11 August 2014, and the board approved the appointments on 13 August 2014. There was no allegation of fraud against the selected candidates personally.

The problem started here: under Rule 3 (amended on 28 January 2011) of the **Primary Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Societies Ltd. Staff Service Rules, 2003**, the presence and consent of three officers — the Assistant Registrar, the Inspector Cooperative Societies and the District Manager HAFED — was required at the board meeting approving such an appointment. These three officers were not present at the meeting of 13 August 2014.

Some members challenged the board's resolution by filing a petition under **Section 27** (cancellation of a resolution by the Registrar) of the Haryana Co-operative Societies Act, 1984 — the Additional Registrar cancelled the appointments on 6 June 2017 on the basis of Rule 3 (and Rule 14(a)), the Additional Chief Secretary (Appeals) upheld the cancellation on 29 September 2017, and the Punjab and Haryana High Court's single judge bench confirmed it on 22 April 2024 and the division bench in its order of 29 July 2025. On 19 August 2025, after about 11 years of service, the employees' services were terminated.

> ⚠️ A point to note: the complaining members had alleged bias in the process (para 10 of the judgment), but no forum held the advertisement or the interview to be wrong. The ground for cancelling the appointments remained only the procedural condition of the board meeting (Rule 3), yet the appointments kept being cancelled at every earlier level.

## The Supreme Court's reasoning — recruitment must be seen in three stages

The Supreme Court adopted the principle of looking at the recruitment process not in pieces but as a whole (holistically), and divided it into three stages:

1. **Advertisement stage** — the public notice of vacancies was issued with enough time (21 days). No flaw was found.
2. **Selection stage** — interviews were held; there was no personal allegation against the selected candidates, no unsuccessful candidate challenged it, and no forum found a flaw here.
3. **Final approval stage** — this is where Rule 3 was violated, because three officers were not present at the meeting. The court said that the role of these officers is basically supervisory, and this flaw can be corrected.

The court clearly said that not every procedural flaw is enough to cancel a recruitment, and distinguished between two kinds of flaws:

> 📘 **Flaws that go to the root (fundamental defects)** — such as not issuing an advertisement at all, not giving equal opportunity, or fraud — can make the whole recruitment invalid. **Curable defects** — such as a supervisory lapse in the approval process after selection — do not affect the foundation of the recruitment and can be corrected.

The most quoted part of the court's judgment (from paragraph 59): "We find force in the submission made on behalf of Appellants that for the irregularity committed by the officials, the Appellants ought not be made to suffer, as no allegations had been made as regards the validity of advertisement or the manner in which interview was conducted or that any ineligible candidates were recommended/appointed."

## The simple meaning of this principle

In plain language, this judgment means — **whoever made the mistake bears the responsibility.** If the officers failed in their duty (attending the meeting), the penalty should not fall on the innocent employees who were selected through a fair process. The court applied this basic principle of justice — that no one can be punished for another's mistake, especially when they themselves did nothing wrong. This is what makes the judgment so important and wide-reaching.

## The Supreme Court's final order

Setting aside the Punjab and Haryana High Court's order of 29 July 2025, the court directed the society to call the board meeting again in the presence of all three mandatory officers. This reconsideration will be limited to these points — whether the candidates were qualified, whether there was any disqualification, whether the selection committee's recommendation was proper, and whether any more qualified eligible candidates were overlooked (para 64). The validity of the advertisement or interview cannot be questioned again. This process was to be completed within one month. If the board approves again, the employees will be reinstated and their past service will be counted for future benefits — but no arrears will be paid for the period in between.

## Why this matters for other cooperative societies in Haryana

The Primary Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Societies Ltd. Staff Service Rules, 2003 apply to the Thanesar society, while separate PACS Service Rules apply to PACS ([a full comparison of these is here](/blog/haryana-pacs-cms-service-rules-recruitment-guide)). So it would be wrong to assume, clause by clause, that this judgment will apply exactly the same way to all institutions.

Even so, its real importance lies in a general principle that applies to almost every cooperative institution formed under the Haryana Co-operative Societies Act, 1984 — PACS, CM-PACS, dairy society, marketing society: a procedural lapse by the society and the government's supervisory machinery (Registrar/Assistant Registrar), on that ground alone, cannot be a reason to take away the job of an employee selected in a fair manner — unless the foundation of the recruitment (advertisement, equal opportunity, fairness of selection) is in question.

> 💡 This case came not through arbitration under Section 102 but through the route of **Section 27** (cancellation of a board resolution by the Registrar) — first the Additional Registrar, then the Additional Chief Secretary in appeal. The Supreme Court also referred to **Section 36** of the Act, which says that a defect in the constitution or procedure of the committee does not automatically make its acts invalid. For the other routes of dispute resolution, see: [The article on PACS Service Rules](/blog/haryana-pacs-cms-service-rules-recruitment-guide). Also, a [separate detailed article](/blog/cooperative-society-writ-jurisdiction-article-226) already exists on the question of not treating a cooperative society as "State" under Article 12.

## What it means for whom

**For the candidate/employee:** If your appointment was made after a proper advertisement and a fair interview, but was later cancelled on the basis of some administrative/approval-related flaw, this judgment becomes a strong precedent — not a guarantee, but a solid basis for argument.

**For the society's Managing Committee/board:** Do not take lightly the presence of the mandatory officers set in the rules — this lapse can put the whole recruitment into a legal dispute, even if the recruitment itself was completely fair. Ensure the presence of the required officers at every approval meeting and keep a record of it.

**For officers like the Registrar/Assistant Registrar:** This judgment also indirectly says that punishing employees for the supervisory machinery's own absence does not stand in court — so the officers' timely participation in the approval process is just as necessary.

> ⚠️ It is important to remember that this article is not legal advice. If your appointment has been cancelled on the basis of a procedural flaw, first check whether the flaw was at the advertisement/selection stage or only at the approval stage — this difference is the key to whether this judgment applies — and in any specific case, do look at the original order and take the advice of a qualified lawyer.

## A big lesson — records are the defence

The whole case gives a practical lesson for every society — **correct records are the biggest protection.** If the record of the presence of the mandatory officers at the board meeting (or their due notice) had been correct, perhaps this whole 11-year-long dispute would not have arisen at all. So at every recruitment-approval meeting, record everything clearly and in a verifiable form — attendance, quorum, presence of the mandatory officers, and the whole process. This discipline protects both the society and its employees from future legal risk. (See: [Board Resolution guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the Gaurav Mehla case about?** — A 2026 Supreme Court judgment which held that the whole recruitment cannot be cancelled only because the mandatory officers were absent from the board meeting — when the advertisement and selection process were fair.
**Does this judgment also apply to PACS?** — Directly, it is based on the marketing-cum-processing society's own Staff Service Rules, 2003; the PACS Service Rules are a separate document. But its general principle — the employee is not punished for an administrative lapse — can be used as an argument in the case of any Haryana cooperative society.
**What is the difference between a "fundamental defect" and a "curable defect"?** — A fundamental defect affects the foundation of the recruitment (such as no advertisement, fraud) and can cancel the whole recruitment. A curable defect is a lapse at a later administrative stage (such as an officer's absence from the approval meeting), which can be corrected.
**Were the appointments automatically restored by this?** — No. The court directed the society to reconsider within one month in the presence of all three officers; if the board approves, there will be reinstatement and past service will be counted, but no arrears will be paid for the period in between.
**What is the basic principle of this judgment?** — "Whoever made the mistake bears the responsibility" — an innocent employee selected fairly cannot be punished for the officers' procedural lapse.
**What is the main lesson for the society board?** — Keep a clear, verifiable record of the presence of the mandatory officers and of the whole process at every recruitment-approval meeting.

## Three things to remember

- ⚖️ The Supreme Court laid down the principle of seeing recruitment in three separate stages — advertisement, selection and approval; a procedural lapse at the approval stage alone is not a ground to cancel the whole recruitment.
- 📋 The difference between a fundamental defect (no advertisement, fraud) and a curable defect (an officer's absence from the approval meeting) is the key to this judgment.
- 🏛️ The lesson for the society board is clear — ensure the presence of the mandatory officers set in the rules, and its record, at every approval meeting.

## In the end

The Gaurav Mehla judgment is a big relief and a clear principle for employees in the cooperative sector — a fairly selected employee cannot be punished for the officers' procedural lapse. Its biggest lesson lies in the difference between a "fundamental" and a "curable" flaw: a fault in the foundation of the recruitment (advertisement/selection/fraud) can bring down the whole recruitment, but a curable lapse at a later administrative stage cannot. Its direct message for the society's board is — ensure the presence of the mandatory officers and the full record at every approval meeting, so that even a fair recruitment does not get stuck in years of dispute because of a technical lapse. And for the employee — identify the nature of the flaw in your case and do take the original order/a lawyer's advice.
