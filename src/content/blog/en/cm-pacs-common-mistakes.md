After looking separately at the legal status, registration, tax and accounting rules of CM-PACS, here we collect in one place the most common mistakes and misunderstandings people have while starting or running a CM-PACS — so that you can avoid them from the very beginning.

Points matched against the Act/Rules are listed below under "Sources".

## 1. Treating CM-PACS and PACS as the same thing

This is the most basic and most common mistake. **CM-PACS** (Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activities Cooperative Society) and **PACS** (Primary Agricultural Credit Society) — both are separate societies. According to a January 2025 news report, a CM-PACS will be separate from the PACS already running in the village: a PACS mainly gives farmers loans, fertiliser and seed, while the aim of a CM-PACS is business — such as a petrol pump, grain godown, crop procurement, making food items and cottage industries. The actual activity list of any CM-PACS is in its registered bye-laws. Never treat the two as substitutes for each other (see: [What is CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-kya-hai)).

## 2. Thinking that just completing the headcount is enough

Many people think the application is ready as soon as 10 interested persons sign. Section 5 of the Act asks for at least 10 adults (or a higher number fixed by the Registrar), and Rule 4 adds one more condition — these people must be from at least 5 different families. 10 people from the same household cannot form a society. For CM-PACS, a news report (January 2025) says "at least 11 members", but we did not find its government order — confirm the current number with the ARCS (full process: [CM-PACS registration guide](/blog/cm-pacs-registration-process-haryana)).

## 3. Arriving with incomplete documents

Apart from proof of identity and address, the KYC the ARCS asks for (such as Parivar Pehchan Patra, land/crop records of farmer members) is often missed, and the signatures of 2 authorised applicants on 3 copies of the bye-laws (Rule 6) are also forgotten — these delay the application. Get the full list from the portal and the ARCS.

## 4. Not knowing the legal time limit

Under Section 8(2) of the Act (2006 amendment), if the Registrar wants to refuse, an order with reasons must be sent within **one month** of receiving the application; if this does not happen, the application is deemed approved. Even so, incomplete documents, verification and building consensus in the area take time. An idea of the pace: a January 2025 report put the first-phase target at 500 villages, according to The Tribune (6 July 2025) 141 CM-PACS had been formed by then, and on the RCS dashboard 546 were registered on 18 October 2026.

## 5. Relying on wrong information about fees

Many kinds of information about fees and share amount circulate on the internet. A January 2025 news report says the share capital was cut from ₹20,000 to ₹1,000 (refundable) — but we did not find its government order, and we found no source for a ₹1,000 admission fee. We also found no registration-fee amount in the Haryana Rules 1989. The correct amount is in the society's bye-laws — get confirmed information in writing from your ARCS office.

## 6. Thinking of CM-PACS as only a government scheme

Many people believe CM-PACS is a temporary government programme. In fact it is registered by meeting the condition of Section 4 and under **Section 39** becomes an independent "body corporate" — an institution with its own property, its own right to make contracts and its own legal identity.

## 7. Treating all income the same for tax

It is wrong to think that under the cooperative deduction of income tax (Section 80P in the 1961 Act) the same tax exemption is available on all income. In fact different rules apply to agriculture-related activities, general business (LPG, taxi) and supplies made to members — all three (see: [CM-PACS accounting, audit and tax guide](/blog/cm-pacs-accounting-tax-audit-guide)).

## 8. Trying to distribute the entire profit among members

Under Section 87(1) of the Haryana Act, before profit is distributed, at least 10% goes to the reserve fund every year and at least 10% to the bad and doubtful debt fund, and this fund cannot be distributed among members. Treating it as share capital and trying to distribute it is a violation of the rules (see: [Profit distribution and reserve fund](/blog/profit-distribution-and-reserves)).

## 9. Not keeping activity-wise accounts

Since a CM-PACS runs many kinds of business, not recording the income and expenses of each activity separately makes it hard to claim tax exemption correctly and to get through the annual audit.

## 10. Not realising that approval is needed to add a new activity

If a CM-PACS wants to start a new activity that is not recorded in its original bye-laws, a bye-law amendment is needed — under Rule 10 a general body resolution (with at least two-thirds of members present) and then registration by the Registrar under Section 10. The managing committee cannot take this decision alone.

## 11. Not keeping a record of meetings (minutes)

It is necessary to keep written minutes of every meeting of the general body and the managing committee — which resolution was passed, who took part, what decisions were taken. Many societies put this off, and then at the time of audit or a dispute cannot prove that a decision was validly taken. Especially big decisions (new activity, loans, election of office-bearers) become legally weak without a recorded resolution.

## 12. Taking the general body meeting (AGM) lightly

The general body is not just a formality — it is the society's highest decision-making body, where the annual accounts, budget and big decisions are placed before members and approved. In Haryana, after the 2013 amendment, under Section 25(3) the general body meeting must be held within 6 months of the end of the financial year. Not calling the AGM on time, or not presenting the accounts properly in it, is a compliance lapse and also weakens members' trust. The "one member–one vote" rule of Section 20 comes alive here — take it seriously.

## 13. Mixing society money and personal money

Office-bearers sometimes keep the society's cash and their own personal money in the same place, or meet personal expenses from the society's account. This is among the most dangerous habits — it muddles the accounts, creates audit objections, and breaks members' trust. Always keep the society's money in a separate account, with separate accounts; every withdrawal should have a proper voucher and approval.

## 14. Neglecting member records and backup

An incomplete member register (name, share, deposit, loan, nominee) later becomes the root of payment disputes and audit objections. And if the entire accounts are in only one register or one computer, without backup — one accident can wipe out the whole record. Digital records with backup protect against both these risks.

## 15. Treating the audit as something to finish at the end

The annual audit is compulsory in the cooperative sector, but many societies keep their accounts loose all year and rush to reconcile registers at audit time. The result — mismatched figures, missing vouchers, and a pile of audit objections, which lower the society's grading. The right way — keep accounts clean all year and reconcile every month; then the audit remains just a check, not a crisis. (See: [Audit preparation](/blog/audit-preparation-checklist).)

## 16. Not knowing the rules of membership and share refund

While adding or removing members, the conditions of share refund are often overlooked. Under Rule 18(3), in a society with limited liability a member is generally not allowed to withdraw shares — a refund is allowed only when the society has created a share transfer fund out of profit, and even then the total refund at any time cannot exceed 5% of the paid-up share capital (excluding the government's portion) as on the previous 31 March. The other conditions are in the bye-laws. Making a promise to a member without knowing this later becomes a dispute. Explain these conditions clearly to a new member at the start, and keep every member's share record accurate.

## 17. Treating digital accounting as "something for later"

Many societies think they will first build up the business and make the accounts digital later. This is backwards — the more old data piles up, the harder it is to digitise later. The best time is to start digital from the first day, so that activity-wise accounts, tax claims and audit — all three stay correct from the start.

## 18. Not being transparent with members

The life of a cooperative is the trust of its members, and trust lasts through transparency. When a member cannot clearly see their share, deposit, loan and dividend, or the accounts are not kept open, doubt grows — and this later turns into disputes, complaints and inactivity. Showing every member their position clearly, and presenting the accounts openly at the AGM — this is both mistake-prevention and the core spirit of cooperation.

## 19. Not taking guidance

Last, but important — many societies try to do every task alone, by guesswork, and get stuck on small things. The ARCS office, experienced cooperative workers, or good cooperative software and its guide — all these are available resources. Taking the right guidance on time is not weakness but wisdom, and it avoids most of the mistakes described above right at the start.

## How to avoid these mistakes — in short

- Gather **correct information** from the start (ARCS office + official documents, not internet rumours).
- **CM-PACS ≠ PACS** — always remember the separate identity of the two.
- **Complete documents** and members from 5 different families (Rule 4).
- **Activity-wise accounts**, respect for the reserve fund, and a correct 80P claim.
- **Meeting minutes, AGM on time, separate society account, backup** — daily discipline.

> 💡 Most of these mistakes come from lack of information, not bad intent. If correct information is available at the start, a CM-PACS not only registers easily, but also stays clear of legal and financial trouble later.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between CM-PACS and PACS?** — A PACS mainly gives loans, fertiliser and seed; a CM-PACS is said to be formed for business (petrol pump, godown, crop procurement, processing) — both are separate societies.
**Can the entire profit be distributed among members?** — No; under Section 87(1), first at least 10% goes to the reserve fund and 10% to the bad and doubtful debt fund.
**What is needed to add a new activity?** — A bye-law amendment: a general body resolution (Rule 10) and registration by the Registrar (Section 10) — the managing committee cannot decide alone.
**What is the minimum number of members and families needed?** — Section 5: at least 10 adults (or a higher number fixed by the Registrar); Rule 4: from at least 5 families. The "11" for CM-PACS is only in a news report — confirm with the ARCS.
**Is all income tax-free under the cooperative deduction of income tax (old Section 80P)?** — No; different rules apply to agricultural activity, general business and member supplies.
**Why activity-wise accounts?** — In a multi-business society, both a correct 80P claim and passing the audit depend on this.
**Where to get correct information about fees?** — From the ARCS office; do not rely on old/misleading figures from the internet.
**In how many days does the Registrar decide?** — Section 8(2): if refusing, an order with reasons within one month; if none comes, the application is deemed approved.
**Is CM-PACS a permanent institution or a scheme?** — Once registered, it is an independent body corporate under Section 39 — a permanent institution with its own property, contracts and legal identity, not a temporary scheme.
**When to prepare for the audit?** — All year; keep the accounts clean and reconcile monthly, and the audit remains just a check, not a crisis.
**Why are meeting minutes necessary?** — Big decisions (new activity, loans, elections) are legally weak without a recorded resolution; in an audit/dispute these are the proof.
**When to start digital accounting?** — From the first day; digitising old data later keeps getting harder.
**Why is mixing society and personal money dangerous?** — It muddles the accounts, creates audit objections and breaks members' trust; always keep the society's money in a separate account.
**What is the root cause of these mistakes?** — The root of almost every mistake is incomplete/wrong information — getting correct information at the start (from the ARCS + official sources) is the biggest protection.

## Finally

Starting or running a CM-PACS is not hard — but the mistakes above make it needlessly complicated. If you look closely, almost every one of these mistakes has the same root — incomplete or wrong information. If correct information is available at the start (from official sources and the ARCS office), documents are complete, the difference between CM-PACS and PACS stays clear, and daily discipline (activity-wise accounts, minutes, AGM, separate account, backup) is kept up — then the society not only registers easily, but also stays clear of legal and financial trouble later. In short — keep the information correct, documents complete, accounts clean and transparency intact, and a CM-PACS runs smoothly.
