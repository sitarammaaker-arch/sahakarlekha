# Year-end process and FY Lock: how to close the year

At the end of every financial year the society has to "close" its accounts properly — so that the final accounts come out right, the audit is smooth, and the next year starts with correct figures. This article covers the full process of closing the year, the reason for each step, the importance of FY Lock and common mistakes — all in simple language.

## Why closing the year is necessary

All year you record daily transactions, but at the end of the year it is necessary to "tie up" all of them — this is "year-end closing". Without it you cannot know the correct profit or loss, cannot give accounts to members, and the audit cannot happen. It is the point each year where scattered figures turn into one clear picture (the final accounts). So think of it not as a burden but as the "closing ceremony" of the year's hard work — where everything comes together and makes sense.

In India the financial year is usually from 1 April to 31 March, so this closing process happens at the end of March/start of April. And it is not just an accounting formality — for a cooperative society it is also a legal duty and a duty in members' interest, because the final accounts are what is placed in the general meeting, they form the basis of the audit, and the profit distribution is calculated from them. That is, the whole year's transparency cycle is completed here.

## Preparation first, then closing

One important point — year-end closing does not happen suddenly; preparation comes before it (recovery, stock count, pending reconciliations, documents). A society that starts preparing from February-March has a smooth closing; one that leaves everything to the last week has a rush and mistakes. So read this article together with [10 essential tasks before year-end](/blog/year-end-readiness-checklist) — that one is the preparation, this one is the closing.

## What to do at year-end (checklist)
1. All **transactions recorded** up to the last day of the year — no voucher missed.
2. **Bank reconciliation (BRS)** of every bank account updated and the balance matched with the statement.
3. A single calculation of **closing stock** from the physical count + entry.
4. Apply **depreciation** on every fixed asset.
5. **Outstanding adjustments** — expenses payable, income receivable, prepaid.
6. Tally the **Trial Balance** (Dr = Cr) — if it does not tally, find the difference first.
7. Prepare the **final accounts** — Trading Account (where applicable), Income & Expenditure, Balance Sheet, Receipts & Payments.
8. **Profit distribution** (reserve first, then dividend/bonus) approved by the general meeting.
9. **FY Lock** once everything is stable.

> 💡 The order matters — first complete the entries, then the adjustments, then the final accounts. If something is missed in between, the Balance Sheet will not tally.

### Each step in a little more depth

- **All transactions recorded:** First make sure every voucher up to the last day of the year is recorded — no receipt, payment or adjustment missed. One missed entry spoils the whole Balance Sheet.
- **Bank reconciliation (BRS):** Match the balance of every bank account with the statement; record missed bank charges/interest in the books. (See: [Bank reconciliation (BRS)](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide) — if available.)
- **Closing stock:** Fix a single value from the physical count, and use the same in both the Trading Account and the Balance Sheet — do not count it twice separately. (See: [Inventory and stock management](/blog/inventory-and-stock-management).)
- **Depreciation:** Pass the year-end depreciation entry on every fixed asset, otherwise profit will look inflated. (See: [How to do depreciation](/blog/depreciation-explained).)
- **Outstanding adjustments:** Expenses that are due but not paid (salary payable), and income that has been earned but not received (interest receivable) — record these, so the year's correct profit shows.
- **Trial Balance:** Tally Dr = Cr; if it does not tally, find the difference first, and only then move ahead.
- **Final accounts:** The Income & Expenditure, Balance Sheet and Receipts & Payments are prepared from these. (See: [How to read reports](/blog/how-to-read-financial-reports).)
- **Profit distribution:** Distribution of net profit in the rule order (reserve first, then distribution) and with the general meeting's approval.

## Final accounts — what gets prepared

The real result of year-end closing is the **final accounts**, which give the full picture of the year's work:

- **Trading Account** (where applicable) — gross profit from goods.
- **Income & Expenditure / Profit & Loss Account** — the year's net profit or loss.
- **Receipts & Payments Account** — the year's cash inflow and outflow.
- **Balance Sheet** — the society's position on the year-end date (assets = liabilities + capital).

All four are prepared from the same data, so if entries and adjustments are correct, they agree with each other. This "agreeing" is the proof that the year was closed correctly.

## Who is responsible for what

At year-end the roles are divided — the **secretary/accountant** prepares the entries, adjustments and final accounts; the **treasurer** looks after cash/bank reconciliation; the **managing committee** recommends the final accounts and profit distribution; and the **general meeting** approves them. Only after this is FY Lock appropriate. When everyone plays their role on time, year-end runs smoothly. So keep the responsibilities fixed and clear in advance.

## Then why FY Lock
Once everything is final, **lock the financial year**. Its benefits:
- During the audit **no old figure is changed by mistake**.
- The final accounts stay stable.
- Next year's **opening = this year's closing** links correctly and automatically.
- Figures placed before members/the auditor stay stable and reliable.

> ⚠️ Check thoroughly before locking — after the lock, changes are blocked (that is its purpose). (For the start, see the [Opening balance guide](/blog/opening-balances-new-society).)

### FY Lock — a safety lock

Think of FY Lock as a "lock" that freezes the year-end figures. Once the final accounts are prepared and the audit is due, you do not want anyone (knowingly or unknowingly) to change an old voucher — because that would create a difference between the final accounts and the audit report. The lock makes sure that the accounts placed before members/the auditor stay the same. That is why a full check before locking is necessary — because the very purpose of the lock is to stop changes.

## closing → opening: the most common slip
This year's **closing balance** becomes next year's **opening balance**. If the closing was not locked properly or came out wrong, the Balance Sheet of the whole next year goes wrong. (See [10 common mistakes](/blog/common-accounting-mistakes).)

This is so important that it is worth repeating — the two years are tied by one thread. If this year's closing was wrong by ₹5,000, next year's opening will be wrong by the same amount, and that mistake will keep running for the whole next year. In a digital system the opening comes automatically from the previous closing, so do not try to enter it again by hand — this is the most common and the most costly slip.

## Adjustment entries — with examples

Understand some special year-end entries (adjustments), because this is where slips often happen:

- **Outstanding expense:** Suppose March rent of ₹8,000 has not been paid yet. Entry: Rent (expense) Dr ₹8,000, Rent payable (liability) Cr ₹8,000 — so the expense is counted in the same year.
- **Accrued income:** ₹5,000 interest has been earned but not received yet. Entry: Interest receivable (asset) Dr ₹5,000, Interest income Cr ₹5,000.
- **Prepaid expense:** ₹3,000 insurance for next year was paid this year. Do not treat it as this year's expense; keep it in "prepaid" (asset).
- **Depreciation:** The year-end depreciation entry on every asset.

These adjustments have one purpose — to show every income and expense in **the same year** in which it actually arose, whether or not the cash came in or went out in that year. This is the key to "correct profit".

## A short year-end example

Suppose a society closes its year like this: first all vouchers were recorded; then bank reconciliation brought up a missed bank charge of ₹200, which was recorded; closing stock was counted at ₹15,000; depreciation of ₹5,000 was charged on furniture; March rent payable of ₹8,000 was adjusted. After all this the Trial Balance tallied (Dr = Cr), and from it the Income & Expenditure Account, Balance Sheet and Receipts & Payments were prepared. The general meeting approved the profit distribution, and then the society did the FY Lock. Next year the opening came automatically from this closing.

This order shows that year-end is not one "big job", but a chain of small correct steps — each step resting on the previous one. And in a digital system many of these steps (Trial Balance, final accounts, opening carry-forward) happen automatically — the secretary only has to focus on entries, adjustments and checking. This reduces the year-end effort and the scope for mistakes too. The main thing is that every step is taken thoughtfully, in order, not all together in a hurry.

## Common year-end mistakes

- **Preparing final accounts with entries incomplete** → first record every transaction fully.
- **Counting closing stock twice** → one single calculation and value in both the Trading Account and the Balance Sheet.
- **Forgetting depreciation/adjustments** → profit will look wrong.
- **Moving ahead without tallying the Trial Balance** → the final accounts will not tally; first make Dr=Cr.
- **Locking too early** → a full check and approval of everything is needed before the lock.
- **Entering the opening by hand** → let it come automatically from the closing.
- **Putting adjustments in the next year** → every income and expense in the same year in which it arose.
- **Profit distribution without general meeting approval** → distribution always with the general meeting's approval.

## A practical order — what when

To make year-end easy, divide it into stages: preparation in February-March (recovery, stock count, bank reconciliation); all entries and adjustments at the end of March; then the Trial Balance, final accounts; after that the general meeting's approval and profit distribution; and FY Lock once everything is stable. Following this order avoids the last-week rush. (Full preparation list: [10 essential tasks before year-end](/blog/year-end-readiness-checklist).)

## Frequently asked questions

**What is year-end closing?** — Completing all the year's transactions, adjustments and reconciliations, preparing the final accounts and closing the year.
**What is FY Lock and when should it be done?** — A lock that freezes the year-end figures; do it only after the final accounts are prepared and the audit figures are stable.
**Why does the order matter?** — Entries first, then adjustments, then final accounts; if something is missed in between, the Balance Sheet will not tally.
**What is the link between closing and opening?** — This year's closing is next year's opening; so the closing must be correct and locked.
**What if a mistake is found after the lock?** — Correction only through the set process (in an authorised way); so a full check before the lock is best.
**Does the opening have to be entered by hand?** — No; in a digital system it comes automatically from the previous closing.
**Why depreciation at year-end?** — So that the reduced value of assets and the correct profit show in the final accounts.
**What are adjustment entries?** — Entries like outstanding expenses, accrued income, prepaid and depreciation, which record each item in the correct year.
**Which final accounts are prepared?** — Trading Account (where applicable), Income & Expenditure, Receipts & Payments and Balance Sheet.
**Who has what role at year-end?** — Secretary preparation, treasurer cash/bank, managing committee recommendation, general meeting approval.
**What if the final accounts do not tally?** — First check the Trial Balance and adjustments; the cause is often a missed entry or double-counted stock.

## Three things to remember
- ✅ Complete entries/reconciliations/adjustments before preparing the final accounts.
- 🔒 Do the FY Lock only when everything is correct.
- 🔁 Let next year's opening come automatically — do not enter it again by hand.
- 🧾 Record every adjustment (outstanding expense/income, depreciation) in the same year, so the correct profit shows.

## In the end
Closing the year is nothing to fear — a fixed checklist (entries → reconciliation → adjustments → Trial Balance → final accounts → distribution) and a well-considered FY Lock keep it clean, stable and audit-ready. Most important — keep the closing correct, because it is the foundation of the next year.
