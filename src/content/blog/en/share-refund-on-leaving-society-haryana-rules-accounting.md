# Is share money refunded on leaving a cooperative society? Haryana rules and the accounting process

For example, suppose an old member comes to the secretary and says, "I am leaving the village and moving to the city. I have shares of ₹5,000 in the society; please give that back and strike off my name." The secretary wonders: is share money refunded? The member also owes ₹3,500 in loan and interest. No one's shares have ever been refunded till today, so what now? And if the money is refunded, how will it go into the books, and when, and from what date, will the member's name be struck off?

This question comes up two to four times a year in every society, and a wrong answer is costly. Refund the money in a hurry and there is an audit objection; refuse it and the member goes to the Registrar with a complaint. In this article, based on the text of the **Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 and Rules, 1989**, we look at when share money is refunded, when it is not, and when it is "transferred" to another member, and then run the full accounting process on an imaginary example.

> 📘 **In short:** The answer depends on **the society's type of liability**. In a society with **unlimited liability**, a member with no dues can leave after the notice set in the bye-laws and gets the share money **without interest** after the period fixed in the bye-laws. In a society with **limited liability**, refund or withdrawal of shares is "ordinarily" not allowed: the routes are to **transfer** the shares to an eligible person, or a refund from the society's **share transfer fund** with the managing committee's permission, which too is limited to **5%** of paid-up capital in a year. If the society has dues against the member, it can deduct them from the share money under Section 55.

## First decide: what is your society's type of liability

As per Sections 2(g) and 2(h) of the Act:

- **Society with limited liability:** one whose members' liability is **limited by its bye-laws**.
- **Society with unlimited liability:** one whose members are liable, in the event of winding up, to make good any deficiency in the society's assets **jointly and severally**.

How to decide? Look at your society's **registration certificate and registered bye-laws**, where this is written. Do not decide by guesswork or by "this is how it has always been done here", because the route for withdrawal, share refund and defaulters changes with this. In this article we are not saying which type a PACS or any other kind of society usually is: the text we read does not decide this by type of society.

## Unlimited liability: withdrawal and share money

As per Rule 18(1) and (2) (from a third-party copy of the Rules, see sources below):

- A member who is **not indebted to the society** and is **not a surety for any unpaid loan** can leave the society **by giving notice to the secretary**, with the notice laid down in the bye-laws.
- The withdrawing member, who ceases to be a member, is entitled to an **interest-free refund** of the **share money** paid by him or his predecessor, **after the period fixed in the bye-laws**.

How long that period is was not found in the Rule; it is fixed by the **bye-laws**. So see both the notice period for withdrawal and the refund period in your bye-laws.

## Limited liability: shares are "ordinarily" not refunded

Here it is different. Rule 18(3) says a member of a society with limited liability shall **ordinarily** not be allowed to ask for refund or withdrawal of shares. The same rule has two exceptions:

1. **If there is a share transfer fund:** where the society has built a "share transfer fund" **out of its earned profits**, its managing committee, keeping in view the overall interest of the society, **shall allow** withdrawal of shares.
2. **But a 5% limit:** such withdrawal cannot "at any time" exceed **five percent** of the **total paid-up share capital** (excluding government contribution) as on the previous **31 March**.

**These words ("at any time") are not clear.** The text does not say whether the 5% limit applies to the total withdrawals of the whole year or to each withdrawal. SahakarLekha treats it as **the total refunds in one financial year** (adding the refunds made after the previous 31 March), which is a cautious reading. Confirm with the Registrar's office or your auditor whether the same reading applies to your society.

**How is the share transfer fund built, how big should it be, what part of the profit goes into it?** The text of Rule 18 says only that it should be built "out of earned profits". Beyond this, no formula or percentage was found in the text we read. So if your society has no such fund at all, this route of share refund is not open in a society with limited liability, and the route that remains is **transfer**.

### The other route: transfer of shares

The member can transfer his shares to an **eligible** person (a new member or an existing member); then no money leaves the society, the money goes from member to member. Section 22 puts two conditions on this:

- The **maximum holding limit** of shares stays the same as in Section 6 (under Section 6, no member, other than the government or a cooperative society, can hold an interest in shares above the limit of up to one-fifth of the capital or above ₹5 lakh, whichever is less; the exact proportion is fixed in the Rules).
- **The two-year condition:** no transfer can take place unless the member has held those shares for **at least two years**.

## Other routes found separately in the Act

- **On change of liability or amalgamation/division of the society:** under Sections 12 and 13, when the society passes a resolution to change its form of liability, or to transfer its assets/liabilities to another society, divide or amalgamate, written notice goes to every member and creditor, and the member has an option within **one month** of receiving the notice: to withdraw his shares, deposits or loans. Anyone who does not exercise the option within the month is deemed to have agreed.
- **On a member's death (Section 23):** the society can **transfer** the deceased member's share or interest to his **nominee** (if there is no nominee, to the person who appears to the society to be the heir/legal representative), or pay its value. In a society with unlimited liability, the nominee/heir can demand payment of the value; in **limited** liability the society will **transfer** the shares to that nominee/heir, provided he is eligible to become a member under the bye-laws, or, on an application **within one month** of the death, to an eligible person. The **consent** of the nominee/heir is needed for any transfer or payment. Other amounts due, under Section 52, the society will pay within **six months** unless a court order stops it.
- **Nomination made in advance (Rule 21):** every member names a nominee for his shares or interest; the number of nominees cannot be more than the number of shares the member holds. So keep the nomination form ready for checking at the time of withdrawal or death.

## Expulsion: what happens when a member is removed

If the member does not leave on his own but the society removes him, under Rule 19 this can be done only **in the general body**, **at a meeting called for this purpose**, by a resolution of **at least two-thirds of the members present and entitled to vote**, and only for acts **harmful** to the interest or proper working of the society. Under Section 114(1)(d) and (2), the expelled member has a right to appeal within **sixty days** of the decision.

On whether the expelled member gets share money and how much, Section 131(2)(xx) of the Act (power to make rules) says the rules may fix the amount payable to members who withdraw or are expelled. But in the copy of the Rules we read, a separate rate or method of share refund to an expelled member was **not found**. So do not fix any amount without checking the expulsion clause of the bye-laws and asking your auditor/the Registrar's office.

## If there are dues: Section 55 and Section 24

If the leaving member owes the society any money, two things apply:

- **Section 55:** the society has a **charge** on the share or interest in the capital of a member, past member or deceased member, on his deposits and on the dividend/bonus/profit payable to him, and the society can **set off** his dues against these. That is, his loan and interest can be adjusted from the member's share money.
- **Section 24: liability of a past member.** Even after leaving membership, the past member (or the estate of a deceased member) remains liable for **two years** for the society's debts as they stood on the date he left. If an order for winding up of the society is made in the meantime, this liability continues until the winding up proceedings are complete.
- **Section 56:** a member's share, subject to Section 55, cannot be attached or sold under any court decree.

So before handing the member a letter like "all paid", add up **all his liabilities** in one place (loan, KCC, shop credit, rent/maintenance, fertiliser-seed dues). But the text we read does not say whether the part deducted under Section 55 is counted within the 5% limit or not. This question comes up again after an example below.

## A full imaginary example

> **Member "Suresh" (imaginary)**, society with limited liability. 10 shares of ₹500 = **₹5,000** share capital, three years old. He owes the society loan principal of **₹3,200** and interest of **₹300**, total dues ₹3,500. The society has a share transfer fund. Paid-up capital as on the previous 31 March (excluding government share) is **₹8,00,000**, so 5% = **₹40,000**. Refunds made so far in this financial year are **₹36,000**.

**1. Check eligibility.** See the member's application, the notice period in the bye-laws, the managing committee resolution, and that the fund exists (limited liability).

**2. The 5% limit.** The limit is ₹40,000 and ₹36,000 has already been refunded this year, so only ₹40,000 − ₹36,000 = **₹4,000** of room is left; Suresh's refund is ₹5,000. So the full refund cannot be made this year. Routes: ₹4,000 now and ₹1,000 in the next financial year; or the member **transfers** his shares to an eligible person. (Whether the ₹3,500 deduction under Section 55 is counted within this limit was not found in the text, so decide after asking the auditor; the cautious route is to count the whole ₹5,000 withdrawal within the limit.)

Suppose the managing committee approved a refund of **₹4,000**, and its accounting is below:

| Step | Event | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Approval of refund (managing committee resolution) | Share Capital ₹4,000 | Share Refund Payable ₹4,000 |
| 2 | Deduction of dues (Section 55) | Share Refund Payable ₹3,500 | Loan account ₹3,200 + Interest ₹300 |
| 3 | Payment of the balance | Share Refund Payable ₹500 | Cash/Bank ₹500 |

The first entry reduces the member's share capital and creates the society's liability to the member. The second entry clears the member's loan and interest without cash. In the third, only ₹500 actually goes out of cash/bank. After this, ₹1,000 in shares and ₹0 in loan remain in the member's name; the member can ask for the remaining shares next year, or transfer them.

This entry structure is **general accounting practice**; no section lays down this entry directly. The actual account heads will follow the society's chart.

**3. Member ledger and past-member record.** After paying (or transferring) the share balance, record the member's **date of ceasing membership** in the member register (under Rule 22 the register records the date on which a person ceased to be a member). Do not erase the record. Because of Section 24, his name should stay in the record as "past member, liability continuing" for two years from that date.

**4. Do not back-date dates.** Keep the real date of leaving membership (when the member applied or the meeting decided), not the day you are entering it in the system. This decides the two-year count of Section 24.

## How this works in the SahakarLekha portal

This part covers only what was checked in the portal's code (as per the version of 21 October 2026). The rule checks run for **Haryana societies** (state: Haryana).

**What the portal does:**

- In **Society Setup**, the society's type of liability (limited/unlimited) is recorded **with a proof reference**; the correct withdrawal conditions are decided from this.
- **Change status from the Members page** (resignation, expulsion, death, disqualification): the **real date** of exit (not in the future, not before the membership date), resolution number and date are recorded.
  - *Unlimited liability, resignation:* blocks it if there are dues; asks for confirmation of "not a surety for any unpaid loan" and the date of notice to the secretary; shows a reminder that the share money is refunded without interest.
  - *Limited liability, resignation:* you must first choose the share route: **transfer** to an eligible member, or **refund from the share transfer fund**. It asks you to confirm that the fund exists and asks for the managing committee resolution number; it shows a message about the 5% limit.
  - *Expulsion:* asks for the general body date, the number of members present and entitled to vote, and the votes in favour, and does not let the expulsion be recorded if they are **less than two-thirds**. It also shows the date for the 60-day appeal.
  - On every exit, "liability until this date" (two years) is shown.
  - The member's **total dues** (loan, KCC, consumer, housing, dairy input) are shown with the status change.
- In the **Share Register** there are three separate tasks: (1) **refund** in one step (Dr Share Capital / Cr Cash-Bank), (2) **two-step refund:** first "refund approved" (Dr Share Capital / Cr Share Refund Payable; the society's resolution reference is mandatory) and later "pay payable" (Dr Share Refund Payable / Cr Cash-Bank), (3) **transfer** from one member to another.
- In a Haryana society, a **refund** brings a warning message about the **5% limit** (counting paid-up capital as on the previous 31 March, excluding government share, and the refunds made after it). A **transfer** brings a check for **shares two years old** (each share separately, oldest first) and a warning.
- For a **deceased member** there is a separate "death settlement": nominee's name, share amount, dues, amount payable to the nominee, the six-month date, and a checkbox for "written consent of nominee/heir has been taken".
- Vouchers are made for all of these; if the voucher is not made, the member's share capital does not change, and if the cloud save fails, the change is rolled back.

**What the portal does not do (know this clearly):**

- **It does not deduct dues automatically.** The deduction of loan/interest between approval and payment of the share refund (entry 2 in the example above) is recorded by you; the portal only reminds you.
- **It does not build or run the share transfer fund.** There is only a checkbox to confirm that the society has built that fund out of profits; the portal does not count the fund's balance or what goes into it.
- **The 5% limit is a warning, not a lock.** A refund can be recorded, with the warning. Giving approval is your responsibility.
- **It does not apply any rate or method of share refund for an expelled member**, because no text source for it was found.
- Changing a member's status **does not refund or transfer shares automatically**; settling the shares is a separate step, and you link the two.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying "yes" or "no" without knowing the type of liability.** See the bye-laws first.
- **Back-dating the withdrawal date or putting today's date.** The two-year liability is counted from when membership ended.
- **Refunding the full amount without deducting dues.** The society's right is in Section 55, but use it with a written account.
- **Not checking the 5% limit.** This is the most common hurdle for refunds in limited liability.
- **Payment without a resolution.** Keep the managing committee resolution (for refund) and the general body's two-thirds resolution (for expulsion).
- **Paying without the consent of the deceased member's nominee.** Consent is required under Section 23(1)(iii).
- **Striking off the name and erasing the record.** The past member's record should stay for two years.

## Three things to remember
- 🔎 Share refund depends on the type of liability: in unlimited, without interest after the bye-law period; in limited, ordinarily not.
- 🔁 In limited liability there are two routes: transfer to an eligible member (shares two years old), or a refund of up to 5% from the share transfer fund.
- 🧾 First add up all the dues of the leaving member, then make the refund entry, and record the real date of ceasing membership.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is share money refunded on leaving membership?** — In a society with unlimited liability, yes (without interest, after the bye-law period, provided the member is not a debtor or surety). In a society with limited liability, ordinarily not; the route is transfer or a refund of up to 5% from the share transfer fund.

**How do I find out whether my society is limited or unlimited?** — It is written in the registration certificate and the registered bye-laws. Section 2(g)-(h) of the Act defines both.

**What is a share transfer fund?** — Under Rule 18(3), a fund built out of the society's earned profits. No formula or percentage for building it was found in the text we read.

**On what does the 5% limit apply?** — On the total paid-up share capital (excluding government contribution) as on the previous 31 March. Whether the limit is on the total refunds of the whole year or on each refund is not clear in the text; SahakarLekha counts it on the total refunds of the whole financial year.

**Can shares be sold to someone else?** — Yes, they can be transferred to an eligible person, but only shares held for at least two years (Section 22) and within the holding limit (Section 6).

**What if the member still owes a loan?** — Under Section 55, the society keeps a charge on his shares and deposits and can set off his dues. In unlimited liability, an indebted member cannot leave at all (Rule 18(1)).

**Does liability remain even after leaving membership?** — Yes, under Section 24, for two years for the debts the society had on the date of leaving; if winding up happens, until its proceedings are complete.

**Will an expelled member get his shares back?** — No separate method of refund for an expelled member was found in the text of the Rules. Ask from the bye-laws and the Registrar's office. Also, he has a right to appeal within sixty days.

**What happens to a deceased member's shares?** — Section 23: transfer to the nominee (in limited liability, if he is eligible to become a member) or payment of the value, with the consent of the nominee/heir; other amounts within six months, unless a court stops it.

**Will interest or dividend be paid on the shares?** — In unlimited liability, share refund is without interest (Rule 18(2)). No direction was found in the text we read about the dividend for the incomplete year at the time of leaving; ask from the bye-laws and the auditor.

## In the end

The question of share money is not one of "yes or no" but a sequence of four things: type of liability, the route in the bye-laws, deduction of dues, and the real date and resolution in the record. Complete all four on one page and the member gets a clear answer and no objection arises in audit.
