# What is NCDC and why it matters for cooperative societies

When a society starts a new project, a godown, a processing unit or marketing work, the name "NCDC" comes up. This article explains what NCDC is, its history and purpose, how it differs from NABARD, and what your society can get from it.

## What NCDC is

NCDC stands for the **National Cooperative Development Corporation**. It was created under the **NCDC Act, 1962** and came into being on **14 March 1963**. Its purpose is to promote production, processing, marketing, storage, export and import through cooperative societies. It now comes under the **Ministry of Cooperation**; its head office is in **New Delhi**.

NCDC is a **statutory corporation** — that is, a formal institution created by an Act of Parliament, whose sole focus is the development of the cooperative sector. This is what makes it special — its whole attention is on making cooperative societies strong and self-reliant.

## Why NCDC was created

Cooperative societies did organise small producers, but they lacked the capital to build large facilities (godowns, processing plants, cold storage). A dedicated institution was needed that could give cooperative societies finance and guidance for these big projects — and NCDC filled that need. Its main goal is **strengthening farmers' cooperative societies** — raising production and productivity and setting up post-harvest facilities (storage, processing, marketing).

## What NCDC does for a society

- **Finance (Loan/Grant):** Loans and assistance to societies for projects — godowns, cold storage, processing plants, farm inputs, sugar mills, dairy and so on.
- **Marketing and storage:** Funds for procurement, storage and marketing of produce.
- **Weaker sections and areas:** Special attention to societies in backward areas and of weaker sections.
- **Training and advice:** Guidance in preparing and running projects.

## The sectors NCDC works in

NCDC's scope is very wide — from agriculture to non-farm sectors:

- **Agriculture and allied:** Farm inputs (seed, fertiliser), procurement-storage-marketing of produce, cold chain.
- **Processing:** Sugar mills, pulse and oil mills, food processing units.
- **Livestock and allied:** Dairy, poultry, fisheries.
- **Non-farm/weaker sections:** Handloom, sericulture, and other income-generating activities, especially for weaker sections.

That is, if a cooperative society wants to set up any project in production, processing, storage or marketing, NCDC can be a natural financial partner for it.

## Difference between NABARD and NCDC

| Point | NCDC | NABARD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main work | Direct project finance to cooperative societies | Refinance to banks/societies |
| Focus | Production, storage, marketing, processing | Agricultural and rural development, banking supervision |
| Comes under | Ministry of Cooperation | Ministry of Finance |

> 💡 The easy difference: for large facilities/projects (godown, plant) it is often **NCDC**, and for banking/refinance it is **NABARD**.

### Understanding the difference a little more

Both help the cooperative sector, but in different ways. **NABARD** is mainly a "bankers' bank" — it gives refinance to cooperative banks, which in turn lend to farmers (see: [The role of NABARD](/blog/nabard-role-in-cooperatives)). **NCDC** gives project finance directly to the cooperative society — for example, a loan to a society to build its own godown or processing plant. So if your society needs day-to-day crop loans, the route is the bank (with NABARD behind it); but if a large facility/project has to be built, NCDC is a direct option.

## How a society can get finance from NCDC

The usual route to NCDC finance — either through the state government, or directly to eligible societies. Broadly, the steps:

1. **Decide the project:** What is to be built (godown/plant/cold storage) and an estimate of its cost and income.
2. **Project report (DPR):** Prepare a Detailed Project Report — this is the basis of the application.
3. **Application:** Apply through the State Cooperative Department/NCDC.
4. **Sanction and instalments:** After sanction, the finance comes in instalments, according to progress.
5. **Use and repayment:** Proper use of funds, project-wise accounts, and timely repayment.

Exact eligibility, conditions and procedure change from time to time — so before applying, confirm the current guidelines with NCDC/the State Cooperative Department.

## An example — a society builds its own godown

Suppose an agricultural cooperative society wants its member farmers not to be forced to sell their produce at low prices, but to store it and sell at a good price. For this the society needs a godown, but it does not have that much capital. This is where NCDC helps:

1. The society prepares a **project report (DPR)** — the godown's cost, capacity, and estimated income (storage charges).
2. It applies through the State Cooperative Department/NCDC.
3. On sanction, NCDC gives the finance (loan/assistance) in instalments, according to construction progress.
4. Once the godown is built, the society earns storage income from it, and repays the NCDC loan from that income.

The result — storage facility for the farmer, new income for the society, and a lasting asset in the village. This is the practical form of NCDC's project-finance role. (See: [PACS godown/storage schemes](/blog/pacs-godown-grain-storage-scheme-ami-aif-nabard).)

## The cooperative sectors NCDC supports

NCDC's support extends to many cooperative sectors — such as cooperative sugar mills, agro-processing, storage and cold chain, dairy and livestock, fisheries, handloom and handicrafts, and consumer cooperatives. The common thread in all of them is that NCDC gives finance where a cooperative society wants to set up a productive, processing or marketing activity. In this way NCDC is a major channel of "asset creation" in the cooperative sector — that is, not just running expenses, but building lasting facilities and capacity.

## What a society gains from NCDC

NCDC finance has several concrete benefits for a cooperative society: funds for a large project (which is not possible from its own capital); often cheaper/concessional terms; guidance in preparing and running the project; and special attention to societies of weaker sections/backward areas. With this the society can not only start a new activity, but also become self-reliant by creating new sources of income. That is why NCDC is an option worth knowing for every cooperative society that is thinking of expanding.

## Link with accounting

When a loan/assistance is taken from NCDC, its use, repayment and project accounts must be shown clearly and separately — so that there is no trouble in the audit or with later instalments. Project-wise accounts and proper reserve/fund accounting help here. Especially when finance comes in instalments and is released according to progress, it is essential to show the proper use and accounts of each instalment — otherwise the next instalment can be held up. So it is wise to keep a separate, transparent project account for an NCDC project.

## Under the Ministry of Cooperation — growing importance

Earlier the cooperative sector was within the scope of the Ministry of Agriculture; after a separate **Ministry of Cooperation** was formed in 2021, NCDC came under it. This means there is now focused, dedicated policy attention on cooperative development — and NCDC is an important financial tool of this effort. Under thinking such as "Sahakar se Samriddhi" (prosperity through cooperation), the emphasis is on making cooperative societies strong, multi-purpose and self-reliant, and NCDC's project finance plays a big role in this. From a society's point of view this means — the climate for finance and support for cooperative projects is better than before.

## Practical tips for using NCDC

- **Prepare a strong DPR:** A project report with realistic cost, income and repayment plan is the heart of a successful application.
- **Coordinate with the state department:** Most applications go through the State Cooperative Department — understand the correct procedure from them.
- **Separate project-wise accounts:** Keep a separate account for the project from the start, so that instalment accounts and the audit stay clean.
- **Timely repayment:** A good repayment record opens the way to further support.
- **Check current schemes:** NCDC's schemes and conditions keep changing — confirm the current guidelines before applying.

## NCDC finance vs an ordinary bank loan

A society may ask — why NCDC for a project, why not straight from a bank? Both have their place. A bank loan is generally general-purpose and on market terms, while NCDC gives finance specifically for **cooperative projects**, often on concessional terms and with cooperative-focused guidance. NCDC also pays special attention to societies of weaker sections/backward areas, where an ordinary bank may hesitate. So for a large cooperative project (godown, processing), NCDC is often a more suitable, cooperative-friendly option — though the final choice depends on the project and the terms.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing project accounts with the general accounts** → Keep a separate project-wise account, so that instalment accounts stay clean.
- **A weak project report (DPR)** → Prepare a strong DPR with realistic cost-income estimates.
- **Misuse of funds** → The finance must go into the same project for which it was given.
- **Delay in repayment** → Timely repayment is essential for further support and credit standing.
- **Treating NABARD and NCDC as the same** → Their roles are different; choose the right institution.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does NCDC lend directly to a society?** — Yes, through the state government or directly to eligible societies.
**Which ministry does NCDC come under?** — The Ministry of Cooperation.
**What is NCDC for?** — Cooperative projects in production, processing, storage, marketing, export-import and so on.
**When was NCDC set up?** — Under the NCDC Act 1962; it came into being on 14 March 1963; head office New Delhi.
**Main difference between NCDC and NABARD?** — NCDC gives project finance directly to societies (Ministry of Cooperation); NABARD gives refinance to banks (Ministry of Finance).
**How to get finance from NCDC?** — By preparing a project report (DPR) and applying through the State Cooperative Department/NCDC.
**Does NCDC pay attention to weaker sections?** — Yes; special attention to societies in backward areas and of weaker sections (dairy, handloom, fisheries and so on).
**Which sectors does NCDC finance?** — Sugar mills, processing, storage/cold chain, dairy, fisheries, handloom, consumer cooperatives and so on.
**What is a DPR and why is it needed?** — A Detailed Project Report (cost-income-repayment); it is the basis of an NCDC application.
**Which ministry is NCDC under now?** — The Ministry of Cooperation (formed in 2021), which has increased dedicated attention to cooperative development.
**How to keep accounts of NCDC finance?** — A separate project-wise account; record the proper use and repayment of every instalment.

One last tip — before approaching NCDC, be clear about your society's need: do you need day-to-day loans (then the route is the bank/NABARD) or do you want to build a large project (then NCDC). Choosing the right institution for the right need is half the success.

## Three things to remember
- 🏛️ NCDC is the finance corporation for cooperative projects (Act 1962, working from 14 March 1963, Ministry of Cooperation).
- 🏗️ Loans/assistance for projects such as godowns, processing and marketing — directly to the society (not refinance through a bank).
- 🧾 Keep clean project-wise accounts, so that later instalments are not held up and the audit goes smoothly.

## In the end
NCDC is like a "project partner" for cooperative societies — when a society wants to stand on its own feet and build a large facility (godown, processing, cold chain), NCDC gives it finance and guidance. While NABARD is the backbone of banking/refinance, NCDC is a direct source of finance for cooperative projects — both different, both necessary. The key to the success of any NCDC project lies in two things: a strong, realistic project report, and clean, project-wise accounts that show the proper use of every instalment.

The simplest formula to remember — **NABARD is the backbone of banks, NCDC of projects.** Whenever your society thinks "we have to build something big" — a godown, a plant, a cold chain — remember the name NCDC, prepare a strong project report, and keep clean project-wise accounts from the start. These three things take any NCDC project from sanction to success.
