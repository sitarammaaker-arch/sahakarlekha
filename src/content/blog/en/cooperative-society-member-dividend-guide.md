# Member dividend: who gets it in a cooperative society, when and how?

As an example, suppose Ramkumar holds a share of ₹10,000 in a society. The year ends, the audit is done, and talk starts in the village — "the society has made a good profit this time, there will be a dividend." Ramkumar asks: how much will I get? When will I get it? And if the society refuses to pay, can he demand it?

And the same questions come to the secretary and accountant too — every year, from every member, often in the same week when the audit and general meeting preparations are also going on. "What rate to keep, what is the limit, how will the list be made, how much will be deducted from whom" — all this means adding things up again in registers and Excel, where one wrong figure spoils the whole list.

The answers to these questions are usually found in rumours, not looked up in the law. In this article we will understand **member dividend (labhansh)** through four questions — **who, when, how and who pays** — and with every legal point we will say which section or rule of the Act it comes from, and where the point rests only on the society's bye-laws or practice.

> 📘 **In short:** Dividend is paid to members out of the society's **audited profit**, on **paid-up share capital**, **within the limit of the bye-laws/rules**. The one who pays is **the society itself** — no government department, bank or secretary pays from their own pocket. In Haryana, under Rule 72, the rate **cannot be more than 10% per year of paid-up share capital**. Dividend is not a "guaranteed amount that can be demanded as a right" — first the statutory funds, then out of what remains.

## What is a dividend?

**Dividend (labhansh)** is the amount the society pays out of its profit (surplus) on members' **share capital**. It is the return on the capital the member has put in — not fixed like bank interest, but dependent on the society's earnings.

Two things are often mixed up:

- **Dividend ≠ interest.** Interest on deposits or loans is fixed by contract, whether the society earns or not. Dividend comes only when there are earnings and the distribution has been approved.
- **Dividend ≠ patronage bonus.** The basis of dividend is *share capital*; the basis of bonus/rebate is *business done with the society* (purchases and sales). The difference between the two is explained in detail in the article on [Profit distribution](/blog/profit-distribution-and-reserves).

| Aspect | Dividend | Interest | Patronage bonus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Paid-up share capital | Amount of deposit/loan | Business done with the society |
| Fixed? | No, depends on profit | Yes, rate fixed in advance | No, depends on profit and bye-laws |
| When it arises | After the year-end audit | Runs through the year | After the year-end audit |
| In the accounts | Appropriation of profit | Expense/income in Income & Expenditure | Appropriation of profit |

There is also a basic difference between a cooperative society's dividend and a company's — in a company the share is everything; in a society it is "one member, one vote" and statutory funds come before profit is shared. See this comparison in [Cooperative society vs company](/blog/cooperative-vs-company-difference).

## Who gets a dividend?

**1. Ordinary members who have paid-up share capital.** The basis for calculating dividend is *paid-up* share capital — that is, the money the member has actually deposited, not just the shares allotted. The language of Rule 72(1) is also "paid up share capital". If the share is ₹10,000 but the member has paid only ₹6,000 in instalments, the calculation will be on ₹6,000.

**2. Membership rights only when payment is complete.** **Section 19** of the Act says that a member cannot exercise the rights of a member until he has made the payments set in the bye-laws or acquired the set interest. So a person whose conditions of membership are not complete has a weak claim.

**3. Not to an associate member.** **Section 18(2)** clearly says that an associate member is **not entitled to any share in any form in the property or profits** of the society. Similarly, the general understanding about a "nominal member" is also that such a member does not share in the profits — but the member's rights are set by the society's bye-laws, so check your society's bye-laws. (To understand the terms: [Member](/glossary/member), [Nominal member](/glossary/nominal-member), [Associate member](/glossary/associate-member).)

**4. A member who owes the society does not get full payment.** **Section 55** gives the society a charge and right of set-off on the member's shares, deposits and the **dividend, bonus or profits** payable on them — if the member owes a loan to the society. So the society can deduct the dues from the dividend payable. The example below shows this.

> ⚠️ If a member is a defaulter of the society, saying "I want my full dividend" does not stand against Section 55. But also remember that the set-off calculation should be visible to the member — a silent deduction goes against transparency.

## When is it paid? — The full timeline

Dividend is not paid straight at year-end. There are several steps in between, and the money can stop at every step:

| Stage | What happens | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Financial year ends | Accounts are closed, the Income & Expenditure account is made | [Financial year and closing](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock) |
| 2. Audit | The audited Balance Sheet is made | **Section 87(1)** bases distribution on the "audited balance sheet" |
| 3. Statutory funds | Reserve, bad-debt etc. are set aside | Detailed below |
| 4. Resolution | The managing committee recommends the dividend | Usually as per the bye-laws |
| 5. General meeting | The general body decides the rate/distribution | **Section 25(1):** the final authority in the society is the general body |
| 6. Declaration entry | Dividend payable (a liability) is created | Until payment it is a liability of the society |
| 7. Payment | In cash/bank/member account | After set-off |

Note: the mention of general meeting approval comes from the "final authority" in **Section 25(1)** and from the general scheme of the bye-laws; check your society's bye-laws for the specific procedure for declaring dividend. Do not forget to include this agenda item in [AGM preparation](/blog/agm-preparation-checklist).

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant:** In stages 2 to 7, most mistakes happen in the *member list and share amount*, because the list is made by hand from the register. In SahakarLekha the dividend is worked out directly from the paid-up share capital in the member register, and if the members' total share capital does not match the books (ledger), the portal does not let you post the distribution entry at all — it first shows how much the difference is. So the mistake is caught before dividend is shared out on a wrong list.

**One important point:** the Act gives no date saying "dividend must be distributed within so many days" — at least in the text I read, no such direct provision was found. So the time limit usually depends on the general meeting's decision and the bye-laws. This is a real gap: members often assume there is a "legal deadline".

## Who pays — and where does the money come from?

**The one who pays is the society itself.** Dividend comes out of the society's own audited profit. Not HAFED, not the district cooperative bank, not the Registrar's office, not the secretary — none of them "gives" it on their own behalf. This means several things:

- No profit, no dividend. In a loss year the question of dividend does not even arise.
- The words of **Section 87(1)** are: the society *may* distribute among members "out of the profits of any year according to the audited balance sheet, and out of the balance of profits of past years" — "may", that is, discretionary, not mandatory.
- **Section 86:** **funds** created out of the society's profits (such as the reserve fund) **cannot be distributed** among members as bonus or dividend. The "indivisibility" of the reserve fund comes from here, and it is repeated in [Rule 74](/blog/profit-distribution-and-reserves) too.

The government's role here is different: the Act allows the government to take shares in the society, and even to give a **guarantee** for the repayment of share capital and dividend on it — but that is a matter of the government's own shareholding/assistance, not of an ordinary member's dividend.

## How much will you get? — Statutory funds first, then dividend

Before dividend is distributed to members, statutory portions are taken out of the profit. For Haryana, according to the text of the Act and Rules I read, the picture is this:

| Item | Provision | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reserve fund | Section 87(1)(a); Rule 74 | **At least 10%** of the year's profit; the Registrar can raise it **up to a maximum of one-fourth (25%)** |
| Bad and doubtful debt fund | Section 87(1)(a) | **At least 10%** of profit |
| Cooperative education fund | Section 87(1)(b); Rule 73 | **Up to 5%** in the Act; **up to 2%** in Rule 73 |
| Other funds | Section 87(1)(c) | Percentage set by the bye-laws or the Registrar |
| Guarantee fund | Rule 75 | **Only when** the government decides to create such a fund |
| **Limit on dividend** | **Rule 72(1)** | **Maximum 10% per year of paid-up share capital** |

The "Explanation" to Section 87 of the Act also states how *net profit* is worked out — by deducting from gross profit the interest earned on overdue amounts, establishment expenses, interest on deposits/loans, audit fees, depreciation, staff bonus, provision for income tax, bad debts and other provisions. So dividend is based not on "paper profit" but on **profit after recovery**. (More: [Loan and interest accounting](/blog/loan-and-interest-accounting).)

> 💡 **Before placing the rate in the general meeting:** The secretary's usual fear of an audit objection is exactly that the rate may go above the limit. In SahakarLekha, for a Haryana society, posting stops as soon as a rate above 10% is entered and the limit is cited, and the statutory limits also show as warnings on the reserve/education fund page — so that the resolution placed in the meeting is already within the rule.

### The three conditions of Rule 72

The text of Rule 72 (payment of dividend) says:

1. **72(1):** In any cooperative society, dividend **cannot be more than 10% per year** of paid-up share capital.
2. **72(2):** In a society with **unlimited liability**, **no dividend can be paid until five years from registration are complete**.
3. **72(3):** As long as a claim of any **depositor or creditor** against the society is outstanding (unsatisfied), dividend cannot be paid.

> 🔍 **Caution:** The text of Rule 72(3) found in the online copy has a somewhat incomplete sentence structure. So I have written it above as its *sense*, not as a word-for-word quote. Before any legal decision, check the original text of the rule as printed in the Gazette for your society.

The third condition is practically the most important: for a society that cannot return depositors' money on time, declaring a dividend appears to go against the rules. This can become an audit objection.

## A full example: how much reached the member's hand?

*(All figures below are imaginary, only for explanation; not of any real society.)*

Suppose a primary society in Haryana has total **paid-up share capital of ₹10,00,000** and audited **net profit of ₹2,40,000**. The society's bye-laws have no other fund.

| Stage | Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net profit | — | ₹2,40,000 |
| (−) Reserve fund (10%) | 2,40,000 × 10% | ₹24,000 |
| (−) Bad debt fund (10%) | 2,40,000 × 10% | ₹24,000 |
| (−) Education fund (assume 2%) | 2,40,000 × 2% | ₹4,800 |
| **Balance available for distribution** | | **₹1,87,200** |
| Maximum dividend (10% limit) | 10,00,000 × 10% | ₹1,00,000 |
| General meeting decided: 8% | 10,00,000 × 8% | **₹80,000** |
| Balance after dividend | 1,87,200 − 80,000 | ₹1,07,200 |

This balance of ₹1,07,200 can be carried forward or used for other heads (such as bonus) as per the bye-laws. Note that the 10% limit does not mean 10% *must be paid* — it is only the upper wall.

Now three members, at the rate of 8%:

| Member | Paid-up share | Dividend (8%) | Dues to society | Set-off | In hand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ramkumar | ₹10,000 | ₹800 | — | — | ₹800 |
| Sunita | ₹25,000 | ₹2,000 | ₹1,500 overdue loan | ₹1,500 | ₹500 |
| Joginder | ₹5,000 | ₹400 | — | — | ₹400 |

In Sunita's case, ₹1,500 was deducted from the dividend payable under **Section 55**. This deduction should be credited to her loan account in the society's books and she should be told about it.

(Make the set-off deduction only after matching with your society's dues register — that is the society's decision, not the software's.)

### How is it entered?

| When | Debit | Credit | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Declaration after the general meeting | Profit appropriation account | Dividend payable (liability) | ₹80,000 |
| Sunita's set-off | Dividend payable | Member loan account (Sunita) | ₹1,500 |

No cash moves until the declaration. After payment, "dividend payable" should be zero — whatever remains is **unclaimed dividend** and will keep showing as a liability in the Balance Sheet.

## Different situations: what happens in practice?

**A member who joined in the middle of the year.** Will the member get a full year's dividend or a proportionate one? Some societies calculate on year-end share capital, some on average or time-proportion. This question is settled not in the Act but in **the bye-laws/general meeting's decision** — and whatever method is chosen should apply equally to all members.

**A member who withdraws shares in the middle of the year.** The conditions for refund of share capital are set by the bye-laws and the Act (restrictions on share transfer in Section 22). Whether dividend for the year of withdrawal is paid also depends on the bye-laws. Check your society's register.

**Death of a member.** **Section 23** deals with the transfer of the interest of a deceased member; dividend will also be treated as part of that interest and will go to the nominee/legal heir as per the bye-laws. Under **Section 55**, set-off of the deceased member's dues may also apply.

**A member who never collected the dividend.** The money remains a liability of the society. What to do with it after how many years — I could not verify any direct Haryana provision on this; so confirm it from the bye-laws/Registrar. Never quietly convert it into "income".

**Member-wise record.** The dividend list should be made from the member register: name, paid-up share, rate, amount, deduction, date of payment, receipt/signature. Only if the [member and share register](/blog/member-and-share-accounting) is correct will the dividend be correct.

## Common mistakes

1. **Crossing the 10% limit.** In Haryana, after Rule 72(1), a rate above the limit cannot be passed — even the general meeting cannot raise it.
2. **Dividend before the reserve/bad debt fund.** If the order is reversed, the total distribution becomes more than the "balance available for distribution".
3. **Calculating on allotted shares**, not paid-up.
4. **Dividend while depositors' money is outstanding.** A breach of Rule 72(3).
5. **Dividend in a new society before five years (unlimited liability).** Rule 72(2).
6. **Declaration without the general meeting/bye-law procedure.**
7. **Forgetting a defaulter's set-off, or deducting it silently.** Both are wrong — the calculation should be open.
8. **Distributing without audit.** Section 87(1) speaks of the "audited balance sheet".
9. **Paying dividend from the reserve fund.** Section 86 clearly forbids this.
10. **Not keeping a separate register of unclaimed dividend.**

## Checklist for members: how to make sure of your dividend

- [ ] Check the **paid-up share** amount in your passbook/share register.
- [ ] Look at the dividend **rate** in the general meeting notice/proceedings (as a member you are entitled to know this).
- [ ] Is the rate **above 10%** (Haryana)? If so, ask the managing committee for a written explanation.
- [ ] Do your own **calculation**: paid-up share × rate.
- [ ] Find out the **dues** you owe the society — the set-off will be deducted from that.
- [ ] Take a **receipt** for the payment or the account entry.

## Checklist for the secretary/accountant

- [ ] Audited Balance Sheet and net profit (as per the Explanation to Section 87) ready.
- [ ] Statutory funds set aside, and in the right **order**.
- [ ] Rate within the limit of Rule 72, and no depositor's claim outstanding.
- [ ] Resolution of approval by the general meeting/bye-law procedure recorded.
- [ ] Member-wise list (on paid-up shares), register of deductions and unclaimed amounts.
- [ ] Declaration and payment entries made separately.
- [ ] Everything posted before the [financial year close/lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).

| Funds created from profit cannot be distributed among members | **Act** (Section 86) |
| Dividend ≤ 10% of paid-up share capital; 5-year condition; depositor-claim condition | **Rules** (Rule 72) |
| Associate member has no share in profit | **Act** (Section 18(2)) |
| Set-off against dues | **Act** (Section 55) |
| Decision by the general meeting | **Act** (final authority under Section 25(1)) + **bye-laws** |

Other states' limits may differ — the 10% limit in this article is based only on the text of the **Haryana** rules.

## The real difficulty of the secretary, accountant and manager

For a member, dividend is a question of "how much will I get". For the society's **secretary, accountant, manager and office-bearers**, it is a question of "nothing should go wrong" — because the calculation, list, set-off, entries and payment all pass through their hands. Done by hand (in a register or Excel), this work often gets stuck at these points:

- **Member-wise list:** adding up the paid-up share amounts of hundreds of members again and again; even one wrong line spoils the total.
- **The 10% limit:** if nobody notices while the rate is being set in the general meeting, an audit objection comes later.
- **Set-off:** how much loan each member owes has to be matched from another register.
- **Payment record:** who has been paid, who is left — and where the amounts not collected went in the unclaimed register.
- **Members' questions:** working out the answer to "how much is mine?" by hand every time.

## Three things to remember

- 💰 Dividend comes from audited profit, on paid-up share capital, after setting aside statutory funds; the one who pays is the society itself — no guarantee, nothing in a loss.
- ⚖️ In Haryana, Rule 72: maximum 10% per year; in a society with unlimited liability, only after 5 years; and not if a depositor's/creditor's claim is outstanding.
- 🧾 There can be set-off against dues (Section 55), an associate member has no right (Section 18(2)) — and money not collected stays a liability, not income.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a member dividend?** — The portion paid out of a cooperative society's audited profit on members' paid-up share capital; it is not fixed interest, it depends on profit.
**Who gets a dividend?** — Members who have paid-up share capital and who have met the conditions of the bye-laws. An associate member does not share in profits (Section 18(2)); a nominal member's rights depend on the bye-laws.
**What is the maximum rate of dividend in Haryana?** — Under Rule 72(1) of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules 1989, 10% per year of paid-up share capital. Other states' limits may differ.
**Who pays the dividend?** — The society itself, out of its profit. The government, bank, Registrar or secretary do not pay on their own behalf.
**When is the dividend paid?** — After the audit, statutory funds and general meeting approval. I found no direct statutory date; the timing depends on the general meeting's decision and the bye-laws.
**Can a dividend be paid even in a loss?** — No. Section 87(1) speaks of distribution of profit; distributing funds created from profit (such as the reserve) is forbidden in Section 86.
**Can the society deduct my dues from the dividend?** — Yes, under Section 55 the society has a right of set-off on the dividend/bonus payable against a member's dues. The calculation should be visible to the member.
**When can a new society pay dividend?** — A society with unlimited liability only after five years of registration (Rule 72(2)); for others, check the limits of the bye-laws/rules.
**What is the difference between dividend and patronage bonus?** — The basis of dividend is share capital; the basis of bonus/rebate is business done with the society.
**What if a member does not collect the dividend?** — The amount stays in the books as a "dividend payable" liability. What happens after how long should be confirmed from the bye-laws/Registrar; do not quietly turn it into income.

## In the end

The dividend rule is simple, but incomplete information turns it into a dispute: members think it is a "right", the society thinks it is a "favour". In reality it lies between the two — **a return on capital paid within profit and statutory limits, with transparent accounts**. For a member this means knowing your paid-up shares, the rate and your dues; for the society — the audit, the order of funds, the 10% limit and the general meeting record.
