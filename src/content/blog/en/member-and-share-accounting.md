# Member and share register: correct accounting in a cooperative society

A cooperative society is "of the members, for the members" — so the record of members and their share capital is the society's most basic and most sensitive account. A small mistake here directly affects a member's trust. This article explains how to do the accounting for membership, share capital and dividend the right way.

## Membership and share capital — two different things

When a new member joins, usually two amounts come in, and they should **never be mixed**:

- **Entry/Admission Fee** — this is the society's **income** (taken once, not refunded).
- **Share Capital** — this is the society's **capital/liability** (the member's entitlement, can be returned on withdrawal).

> ⚠️ The most common mistake: putting the admission fee and share capital into the same account. Then neither the income shows correctly nor the member's share balance. Keep separate accounts for the two.

Its effect shows directly: if the admission fee goes into share capital by mistake, the member's share balance will look higher than it really is and there will be a risk of returning the wrong amount at withdrawal; and if it is the other way round, the society's income will look lower. So the very first step — choosing the correct account — is the most important.

## Who can become a member, and the process

Membership is not automatic — it has a set process, and the accounting runs in the same order:

1. **Application:** The interested person applies on the prescribed form, with the required share amount and admission fee.
2. **Managing committee approval:** The committee considers the application and approves it — do not add the name to the register *before* approval.
3. **Share allotment:** After approval, shares are allotted and the entry is made in the member register.
4. **Accounting:** Only then is the entry for share capital and admission fee made.

> 🔍 This is why "adding before approval" is a common audit objection — keep the order of both process and accounting the same.

## Entry for adding a member

Suppose a new member pays ₹100 admission fee + ₹2,000 share capital in cash. This is a **compound receipt** voucher:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash | ₹2,100 | — |
| Admission fee (income) | — | ₹100 |
| Share capital | — | ₹2,000 |

Dr = Cr = ₹2,100 — the entry is balanced. Here a single receipt has two Cr accounts (income and capital), so it is a compound voucher. (For the basics of Dr/Cr and compound entries, see the [voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide).)

## Member ledger and share register

It is necessary to keep a separate record of every member, so that at any time you can say how many shares they hold and how much capital in total is deposited. In a digital system:

- Every member's own **member ledger** is created automatically.
- The **share register** shows every member's number of shares and amount in one place.
- Total share capital must always match the sum of all members' share balances.
- Every change (new shares, refund, transfer) should show at once in both the register and the ledger — delay spoils the reconciliation.

> 🔍 Reconciliation check: share register total = balance of the "Share Capital" account in the Trial Balance. If the two do not match, an entry has been missed somewhere or has gone to the wrong account.

### Share capital vs member deposit — keep them separate

Not every rupee that comes from a member is share capital. Keep three clearly separate:

| Item | Nature | Is it returned? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Share capital** | Capital (member's entitlement) | On withdrawal/as per rules |
| **Member deposit** | Liability | Yes, on conditions, with interest |
| **Admission fee** | Income | No |

Keep separate accounts for all three, otherwise neither the member's share balance, nor the deposit, nor the income will show correctly. (See the accounting of deposits in detail in a separate guide.)

### One member's ledger — an example

Suppose member "Ramkumar" has these transactions in the year:

| Date | Particulars | Shares added | Balance share capital |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 05 April | 20 shares × ₹100 | +2,000 | 2,000 |
| 12 August | 10 more shares | +1,000 | 3,000 |
| 20 January | 5 shares returned | −500 | 2,500 |

At year-end Ramkumar's share balance is ₹2,500 — and in the same way, the sum of all members' balances should match the "Share Capital" account.

## Accounting for Dividend

At the end of the year, out of the distributable profit, dividend is given to members in proportion to their share capital. Keep in mind:

- The rate and total amount of dividend are decided **by the general body/as per rules**.
- Declared dividend first becomes a **liability** (dividend payable), then on payment it reduces from cash/bank.
- The reserve fund (usually 25% of net profit) is set aside first — distribution comes only after that.

> 💡 Avoid over-distribution: distribution should never be more than the distributable profit, otherwise the society's capital reduces and an audit objection arises.

The "distributable profit" left after the reserve and other fixed appropriations is the upper limit for dividend. So before distribution, following the order — first reserve, then other fixed funds, then dividend — is necessary. (In detail: [Profit distribution and reserve fund](/blog/profit-distribution-and-reserves).)

### Dividend — a quick example

Suppose the general body declared dividend at the rate of 8%, and Ramkumar's share balance is ₹2,500. His dividend = ₹200. The accounting is in two steps:

1. **On declaration:** Dividend (appropriation) Dr ₹200, Dividend payable (liability) Cr ₹200.
2. **On payment:** Dividend payable Dr ₹200, Cash/Bank Cr ₹200.

This way, a liability is created first on declaration, and it reduces on payment. (The rate and total amount are decided by the members' meeting/as per rules — 8% here is only a calculation example.)

## Why share capital matters

Share capital is not just a figure — it is the society's basic strength:

- **Ownership and voting right:** The basis for being a member and taking part in the meeting.
- **Borrowing capacity:** Often the society's borrowing limit is linked to its shares and owned funds.
- **Basis of dividend:** Distribution is in proportion to share capital.

That is why it is necessary to keep every member's share balance exactly right — it is a matter of both trust and compliance.

## Nomination — why it is necessary

Get a **nominee** registered for every member at the time of joining itself. On a member's death, settling the shares and deposits is simple through this nomination — without a nominee, the family has to go through a long process of legal succession. Keeping the nominee's name updated in the register is necessary for both audit and the member's interest.

## Share refund on a member's withdrawal

When a member leaves the society, their share capital is returned as per rules:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Share capital | ₹2,000 | — |
| Cash/Bank | — | ₹2,000 |

This makes the member's share balance zero and reduces total share capital by that much. Keep in mind — before withdrawal, check that the member has no dues to the society (loan, recovery); if there are dues, adjust them first, and only then return the remaining amount. Also record the "Date Ceased" in the register at the same time; do not delete the record.

## Member register in Haryana: mandatory details under Rule 22
**Rule 22** of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules, 1989 makes at least these details mandatory in the member register: name, address, occupation, shares held, date of entry, date of cessation, and nominee.

> ⚠️ The lapses caught most often in audit: adding a member to the register even before managing committee approval, not updating the register when shares/nominee change, and leaving the "Date Ceased" column blank when membership ends — never delete an old record; note it instead.

## Share transfer process

A living member can transfer their shares to someone else of their own will, but this is not an automatic right — managing committee approval and a process in line with the bye-laws are required. In Haryana, under **Section 22** of the Act, the member must have completed a minimum membership period of 2 years before a transfer (except for some exceptions), and under **Section 55**, if the member owes any dues to the society, the society has a charge on their shares/deposits — the transfer is not completed without paying the dues.

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old member's share account | ₹X | — |
| New member's share account | — | ₹X |

> ⚠️ On a member's death, share transfer happens by a different process — through nomination or legal succession under Section 23, not through the voluntary transfer process.

## Three things to remember

- 🧾 Admission fee = income; share capital = capital — always separate.
- 🔁 Every month, match the share register total with the Trial Balance.
- 📊 Before distribution, do check the reserve fund and the distributable profit.

## Common mistakes — and how to avoid them

- **Putting the admission fee into share capital** → keep a separate income account.
- **Treating member deposit as share capital** → a deposit is a liability, separate account.
- **Register total ≠ Trial Balance** → reconcile every month.
- **Adding before approval / leaving "Date Ceased" blank** → follow the process order and keep the register updated.
- **Over-distribution** → distribution never more than the distributable profit.
- **Deleting an old record** → never; note it, preserve the audit trail.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between admission fee and share capital?** — The admission fee is the society's income (not returned); share capital is the member's entitlement (returned on withdrawal as per rules).
**Is a member deposit share capital?** — No; a deposit is a liability that is returned with interest on conditions; keep a separate account for it.
**What should the share register total match?** — The balance of the "Share Capital" account in the Trial Balance.
**How is dividend decided?** — At a rate set by the general body/as per rules, in proportion to share capital; declared dividend first becomes a payable liability.
**What happens to the shares when a member leaves?** — Share capital is returned as per rules; their share balance becomes zero.
**Who gets the shares on a member's death?** — As per nomination or legal succession, not through voluntary transfer.
**What should be recorded in the register?** — Name, address, occupation, shares held, date of entry, date of cessation and nominee (Haryana Rule 22).
**What are the conditions for share transfer?** — Managing committee approval and a process in line with the bye-laws; in Haryana, conditions like a minimum membership period and clearing dues apply (Section 22/55).
**Why is a nominee necessary?** — It makes settling shares/deposits simple on a member's death; without a nominee, a long process of legal succession is needed.

## Conclusion

When member and share accounting is correct, every member can clearly see their entitlement — and this is the soul of cooperation. Never forget three things: keep admission fee–shares–deposits separate, match the register with the Trial Balance every month, and check the reserve and distributable profit before distribution. In a digital system all this matches automatically, provided you choose the correct accounts at the start.
