# Salary and Payroll Accounting: With EPF, ESI and TDS

For societies that have employees, **correct salary accounts** are essential — not just "how much was paid", but deductions like EPF, ESI and TDS must also be recorded correctly. This article makes payroll easy.

## Why payroll is separate and important

Salary accounting is different from ordinary expenses, because it is not just "paying money" — several legal responsibilities are attached. When the society pays an employee a salary, deductions such as EPF, ESI, professional tax and (where applicable) TDS come with it, and deducting them and depositing them with the government on time is a legal duty. A slip here attracts interest and penalty, and harms the employee's interests (such as provident fund, insurance). So treating payroll as "just another expense" is a mistake — it is a separate process that demands discipline, and running it correctly is both the society's legal and moral responsibility.

## How attendance and salary are linked

Salary begins with attendance — who worked how many days/hours, how much leave was taken, whether there is any overtime or deduction for absence. So a reliable attendance record is the first condition for a correct salary. Wrong or incomplete attendance directly produces a wrong salary, which then spoils the deductions and records too. In digital payroll, salary can be calculated automatically by linking to attendance, which keeps this link strong.

## The three parts of a salary
1. **Gross salary** — the total fixed salary.
2. **Deductions** — EPF, ESI, professional tax, TDS.
3. **Net salary** — what the employee actually receives.

> 📘 **Net salary = Gross salary − Total deductions**

## An example — ₹20,000 gross salary
Suppose EPF ₹1,800 + ESI ₹150 were deducted:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salary expense | ₹20,000 | — |
| EPF payable (liability) | — | ₹1,800 |
| ESI payable (liability) | — | ₹150 |
| Bank/cash (net payment) | — | ₹18,050 |

That is, the employee received ₹18,050; the EPF/ESI amount stayed with the society as "to be deposited".

## Depositing deductions with the government
EPF, ESI, PT and TDS — each one is a **separate liability**, which must be deposited with the department concerned on time. On depositing, that liability reduces and payment goes out from the bank.

> ⚠️ EPF/ESI/TDS rates and limits change as per the rules — confirm the **current rates** applicable to your society from the portal concerned/your CA.

> 🔍 The net salary the employee takes home is not the whole expense — **the full gross salary is the expense**; deductions only decide "whom to pay and when".

## The full payroll process — at a glance

Salary accounting is not just payment, it is a full cycle:

1. **Attendance/working days** — who worked how many days.
2. **Gross salary calculation** — fixed salary + allowances (where applicable).
3. **Deductions** — EPF, ESI, professional tax, TDS (wherever applicable).
4. **Net salary** — gross − deductions, what the employee receives.
5. **Payment and slip** — net payment + giving the salary slip.
6. **Deposit** — depositing deductions with the department concerned on time.

Running these six steps in an orderly way every month is the mark of good payroll.

## Salary components — a general understanding

A salary is often split into several parts — basic pay (basic), dearness allowance (DA), house rent allowance (HRA) and other allowances. Some deductions (such as EPF) are usually based on basic pay. Every society's salary structure can be different, so correct classification of components is essential — it matters not only for the correct deduction, but also for correct records and compliance.

## EPF, ESI, PT and TDS — in brief

- **EPF (Employees' Provident Fund):** the employee's retirement savings; contributions from both employee and employer.
- **ESI (Employees' State Insurance):** medical and social security; applies to employees up to a set wage limit.
- **PT (Professional Tax):** a state-level tax that applies in some states.
- **TDS:** tax deducted at source when the salary is taxable.

> ⚠️ The eligibility, rates and limits for each of these differ as per the rules and by state, and change over time — confirm the **current rules and rates** applicable to your society only from the EPFO/ESIC portal, state rules and your CA. The example figures above are only to explain the process, not any fixed rate.

## EPF and ESI — what they are for

**EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)** is the employee's retirement savings — every month contributions from both the employee and the employer are deposited in it, and the employee gets it with interest on retirement. **ESI (Employees' State Insurance)** is a social-security scheme that gives medical and other benefits to employees up to a set wage limit. The purpose of both is the employee's protection — that is why deducting them and depositing them on time is not just compliance, but in the employee's interest. Which employee is eligible, what the rates are and what the limits are — this is fixed by the rules and keeps changing, so confirm the current rules from the EPFO/ESIC portal or your CA.

## Another example — depositing deductions with the government

In the first example, EPF ₹1,800 and ESI ₹150 were held back as "payable". When the society deposits them with the department concerned, the entry will be:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EPF payable (liability) | ₹1,800 | — |
| ESI payable (liability) | ₹150 | — |
| Bank | — | ₹1,950 |

That is, on depositing, the liability reduced and payment went out from the bank. This full cycle — deducting (creating the liability) → depositing (paying off the liability) — is the core of payroll accounting. (Figures are only examples; confirm the actual rates/limits.)

## Regular vs contract/daily-wage workers

Not every worker is the same — some are regular (permanent), some on contract/daily wages. The rules for their salary accounting and deductions can differ (for example, when work is done through a contract, TDS rules may sometimes apply, not salary rules). So it is essential to be clear who is an "employee" (to whom payroll/EPF/ESI apply) and who is a "service provider/contractor" (to whom TDS possibly applies). Wrong classification becomes the root of compliance trouble — when in doubt, confirm with your CA.

## Benefits of digital payroll

Doing payroll by hand — each employee's gross, deduction-wise calculation, slip and deposit entry — takes a lot of time and is error-prone. In digital payroll, as soon as attendance is entered, salary, deductions and net amount are calculated automatically, the salary slip is ready, and the accounting entry (expense and liability) is created automatically. This saves the secretary's time, reduces errors, and lowers the risk of missing compliance dates — especially in societies where one person handles everything.

## For a small society

A society with few employees thinks "why a payroll system for so few?" — but legal responsibilities (EPF/ESI/TDS where applicable) are decided not by the number of employees but by eligibility under the rules. Even with two to four employees, correct deduction, timely deposit and giving slips are just as necessary. So even for a small society, a simple, orderly payroll process — preferably digital — is a big relief.

## The discipline of monthly compliance

The most delicate part of payroll — depositing deductions **on time**. EPF, ESI and TDS each have their own deposit dates and returns, and delay attracts interest/penalty. So keep a monthly compliance calendar, so that no date is missed. (Confirm exact dates and procedure from the portal concerned/your CA; see: [Quarterly compliance calendar](/blog/quarterly-compliance-calendar).)

## Professional tax and TDS on salary

Two more deductions need attention. **Professional tax (PT)** is a state-level tax that applies in some states — it differs from state to state (some have it, some don't), so check your own state's rules. **TDS on salary** applies when the employee's annual income is above the taxable limit — it is deducted by taking the employee's investment/exemption claims into account and spreading the estimated annual tax over the months. This calculation is detailed and keeps getting adjusted through the year, so it is better done only by a CA or a reliable payroll system. (Rates/limits change — confirm the current rules.)

## The picture over a year

If payroll runs correctly every month, the picture is clear at year-end — each employee's slips, total salary expense, and the deposit record of all deductions are ready. This makes audit and annual compliance (such as annual returns, Form 16 where applicable) smooth. On the other hand, if payroll stayed loose all year, then at year-end the reconciliation of deductions, penalties for missed deposit dates and incomplete slips — all land on your head together. So "right every month" is the road to "peace at year-end".

## Records and salary slips

Keeping each employee's salary slip (gross, deduction-wise details, net) and a salary register is not just a good habit, it is often a legal expectation too. These records are useful in all three — audit, employee disputes and compliance checks. In digital payroll these slips and registers are created automatically, which saves the secretary's time and reduces errors.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating gross as net:** the full gross salary is the expense; deductions are only liabilities.
- **Not depositing deductions:** the EPF/ESI/TDS deducted does not belong to the society — depositing it on time is mandatory.
- **Not giving slips/not keeping records:** costly in audits and disputes.
- **Relying on old rates:** rates change — check the current rate every time.

## Three things to remember
- 🧾 Keep each employee's **salary slip** + records.
- 🏦 Deposit EPF/ESI/TDS on time every month (penalty on delay).
- 📊 Gross salary = expense; deductions = liability, until deposited.

## Digital payroll — why, in brief

In manual payroll the same calculation has to be repeated every month, and one mistake can spoil the whole record. In digital payroll, as soon as attendance is entered, gross, deductions and net amount are produced automatically, the salary slip and accounting entry are ready, and the risk of missing deposit dates goes down. This setup makes even the lone secretary of a small society able to run accurate, compliant payroll — without becoming an expert.

## In closing
The secret of payroll — treat the gross as the expense, a separate liability for each deduction, and timely deposit. In a digital system the slip, deductions and deposit entry are created automatically.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is net salary worked out?** — Gross salary − total deductions (EPF, ESI, PT, TDS where applicable) = net salary.
**Is the full gross salary the expense or only the net?** — The full gross salary is the expense; deductions are only liabilities until deposited.
**When should deductions be deposited?** — EPF/ESI/TDS each have their own due dates; delay attracts interest/penalty — confirm exact dates from the portal/your CA.
**Where do we find the rates?** — From the EPFO/ESIC portal, state rules and your CA; the blog's example figures are only for understanding the process.
**Why is a salary slip necessary?** — As proof in audits, employee disputes and compliance checks; often a legal expectation too.
**What is the difference between regular and contract workers?** — Payroll/EPF/ESI apply to regular employees; TDS possibly applies to contractors/service providers — wrong classification becomes the root of compliance trouble.
**Does a small society have to follow payroll rules?** — Yes; legal responsibilities are decided not by the number of employees but by eligibility under the rules.
**When does TDS on salary apply?** — When the employee's annual income is above the taxable limit; the calculation is detailed — do it through a CA/payroll system.
**Who owns the deductions made?** — The employee/the government — they do not belong to the society; depositing them on time with the department concerned is a legal duty.
**What is the full payroll process?** — Attendance → gross salary → deductions → net salary → payment and slip → timely deposit of deductions — these six steps.
