In Haryana's cooperative societies, the word "Ex-gratia" is used for two completely different things, and that is the root of most of the confusion. Some people take it to mean an amount paid in place of bonus; others take it to mean help given to the family after an employee's death. The rules, documents and authorities for the two are entirely different. This guide separates the two clearly and explains CMS's 2024 compassionate assistance policy in detail.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** The details of the CMS 2024 policy have been matched against the full text of the 10 July 2024 order of the Registrar Cooperative Societies (RCS), Haryana, and its Annexure-E (see "Sources" below).

## Two kinds of Ex-gratia — get this clear first

**Type 1 — Ex-gratia in place of bonus**: which societies statutory bonus applies to, and how much (earlier the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965; the Code on Wages, 2019 is in force from 21 November 2025), has to be checked separately — this article does not open those sections. In practice, many societies pay an extra amount called "Ex-gratia" in addition to, or in place of, statutory bonus. Note that the statutory bonus liability should not be assumed to end automatically just because there is a loss — confirm with your CA. This depends entirely on the society's own discretion and financial capacity — we did not find any separate CMS document for it in the Haryana Cooperation Department's list of service rules.

**Type 2 — Compassionate Ex-gratia**: financial assistance or an appointment given to the family when an employee dies while in service. Haryana has concrete, documented policies for this type — separate ones for CMS and for PACS/PCCS.

> ⚠️ The document listed on the Haryana Cooperation Department's site as "Primary Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Society Ex-gratia Policy (2024)" can be confusing to read — because the name only says "Ex-gratia", it may look like the bonus kind (Type 1), but it is actually entirely a Type 2 policy, that is, compassionate assistance/appointment.

## A common misunderstanding — the state's general compassionate policy does not apply

The Haryana Civil Services (Compassionate Financial Assistance or Appointment) Rules, 2019 (notified on 2 August 2019) are for the family of a **"Government employee"** who dies in service — the Supreme Court described this as the scope of these rules in Atul Chauhan v. State of Haryana (11 June 2026, para 14). Cooperative society employees are not covered by them — this is the root of a very common confusion. There are separate, specific policies for cooperative society employees — for PACS/PCCS, the RCS order of 4 February 2022 (the third amendment to the PACS/PCCS Service Rules 2014, Rule 33 replaced, Annexure-E policy 2022; earlier there was a lump sum of ₹4 lakh), and for CMS, the 2024 amendment.

## The CMS 2024 policy — what changed

By the order of the Registrar Cooperative Societies, Haryana, dated **10 July 2024** (Endst. No. 8/18/87/Mkt-II/13952-14127), the **4th Amendment, 2024** was made to the "Primary Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Societies Ltd. Staff Service Rules" — the order says it was issued under Section 37 of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 and Rule 29 of the Rules 1989, after consulting the mandi-level societies. (Section 37 of the Act basically relates to the "common cadre", and Rule 29 says every society shall frame its own service rules on the basis of model rules, with the Registrar's approval.)

This amendment fully replaced **Rule 6(c) "Ex-Gratia Assistance"** of CMS's 2003 Staff Service Rules — under the old arrangement, on death in service, the dependants got a lump sum of ₹2.50 lakh (or as per the RCS's instructions issued from time to time). The new arrangement (effective from 10 July 2024) replaced this flat amount with a detailed, tiered policy — **Annexure-E, "Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Society (Compassionate Financial Assistance or Appointment) Policy, 2024"** — whose entire financial burden is to be borne by the society concerned from its own funds.

> 💡 The practical effect is that, compared with the old flat amount, the new arrangement can in many cases be worth much more — especially in the form of long-term monthly assistance or a compassionate appointment.

## Eligibility conditions

This policy applies to the family of a CMS employee working on a regular basis who dies or goes missing while in service (cases of death by suicide after the policy was issued are also covered). For a compassionate **appointment**, three conditions must all be met together — the employee must have completed at least **5 years** of service on a regular basis (sanctioned leave also counts), must not have been older than **52 years** at the time of death/going missing, and must not be suspected of fraud, links with a terrorist organisation, or fleeing abroad.

> ⚠️ The policy makes clear that a compassionate appointment is not a "matter of right" — it depends on all conditions being met, including the availability of a vacant post. Casual/daily-wage/part-time staff, those paid from contingency, work-charged staff, those on contract/ad hoc/DC-rate or outsourced, those re-employed after retirement, or those whose appointment is disputed in court or was made in breach of the service rules — such employees are outside the definition of "CMS employee".

## Order of priority within the family

For financial assistance: widow/widower (until remarriage or death) → judicially separated spouse (if the separation is not on the ground of adultery) → childless widow who has not remarried → eldest unmarried/dependent son or daughter (up to 25 years) → divorced/widowed daughter below 25 years → unmarried/widowed/divorced daughter above 25 years → disabled son or daughter (no age limit, if fully dependent) → dependent parents → unmarried, disabled and fully dependent brothers and sisters.

For a compassionate **appointment** the order is a little different — widow/widower → children (provided they are not already in a job with a cooperative institution, board, corporation or government) → dependent brothers and sisters (only in the case of an unmarried deceased/missing employee).

## How long the monthly assistance is paid

| Age at the time of death | Period of monthly assistance |
| --- | --- |
| Before completing 35 years | Up to 15 years (subject to conditions) |
| After 35, before 48 | Up to 12 years, or until retirement/60 years — whichever is earlier |
| 48 years or more | Up to 7 years, or until retirement/60 years — whichever is earlier |

The amount is calculated on the basis of the deceased's last basic pay, with dearness allowance (DA) added at the fixed rate, but no annual increment or other allowance is included. Only a family whose total income (from all sources) is less than the deceased's total pay is treated as "dependent".

## Procedure and competent authority

The Board of Directors/Board of Administration (BOD/BOA) of the CMS concerned, subject to the prior approval of the DRCS (Deputy Registrar) concerned, is the competent authority to give a compassionate appointment. On receiving information of the death, the society's manager or senior-most employee sends the family a list of documents; after scrutiny a recommendation is sent to the ARCS/DRCS, and after the DRCS's approval the final decision is taken in the BOD/BOA meeting.

The required documents include the application, an income certificate, a marriage/remarriage certificate (where applicable), a medical certificate for a disabled child, and a certificate that no criminal case is pending in court against the person receiving the assistance. The deceased's family has to apply for the appointment within **6 months** of the date of death. If the appointment could not be given within one year because no post was vacant, monthly assistance is sanctioned retrospectively in that case.

> ⚠️ The person receiving assistance has to appear in person **every six months** and give a life certificate and income certificate, attested by a gazetted officer, sarpanch or municipal councillor (clause 32(2) of the policy). In addition, a son/daughter/parent/brother or sister/guardian receiving assistance has to give the manager a certificate every **March and September** that they have not started earning (January and July for a disabled child). The policy does not separately state the effect of not giving these papers on time, but avoid delay. If the assistance is not drawn at all for more than one year, it lapses automatically — if claimed later, the ARCS can restart it after examining the circumstances, but claims older than three years are decided at the DRCS level.

## When a claim does not arise, or when assistance stops

- On refusing the post offered — once a post is offered, a request to change to another post/department is never accepted, and if the offer is not acceptable, no further claim is considered.
- If the dependants are not maintained as promised in the affidavit, the appointment can be terminated immediately.
- If a family member is accused of murdering the deceased employee or helping in it, the claim of that member **and of the other eligible members too** stays on hold until the criminal proceedings are over. If the accused is found guilty, the assistance goes to the next eligible member from the day after the death (clause 17 of the policy).
- A spouse found guilty in a judicial separation on the ground of adultery does not get monthly assistance.
- On the remarriage or death of the widow/widower, their assistance ends there — the remaining period goes to the next eligible member on a fresh application.

## An important legal point — is a cooperative society the "State"?

When Ex-gratia claims are held up or refused, a question often comes up — can a writ petition (Article 226) be filed directly in the High Court against it? A seven-judge bench of the Supreme Court in Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002) laid down the test that a body is "State" under Article 12 only when it is under the financial, functional and administrative domination of the government, and this control is deep and pervasive, not merely regulatory. In Kisan Sahkari Chini Mills (2003) the same test was applied to a cooperative sugar mill and it was held not to be "State". That is, a registered cooperative society does not automatically become "State". Read the full discussion of this test and related cases (such as the exception that a writ can lie for breach of a statutory rule) here: [Can the High Court hear a writ against a cooperative society?](/blog/cooperative-society-writ-jurisdiction-article-226)

A related lesson is also worth remembering — in **Samarjit Singh v. State of Punjab** (Punjab and Haryana High Court, CWP-1422-2026 and connected cases, 5 March 2026), the court held **Punjab's** 1997 cooperative employee service rules to be beyond the Punjab Cooperative Societies Act 1961 (ultra vires), because they were made by the Registrar, whereas under Section 85 of the Act the rule-making power lay with the state government and could not be delegated further. In the same judgment, the employees' writ petitions were disposed of as not maintainable. This judgment is on Punjab law; its effect on Haryana will have to be examined separately, because Haryana's CMS policy too was made by an RCS order. The lesson — the strength of a policy depends not only on what is written in it, but also on whether the authority that made it had the power to do so.

## If a claim is held up or refused — practical steps

1. **Identify the right policy** — is your case Type 1 (in place of bonus) or Type 2 (compassionate assistance/appointment)? The conditions and authorities for the two are different.
2. **Ask for the written policy** — ask the society's secretary or the ARCS office concerned for a full written copy; if you don't get it, apply under the RTI Act, 2005.
3. **Understand statutory vs administrative** — is the claim based on a clear breach of a written provision, or is it only an administrative matter like a delay in payment? This decides the right forum.
4. **Choose the right forum** — note that Section 102 of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act (arbitration by the Registrar) **excludes** employees' service and disciplinary matters. So look at the Labour Court for the "workman" category, and in other matters a civil remedy or, within a limited scope, a High Court writ — get the right route decided by a lawyer.
5. **Keep documents safe** — copies of the appointment letter, salary slips, society resolutions and any written assurance/refusal.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between the two types of Ex-gratia?** — Type 1 is in place of bonus (at the society's discretion); Type 2 is compassionate assistance/appointment for the family on death/going missing — the conditions and documents are both different.
**Do the state government's general Civil Services Compassionate Rules, 2019 apply to cooperative society employees?** — No; they are for the families of government employees. CMS (2024) and PACS/PCCS (2022) have their own separate policies.
**What replaced the old flat ₹2.50 lakh amount in the CMS 2024 policy?** — A tiered arrangement — monthly assistance for 7 to 15 years depending on age, or a compassionate appointment.
**By when must one apply for a compassionate appointment?** — Within 6 months of the date of death.
**Is a compassionate appointment a "right"?** — No; it is assistance that depends on all conditions being met, including the availability of a vacant post, not an enforceable right.
**What has to be done to keep the assistance going?** — Give an attested life certificate and income certificate in person every six months; and children/parents/brothers and sisters must give a "have not started earning" certificate every March and September.
**Who is the competent authority?** — The BOD/BOA of the CMS concerned, subject to the prior approval of the DRCS.

## Three things to remember

- 🔀 "Ex-gratia" has two completely different meanings — in place of bonus (Type 1) and compassionate assistance (Type 2) — first decide which one your case is.
- 📋 The state's general government compassionate policy does not apply to cooperative society employees — look at the separate policies of CMS and PACS/PCCS.
- 📅 Documents, time limits (apply within 6 months of the death, life-and-income proof every six months) and the right forum — taking care of all three is necessary for a claim to succeed.

## In closing

The biggest confusion about Ex-gratia lies in its two different meanings — so before any claim, make sure whether your case is the bonus kind (Type 1) or compassionate assistance (Type 2). The CMS 2024 policy has replaced the old flat ₹2.50 lakh amount with a tiered, often more valuable arrangement — but its entire burden is on the society's own funds, and the benefit is subject to strict eligibility, time limits and documents. Most important — the state's general government compassionate policy does not apply to cooperative employees; they have their own separate policy. So the family should identify the right policy, apply on time (within 6 months), keep the documents complete, and when needed, take the right forum and expert advice.
