The national-project part of computerising PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies) in Haryana is complete. According to the Ministry of Cooperation's PIB release (21 July 2026, figures up to 15 July 2026), **710 PACS** of Haryana were sanctioned under the project, and all 710 of them are now on the ERP and have become e-PACS. On the other hand, the budget speech of March 2026 said that only 33 of the state's 804 PACS are in profit. This guide explains the full story of this change and its practical effect.

> 💡 **Figures checked up to 18 October 2026.** "710" is the number of PACS **sanctioned** for Haryana under the national computerisation project (PIB). Note — this is not the total number of PACS in Haryana: the budget speech (2 March 2026) mentioned 804 PACS, and the National Cooperative Database recorded 820 functional PACS as of 15 July 2026.

## Part of a national project

This computerisation is part of the Central Government's national PACS computerisation project, which NABARD is implementing. The project's budget was first ₹2,516 crore; it was raised to ₹2,925.39 crore, and 79,630 PACS of the country are now sanctioned under it. As of 15 July 2026, 63,686 PACS had come onto a common national ERP software, of which 54,707 had become "e-PACS" (PIB, 21 July 2026) — that is, they are working on a digital accounting, loan and member management system. According to PIB, this ERP covers more than 25 economic activities. The technical details of this system are covered in [What is e-PACS](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).

This national initiative started because for decades most PACS ran on paper registers, where delays, mistakes and a lack of transparency in accounts were common. Its basic aim is to bring PACS across the country onto one common, central ERP and give them a uniform, modern and transparent system. This not only improves the work of each PACS, but also makes data across the whole cooperative credit structure available in one place, comparable and reliable. (General background: [Role of NABARD](/blog/nabard-role-in-cooperatives).)

## What actually changes in a PACS with computerisation

Beyond the figures, several concrete changes come to a PACS on the ground after computerisation:

- **From paper registers to digital records:** Membership, loans, deposits and accounts are now recorded in the common ERP system instead of handwritten registers.
- **Faster service:** The farmer gets loan sanction, deposits and account statements quickly, because records are available instantly, in digital form.
- **Better control:** Processes like Maker-Checker and day-end reduce errors and bring discipline.
- **Transparent audit:** Digital records and an audit trail make the audit smooth and free of objections.
- **New services:** Through the CSC link, a PACS can offer not only loans but other digital services too.
- **Less leakage:** The digital trail and controls reduce the scope for errors and leakage.

So computerisation is not just "installing computers" — it is a basic, lasting improvement in the way a PACS works. (Technical details: [What is e-PACS](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).)

## Practical changes — what difference did it make?

- **Profit target:** The budget speech of 2 March 2026 said only 33 out of 804 PACS were in profit, and set a target of bringing at least 300 loss-making PACS into profit in 2026-27.
- **Digital audit:** Computerised PACS now come under the audit module with Maker-Checker control.
- **Faster transactions:** Services for farmers like loan disbursement and deposits now happen through the digital system instead of a paper process.
- **CSC services:** Many PACS now also offer additional digital services as Common Service Centres. (See: [PACS are now CSCs too](/blog/pacs-csc-haryana-common-service-centre-income).)

## Rising profit — why it matters

According to the budget speech, only 33 of 804 PACS were in profit as of March 2026, and the government has set a target of bringing at least 300 more PACS into profit in 2026-27. (We did not find any newer government count after that.) This matters because a large number of PACS have struggled with losses or inactivity for years. A digital system helps in several ways — better recovery tracking, accurate accounts, less leakage, and extra income from new services (CSC). However, computerisation alone does not automatically bring a weak PACS into profit — that also needs good management, recovery discipline and active membership. Computerisation is a powerful tool, but using it the right way is in the hands of the society.

## Responsibilities of a PACS after computerisation

Going digital is not a one-time job — it also brings some everyday responsibilities that must be met:

- **Doing day-end every day:** Closing each day's transactions, so the next day's work does not stop.
- **Following Maker-Checker:** A two-level check of every sensitive entry.
- **Accuracy of data:** Correct, up-to-date entry of members/loans/deposits.
- **Regular reconciliation:** Trial Balance and bank reconciliation on time.
- **Training:** Enough training for the secretary/operator to run the system.

The PACS that adopts these habits is the one that gets the full benefit of computerisation; not merely by having software installed. (See: [Day-end and audit process of e-PACS](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).)

## "804" or "820" — why the count is confusing

One thing that often creates confusion — why does Haryana's total PACS number look different in different government documents? The budget speech (March 2026) says 804, while the National Cooperative Database (15 July 2026) has 820 functional PACS. There are a few reasons for this: counts at different times (some new ones formed, some became inactive); different classifications (whether categories like PCCS/FSS are included or not); and the difference between "registered" and "functional". So when reading these figures, always check which date, which definition and which source they are from. "710" is not the same as any of these totals — it is only the sub-group identified for computerisation. This is the most important message of this whole article — read numbers in their context.

## Practical effect for the farmer

For a farmer-member, the most direct effect of computerisation is in the speed and transparency of service. Earlier, loan sanction or an account statement meant days of waiting and trips to the office; now digital records make this work faster. With transparent accounts, disputes over "who owes how much / who has how much deposited" go down. And through the CSC link, the farmer can also get services like bill payment, certificates and banking-sakhi at the PACS in his own village — that is, the PACS becomes a multi-service centre. Provided the society uses the system properly, this change makes a clear difference to the farmer's everyday convenience.

## What next?

Completing the computerisation of 710 is an important milestone, but it is only a part of Haryana's total PACS number. The computerisation status of the remaining PACS — especially those that come under different classifications (PCCS, FSS) — is not publicly clear at present.

> ⚠️ The figures given above (710, 804, 33) are from PIB's release of 21 July 2026 and the budget speech of 2 March 2026 — this is a point-in-time position, not a permanent number. For the latest position, see the official announcements of the Cooperation Department, Haryana.

## Computerisation vs "e-PACS" status

A fine but important difference — "computerisation being complete" and "becoming e-PACS" do not happen at exactly the same moment. Computerisation means bringing the PACS onto the common ERP system (hardware, software, data migration). The "e-PACS" status is complete when that PACS actually starts running all its work — membership, loans, deposits, accounts and audit — on that same digital system. That is why, in the national figures, the number "computerised" (63,686) and "fully e-PACS" (54,707) differ a little — some PACS have come onto the system but take some time to run fully on it. Understanding this gap is important, because the real benefit comes only when the PACS becomes fully digital. (Details: [What is e-PACS](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).)

## There are challenges too

For a balanced picture, also understand that computerisation brings some challenges that need attention: training staff on the new system; dependence on internet/electricity (especially in remote villages); bringing old paper data correctly into digital form (migration); and a temporary slowdown in work in the early phase, until the team gets used to it. These challenges are not permanent — with proper training, support and practice they go away within a short time. But it is important to recognise them, so the society moves ahead prepared and is not discouraged by early difficulties.

## How the society can get the full benefit of the system

The full benefit of computerisation comes only when the society is active in adopting it. Some practical steps: train the secretary/operator well; do the daily day-end without fail; make every entry correctly and on time; do regular reconciliation (Trial Balance, bank reconciliation); and use the system's report features to give the managing committee a regular picture. For a society that adopts these habits, computerisation becomes not just compliance, but a solid step towards better service, easier audit and profit.

**What is the main responsibility of a PACS after computerisation?** — Daily day-end, following Maker-Checker, correct data entry and regular reconciliation.
**Why does Haryana's PACS number look different in different places?** — Because of different times, classifications (PCCS/FSS) and the definition of "registered vs functional" (for example 804 in the budget, 820 in the national database); so read figures with their context.
**What are the main challenges in computerisation?** — Training, dependence on internet/electricity, migration of old data and early practice — all go away with time.

**Difference between "computerised" and "fully e-PACS"?** — Computerised = coming onto the system; e-PACS = running all work on that same system — so the two numbers differ a little.

## Three things to remember

- 🔢 "710" is the sub-group identified for computerisation, not Haryana's total number of PACS — do not read the two together.
- 💻 In computerisation a PACS comes onto the national common ERP system, and is counted as "e-PACS" when it runs its work fully on it.
- 📊 Figures on profit/status change over time — always read them as the position on a particular date, not as a permanent truth.

## In the end

Completing the computerisation of Haryana's 710 identified PACS is an important step in modernising the cooperative sector — it shows a move from the old paper structure to a transparent, fast and reliable digital system. But the real success is not in the figures; it is in how well each PACS uses this digital system — through daily day-end, correct accounts, disciplined recovery and active service. Computerisation has opened the road; walking on it to reach profit and service is in each society's own hands. And yes — always treat all figures as the position on a particular date, not a permanent truth.
