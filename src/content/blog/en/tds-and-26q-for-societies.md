# TDS and 26Q (now Form 140): a simple guide for cooperative societies

When a society pays someone a large amount — a contractor, rent, commission, professional fees — it often has to deduct **TDS (tax deducted at source)** from it, and file a TDS statement every quarter. People still call it "26Q", but from 1 April 2026 its name is **Form 140**. This article makes the whole thing easy.

> ⚖️ **The law has changed — information checked up to 18 October 2026.** From 1 April 2026, the **Income-tax Act 2025** and the **Income-tax Rules 2026** are in force. The old sections (194C, 194H, 194-I, 194J) are now rows of the table in Section **393**, and the form numbers have also changed: quarterly statement 26Q → **Form 140**, TDS certificate 16A → **Form 131**, 26AS → **Form 168**. Every rate, threshold and date below has been matched against the text of the Act and Rules on the Income Tax Department's website (see "Sources" below). This is not tax advice — confirm with a CA for your case.

## What TDS is
When making a payment, the society **deducts a fixed percentage** and pays the rest, and deposits the deducted amount with the government **in the payee's name**. That is, the society is just a "collector in the middle".

## Why TDS — the government's reasoning

The basic idea of TDS is simple — collect tax at the source of income itself, little by little, instead of in one lump sum at year-end. This gives the government tax regularly, and a record of income is created in advance. The society deducts the payee's tax and deposits it with the government in the payee's name, which is then credited in the payee's tax statement (Form 168, earlier 26AS). So it is not the society's own tax, but a responsibility that must be carried out correctly.

## Which rows apply to a society

Section 402 of the Act defines "designated person" (for the contract row) and "specified person" (for rent, commission, professional fees). **A cooperative society falls in both** — the list of "designated person" clearly says "any co-operative society", and "specified person" covers every person who is not an individual or HUF. So once the threshold is crossed, the society has to deduct these TDS:

| Payment | Old name | Section 393(1), table | Rate | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contract (work, including supply of labour) | 194C | Sl. 6(i) | 1% if the contractor is an individual/HUF, 2% for others | More than ₹30,000 at one time, **or** a yearly total of more than ₹1,00,000 |
| Commission / brokerage (other than insurance commission) | 194H | Sl. 1(ii) | 2% | ₹20,000 |
| Rent — machinery, plant, equipment | 194-I | Sl. 2(ii) | 2% | ₹50,000 per month (or part of a month) |
| Rent — land, building, furniture, fittings | 194-I | Sl. 2(ii) | 10% | ₹50,000 per month (or part of a month) |
| Fees for professional / technical services | 194J | Sl. 6(iii) | Professional 10%, technical 2% | See the table in Section 393 |

**Deducted on the full amount:** under Section 393(1)(a), when the amount (or the yearly total) exceeds the threshold, TDS is deducted on the **full amount**, not just on the part above the threshold.

### What if the society pays interest to its member?

Under the table in Section 393(4) (Sl. 7(b)), no TDS is deducted on interest paid by a cooperative society, **other than a cooperative bank**, to its **member**, or by a cooperative society to **another cooperative society**. Exception: if the society's sales/turnover in the previous year was **more than ₹50 crore** **and** the interest amount crosses the set threshold. Interest on deposits in a PACS also gets this exemption. The rules for a cooperative bank are different — ask your CA.

## An example — ₹50,000 to a contractor firm
Suppose the society paid a contractor **firm** ₹50,000 at one time for repair of a godown. The amount is more than ₹30,000, and the firm is not an individual/HUF, so the rate is 2%:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repair expense | ₹50,000 | — |
| TDS payable (liability) | — | ₹1,000 |
| Bank (actual payment) | — | ₹49,000 |

That is, the contractor got ₹49,000, and ₹1,000 TDS was held back. Then that ₹1,000 has to be deposited with the government. If the same contractor were an individual, the rate would be 1%, so TDS would be ₹500.

## Another example — godown rent

Suppose the society pays rent of ₹60,000 per month for a godown (building). The threshold is ₹50,000 per month, which has been crossed, so 10% on the full amount = ₹6,000:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent expense | ₹60,000 | — |
| TDS payable (liability) | — | ₹6,000 |
| Bank (actual payment) | — | ₹54,000 |

Note: if the rent is exactly ₹50,000 per month, no TDS will be deducted — the threshold is "more than ₹50,000". And if the rent is for a machine or equipment, the rate will be 2%, not 10%.

## From the deductee's point of view

For the person whose TDS is deducted (the deductee), it is not "lost money" — it is an advance payment of their tax, which is credited in their tax statement and reduces their tax liability (or becomes a refund). But this happens only if the society has filed the correct statement with the correct PAN and has given the certificate (Form 131, earlier 16A). That is why the deductee often asks for their TDS certificate — giving it on time is the society's responsibility.

## Benefits of digital TDS

Handling TDS by hand — how much was deducted on which payment, when it was deposited, whose certificate was made — quickly gets messy. In a digital system, the liability is created as soon as TDS is deducted, the challan is recorded, and the data for the TDS register and the quarterly statement is ready. This reduces the burden of the quarterly statement and reconciliation.

## Deposit: by the 7th of next month

**Rule 218(2)** of the Income-tax Rules 2026 (for deductors other than government offices):

- Deposit TDS **within 7 days from the end** of the month in which it was deducted — that is, by the 7th of the next month.
- TDS deducted in **March** must be deposited by **30 April**.

Late deposit attracts interest, so make this a firm monthly habit.

> 🔍 Deduct TDS **only once** — under Section 393(1)(c), **at the time of credit to the payee's account or at the time of payment, whichever is earlier**. If you deducted it on the bill, do not deduct it again on payment. (See [Loan and interest accounting](/blog/loan-and-interest-accounting).)

## Quarterly statement: Form 140 (earlier 26Q)

The TDS statement for non-salary payments is now filed in **Form 140** (Income-tax Rules 2026, Rule 219; Section 397(3)(b) of the Act). The statement for salary TDS goes in a separate form (Form 138, earlier 24Q). Due dates under Rule 219(4):

| Quarter | Period | Form 140 due date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | April–June | 31 July |
| Second | July–September | 31 October |
| Third | October–December | 31 January |
| Fourth | January–March | 31 May of the next year |

> 💡 Payments made up to 31 March 2026 come under the old 1961 Act. Ask your CA in which form their corrections will be filed.

## Does every society have to deduct TDS

Not necessarily. It depends on what kind of payments, and how large, the society makes. A small society that makes only small payments will rarely cross the threshold of ₹30,000 / ₹1,00,000 (contract), ₹20,000 (commission) or ₹50,000 per month (rent). But a society that gives large contracts, rent or commission has to take care of TDS. So the first step is to match your payments against the table above.

## Before you start — TAN and basic terms

A society that deducts TDS must have a **TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number)** — this is the society's identity number for TDS, separate from PAN. It is applied for under Section 397 (Form 134/135 in the new Rules, earlier 49B). Some important terms:

- **Deductor:** the one who deducts TDS (here, the society).
- **Deductee:** the one whose TDS is deducted (contractor, landlord, etc.).
- **Challan:** the form through which deducted TDS is deposited with the government.
- **Form 140 (earlier 26Q):** the quarterly TDS statement for non-salary payments.
- **Form 131 (earlier 16A):** the TDS certificate given to the deductee.
- **Form 168 (earlier 26AS):** the taxpayer's annual information statement, which shows their TDS credit.

## The full TDS process — step by step

1. **Applies or not:** match the type and amount of the payment against the table above.
2. **Deduction:** at the correct rate, on credit or payment, whichever is earlier — only once.
3. **Deposit:** by challan, by the 7th of the next month (March's by 30 April).
4. **Statement:** Form 140 every quarter, by the due date.
5. **Certificate:** give the deductee Form 131, so they can take their credit.

## Why certificates and records matter

In TDS, just deducting and depositing is not enough — keeping a record of every challan, every quarterly statement and every certificate is just as important. These records become evidence in audit, in settling a deductee's complaint and in any tax inquiry. An orderly TDS register — whose TDS, when, how much was deducted, when it was deposited, which certificate was given — keeps the whole process transparent.

## Reconciling the tax statement — why it matters

The TDS you deducted and deposited should show in the deductee's annual statement (Form 168, earlier 26AS) — only then will they get credit. If the PAN was entered wrongly or there was a slip in the statement, the deductee will not get credit and a complaint will come. So taking the correct PAN and reconciling your TDS records every quarter is essential.

## Common mistakes

- **Sticking to the old form name:** the statement for payments after 1 April 2026 is in Form 140, not 26Q.
- **One rate for all:** in contracts, 1% for an individual/HUF and 2% for a firm/company; 10% on building rent, 2% on machinery rent.
- **Deducting only on the part above the threshold:** once the threshold is crossed, it is deducted on the full amount.
- **TAN/PAN errors:** a wrong TAN or a wrong PAN of the deductee — the credit gets stuck.
- **Double deduction:** deducting on both the bill and the payment — deduct only once.
- **Late deposit or statement:** the root of interest and fees.

## One more example — commission/brokerage

Suppose the society pays an agent a total commission of ₹30,000 in a year. The threshold of ₹20,000 has been crossed, so 2% on the full amount = ₹600. The principle is the same — the full commission is an expense, the TDS deducted is a liability, and the rest is paid to the agent. Then that TDS is deposited by the 7th, reported in the quarterly Form 140, and the agent gets Form 131.

## Small vs large society

The relief for a small society is that if its payments stay below the threshold, it may not have the TDS hassle at all. But as a society starts giving large contracts, rent or commission, TDS compliance becomes necessary — and then TAN, quarterly statements and certificates all have to be handled. So be ready in time; don't wait for a notice to come.

## Three things to remember
- 📅 Deposit deducted TDS by the 7th of the next month (March's by 30 April) — interest on delay.
- 🧾 The quarterly statement is now Form 140 (earlier 26Q): 31 July, 31 October, 31 January, 31 May.
- ✅ TDS only once on a payment, and at the correct rate of the correct row.

## The role of a digital system — in short

Good cooperative software turns TDS from a burden into a habit — the liability is created automatically with the deduction, the deposit challan is recorded, and the data for the TDS register and quarterly statement is ready. This reduces the risk of missing something.

## In the end
TDS is not hard — deduct under the correct row at the correct rate, deposit by the 7th, file the quarterly Form 140 and give the deductee Form 131. In a digital system, the **TDS register** is ready on its own.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is needed to deduct TDS?** — A valid TAN (applied for under Section 397).
**Do I still have to file 26Q?** — The quarterly statement for payments after 1 April 2026 is in **Form 140** (Income-tax Rules 2026, Rule 219). Ask a CA about corrections for old payments.
**What happened to Form 16A?** — In the new Rules, the non-salary TDS certificate is **Form 131**.
**What is the TDS rate on a contractor?** — 1% for an individual/HUF, 2% for others; on more than ₹30,000 at one time or more than ₹1,00,000 in a year (Section 393(1), Sl. 6(i)).
**On building rent?** — 10%, when the rent is more than ₹50,000 per month; 2% on rent of machinery/equipment.
**By when must deducted TDS be deposited?** — By the 7th of the next month; March's by 30 April (Rule 218).
**By when is the quarterly statement due?** — 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and (for the fourth quarter) 31 May (Rule 219(4)).
**TDS on interest paid to members?** — Other than a cooperative bank, not on interest paid to a member of the society — unless the society's previous-year turnover was more than ₹50 crore and the interest crosses the threshold (Section 393(4), Sl. 7(b)).
**When should TDS be deducted?** — On credit or payment, whichever is earlier — and only once.
**Does every society have to deduct TDS?** — No; only when payments cross the threshold.
**What if the deductee's PAN is wrong?** — The credit gets stuck; so taking the correct PAN and filing the statement correctly is essential.
**Is deducting TDS the society's tax?** — No; the society is just a "collector in the middle".
