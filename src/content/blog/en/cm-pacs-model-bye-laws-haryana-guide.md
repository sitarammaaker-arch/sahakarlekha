In Haryana, CM-PACS look like "PACS" only by name, but their purpose is different from traditional agricultural credit societies. After [the current number of CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-list-haryana-district-wise-476-registered), this guide now explains their concept, membership structure and governance model. It also explains what "Model Bye-laws" are, and what a member or organiser should look for in them.

## The full name and purpose of CM-PACS

The full name of CM-PACS is "Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activity Co-operative Society" (The Tribune's report of 6 July 2025, quoting the minister, writes "Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activities Cooperative Societies"). Whereas traditional PACS are mainly limited to agricultural credit and seed distribution, CM-PACS are said to be designed for business activities such as running petrol pumps, grain storage (godowns), crop procurement and food processing — that is, it is a concept that matches directly with the multipurpose approach of the [national PACS model bye-laws](/blog/pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-explained).

The word "Multi-Purpose" hidden in the name is its real message. The thinking is that the village-level cooperative institution should not remain just a means of giving loans, but should meet many economic needs of the village — inputs (fertiliser/seed), storage, sales, fuel, processing — under one roof. This way the member-farmer does not have to wander to different places for each service, and the society's sources of income also become diverse, which makes it more economically sustainable.

## What exactly are Model Bye-laws

The **bye-laws (rules)** of any cooperative society are its internal constitution — the document that decides for what purpose the society was formed, who can become a member, how it will run, who will take decisions, and how profit and loss will be divided. They are made within the limits of the state's cooperative Act and Rules, and are approved by the Registrar (RCS/ARCS) at the time of registration.

"**Model Bye-laws**" are a ready-made sample, issued by the government or an apex body, so that new societies, instead of drafting rules from zero, adopt a standard framework and make small changes as per their need. This has two benefits — a new society is formed faster, and uniformity is maintained across all societies, which makes supervision and audit easier. In the context of CM-PACS too, the expectation is that one standard multipurpose framework is adopted.

> 💡 In simple language: Act = the state's big law; Rules = the procedure for applying it; and bye-laws = the rules inside your own society. When the three conflict, the Act stays on top.

## What a society's bye-laws usually contain

Whether it is a CM-PACS or any other cooperative society, its bye-laws usually include these points:

| Point | What it decides |
| --- | --- |
| Name and area of operation | The society's name and the village/area where it will work |
| Objects | The activities the society is formed for (credit, storage, sales, etc.) |
| Membership | Who is eligible, admission fee, share amount, the member's rights and duties |
| Share capital | Shares per member, maximum limit, conditions for refund |
| Meetings | Frequency and quorum of the general body (AGM) and board meetings |
| Management | Constitution of the board/managing committee, elections, term |
| Funds | Reserve fund, other funds, appropriation of profit |
| Accounts and audit | Keeping accounts, annual audit, reporting |
| Disputes and dissolution | Settlement of disputes and the process for closing the society |

When you are going to become a member of a CM-PACS or to form one, the most reliable way is to read and confirm these same points in its approved bye-laws — because the general figures given here are scheme-level, while the exact conditions of your society will be written in its own bye-laws.

## Where they are starting — phased expansion

**How many have been formed so far:** on 18 October 2026, the dashboard of the RCS Haryana online portal showed **546 registered CM-PACS** (including 61 women CM-PACS). In July 2025 the minister had said 141 (The Tribune). We did not find any government source for the first-phase target or for how many villages in total are to be covered, so those figures are not given here.

The logic of this phased thinking is worth understanding. In villages that already have an active PACS, it is better to strengthen the existing one than to set up a new institution. But Haryana has had thousands of villages with no working cooperative institution — there the farmer had to depend on the private market or distant institutions for credit, inputs and sales. The aim of CM-PACS is to fill this "cooperative vacuum", so that every village has a multipurpose cooperative centre.

## Activities of CM-PACS — in detail

If we open up the commonly stated activities a little (which activity is allowed to which society is decided by its bye-laws), each one shows a direct link with the farmer's interest:

- **Running a petrol/diesel pump:** making fuel available in or near the village, so the farmer does not have to go far; along with this, a regular source of income for the society.
- **Grain storage (godown):** the compulsion to sell at throwaway prices right after harvest is reduced — the farmer can keep his produce in the godown and wait for a better price (and, where applicable, a loan against storage too).
- **Crop procurement:** the society can buy produce directly from the farmer and sell it on, which reduces the role of the middleman.
- **Food processing:** adding value by processing raw produce (such as cleaning, grading, packing or further processing), which gets a better price for the produce and creates additional income.

Note that it is not necessary for any society to start all these activities at once — in practice a society adds activities step by step according to its capacity, capital and the village's need. Which activity is in its allowed list is decided by its approved bye-laws and registration.

## Membership structure — how many members, who is eligible?

**What the law says:** according to Section 5 of the Haryana Act, (for a society that does not have another cooperative society as a member) registration needs **at least 10 adult (above 18 years) persons** — or such higher number as the Registrar may fix from time to time; and under Rule 4 they must be from at least **5 families**. Whether the Registrar has fixed a higher minimum number for CM-PACS, and which farmers/workers membership is open to — this will be in the CM-PACS Model Bye-laws, whose text we have not seen. Ask your ARCS.

If membership is also open to small and marginal farmers and the village's non-agricultural workers, this "inclusive membership" becomes the strength of this model.

This "inclusive membership" is an important feature of this model. By connecting both small and marginal farmers and the village's non-agricultural workers, the society ensures the economic participation of a large part of the village. In practice, the process of becoming a member usually looks like these steps (the exact process depends on the society's bye-laws):

1. Filling in the membership application in the prescribed form.
2. Checking of eligibility and documents (identity, residence and — where applicable — land/farmer-related details).
3. Approval of the application by the board/managing committee.
4. Depositing the prescribed share amount and admission fee (where applicable).
5. The name is entered in the member register — only after this do the member's rights (voting, etc.) take effect.

## Share amount — where it is written

The Act itself does not state any fixed share amount; under Section 9A(2), the society's capital and the conditions of membership are decided by its bye-laws. Different figures for the CM-PACS share amount (and whether it is refunded or not) circulate on the internet, but we did not find any government source for them — so we have not written any amount here. (See: [CM-PACS Share Amount guide](/blog/cm-pacs-share-amount-haryana-membership-cost).)

Do know one important point of the law on share refund: under Rule 18(3), in a society with limited liability a member is usually not allowed to withdraw shares — only when the society has created a share transfer fund out of its profits, and even then the total refund at any time cannot be more than 5% of the paid-up share capital (excluding the government's share) as on the previous 31 March. So read your society's bye-laws before trusting claims like "refundable".

## Governance — who runs the society

Every cooperative society is run at two levels, and CM-PACS also follow this general cooperative structure:

- **General Body:** the meeting of all members — under Section 25(1), the final authority of the society rests with it. After the 2013 amendment, under Section 25(3) the general body meets within 6 months of the end of the year, taking up subjects such as the next year's programme, the audit report, the list of defaulters, elections and amendment of bye-laws. Under Section 20, **every member has one vote** (the vote does not increase with the number of shares); a defaulting member cannot vote.
- **Board/managing committee (Board):** members elected by the general body, who look after day-to-day running. Their term, number and powers are laid down in the bye-laws.

This democratic structure is what sets a cooperative institution apart from a private company — here control rests not with capital but with the members. So it is necessary for a good member to take part in the general body, look at the annual accounts, and vote in elections. (For general principles see: [Principles and values of cooperation](/blog/cooperative-principles-and-values).)

## Division of profit

The profit from the business activities of a CM-PACS is also divided according to law, like other cooperative societies. Under Section 87(1) of the Haryana Act, before profit is distributed, **at least 10% to the reserve fund** and **at least 10% to the bad and doubtful debt fund** go out of each year's profit, and **up to 5% to the cooperative education fund** (under Rule 73 this contribution is up to 2% on the Registrar's direction); the remaining funds as per the bye-laws or the Registrar.

That is, the net surplus is not divided directly in full — first these statutory funds are created, and after that, out of the remaining surplus, a **dividend** (on shares) and — where applicable — a **patronage bonus/patronage rebate** (in proportion to how much business the member did with the society) can be given. This appropriation is done with the approval of the general body. What the ratio of reserve/dividend will be in a society depends on the state rules and its bye-laws.

> ⚠️ The limit on dividend is in the Rules (under Rule 72, dividend cannot be more than 10% of the paid-up share capital per year — details in the [Act guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-act-1984-rules-1989-guide)), and the patronage bonus depends on the society's bye-laws — confirm from your society's bye-laws.

## How to keep the accounts of a multi-activity society

For a multipurpose society like a CM-PACS, the biggest accounting challenge is that one institution runs many different businesses (credit, input sales, godown, pump, processing) at the same time. If all their accounts are kept in one heap, it will never be known which activity is in profit and which is in loss. So **activity-wise accounts** are necessary — separate income, separate expenses, separate profit and loss for each business.

In practice this means — putting each activity's sales/purchases in its own account (such as "fertiliser sales", "godown income", "pump sales" separately), keeping a correct account of stock, and making an activity-wise trading account at year end. This transparency clearly shows members in the general body how much each part of the society is contributing, and it is also the foundation of a correct dividend decision. Good cooperative accounting software makes this activity-wise division automatically, which reduces the secretary's burden and keeps the reports audit-ready. (See: [Basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics) and [Inventory and stock management](/blog/inventory-and-stock-management).)

## CM-PACS, PACS and the national scheme — don't get confused

An important clarification is needed here, because three different things are often mixed up with one another:

- **PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Society):** the village-level primary agricultural credit society — it is the lowest unit of the cooperative credit structure across the country.
- **CM-PACS (Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activity Co-operative Society):** this is **Haryana's initiative** — the RCS Haryana portal counts them separately from PACS ("Registered CM-PACS"). This name and model are Haryana-specific.
- **National multipurpose PACS effort:** a separate nationwide programme of the Central Government for cooperative expansion and PACS computerisation.

It would be wrong to treat these three as one. Understand CM-PACS by its full name (Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activity Co-operative Society), not by any other expansion of the acronym. For the exact scope and eligibility, treat only the official information of your Haryana Cooperation Department/ARCS office as final.

## Frequently asked questions

**What activities are CM-PACS made for?** — For business activities such as running petrol pumps, grain storage, crop procurement and food processing, going beyond the agricultural-credit-centred role of traditional PACS.
**What is the minimum number of members needed to form a society?** — Under the Act (Section 5), at least 10 adult persons, or a higher number fixed by the Registrar; under Rule 4, from at least 5 families. Ask the ARCS for the number fixed for CM-PACS.
**Is the share amount refunded?** — The amount and the refund condition are in the society's bye-laws. In a society with limited liability, Rule 18(3) puts strict conditions on share refund (share transfer fund and the 5% limit).
**How many CM-PACS are there in Haryana?** — On the RCS Haryana portal on 18 October 2026, 546 registered CM-PACS (61 women CM-PACS).
**What are Model Bye-laws?** — A ready-made sample of a society's internal rules, adopting which a new society can be formed quickly and in a standard way; it stays within the limits of the state Act/Rules and is approved by the Registrar.
**What is the basic difference between CM-PACS and traditional PACS?** — PACS is mainly agricultural-credit-centred, while CM-PACS are said to be designed for multipurpose business activities (pump, godown, procurement, processing).
**Are CM-PACS across the whole country?** — No, it is Haryana's initiative (the RCS Haryana portal counts them separately); do not link it with national PACS programmes. Confirm the exact scope from the ARCS/Cooperation Department.
**Where will the society's real rules be found?** — In its approved bye-laws — membership, shares, meetings, board, profit sharing are all written there; the figures given here are general/scheme-level.

## Three things to remember

- 🏗️ CM-PACS are different from traditional PACS — they are said to be designed for multipurpose business activities (petrol pump, godown, processing).
- 👥 Under the law, registration needs at least 10 adults (from 5 families); the specific conditions of a CM-PACS are in its bye-laws.
- ⚠️ Before profit, the reserve fund (10%) and the doubtful debt fund (10%) — Section 87; dividend after that.

## In the end

The core idea of CM-PACS is simple but powerful — to take the village cooperative institution beyond being just a credit centre and make it a multipurpose economic centre, which meets many of the farmer's needs in one place and also keeps its own sources of income diverse. But real success lies not in the structure but in discipline — inclusive membership, democratic governance (one member–one vote), clean activity-wise accounts, and transparent profit sharing. And since we have not seen the text of the CM-PACS Model Bye-laws, before taking any step do confirm from your society's approved bye-laws and the ARCS office.
