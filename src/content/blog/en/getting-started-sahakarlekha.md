# Getting started with SahakarLekha: from setup to your first report

When you open the software for the first time, the question is always — "Where do I start, and in what order?" This article gives a clear, step-by-step path — from registering to taking out your first report — so that your society is ready to work quickly.

## The first path — at a glance
1. **Register** — create your society's account (plans from ₹2,999/year + GST; [see prices](/pricing)).
2. **Society Setup** — name, type, financial year, chart of accounts.
3. Add the **necessary accounts and members**.
4. Enter the **opening balance**.
5. Create your **first voucher**.
6. Take out your **first report**.

> 💡 The most important order — setup and opening balance first, then daily entries. Doing it the other way round means doing the work again.

## Steps 1–2: Register and setup
After registering, do the **Society Setup** — choose the society's name and **type**, set the **financial year** (April–March in India), and look over the standard chart. The type and the financial year affect every later report.

## Steps 3–4: Accounts, members and opening
Create the necessary **accounts (ledger heads)** (look at the existing standard chart first), then add **members**. Now enter the **opening balance** of each account — from the previous closing position, and make sure that **Dr = Cr** matches.

> ⚠️ A wrong or non-matching opening balance spoils every report for the whole year — get it right here and the whole year is right.

## Steps 5–6: First voucher and report
Now create your **first voucher** — the right type (Receipt/Payment/Journal/Contra), accounts, amount, date and a clear narration. Then open the **reports** and look at the Trial Balance or Cash Book — and that's it, your society is live!

> 💡 The biggest benefit of digital — you don't have to make reports by hand, you get them in one click.

## What changes with the society type

Every cooperative society's needs are not the same, so the type you choose in setup decides a lot of what follows:

- **PACS / agricultural credit society:** focus on members, shares, deposits, loans/KCC and interest; monitoring of recovery and NPA.
- **Consumer store / retail:** purchases and sales, stock and POS billing at the centre.
- **Marketing/processing:** purchases, commission, godown and an activity-wise trading account.
- **Dairy:** daily milk collection, fat/SNF-based member payments.
- **Multipurpose:** several of the activities above together — activity-wise accounts are needed.

Choosing the right type gives you a suitable standard chart and report structure ready-made, which makes the start faster.

## Before you start — what to keep ready

A little preparation makes the start much easier. Keep at hand:

- The society's **name, registration number and type**.
- Last year's **closing accounts** (closing balances) — for entering the opening balance.
- The **member list** (name, shares, deposit/loan position).
- Details and latest balances of **bank accounts**.
- A list of running **loans/deposits** (where applicable).

If you gather all this beforehand, the setup can be finished in a single sitting.

## How to choose the society type

Choosing the society type in setup is important, because it affects the chart of accounts and the report structure. PACS, consumer store, marketing, dairy, multipurpose — each type has its own needs. Choose the type written in your society's registration certificate; if you are unsure, take the closest option (accounts can be added later). Choosing the right type gives you a suitable standard chart ready-made, and the society becomes ready to work with less effort.

## What is the chart of accounts (list of accounts)

The "chart of accounts" is an organised list of all your society's accounts (ledger heads) — such as cash, bank, share capital, member deposits, loans, interest income, salary expense and so on. The software gives a standard chart in advance based on the type you chose, so you don't have to create accounts from zero. Look at this standard chart first; add only the account your society needs that is not already there. Avoid creating unnecessary accounts — a clean, short chart keeps the reports clean too.

## Opening balance — a little deeper

The opening balance means the balance of each account at the start of the financial year — which comes from last year's closing balance. This is the most delicate step, because one mistake here will show in every report for the whole year. Two things are essential:

1. Take **the correct balance of each account** from last year's Balance Sheet/closing.
2. **Total Dr = total Cr** must match — if it doesn't, some account has been missed or an amount is wrong. Don't leave it saying "we'll look at it later".

For a new society (its very first year), the opening will be limited to the starting capital/cash only.

## Voucher types — which one when

Every daily entry is a voucher. The four main types:

- **Receipt:** money came into the society (such as a member deposit, interest recovery).
- **Payment:** money went out of the society (such as salary, expenses).
- **Journal:** an adjustment entry without cash/bank (such as charging interest, depreciation).
- **Contra:** a transfer between cash and bank (such as withdrawing cash from the bank).

Choosing the right type is important — this is what posts the accounts to the right place.

## Which reports you get automatically

The biggest reward of correct entries is that reports don't have to be made by hand — they are ready in one click:

- **Cash Book / Bank Book:** daily cash and bank transactions and balances.
- **Ledger:** detailed transactions and balance of each account.
- **Day Book:** a date-wise serial record of all vouchers.
- **Trial Balance:** balances of all accounts, always Dr = Cr.
- **Trading and Profit & Loss account (Trading, P&L):** income, expenses and profit or loss.
- **Receipts & Payments:** an important report in the cooperative sector.
- **Balance Sheet:** the overall financial position of the society.

The base of all of these is your correct daily entry — so keep entries clean to avoid "garbage in, garbage out".

## More than one user and roles

So that one person does not handle everything in a large society, role-based access is useful — for example, full control for the secretary, only receipts/payments for the cashier, and for some people only the right to view reports. This divides the work and lowers the risk of wrongdoing, because each person can only do their own part. At the start one user is enough; add more as the need grows.

## If you are coming from paper or Tally/Excel

If your society has so far been on paper registers or Tally/Excel, don't worry — you don't have to enter all the old data at once. The most practical way — treat the start of a financial year as the "cut-off", enter the closing balances of that day as the opening balance, and make every entry after that digital. The records of old years can be kept for reference. This way, without a heavy migration, the society goes digital from a clean point.

## Data security and backup

A big benefit of going digital — the fear of losing data ends. In a cloud-based system your accounts stay on a secure server and are backed up automatically, so the records are safe even if the computer breaks or a register is lost. Still, keep two good habits — keep regular backups on, and keep login details safe and limited to few people. This small care protects your whole year's accounts from every risk. (See: [Data security and backup](/blog/data-security-and-backup).)

## A realistic goal for the first week

Don't try to do everything on the first day. A good first-week goal: setup and chart on day 1-2, members and opening balance (with Dr=Cr matched) on day 3-4, the first voucher and first report on day 5. Once this is done your society is "live" — the rest (GST/TDS, bulk entry, extra users) you can add later as needed. Small goals that you actually finish are what give real speed.

## If you get stuck

Small questions at the start are natural. Every page has "how to" and glossary links, and the help pages (such as adding a member, opening balance, first voucher) give step-by-step guidance. The best way — first try one small, real entry; learning by doing is faster than learning by reading. If you get stuck, contact/support options are also available.

## A real example — the first entry

Suppose your society gets a ₹5,000 deposit from a member. Setup and opening are done, so now: open a **Receipt** voucher → enter the date → debit "Cash/Bank" (where the money came) → credit "Member deposit" → amount ₹5,000 → narration "Deposit of member X" → save. That's it — this one entry will automatically show in the cash book, that member's account, the Trial Balance and the reports. This is the magic of double entry: record it correctly once, and the system handles the rest. Do the first few entries carefully; after that it becomes easy daily work.

## After setup — the daily routine

Once setup is done, the real benefit comes from small daily habits — record every transaction the same day, do the bank reconciliation every month, and keep regular backups. This "a little, every day" discipline ends the year-end rush.

## What not to do at the start

Knowing what to avoid is as important as knowing what to do. At the start — don't try to enter all the old data at once (take a cut-off and start from the opening); don't create every possible account in advance (add only what you need); don't add many users at once (first understand the flow yourself); and most important — don't start daily entries without matching the opening. Avoiding these four things is half the success, because all four create rework later. A slow but correct start is always better than a fast but tangled one.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Starting daily entries before the opening** — then you have to reconcile again.
- **Ignoring Dr=Cr not matching** — it follows you all year.
- Choosing the **wrong voucher type** — accounts go to the wrong place.
- Creating **too many unnecessary accounts** — reports get confusing.
- **Not taking backups** — you miss the biggest benefit of digital.

## Frequently asked questions
**Where do I start?** — Register → Society Setup → accounts/members → opening → first voucher → report.
**Do I need to learn everything first?** — No; the first few steps are enough, you will learn the rest as you go.
**Is the opening balance needed first?** — Yes; setup and opening first, then daily entries.
**Where do I find the society type?** — Choose the type written in the registration certificate; if unsure, take the closest one.
**What should a new society's opening be?** — In the first year, the opening will be limited to the starting capital/cash only.
**What if Dr=Cr doesn't match?** — Some account is missed or an amount is wrong; don't put it in suspense, match it first.
**Which voucher when?** — Money came = Receipt, went = Payment, cash-bank transfer = Contra, non-cash adjustment = Journal.
**Do I have to enter all the old data?** — No; treat one financial year as the cut-off, put its closing balances in the opening, and run digitally from there.
**What should the first-week goal be?** — Setup + chart, members + opening (Dr=Cr), then the first voucher and first report — with that the society is live.
**Which reports do I get?** — Cash/Bank Book, Ledger, Day Book, Trial Balance, Trading/P&L, Receipts & Payments and Balance Sheet — all in one click.
**Can I add more than one user?** — Yes; with role-based access each person can be given only the rights they need. Start with just one.
