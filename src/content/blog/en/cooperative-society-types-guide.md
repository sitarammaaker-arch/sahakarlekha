# Types of cooperative society: which setup suits whom

"Cooperative society" is one name, but it takes many forms — PACS, dairy, consumer, marketing, multipurpose, housing, labour and many more. The cooperative movement in India is very large: according to the National Cooperative Database, the country has about **8.54 lakh cooperative societies** and around **31.54 crore members**, spread across more than 30 sectors. That is why it is important to understand the **types of cooperative society** — only then can you decide what kind of accounting setup your society needs. This article explains each classification in simple language, with examples and sources. (See: [Ministry of Cooperation](https://www.cooperation.gov.in/en/ministry-cooperation))

## Classify a cooperative society from three angles

The same society can be seen on three different bases — and all three apply at the same time:

1. **By function** — what it does: credit, milk, marketing, housing, etc.
2. **By structure** — at what level: primary → central/district → state/apex.
3. **By jurisdiction** — in one state or in many states.

Example: a PACS = a credit society **by function**, primary level **in structure**, and usually single-state **in jurisdiction**. Together, the three make up its identity.

## 1) Types by function

This is the most common classification, and it decides the accounting needs:

| Type | Main work | Special accounting need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PACS / agricultural credit** | Short-term loans to farmers (KCC) | Loans, interest, recovery, NPA |
| **Dairy** | Milk collection and payment | Member-wise milk, rate, payment cycle |
| **Consumer** | Retail sale of goods | Stock, sales, GST |
| **Marketing** | Purchase and sale of produce (MSP) | Stock, commission, MSP entries |
| **Multipurpose** | Many activities together | All of the above — a flexible setup |
| **Housing** | Buildings/plots, member contributions | Member accounts, funds, project |
| **Producer/weaver/fisheries/labour** | Production and joint marketing | Turnover, wages/dividend, stock |

> 💡 So "one software for all" is good only when it understands **the language and needs of every type of society** — not general business software. (See: [all cooperative society types](/software).)

### A closer look at each type

- **PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Society):** the backbone of the village — gives farmers KCC and short-term crop loans, sells fertiliser and seed, and often runs storage too. Its accounts revolve around loan disbursement, interest, recovery % and NPA.
- **Dairy society:** collects milk from members every day, fixes the rate by fat/SNF, and pays in a fixed cycle — here member-wise quantity and accurate payment matter most.
- **Consumer society:** sells goods to members/the public at fair prices — stock, sales, GST and the year-end dividend/patronage bonus are at its centre.
- **Marketing society:** buys and sells members' produce at MSP/market rates and removes middlemen — purchase, commission and godown stock are its main accounts.
- **Multipurpose society:** many jobs at once (credit + supply + marketing) — it needs the most flexible setup.
- **Housing society:** keeps accounts of buildings/plots, member contributions, maintenance charges and the sinking fund.

## 2) By structure — the three-tier system

Especially in the credit sector, cooperative institutions are linked in three tiers:

- **Primary level (village)** — PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Society), directly linked to farmer-members.
- **District/central level** — DCCB (District Central Cooperative Bank), which links the PACS of the district.
- **State/apex level** — StCB (State Cooperative Bank) and state-level unions/federations.

**Example:** a farmer is a member of the PACS in his village; the PACS is linked to the DCCB of its district; the DCCB to the state's StCB. Money and reports move up and down this ladder. (See: [Tiers of cooperative society — primary/central/state/apex](/blog/cooperative-society-tiers-primary-central-state-apex).)

## 3) By jurisdiction — single-state vs multi-state

- **Single-state society** — works in only one state; comes under its state's **Cooperative Societies Act** and the state Registrar (RCS).
- **Multi-State society** — works in more than one state; comes under the **Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002 (MSCS Act)** and the **Central Registrar (CRCS)**.

**Example:** a society selling milk in one district is single-state (state Act); a large federation spread over many states is multi-state (MSCS Act 2002). (See: [Multi-State society member rights](/blog/multi-state-cooperative-society-member-rights) and [Central Registrar — constitutional provisions](https://crcs.gov.in/constitutional_provisions).)

## Legal and policy basis

- **State Cooperative Societies Act** — the basic law for most societies; registration, audit and elections all come from it.
- **Multi-State Act, 2002** — for societies spread over many states.
- **97th Constitutional Amendment (2011)** — linked forming a cooperative society to a fundamental right (Article 19(1)(c)) and added **Part IX-B (Articles 243ZH–243ZT)**; the Supreme Court later clarified that Part IX-B applies mainly to multi-state societies.
- **Ministry of Cooperation** — became a separate ministry on **6 July 2021**, giving cooperation its own policy framework.
- **National Cooperation Policy 2025** — released on 24 July 2025; replaces the old 2002 policy, with the goal of "Sahakar se Samriddhi" (prosperity through cooperation). (See: [PIB — National Cooperation Policy 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2148058))

> ⚠️ Exactly which Act/Rules apply to your society depends on its registration and jurisdiction — confirm with the Registrar/CA. (For a new society see: [How to form a cooperative society](/blog/how-to-register-a-cooperative-society).)

## Why cooperation matters — and why these types count

Cooperative societies are the backbone of India's rural economy — according to the National Cooperative Database they reach about **98% of rural India**. Different types built different revolutions: **dairy** cooperatives built the "White Revolution" (e.g. Amul/NDDB), **fertiliser** cooperatives built farm supply (e.g. IFFCO/KRIBHCO), and **credit** cooperatives (PACS) brought cheap loans to farmers. This is why each type has its own needs and accounts — and the right setup is decided by that.

### Well-known examples at the apex/national level

Above the three-tier structure there are also large national cooperative institutions, each the backbone of its own sector:

- **Fertiliser:** IFFCO, KRIBHCO — farmers' fertiliser-supply cooperatives. (See: [IFFCO](/blog/iffco-fertiliser-cooperative).)
- **Dairy:** Amul/GCMMF and NDDB — the base of the White Revolution. (See: [Amul — the Anand pattern](/blog/amul-anand-pattern-dairy-cooperative).)
- **Marketing:** NAFED — marketing of farm produce.
- **Finance/development:** NABARD (refinance and supervision) and NCDC (cooperative development finance). (See: [Role of NABARD](/blog/nabard-role-in-cooperatives) and [Role of NCDC](/blog/ncdc-role-in-cooperatives).)

## Cooperative society vs company — the basic difference

- **Purpose:** a company's main goal is profit; a cooperative society's is **service to members** ("Sahakar se Samriddhi").
- **Control:** in a company, votes go by shareholding; in a society, **one member = one vote**.
- **Law/Registrar:** company → Companies Act/RoC; society → Cooperative Act/Registrar (RCS/CRCS).
- **Profit distribution:** in a society, a large part of the profit goes to reserves and member welfare/patronage.
- **Membership:** membership of a society is usually limited to people linked to its area/purpose — that is, those who actually use its services (farmers, milk producers, consumers). (See: [Cooperative society vs company](/blog/cooperative-vs-company-difference).)

## What stays the same in every type

Whatever the type, the foundation of accounting is the same:

- Correct **voucher entry** → cash book, ledger, Trial Balance. (See: [Voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide).)
- A clean record of **members and shares**.
- **Final accounts + annual audit** — the same way for everyone. (See: [How a cooperative society audit works](/blog/how-cooperative-society-audit-works).)

## What differs

- **Consumer/marketing** → stock and GST matter more. (See: [Inventory and stock management](/blog/inventory-and-stock-management).)
- **PACS** → loans/KCC and recovery/NPA at the centre.
- **Dairy** → member-wise quantity and payment cycle.
- **Housing** → member-wise contributions, funds and project accounts.
- **Producer/weaver/fisheries/labour** → production cost, joint marketing income, and sharing of wages/dividend among members.
- **Multi-State society** → consolidated accounts of many states and reporting to the Central Registrar (CRCS) — so branch/state-wise accounts are needed.

## Common confusion in identifying the type

- **PACS vs cooperative bank:** a PACS is a primary credit **society** (under the Registrar), while DCCB/StCB are **banks** (under NABARD/RBI). Their reporting and rules are different. (See: [NABARD and federation returns](/blog/nabard-and-federation-returns).)
- **Consumer vs marketing:** a consumer society **sells** to members (retail); a marketing society **buys members' produce and sells it on** (commission/MSP). Both hold stock, but in opposite directions.
- **Single-state vs multi-state:** large size alone does not make a society multi-state — multi-state is only one whose members/business truly spread across **more than one state**.
- **Multipurpose does not mean "anything":** only the activities written in its bye-laws are valid.

> 💡 When in doubt, look at your registration certificate and bye-laws — your type and permitted activities are clearly written there.

## Accounts and registers each type needs (examples)

- **PACS:** loan account, interest, recovery register, KCC limit, NPA classification.
- **Dairy:** member-wise milk collection, rate chart, payment register.
- **Consumer:** stock/inventory, sales, GST ledger, dividend (patronage).
- **Marketing:** purchase (MSP), commission income, godown stock.
- **Housing:** member accounts, maintenance charges, sinking/reserve fund.
- **Producer/labour:** production cost, work orders/projects, wages and dividend sharing.
- **Multipurpose:** a mix of several of the above — separate accounts per activity so that each activity's profit and loss shows separately.

> 💡 The good news: these "special" needs are added on top of the same common foundation — the base stays the same, only a few accounts/registers change with your type.

## The right setup for your society — 3 steps

1. Identify your **type** (function + structure + jurisdiction).
2. Choose the **accounts and registers** you need accordingly (loans/stock/milk etc.).
3. The rest of the foundation (vouchers, reports, audit) is the same for all — set it up correctly once.

**Example:** suppose you are a district **consumer society**. Then — type: consumer (function), primary (structure), single-state (jurisdiction). Special accounts needed: stock/inventory, sales, GST ledger, patronage. The rest (voucher→Trial Balance→Balance Sheet→audit) is the same as every society. Once you decide just this much, your whole setup becomes clear.

## Three things to remember

- 🧭 Look at your society from three angles — function, structure, jurisdiction.
- 🧱 The foundation of accounting (voucher→report→audit) is the same in every type.
- ➕ Just add the special needs of your type (loans/stock/milk/housing) on top.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the main types of cooperative society?** — By function: PACS/agricultural credit, dairy, consumer, marketing, multipurpose, housing, producer/weaver/fisheries/labour, etc.
**What is the difference between a single-state and a multi-state society?** — A single-state society comes under the state Act and the state Registrar; a multi-state society comes under the MSCS Act 2002 and the Central Registrar (CRCS).
**At what level is a PACS?** — A primary-level agricultural credit society, the base of the three-tier system linked to the district (DCCB) and the state (StCB).
**Does every type need separate software?** — No; the foundation is the same. If the software understands the special needs of your type (loans/stock/milk), one is enough.
**How many cooperative societies are there in India?** — According to the National Cooperative Database, about 8.54 lakh societies and ~31.54 crore members (in 30+ sectors).
**When was a separate ministry for cooperation formed?** — The Ministry of Cooperation was formed on 6 July 2021; it also released the new National Cooperation Policy in 2025.
**Can a multipurpose society do any work?** — No; only the activities written and permitted in its bye-laws.
**Where is my society's type written?** — In the registration certificate and bye-laws — the type and permitted activities are clearly recorded there.
**Can the type be changed?** — To add/change activities, the bye-laws must be amended and approved by the Registrar; not at will.
**Can a PACS also sell fertiliser and seed?** — Yes, most PACS also do fertiliser/seed supply and storage along with credit — so their setup covers both loans + stock.

## In the end

Every cooperative society is different, but the principle of correct accounting is one — correct entries, clean records, audit-ready reports. First identify your society's type from three angles (function, structure, jurisdiction), then add its special needs on top of the basic setup — everything else is the same. Identifying the type correctly is the first step to the right setup, the right reports and an easy audit, so getting it clear at the start makes the whole year's work lighter.
