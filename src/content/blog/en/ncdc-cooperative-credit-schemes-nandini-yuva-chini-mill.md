The National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC) is not just a general financing agency — it has made separate, targeted schemes for certain kinds of cooperative societies. Here, understand three such schemes — Nandini Sahakar for women-majority societies, Yuva Sahakar for new and innovative societies, and modernisation loans for cooperative sugar mills. All three share one condition — eligibility is limited only to registered cooperative societies; no private entrepreneur or firm can apply directly. This guide explains all three — for whom, what conditions, how to apply — in simple language.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** The conditions have been matched with NCDC's "Pattern of Assistance of Schemes Implemented by NCDC" document (PDF, July 2025) and the Ministry of Cooperation's PIB releases (see "Sources" below). NCDC's interest rate changes from time to time through its circulars — before applying, confirm the latest conditions with NCDC's regional office.

## Nandini Sahakar scheme — for women-majority societies

According to NCDC's document, eligible are: (1) a **women's cooperative society** registered under any State or Multi-State Cooperative Act, and (2) any cooperative society in which **at least 50 percent of members are women** at the primary level. In projects with new/innovative activities, women's societies that have been running for at least **three months** are also eligible.

**Interest subvention:** **2 percent** on NCDC's term loan rate for new and innovative activities, and **1 percent** for other activities — this subvention is given only on **timely repayment of instalments**.

**Project cost limit:** If the society is 3 months to 1 year old, up to ₹1 crore; if 1 to 3 years old, up to ₹3 crore; and if it has been running successfully for more than 3 years, no limit (as per need). Loan period 5 to 8 years, including a principal moratorium of 1-2 years.

> 💡 **"Swayam Shakti Sahakar Yojana"** is a separate NCDC scheme, in which PACS, DCCBs, state cooperative banks or SHG federations take loans from NCDC and on-lend to **women's self-help groups (SHGs)**. For this, the society should have been running for at least 3 years, its net worth should be positive, and there should be no cash loss in the last 3 years.

Nandini Sahakar covers almost every business activity within NCDC's scope except urban housing — such as agro-processing, supply chain, farm machinery, retail trade, horticulture, rural housing, dairy, poultry, animal husbandry, fisheries, handloom, jute, silk, textiles, PACS infrastructure, tourism, transport, hospitals/health, education and renewable energy.

## Yuva Sahakar scheme — for new and innovative societies

"Yuva Sahakar – Cooperative Enterprise Support and Innovation Scheme" was started in **November 2018** (PIB, 27 February 2019). It is linked to the "Cooperative Start-up and Innovation Fund" created by NCDC, and its aim is to promote new societies with new or innovative ideas.

**Eligibility (NCDC's document):** Any kind of cooperative society whose project is new, innovative or value-chain enhancing; the society should have been running for at least **three months**; net worth should be positive; and there should be no cash loss in the previous year(s).

**Project cost limit:** If the society is 1 year old or more, up to ₹3 crore; if 3 months to 1 year old, up to ₹1 crore. Working capital not more than 20% of project cost.

**Loan and own share:** Societies of the North-East, societies in NITI Aayog's aspirational districts, and societies with 100% women or 100% SC/ST/divyang members — for these, loan : own share = **80 : 20**. For all others **70 : 30**. (PIB, 11 February 2026 also says the same: generally 30% contribution, 20% in special categories.)

**Interest and period:** NCDC charges **2 percent less** than its applicable term loan rate — but this subvention is given **only on timely repayment of instalments**. The loan can be for up to 5 years, including a moratorium on principal of up to 2 years; the moratorium can vary by project.

This scheme is for start-ups in all kinds of cooperative activities. According to PIB (11 February 2026), a total of ₹432.92 lakh had been disbursed under it up to 31.12.2025 — that is, it is a small scheme, but it can give a new society a cheap starting loan.

## Cooperative sugar mill modernisation loan

NCDC lends only to cooperative societies, so this facility is for **cooperative sugar mills** — private sugar mills are not within its scope. NCDC's document has these heads for cooperative sugar mills:

- **New sugar unit:** term loan and share capital/investment loan, member contribution 10% (through the government) or 30% (direct).
- **Modernisation / expansion / working capital:** loan "as per need".
- **By-products (cogeneration and ethanol units):** term loan 90%, member contribution 10%.

> 💡 In this check we did not see information on state governments' own incentives (such as on mechanical sugarcane harvesting) or loans sanctioned to any particular mill. For this, ask your state cooperation department, Sugarfed or NCDC's regional office.

## The three schemes at a glance

| Scheme | For whom | Main focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nandini Sahakar | Women's cooperative societies, or societies with 50%+ women members | Women's empowerment; 2% / 1% interest subvention (on timely instalments) |
| Yuva Sahakar | Societies with new/innovative projects, running for at least 3 months | Cooperative start-ups; up to ₹1-3 crore; 2% lower interest |
| Sugar mill modernisation loan | Registered cooperative sugar mills | Modernisation capital and working capital |

This table shows that the three schemes meet different needs of different kinds of societies — so first it is necessary to identify your category, then look at the detailed conditions of that scheme.

## The thinking behind the three schemes

- The thinking behind **Nandini Sahakar** is — to give economic strength to women-led societies, so that rural women become self-reliant in production, processing and services. NCDC's separate "Swayam Shakti Sahakar Yojana" takes loans to women's SHGs through credit societies. (See: [Women's cooperative societies](/blog/women-cooperatives-role).)
- The thinking behind **Yuva Sahakar** is — to bring newness and entrepreneurship into the cooperative sector, especially by encouraging new societies promoted by youth and deprived sections. It comes under the "Cooperative Start-up and Innovation Fund (CSIF)".
- The thinking behind the **sugar mill modernisation loan** is — to revive old cooperative sugar mills with new technology and machinery, so that they can give sugarcane farmers timely payment and run better.

The shared principle in all three — making the cooperative society self-reliant by giving it capital and support, and ultimately passing the benefit to members/farmers. NCDC gives its help either **directly** to the society, or **through the State Government/UT** — the percentage of own share differs between the two routes. So for a large cooperative project/activity, these targeted NCDC schemes are often the most suitable route.

## Why these schemes matter — what changes for a small society

The shared aim of all three schemes is the same — to help cooperative societies stand on their own feet. A small society often does not have the capital to start a big activity (processing, storage, machinery); these schemes give that capital at concessional interest and good terms, so that the society can build new sources of income. Women-majority societies (Nandini Sahakar), new-innovative societies (Yuva Sahakar) and cooperative sugar mills — all three get support according to their particular need. The real benefit is that the society does not just survive, but grows and becomes self-reliant — and the benefit ultimately reaches the members. (See: [Role of NCDC](/blog/ncdc-role-in-cooperatives).)

## Preparing the application — DPR and documents

The heart of a successful application under these schemes is a strong **project report (DPR)**. It should make clear: what is to be built/done, its cost, the expected income, and the loan repayment plan. Besides this, keep ready — the society's registration and bye-law documents, past audited accounts, member details (such as proof of women's membership for Nandini), and the managing committee resolution. A clear, realistic DPR and clean accounts strengthen the application and improve the chance of sanction. Keep in mind — under these schemes finance often comes in instalments and according to progress, so it is necessary to keep separate, transparent project-wise accounts.

## How to check eligibility

Before applying, identify your society's category, because the eligibility of each scheme is different:

- Is your society a **women's cooperative society**, or does it have 50%+ women primary members? → See **Nandini Sahakar**.
- Is your project **new/innovative**, and has the society been running for at least 3 months? → See **Yuva Sahakar** (societies of the North-East, aspirational districts, and those with 100% women/SC/ST/divyang members get the benefit of 80:20).
- Is it a **registered cooperative sugar mill**? → See the **sugar mill modernisation loan**.

If more than one category applies, take advice from your state cooperation department/NCDC on the most suitable option.

## Which businesses get support

One important point — these schemes are not limited to agriculture. Their scope covers many kinds of cooperative activities: grain and oilseed processing, dairy and animal husbandry, fisheries and poultry, storage and godowns, textile industries such as spinning/handloom/powerloom, and (in the Nandini Sahakar list) also sectors like tourism, hospitality, transport, health and education; Yuva Sahakar is for start-ups in every kind of cooperative activity. That is, if a cooperative society wants to enter any productive or service activity, there is a good chance these schemes have room for it — provided it meets the eligibility conditions. The exact eligible activities vary by scheme, so confirm the current list with NCDC.

## How to apply — general process

The basic path of application is the same in all three schemes:

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Check eligibility | Match the conditions of the relevant scheme according to your society's category (women-majority, new/innovative, or cooperative sugar mill) |
| Submit the application | Through NCDC's website (ncdc.in), or through the state cooperation department |
| Scrutiny and sanction | NCDC sanctions after scrutiny; in direct loans, security to NCDC's satisfaction (such as mortgage of property, bank FDR or guarantee) has to be given |
| Get updated information | Interest rate, subsidy and eligibility conditions change from time to time — before applying, be sure to confirm the current conditions with your state cooperation department or NCDC regional office |

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an individual entrepreneur apply directly for these schemes?** — No; in all three schemes eligibility is limited only to registered cooperative societies.
**What minimum women's membership is needed for the Nandini Sahakar scheme?** — It should be a women's cooperative society, or at least 50% of members at the primary level should be women. In new/innovative projects, a women's society 3 months old is also eligible.
**What is the maximum loan available under the Yuva Sahakar scheme?** — For a society 1 year+ old, a project of up to ₹3 crore (₹1 crore for one 3 months to 1 year old), with a loan of 70% or 80% of cost depending on category.
**How much interest subvention is there in Yuva Sahakar?** — 2% less than NCDC's rate, but only on timely repayment of instalments; loan up to 5 years, moratorium up to 2 years.
**Are private sugar mills also eligible for NCDC's modernisation loan?** — No, this facility is only for registered cooperative sugar mills.
**What should be kept ready for the application?** — A strong DPR (project report), registration/bye-laws, past audited accounts, member details and the managing committee resolution.
**Which businesses do these schemes support?** — Processing, dairy/animal husbandry, fisheries/poultry, storage, textile industries, and (in Nandini Sahakar) even tourism/hospitality/transport/health; Yuva Sahakar is for start-ups in every kind of activity.
**How to choose the right scheme?** — Identify your society's category (women-majority/new-innovative/sugar mill), then match the conditions of the relevant scheme.
**How does NCDC give help?** — Directly to the society, or through the State Government/UT (NCDC's document).
**What is the Swayam Shakti Sahakar Yojana?** — A separate NCDC scheme in which PACS, DCCBs, state cooperative banks or SHG federations take loans from NCDC and on-lend to women's SHGs.

## Three things to remember

- 👩‍🌾 Nandini Sahakar is for women's cooperative societies and societies with 50%+ women members, Yuva Sahakar for societies with new/innovative projects (at least 3 months old), and the sugar mill loan for cooperative sugar mills.
- 💰 The exact rates of interest subvention and subsidy keep changing from time to time — before applying, always confirm the current conditions with NCDC or the state cooperation department; the figures given here are for general understanding.
- 📝 Private entrepreneurs/firms are not eligible in any of the schemes — only a registered, active cooperative society can apply.
- 📄 The heart of a successful application — a strong DPR (project report), clean past accounts, and separate project-wise accounts.

## Finally

These targeted NCDC schemes are proof that cooperative development is not one-size-fits-all, but follows the particular need of each kind of society — whether women-majority, new-innovative, or an old sugar mill. The key to benefiting from any scheme lies in three things: identifying the right category, making a realistic DPR, and keeping clean, transparent accounts of the project. And most important — since the exact rates of interest subvention, subsidy and eligibility keep changing from time to time, always confirm the current conditions with NCDC or your state cooperation department before applying. The figures in this article are for general understanding, not the final authority.
