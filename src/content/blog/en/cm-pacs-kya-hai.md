Say "cooperative society" in a village and most people picture only one thing — a PACS that distributes fertiliser and seed or gives farmers a small loan. Haryana has started a model that goes beyond this idea — the **Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activities Cooperative Society**, that is, the **CM-PACS**. According to a news report of January 2025, the government wants to open a CM-PACS in every village, and these will be separate from the PACS already running in the village. On 18 October 2026, the RCS Haryana dashboard was showing **546 registered CM-PACS**.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** We did not find the official text of the CM-PACS Model Bye-laws (the RCS Haryana order) in this check. So several points from the earlier version of this article — the launch date and memo number, the membership conditions (10+2, 5 acres), the fee and share amounts, the size of the committee, the livestream rule — have been removed. The points matched against the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 and Rules 1989 are listed under "Sources" below.

> 💡 Note — CM-PACS does not mean "Chief Minister PACS"; it means **Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activities Cooperative Society** (this is the name quoted from the minister in The Tribune's report of 6 July 2025). It is a state-specific initiative of Haryana.

## What a CM-PACS actually is — its legal status

A CM-PACS is not a temporary government scheme; it is a registered, independent legal body. Under **Section 4(1)** of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984, a society can be registered only if its object is "the promotion of the economic interests of its members in accordance with cooperative principles" — this can be called the "object test". A society whose members are all individuals (no other society) and whose object is to promote the common interest of its members is called a "primary society" by **Section 2(o)** of the Act — the village-level CM-PACS and the traditional PACS both come under this general framework, but their object and scope are different.

After registration, under **Section 39** every cooperative society becomes a **"body corporate"** — that is, it has its own legal identity separate from its members, perpetual succession, its own seal, and the right to hold property, enter into contracts and file or defend lawsuits. Under **Section 9**, the registration certificate is by itself "conclusive evidence" that the society has been validly registered — that is why, as soon as it gets the certificate, the society can start work such as opening a bank account, buying property or joining government schemes.

> 💡 If the society is registered with limited liability, it is mandatory under **Section 4(2)** that the last word of its name is "Limited" (or its equivalent in an Indian language) — this is a clear sign of the members' limited liability.

## Why the CM-PACS was brought in

One clear problem is visible behind this model — traditional, credit-only societies often stay financially weak, because their income rests on a single source (loan interest). The multi-purpose model is the answer to this: when one society runs several businesses, it gets several sources of income and can become self-reliant. At the same time, the village member gets several services in one place. According to the same January 2025 report, a PACS mainly gives farmers loans, fertiliser and seed, while the main object of a CM-PACS is business activities.

## How it is different from a traditional PACS

A traditional PACS is mostly built around agricultural credit. The idea of the CM-PACS is to bring pre-farming needs (seed, fertiliser, machines), post-harvest needs (storage, procurement, processing, sale) and non-farm business into one body. Note that at the national level too, the Ministry of Cooperation's [PACS model bye-laws](/blog/pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-explained) (5 January 2023) talk about making PACS multi-purpose — so the picture of "PACS = only loans" is also changing now.

## What work can be done

The exact list of activities of a CM-PACS is in its registered bye-laws. A news report of January 2025 gave these examples for CM-PACS: **petrol pumps, grain godowns, crop procurement, making food products and cottage industries**. We have not seen in any official text which other activities are in the CM-PACS Model Bye-laws — so the long list in the earlier version (dairy, LPG, fair price shop, taxi, CSC, manpower etc.) has been removed. Get a copy of your society's bye-laws from the ARCS and check.

Broadly, the work of any multi-purpose society can be seen in these groups:

| Group | How to understand it |
| --- | --- |
| Farm inputs and outputs | Seed-fertiliser, machines, storage, procurement and sale |
| Processing | Making food products from produce, cottage industries |
| Fuel and shop business | Businesses like a petrol pump (with separate approval of the company/department) |
| Services | Any service written in the bye-laws that is in the economic interest of members |

For any regulated work among these — such as a petrol/diesel outlet or a food business — getting separate eligibility, permission or a licence from the department/company concerned is the society's own responsibility. Having an objective in the bye-laws is not by itself a licence for that business — it is very important to understand this difference, otherwise the society may start some regulated work without permission and land in legal risk.

> ⚠️ Having many objectives does not mean a new CM-PACS should start all the work at once. For a practical start, first look at local demand, identify members' skills, and begin with two or three activities — for example input supply + produce aggregation + storage in a farming area.

If, later on, a new activity is not in the bye-laws, a bye-law amendment is needed for it — under **Rule 10**, a general body resolution (with at least two-thirds of members present) and then registration by the Registrar under **Section 10**.

## Who can become a member

Under **Section 16** of the Act, the following can become members: an individual competent to contract under Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act 1872, another cooperative society, the government, and other classes written in the bye-laws (groups of individuals, companies etc.). Any special condition for a CM-PACS (such as education, a land limit, being a resident of the area of operation) will be in its bye-laws — we found no source for the "10+2 qualification" and "not more than 5 acres of land" written in the earlier version, so they were removed. If a question arises about whether a person lives in the area of operation or falls in a class, the Registrar's decision is final under **Section 8A**.

For people who do not want to become full members but want a business relationship with the society, **Section 18** provides for an **associate member** — as per the bye-laws, an individual, self-help group or society can become an associate member. An associate member gets no share in the society's property or profits (Section 18(2)), and under Section 20 has no right to vote. The associate member's fee will be in the bye-laws.

The rules also set a time limit for deciding a membership application: under **Rule 17** (except for a producers' society), the society has to decide within **one month** of receiving the application, and if it refuses, it has to give the reasons. (The earlier version said "3 months" — that does not match Rule 17.) Under **Section 19**, until a member makes the payment written in the bye-laws (such as the share amount), he cannot exercise the rights of a member.

One important rule on share refund: under **Rule 18(3)**, in a society with limited liability, a member is generally not allowed to withdraw shares — refund is allowed only if the society has created a share transfer fund out of profits, and even then the total refund at any time cannot exceed 5% of the paid-up share capital (excluding the government's portion) as on the previous 31 March. The other conditions for leaving membership (notice, deductions) are in the bye-laws.

## Where the money comes from

The main sources of the society's income and capital are — earnings from its own business, members' share capital and admission fees, loans from banks/financial institutions, and grants-subsidies from the central and state governments and their agencies. On the share amount: according to the January 2025 news report, the share capital of a CM-PACS was first fixed at ₹20,000 per member and later made ₹1,000 (refundable) — but we did not find the official order for this. Under **Section 6**, a member (other than the government/another society) cannot hold shares above the fixed part of the total share capital (at most one-fifth) or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less. Every scheme has its own eligibility and conditions — merely being a CM-PACS does not get every subsidy automatically.

## Who runs the society

The supreme authority lies with the **General Body** (Section 25(1)). After the 2013 amendment, under **Section 25(3)** the general body meeting must be held within 6 months of the end of the financial year. Under **Section 20**, every member has one vote (the vote does not increase with the number of shares), and a defaulting member cannot vote.

Day-to-day decisions are taken by an elected **committee**, whose size is in the bye-laws. Under the proviso to **Section 28(1)**, the committee must have at least one Scheduled Caste member and (after 2013) **two women members** coming through election; if Backward Class members are 10% or more of the total membership, at least one of them too. Under **Section 28(4)**, the committee holds office for **5 years** from the date of election. The points written in the earlier version — "7 to 21 members", "2 professional directors", "livestream of every meeting on social media and a website mandatory", and "All for one, One for all" — could not be checked against the CM-PACS bye-laws, so they were removed.

On the distribution of profit, **Section 87(1)**: before profit is distributed, every year at least 10% goes to the reserve fund and at least 10% to the bad and doubtful debt fund, and up to 5% to the cooperative education fund; the rest as per the bye-laws or the Registrar.

## Where it fits in the legal framework

The CM-PACS works within the framework of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 and the Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules 1989 — we did not find any separate special law for it (see: [Act 1984 and Rules 1989: complete guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-act-1984-rules-1989-guide)). That is, the same registration, audit, election and dispute-resolution procedures apply to a CM-PACS as to any other primary society.

## Accounting — one challenge of being multi-purpose

Since a CM-PACS can run many different businesses at the same time, its accounting is more complex than that of a traditional PACS — separate income and cost, separate stock and separate profitability for each activity (input sales, godown, procurement, processing). If the accounts of all of them are mixed together, you will never know which activity is earning and which is in loss. So **activity-wise, transparent accounting** is very important for a CM-PACS — only then can the managing committee take the right decisions and the audit go smoothly. A good cooperative accounting software makes this work much easier. (See: [CM-PACS accounting, audit and tax guide](/blog/cm-pacs-accounting-tax-audit-guide).)

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the full name of CM-PACS?** — Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activities Cooperative Society; it is not "Chief Minister PACS" but a multi-purpose, state-specific cooperative initiative of Haryana.
**How many CM-PACS are there in Haryana?** — 546 registered on the RCS Haryana dashboard on 18 October 2026 (in July 2025 the minister had said 141).
**Is a CM-PACS only a farming society?** — No; the news report lists businesses like petrol pumps, godowns, crop procurement, processing and cottage industries. The exact list is in the society's bye-laws.
**Is a CM-PACS a temporary government scheme?** — No; once registered, it is a permanent body corporate under Section 39, with its own legal identity.
**What are the conditions for becoming a member?** — Section 16: an individual competent to contract, another society, the government, and classes written in the bye-laws. Confirm the special conditions of a CM-PACS from its bye-laws and the ARCS.
**Within how many days is a membership application decided?** — Rule 17: within one month (except for a producers' society); if refused, with reasons.
**Can every activity be started right away?** — An objective in the bye-laws is not a licence; regulated activities (petrol, food) need separate approval.
**What needs special care in accounting?** — With many businesses, separate, transparent activity-wise accounting is needed — so that the profitability of each activity is clearly visible.
**What is the difference between a full member and an associate member?** — An associate member (Section 18) gets no share in property/profits and no vote (Section 20); other rights as per the bye-laws.

## Three things to remember

- 🏛️ A CM-PACS is not a government scheme — once registered, it is a permanent, independent body corporate under Section 39.
- 📜 Rules on membership, shares and the committee come first from the Act and Rules (Sections 16, 20, 28, Rules 17, 18(3)), then from the society's bye-laws — not from figures going around on the internet.
- ⚠️ Every regulated activity (petrol, food) needs separate approval — an objective in the bye-laws is not a licence by itself.
- 🧾 Many businesses = separate, transparent activity-wise accounts are needed, so that the profitability of each activity is clearly visible.

## In the end

The CM-PACS is an ambitious form of cooperation in Haryana — a body that wants to take the village society beyond just "fertiliser-seed-loans" and make it a multi-service, self-reliant economic unit. But its success rests on two things: first, a practical start — not all the work at once, but two or three activities matched to local demand and members' ability; and second, clear, separate, transparent accounts for every activity. And for rules and conditions, always go to the Act, the Rules, your society's registered bye-laws and the ARCS office — these are the four reliable bases.
