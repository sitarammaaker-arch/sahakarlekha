# Women's Cooperative Societies: Role and Importance

Cooperation is not limited to farmers or credit — it has also played a big role in the **economic empowerment of women**. Lakhs of women in villages and towns have built their income, confidence and identity through cooperative societies and self-help groups. This article explains, in simple language, what women's cooperative societies are, their forms, importance, examples and how to run one.

## What is a women's cooperative society

It is a cooperative society whose members and managers are mainly **women**. The aim is the same cooperative one — saving, credit, production or marketing together — but the focus is on women's income and self-reliance. In such a society the women themselves take the decisions, they do the management, and the benefit also reaches them — this is what makes it special.

## The forms they take

- **Thrift & Credit:** depositing small savings and giving cheap loans among themselves.
- **Self-help group (SHG):** small groups of women who get loans by linking with a bank/cooperative.
- **Production and marketing:** collective production and sale of goods like papad, pickles, handloom, milk and handicrafts.

> 💡 A well-known example: **Shri Mahila Griha Udyog Lijjat Papad** — a cooperative initiative of women that became a big story of self-reliance.

### Understanding each form a little more

- **Thrift and credit society:** this is the most common and basic form. Women deposit small savings regularly and, when needed, take loans among themselves at low interest — freedom from the moneylender's heavy interest.
- **Self-help group (SHG):** a small group of 10–20 women that saves regularly and, by linking with a bank/cooperative (SHG–bank linkage), can also get a bigger loan — for a small business, animal husbandry or other needs.
- **Production-marketing society:** where women together make and sell some product (papad, pickles, handloom, handicrafts, milk) — collective production improves both quality and bargaining.

## Some inspiring examples

The strength of women's cooperation is clearly seen in some famous examples:

- **Shri Mahila Griha Udyog Lijjat Papad:** a huge, women-owned enterprise built from a small start by a few women — one of the most inspiring stories of self-reliance.
- **SEWA (Self Employed Women's Association):** an organisation of self-employed women, which organised lakhs of women through its cooperative and banking initiatives.
- **Women in dairy cooperatives:** in the Anand pattern of the White Revolution, women had a large share in village-level milk societies, so household income came directly into the woman's hands.

The common thread of these examples is — by organising and working transparently, women can become a big economic force. (See: [Amul and the Anand pattern](/blog/amul-anand-pattern-dairy-cooperative).)

## Importance

- **Economic self-reliance:** their own income, their own decisions.
- **Financial inclusion:** access to savings and credit, less dependence on the moneylender.
- **Leadership:** running a society builds management skills and confidence in women.

### These benefits reach the home and society

The impact of women's cooperation does not stay limited to one woman. When a woman has her own income, she often spends it on her children's education, nutrition and health — which raises the level of the whole family. Also, running a society builds women's capacity for leadership, accounting and collective decisions, which gives them a strong voice in village society too. In this way women's cooperation is a means not only of economic but also of social empowerment.

## Challenges

Women's cooperative societies also face some special challenges, which are important to understand: limited formal education or experience of accounts, difficulty in reaching markets and banks, and sometimes limited support from the family. The cure is — simple training, easy (Hindi) digital tools, and regular guidance. Where women get the right training and a transparent system, these societies become very strong.

## How to start and run a women's society

Broadly, the steps are the same as for any cooperative society — but with a women-centred approach:

1. **Form a group:** bring together women with a similar need/work.
2. **Decide the aim:** saving-credit, or some production-marketing.
3. **Registration/rules:** registration as a cooperative society, or bank linkage as an SHG.
4. **Clean accounts:** accurate member-wise records of savings, loans, interest and reserve fund — this is the life of trust.
5. **Regular meetings:** transparent decisions and review of accounts.

(The general registration process: [How to register a society](/blog/how-to-register-a-cooperative-society).)

## SHG–bank linkage — how it works

The self-help group (SHG) is the most widespread form of women's empowerment, and its strength lies in **SHG–bank linkage**. It broadly works like this:

1. **The group is formed:** 10–20 women deposit small savings regularly (for example, monthly).
2. **Internal lending:** at first the group gives small loans to members from its own deposited savings.
3. **Linking with the bank:** after some time of regular saving and good records, the group opens a bank account and gets a bigger loan (linkage) from the bank.
4. **Moving ahead:** this loan goes into members' small businesses, animal husbandry or needs, and recovery happens through group discipline.

The basis of this model's success is — **regular saving, good records, and group discipline.** And all three rest on clean accounts — so accurate, transparent records are the backbone of an SHG.

## The support system

Many institutions play a role in strengthening women's cooperatives and SHGs — such as NABARD (which promotes SHG–bank linkage), the State Rural Livelihood Mission, and the cooperative department. They help with training, guidance and financial linkage. A new women's society/SHG should find out about such support available in its area and use it. For accurate information and current schemes, contact the department/bank concerned. (See: [Role of NABARD](/blog/nabard-role-in-cooperatives).)

## Digital tools — making accounts easy

A common difficulty of a women's society/SHG is accounting. When the savings, loans and interest of many women have to be handled, mistakes and confusion come easily in a handwritten register. This is where a simple, Hindi-first digital tool makes a big difference: every member's savings and loans recorded automatically, correct interest calculation, and "who has how much" in one click — which increases both trust and transparency. When accounts are easy, the group's energy goes into moving ahead instead of sorting out accounts.

## An example

Suppose 15 women in a village formed an SHG, each saving ₹200 a month. In a year the group had ₹36,000 deposited, from which they gave small loans to members in need. After two years of good records, the bank gave the group a linkage loan of ₹1,50,000, with which several women started tailoring, animal husbandry and small shops. Throughout, accurate accounts of every member's savings, loans and repayments were kept — this transparency made the group lasting and bank-worthy. This is the practical power of women's cooperation.

## The link with accounting

Whether a women's society or an SHG — trust is built on clean accounts: records of member-wise savings, loans, interest and a correct reserve fund. Transparent books are what keep the group going for long. Especially when many women save together, each one should be confident that her money is recorded correctly and she will get it at the right time — and this confidence comes only from transparent, accurate accounts. An easy (Hindi) digital tool makes this work much simpler, so that accounting does not become an obstacle in running the society. (See: [Member and share accounting](/blog/member-and-share-accounting).)

## Why women's cooperation is growing

In recent years the focus on women's cooperative societies and SHGs has grown — and there are solid reasons for it. First, experience has shown that women are often very disciplined in saving and loan repayment, which makes such societies financially strong. Second, a woman's income has a positive effect on the whole family (education, nutrition). Third, policy attention on increasing women's participation in the cooperative sector has also grown — in many places women-centred societies (such as women's CM-PACS in Haryana) are being encouraged. All of this together is making women's cooperation a growing, effective stream. (See: [Haryana women's CM-PACS](/blog/women-cm-pacs-haryana-45-registered-guide).)

## Leadership and confidence — an unseen benefit

A big but often unseen benefit of women's cooperation is leadership development. When women become the chairperson, secretary or treasurer of a society, run meetings, handle accounts, and take collective decisions — they gain management skills and confidence that perhaps they never got before. This confidence also strengthens their voice at home and in society. In this way the society becomes a school not only of money but also of leadership — and this is the real foundation of long-term empowerment.

## Common mistakes

- **Keeping accounts verbally/from memory** → accurate member-wise records are a must; trust rests on this.
- **Irregular meetings** → regular meetings and transparent decisions keep the group strong.
- **Control in the hands of only a few** → collective decisions and rotating responsibility are better.
- **Ignoring training** → give simple accounting training, so everyone understands.
- **Not building a reserve fund** → keep a reserve for bad times.

## In the end
Women's cooperative societies are not just economic units — they are stories of empowerment. When women organise and handle their savings and income transparently, the impact reaches their homes, families and the whole society. The core mantra of these societies' success is the same as for all cooperation — working together, clean accounts, and collective trust. With the right training and easy tools, every women's group can become a strong, self-reliant society.

In short — women's cooperation has three conditions that make it successful: **organisation** (women joining together), **transparency** (clean accounts of every rupee), and **regularity** (regular saving and meetings). Where all three are present, a women's group survives and grows even in hard conditions. And with today's simple Hindi digital tools, accounting — once the biggest obstacle — can now become the easiest part.
