In Haryana, many PACS are no longer only agricultural credit institutions. Under the central government's "PACS as CSC" (Common Service Centre) scheme, many PACS have been authorised to offer additional digital services. This guide explains the scheme, its legal and administrative background, the services that can be offered, the income model, the requirements, the challenges and the accounting side — all in one place, with sources.

> 📌 Opening note: this scheme keeps moving forward at the central and state level, so the figures (how many PACS are registered/active) change over time. The figures below are based on the official documents in the [Sources](#sources) section; for the latest position that applies to your PACS, confirm with the ARCS/DRCS office concerned and the CSC portal.

## Why are PACS being made CSCs?

Under the central government's scheme, PACS are being registered as Common Service Centres (CSC) to widen the reach of digital services in rural areas. Since PACS are already present in village after village and have the trust of farmers, they were seen as a natural centre for delivering digital services.

There are three main aims behind this:

- **Viability of PACS:** many PACS barely survive on agricultural credit alone; CSC services give them an extra source of income.
- **e-Services at village level:** villagers get services like Aadhaar, bills, insurance and banking in their own village — no need to go to town.
- **Employment:** every CSC-active PACS becomes a centre of local work and employment.

## The national scheme — who signed the agreement, how big is the scope

This initiative rests on a formal agreement. A Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) was signed between **the Ministry of Cooperation, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), NABARD and CSC e-Governance Services India Ltd. (CSC-SPV)**, under which PACS are being added as CSCs. Under this arrangement PACS can offer **more than 300 e-services** — banking, insurance, agriculture, health and other government services.

At the national level this scope is large — according to official information, **by 21 November 2024 about 40,214 PACS** across the country had started offering CSC services. There is also a plan to set up **2 lakh new multipurpose PACS** in uncovered villages/panchayats over the coming years, which will later be able to offer similar services. In other words, "PACS as CSC" is not a small, local initiative but part of the rural digital framework of the whole country.

## The position in Haryana — the gap between registration and activity

A December 2024 SCDC meeting was told that 433 PACS in Haryana had been registered as Common Service Centres, but only 202 of them were actually active (functional) at that time — that is, there was a big gap between registration and real activity.

> ⚠️ Registration and activity are different things. A PACS being registered as a CSC does not guarantee that it is actually delivering services — for the real picture, always look at the "active/functional" figure, not just the "registered" figure. For the latest number, confirm with the ARCS office concerned.

This gap reflects a common challenge across the country — registration is an administrative step, but activity needs training, equipment and continuous operation. So whenever you read "how many PACS became CSCs" in any report, check whether it is the "registered" number or the "active" number — there is often a big difference between the two.

## What services can a PACS-CSC offer?

Services generally available under the national CSC scheme include:

- **Aadhaar services** — enrolment and updates.
- **Utility bill payment** — electricity, water etc.
- **Banking correspondent services** — small deposits/withdrawals, account opening.
- **Insurance services** — crop, life and general insurance.
- **PAN card application**.
- **Online applications for government schemes** — pension, scholarship, certificates etc.
- **Health and other e-services** (as per the scheme).

Since the full arrangement covers more than 300 services, each PACS can choose services according to its needs and local demand. For a PACS, this has the potential to become a source of extra income besides its existing agricultural credit business.

## How income is earned — the commission model

A PACS generally earns from CSC services in the form of **commission/service charges** — that is, a fixed fee or commission on each transaction (bill payment, Aadhaar, insurance policy, banking). It is not a big one-time earning, but a regular income built from many small transactions — the more services and customers, the more income.

> 💡 Commission rates differ by service and service provider and change over time, so this article gives no fixed rate — check the current rates that apply to your PACS on the CSC portal/with the agency concerned.

The biggest advantage of this income is that it does not depend on the farming season — services like bill payment, Aadhaar and banking run all year, which gives the PACS a regular cash flow. This "all-season income" proves decisive for many small PACS, because it keeps bringing in some earnings even in periods when loan recovery or farm activity is slow. Over the long run, this diversified income makes a PACS more financially stable and self-reliant — which is essential for the lasting health of a cooperative institution.

## How to account for CSC income

This extra income must show correctly in the society's books, so that reports and audit stay clean:

- **Separate income account:** keep a separate income head named "CSC Service Income" (or "Commission Income — CSC"), so that it does not mix with the interest income from agricultural loans and shows category-wise.
- **Entry for every transaction/settlement:** when commission is received, pass a receipt-type entry (Cash/Bank Dr, CSC Service Income Cr).
- **Expenses separate:** CSC-related expenses (internet, equipment maintenance) go to a separate expense head.

This shows clearly at year-end how much net profit the society made from CSC activity — and makes it easier to decide which services are worth it. (Accounting basics: [basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics) and [voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide).)

## What a PACS needs to become a CSC

Registration alone is not enough — a basic set-up is needed to start services:

- **Trained operator (like a VLE):** training for the PACS secretary/staff in the new digital services.
- **Equipment:** computer/laptop, printer, biometric device.
- **Internet connectivity:** reliable broadband/mobile data.
- **Onboarding on the CSC portal:** authorised ID and the required permissions.
- **Digital foundation:** this is naturally linked with e-PACS computerisation.

If even one of these is weak — say there is no internet or the staff is not trained — the PACS will stay "registered" but cannot become "active". This is the real root of the registration-versus-activity gap.

## Challenges — what holds things back?

The registration-versus-activity gap shows that merely getting registered as a CSC is not enough — the real challenge is to train PACS secretaries and staff in the new services and to provide the necessary equipment (computer, internet, biometric device). Besides this, there are some other obstacles:

- **Lack of digital skills:** in many rural PACS the staff are not familiar with new technology.
- **Unstable connectivity:** internet is often weak in remote villages.
- **Workload:** handling new services along with the existing loan work adds pressure.
- **Start-up cost:** the initial expense of equipment and set-up.

This issue is naturally linked with [e-PACS computerisation](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained) — where the digital foundation is strong, CSC services run easily; where it is not, registration stays only on paper.

## PACS-CSC vs CM-PACS — don't confuse them

Note — "a PACS becoming a CSC" and "CM-PACS" are two different things. CSC is a **service extension** (any eligible PACS can become a CSC to offer extra digital services), while CM-PACS is a separate **society type/model** with its own structure, membership and rules. A CM-PACS can also become a CSC, but becoming a CSC does not by itself make a PACS a "CM-PACS". (See: [What is CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-kya-hai).)

## Practical steps for a PACS

If your PACS wants to start CSC services, the sequence broadly is:

1. A resolution passed in the managing committee deciding to start CSC services (recorded in the minutes).
2. Onboarding and the required permissions through the CSC portal/agency concerned.
3. Arranging equipment and internet; training the staff.
4. Starting with a few main services as per local demand (such as bill payment, Aadhaar).
5. Keeping a separate account of CSC income and expenses in the books.
6. Periodic review — which services are running and which are not.

Starting small (one or two popular services) and adding services with experience is the most practical path. This keeps the initial risk and cost low, and the staff gradually become comfortable with the new technology.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every PACS automatically become a CSC?** — No, separate registration is needed for this, and even after registration, training and equipment are needed to start services.
**What does the society gain from PACS-CSC?** — An extra source of income besides agricultural credit — such as the commission earned on bill payment, insurance and Aadhaar services.
**What is the difference between a registered and an active PACS-CSC?** — Registered only means authorised; active means the PACS is actually delivering services to customers. The two numbers are not always the same.
**Which services can be offered?** — 300+ e-services, including Aadhaar, bill payment, banking correspondent, insurance, PAN and government-scheme applications.
**How is income earned?** — Mainly as per-transaction commission/service charges; the rates vary by service.
**Under whose agreement does this scheme run?** — Under the MoU between the Ministry of Cooperation, MeitY, NABARD and CSC-SPV.
**Where should CSC income be recorded in the books?** — In a separate "CSC Service Income" head, so that it shows separately and clearly from agricultural-loan income.
**Is becoming a CSC the same as CM-PACS?** — No; CSC is a service extension, CM-PACS is a separate society model.

## Three things to remember

- 🏪 Becoming a PACS-CSC means earning extra income from digital services like Aadhaar, bill payment and insurance, besides agricultural credit.
- 📶 Remember the registration-versus-activity gap — being registered alone is not enough; training and equipment are essential to start services.
- 🔗 CSC services are naturally linked with e-PACS computerisation — it is hard to run them without a digital foundation.

## Conclusion

The "PACS as CSC" scheme is a big opportunity for rural PACS — to move beyond agricultural credit, become a centre of digital services in the village itself, and earn extra, year-round income. But the opportunity bears fruit only when the PACS goes beyond registration and becomes truly active — trained staff, the necessary equipment, reliable internet, and a clean account of CSC income. A PACS that pays attention to these basics strengthens its own viability through this scheme and serves the village too. Start small, keep a separate account of income and expenses, and add services as you gain experience.
