When a person or institution wants to become a member of a registered cooperative society in Haryana — such as a PACS, CM-PACS or any other cooperative society — they have to give a written application to the society. This whole process is governed by the Haryana Co-operative Societies Act, 1984 and the Haryana Co-operative Societies Rules, 1989 made under it — but many members and societies do not know what this application should actually contain, and which provisions (Section 16, Rules 14-22) must be followed. This guide explains exactly that — the parts of the application, eligibility, time limits, nomination and register entry — in simple language.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** The sections have been matched with the government text of the Act (amended up to October 2007) and the 2013 amendment, and Rules 14-22 with the Indian Kanoon copy of the Rules 1989 (see "Sources" below). This is general information, not legal advice — in your case, confirm with the Registrar's office or your society's registered bye-laws.

> ⚠️ We did not find any fixed form for a membership application in the Rules 1989 — its forms relate to registration, attachment-sale and elections. Section 9A(2) of the Act says that the eligibility for membership and the "procedure for getting membership" can be set by the society's bye-laws. The Rules set the conditions on disqualification, time limits and records related to membership (Rules 14-22). So in practice every society uses its own application format as per its registered bye-laws or departmental instructions. If your society has its own prescribed format, use that — the information here is only to understand what a correct application should include.

## Why membership matters

Becoming a member is not just filling a form — it is becoming a co-owner and stakeholder of the society. A member gets access to the society's services (loans, inputs, storage etc.), a share in profit, the right to vote and a voice in the general body. That is why the membership application must be filled correctly, completely and as per the rules — because this is the document from which all these rights begin. An incomplete or rule-breaking application can later create confusion in membership, rights or audit.

## Who can become a member — Section 16 and the bye-laws

Section 16 of the Haryana Co-operative Societies Act, 1984 says that only these can become members: (a) a person who is competent to contract under section 11 of the Indian Contract Act (that is, adult and of sound mind); (b) another cooperative society; (c) the government; and (d) such class of persons, association, company or firm as the society's bye-laws allow. The rights and liabilities of members are in Chapter III of the Act (Sections 16-24), and the disqualifications, time limits and records of membership are in Chapter III of the Rules (Rules 14-22).

Whether a person lives in the society's area of operation or not, or belongs to a particular class or profession or not — on such questions related to eligibility for membership, **the Registrar's decision is final** (Section 8A).

According to Rule 14, a person cannot become a member (1) who has applied to be declared insolvent or who is an undischarged insolvent, or (2) who has been sentenced for an offence and 5 years have not passed since the end of the sentence (political offences are not counted in this). If a member later falls under any of these disqualifications, their membership is treated as ended from that date. According to Rule 15, a person who is a member of a primary society that gives loans to members cannot become a member of another such society without the Registrar's permission; and an officer of a society cannot become a member of another society with similar objects without the Registrar's permission. The committee should check these points before accepting the application.

> 💡 Even after becoming a member, a person cannot use a member's rights until the payment set in the bye-laws (such as share money) has been made (Section 19).

## What a correct application should include

A practical membership application is usually divided into these parts:

| Part | What is recorded |
| --- | --- |
| Society details | Name, registration number, registration date, registered office |
| Applicant details | Name, parent's/spouse's name, date of birth and age, full address, contact, occupation, basis of eligibility, type of applicant (individual/society/institution/firm) |
| Membership sought and payment | Membership class, number and amount of shares, admission fee, total amount, receipt number and date |
| Declaration | Confirmation of eligibility under Section 16, being free of Rule 14 disqualifications, Rule 15 (if applicable), compliance with bye-laws |
| Nomination | Nominee's name, relationship, address, allocation of share/interest, guardian details if the nominee is a minor |
| Attachments | Photo, identity/age proof, address proof, admission fee receipt, Authorization/Resolution for non-individual applicants |
| For office use | Receipt details, eligibility scrutiny, Rule 16 AGM-timing check, committee meeting date, resolution number, decision |

> 💡 Share value, the minimum number of shares and the amount of admission fee — all these depend on each society's registered bye-laws or applicable instructions. Never fill these by copying another society's figures — take the correct figures from your own society's bye-laws.

## Nomination is mandatory — Rule 21

Rule 21(1) clearly says that **every member shall name a nominee** — that is, the person or persons to whom the member's share or interest (Section 23) will be transferred or paid as per the bye-laws on the member's death. This is not an optional facility. Other main points of Rule 21:

- The member can change or cancel the nomination from time to time.
- The number of nominees cannot be more than the number of the member's shares.
- If there is more than one nominee, write each nominee's portion as whole shares as far as possible.
- The value paid to the nominee is based on the amount the member actually paid for the shares.
- The record of nominations is kept in the manner set in the bye-laws.

> 💡 Nomination is often put off as "a later matter", but the rule makes it mandatory, and it is important for the member's family too. If there is no nominee, the committee has to find the heir or legal representative under Section 23, which brings delay and confusion. So always get the nominee filled in at the time of the application.

## By when should the committee decide on the application — Rule 17

According to Rule 17, societies other than producer societies have to dispose of a membership application as soon as possible and within one month at most. If the application is refused, the decision with reasons should be communicated to the applicant — this is an important procedural safeguard against delay and arbitrary refusal.

> ⚠️ **The 60-day rule in societies like PACS:** The proviso to Section 16 says that in a co-operative credit and service society, a farmers service society or a primary agricultural development bank, a person who is eligible for membership **shall be deemed a member automatically 60 days after** the application reaches the society's office. If such a "deemed" member is actually ineligible, the Registrar (on their own or on a complaint) can order their removal, recording reasons; on a complaint this order has to be given within 30 days. So in such societies, keeping an application pending is even more risky.

**Appeal if refused:** Except in a producer society, if the society refuses to admit a person who is eligible as per the bye-laws, an appeal can be filed against that decision under Section 114(1)(c) — within **60 days** from the date of the decision.

> ⚠️ **Admission restriction 14 days before the AGM — Rule 16:** No member should be admitted during the 14 days before the date of the society's annual general meeting (AGM). The aim is that voting or other proceedings cannot be influenced by hurriedly adding new members just before the AGM.

## Entry in the Member Register — Rule 22

According to Rule 22, every society has to keep a Member Register, in which each member's name, address, occupation and details of their shares, the date the name was entered in the register, the date membership ended, and the member's nominees are recorded. The rule does not set any fixed time limit for the entry, but making the entry immediately after approval is a good habit — so that the whole process can be traced in one record. Since 2013, Section 120(1) also says that the society shall keep the Act, the Rules, its bye-laws and the **list of members** open for free inspection at its registered address.

## The whole process — step by step

Seen in sequence, the rules above make the membership process run like this:

1. **Application:** The interested person/institution gives a written application in the format of the society's bye-laws (with payment receipt and attachments).
2. **Eligibility check:** The committee checks Section 16 (eligibility) and Rule 14/15 (disqualification).
3. **Timing check:** Under Rule 16, it checks that the AGM is not within 14 days.
4. **Decision:** Decision within one month at most under Rule 17; if refused, communication with reasons (in societies like PACS, a "deemed" member after 60 days — Section 16).
5. **Register entry:** On approval, entry in the Member Register under Rule 22, including the nominee (Rule 21).
6. **Proof:** Give the member a share certificate/membership certificate and the payment receipt, and keep your own copy.

Following this sequence keeps every membership proper, traceable and audit-ready — and the member is clear about their rights and documents from the start, which avoids later disputes.

## Non-individual applicants — society/institution/firm

Members are not only individuals — under Section 16, another cooperative society, the government, or an association, company or firm named in the bye-laws can also become members. (A statutory body notified by the government or a self-help group can become an "associate member", which has no share in profit or property and no right to vote — Sections 18 and 20.) Individuals cannot become members of a central or apex society unless the Registrar has given an exemption (Section 17). When the applicant is an institution/society/firm, some extra things are necessary: the name of its authorised representative, and an **Authorization/Resolution** of that institution proving that it has properly decided to become a member of this society and appoint a representative. Without this, a non-individual application is treated as incomplete. So in such an application, always take this extra document compared to an individual applicant. (Correct format of a resolution: [Board Resolution guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)

## Common mistakes that become objections in audit/inspection

> 📘 Admitting a member within 14 days of the AGM date (violation of Rule 16); keeping an application pending for more than one month without reason (violation of Rule 17); not communicating the reasons for refusal to the applicant; making someone a member without getting a nominee filled in, or not recording a clear split of portions when there is more than one nominee (Rule 21); not updating the Member Register entry on time; and filling share value/admission fee from another society's figures without looking at your own bye-laws — all these common lapses can later become serious audit objections.

## Digital member records — why useful

When a society has hundreds of members, handling the Member Register, shares, nominees and admission/cessation dates by hand is hard and error-prone. A good digital system keeps this whole record organised, searchable and up to date — which makes compliance with Rule 22 easy, keeps audit smooth, and gives any member's information instantly. It also keeps member-wise accounts of share money and fees transparent. (See: [Member and share accounting](/blog/member-and-share-accounting).)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there one official prescribed membership form in Haryana?** — No. Every society uses its own application format as per its registered bye-laws or departmental instructions; the Act and Rules only set the conditions on eligibility, disqualification and procedure.
**Within how much time should a membership application be decided?** — According to Rule 17, except for a producer society, as soon as possible and within one month at most; the reasons for refusal must be communicated to the applicant. In a credit and service society (like PACS), an eligible applicant is deemed a member automatically after 60 days (Section 16).
**Can a new member be admitted just before the AGM?** — No. According to Rule 16, no member should be admitted during the 14 days before the AGM date.
**Is it mandatory to give a nominee?** — Yes. According to Rule 21(1), every member shall name a nominee; the number of nominees cannot be more than the number of shares, and the record is kept as per the bye-laws.
**Which record is updated after someone becomes a member?** — According to Rule 22, the Member Register with the member's name, address, occupation, shares, the date of entry of name and the date membership ended, and nominees.
**How much are share money and admission fee?** — It depends on each society's bye-laws; do not fill in another society's figures — take them from your own bye-laws (details: [CM-PACS share amount](/blog/cm-pacs-share-amount-haryana-membership-cost)).
**What if the application is refused?** — Under Rule 17, the decision with reasons must be communicated to the applicant; and except for a producer society, an appeal against a decision refusing an eligible person can be filed under Section 114 within 60 days.
**Can an institution/society also become a member?** — Yes, another cooperative society, the government, or an association/company/firm named in the bye-laws (Section 16); take the authorised representative and the Authorization/Resolution with it. A member society appoints its representative by a resolution of its committee (Section 21, Rule 20).
**What are the main grounds of disqualification?** — Insolvency, and 5 years not having passed since the end of a sentence (Rule 14); dual membership in lending societies is not allowed without the Registrar's permission (Rule 15).
**What rights does membership give?** — Access to services, share in profit, voting and a voice in the general body — that is, co-ownership.
**What is the risk of not filling in a nomination?** — Legal complications in transferring shares/interest on the member's death; so get it filled in at the time of the application itself.

## Three things to remember

- 📋 There is no single state-wide "official" membership form — each society's own bye-laws or departmental instructions decide its application format.
- ⏰ Rule 17 (decision in one month) and Rule 16 (bar on admission 14 days before the AGM) — ignoring either becomes the most common objection in audit.
- 📝 After membership is approved, immediate entry in the Member Register under Rule 22 — this is the last and most important link in the process.
- 👪 Nomination (Rule 21) is mandatory by rule — get it filled in at the time of the application.

## In the end

The membership application is the first, basic and most important link in the relationship between the society and the member — so it must be filled correctly, completely and as per the rules. Remember the main points: there is no fixed form in the Rules (use your bye-laws' format); check eligibility (Section 16) and disqualification (Rule 14/15); follow the time limits (one month under Rule 17, 60 days under Section 16 in societies like PACS, the AGM-14-day rule of Rule 16); always get the nomination (Rule 21) filled in; and on approval make the entry in the Member Register (Rule 22). A society that follows all this keeps its membership process transparent, dispute-free and audit-ready — and the member gets all their rights without confusion.
