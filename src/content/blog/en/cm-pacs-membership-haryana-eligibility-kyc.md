Before becoming a member of a CM-PACS, the very first question that comes up is — who is eligible, and which documents (KYC) will be needed? In the series on the [CM-PACS governance model](/blog/cm-pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-guide), this guide explains membership eligibility and how it links to Haryana's digital identity system — and also what the practical path from application to membership looks like.

Conditions like "farmers with up to 5 acres" have been removed because no government source was found for them; the membership rules have been written from the Haryana Act (Sections 16, 18, 19) and Rules 17, 18(3).

## Legal eligibility — Section 16

According to **Section 16** of the Act, the following can become members: a person competent to contract under Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act 1872, another cooperative society, the government, and other classes written in the bye-laws. Any special condition for a CM-PACS — such as a land limit or being a farmer/non-farmer — will be in its bye-laws. The previous version said "farmers with up to 5 acres of land and non-agricultural workers"; we did not find this in any government source (the news report that mentions 5 acres is about FPOs there), so it was removed.

The general meaning of "competent to contract" is — the person should be an adult (usually 18 years+), of sound mind, and not declared disqualified under any law. Besides this, the society's own rules (bye-laws) can add some more conditions — such as residence in the area of operation (village/area), or a real connection with the activity for which the society was formed. On a question such as whether a person lives in the area of operation or falls in a class, under **Section 8A** the Registrar's decision is final. So Section 16 opens the minimum legal door, and the finer remaining conditions are seen in the society's bye-laws.

## Eligibility vs priority — understand the difference

There is a fine but important difference here. **Legal eligibility** (Section 16 + bye-laws) decides *who can apply*. Whereas a scheme's **priority/target group** (as printed in newspapers) tells *whom this initiative focuses on*. The two are not the same thing. So for his exact eligibility, an applicant should rely only on the society's approved bye-laws and the information from the ARCS office, not just on newspaper descriptions.

## Haryana's digital identity system — Parivar Pehchan Patra (PPP)

In Haryana, identification for government schemes and services is now linked to a large extent with the Parivar Pehchan Patra (PPP). RCS Haryana's own official live dashboard publishes a separate figure named "Total Registered Society with Family ID (Parivar Pehchan Patra)", which shows PPP-based identity verification as an established system at the level of cooperative societies too.

In simple language, PPP is Haryana's family-based identity document, which holds the verified details of a family and its members. In many state schemes, the identity and eligibility of a beneficiary are checked through it, which stops duplication and fake benefits. In the cooperative context, this means that when a member's identity is kept linked to PPP, the society's records become more reliable and verifiable.

## Farmer identity — Farmer Registry (AgriStack)

In Haryana a separate digital identity system is also running for farmers — the Farmer Registry, which is part of the national AgriStack initiative. According to a PTI report of December 2025 (on The Week, 17 December 2025), 1.38 crore farmers in Haryana were to be registered in the Farmer Registry, creating a "Farm ID" through Aadhaar OTP; it began with Ambala, Panchkula and Faridabad. We have not seen the government details of which records (PPP, land, bank) the Farmer ID links to — ask your agriculture office or CSC.

The aim of AgriStack is to build a shared, farmer-centred digital base, so that scheme benefits, loans, insurance and advice reach the right farmer directly — without submitting the same documents again and again. The Farmer ID is its identity thread. For farmer-members this may become convenient later, because an identity linked once works across many services.

For the Farmer Registry, it is generally advised to keep these things ready (we have not seen a government list — confirm with the CSC/agriculture office):

- Aadhaar, with a mobile number linked to it (for OTP)
- Parivar Pehchan Patra (PPP), if asked for
- Land details including village/khasra number
- Bank passbook
- Lease/patta documents for tenant farmers

## Three identities together — Aadhaar, PPP and Farmer ID

Applicants often get confused about why these three identities are separate and what each one does. A simple comparison:

| Identity | Whose | Main work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aadhaar | Every person (national) | A person's basic identity and e-KYC/OTP verification |
| PPP (Parivar Pehchan Patra) | Family (Haryana) | Family-based identity and eligibility in state schemes |
| Farmer ID (Farmer Registry) | Farmer (AgriStack) | The farmer's farming-centred identity — made through Aadhaar OTP |

The three do not replace one another; they connect to one another — Aadhaar represents the person, PPP the family, and the Farmer ID the farmer's farming identity. When they are linked correctly with each other, verification becomes fast and reliable, and the member does not have to submit the same documents again and again.

## Who benefits the most

The biggest benefit of the inclusive conditions of CM-PACS membership reaches those who were outside the cooperative fold until now:

- **Small and marginal farmers:** who often get little attention in big institutions — by becoming members they too can be part of input, storage and sales services (if the bye-laws give them membership).
- **Landless villagers:** if the society's bye-laws give membership to non-farmers, they too can join activities like pumps, godowns and processing.
- **Newly joining villages:** for the residents of places where there was no active cooperative institution until now, an organised platform for the first time.

Who can become a member is finally decided by the society's registered bye-laws — provided the membership process and records are kept clean and tidy.

## An example — Ramprasad's membership

Suppose Ramprasad, a farmer with 3 acres, wants to join the new CM-PACS of his village. His path would be something like this: first he prepares his Aadhaar (mobile-linked), PPP, his jamabandi/khasra details and bank passbook; then he fills in the application in the society's form. The society checks his documents — the name matches in Aadhaar, PPP and the land record, and the OTP comes through Aadhaar. The committee accepts the application within one month (Rule 17), Ramprasad deposits the share amount written in the bye-laws, and his name is entered in the member register. After the payment is complete (Section 19), he can now vote in the general body and is entitled to the society's services. If the spelling of his name had been different in any of his documents, the verification could have got stuck — that is why preparation matters.

## Is this compulsory for CM-PACS membership?

> 💡 It is necessary to be clear here — we did not find any official document saying that the Farmer ID is a compulsory condition for CM-PACS membership. But since PPP and Aadhaar-based verification has become a common pattern in almost all agriculture and cooperative schemes of the Haryana government, applicants are practically advised to keep this document ready.

Understand it like this — even if the Farmer ID has not been stated to be legally compulsory, there is no harm in keeping the documents ready in advance. This reduces running around at the time of application, and if any scheme/facility links to them later, you are ready. That is, "not compulsory, but wise to keep ready" — this is the practical approach.

## From application to membership — step by step

In practice, the path to membership usually looks like these steps (the exact process depends on the society's bye-laws and the local office):

1. **Application:** filling in the membership application in the prescribed form, giving details of your activity/land/residence.
2. **Documents:** attaching Aadhaar, PPP, bank details, photo — and, for farmer-members, land details (and a lease agreement if on lease).
3. **Checking:** verification of eligibility and documents by the society.
4. **Approval:** decision on the application by the managing committee — under Rule 17 (except for a producers' society) within one month of receiving the application; if it refuses, with reasons.
5. **Share and fee:** depositing the prescribed share amount (and admission fee, where applicable).
6. **Register entry:** the name is entered in the member register. Under Section 19, until the payment written in the bye-laws is complete, the member cannot use his rights (voting, etc.).

## Types of membership — a general understanding

In cooperative law, membership can usually be in a few categories (the exact categories and rights depend on the society's bye-laws):

- **Regular/ordinary member:** a member with full rights — voting, contesting elections, a share in dividend and services.
- **Associate member (Section 18):** as per the bye-laws, a person, self-help group or society can become an associate member; it gets no share in the society's property or profits (Section 18(2)) and has no right to vote (Section 20).

While applying, it is useful to know which category you are joining in, because this decides your rights (especially voting and dividend). Confirm the categories available in a CM-PACS and their rights from its approved bye-laws.

## Nomination (Nominee) — a small step, a big relief

An often-missed but important step while becoming a member is — **nomination**. A member can register a nominee for his share amount and the interest held in the society, so that if the member dies, that amount/interest reaches the right person without a long legal tangle. This small entry saves the family from big trouble later. While filling in the membership form, do register the nominee's name and relationship, and keep it updated when circumstances change (marriage/death, etc.). (The exact nomination process depends on the society's bye-laws and the state rules.)

## Why KYC is necessary — from the society's point of view

A member's correct identification (KYC) is not just a formality — it is a condition of the society's health. Without proper KYC, these risks increase:

- **Double/fake membership:** so that one person cannot take benefits under many names, or one family through many fake memberships.
- **Wrong payment:** so that dividend, loans or benefits do not reach the wrong person.
- **Audit objection:** incomplete member records become the root of audit objections and a drop in grading.

So Aadhaar/PPP-based verification is beneficial for the society too — records stay clean, verifiable and reliable. That is why keeping identity documents ready in advance is in the interest of both sides (the member and the society).

## After becoming a member — rights and duties

Membership is not just a facility, it is also a responsibility. In general cooperative practice, a member has **rights** — taking part and voting in the general body (one member–one vote), information about the society's accounts/reports, a vote in board elections, and a share in services and dividend as per rules. Along with these come **duties** — attending meetings on time, doing honest business with the society, repaying dues on time, and respecting decisions taken in the society's interest. Only an active, informed member makes a strong society. (See: [Principles and values of cooperation](/blog/cooperative-principles-and-values).)

## For the society — keeping member records

As members join a CM-PACS, the most basic work for the society is — a clean record of every member. This includes the **member register** (name, identity, date of admission), every member's **share account** (how many shares, how much amount, refund status), and his other transactions (deposit/loan, where applicable). When all this is kept digitally and member-wise, the full position of any member is available in one click on any day — which makes the AGM, the audit and communication with members, all three, easier. Good cooperative software gives this transparency automatically by keeping the member register, share account and KYC details in one place. (See: [Basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics).)

## Application rejected or membership ended — what then

It is not necessary that every application is accepted. If eligibility is not met or documents are incomplete, the board can reject the application — in such a case the way to apply again after removing the shortfall usually stays open. Similarly, an existing membership can also end as per rules — such as when the member resigns himself, a rule is violated, or eligibility ends. The exact process in both situations, the right of appeal, and the conditions for refund of the share amount are laid down in the society's bye-laws and the state's cooperative Act/Rules. Pay special attention to share refund: under **Rule 18(3)**, in a society with limited liability a member is usually not allowed to withdraw shares — refund only when the society has created a share transfer fund out of profits, and even then the total refund at any time is not more than 5% of the paid-up share capital (excluding the government's share) as on the previous 31 March.

## Common precautions

- **Aadhaar-mobile link:** if the mobile number is not linked to Aadhaar, OTP-based verification can get stuck — check in advance.
- **PPP updated:** if there is a change in the family (birth/marriage/death/address), keep PPP updated on time, otherwise the details will not match.
- **Name match:** the spelling of the name should be the same in Aadhaar, PPP, the land record and the bank account — even a small difference can stop verification.
- **Tenant farmers:** if you do not own land, keep lease/patta documents ready.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which documents are needed for CM-PACS membership?** — In practice it is advised to keep Aadhaar, PPP (Parivar Pehchan Patra) and land-related documents ready, although no single law clearly states this as compulsory.
**What is a Farmer ID?** — A farmer's unique identity in AgriStack's Farmer Registry; in Haryana it is being made through Aadhaar OTP (PTI, December 2025).
**Is CM-PACS membership only for farmers?** — The Act (Section 16) puts no such bar; who can become a member is decided by the society's bye-laws — confirm with the ARCS.
**What does Section 16 say?** — According to Section 16 of the Act, any person competent to contract can apply for membership; additional conditions may be in the society's bye-laws.
**Is the Farmer ID compulsory?** — We did not find any official document stating it to be compulsory for CM-PACS membership; but given the common pattern of Aadhaar/PPP-based verification, it is wise to keep it ready.
**In how many days is a membership application decided?** — Rule 17: within one month (except for a producers' society); if it refuses, with reasons. (The proviso of Section 16 on "automatically a member in 60 days" applies only to credit and service societies, farmers' service societies and primary agricultural development banks.)
**When does membership take effect?** — Usually after the committee's approval and deposit of the share amount, when the name is entered in the member register; the rights come once the payment is complete (Section 19).

## Three things to remember

- 🪪 Legal eligibility for CM-PACS membership is decided by Section 16 — but in practice it may be necessary to keep digital identity documents such as Aadhaar and PPP ready.
- 🌾 The Farmer ID is part of the national AgriStack initiative — we found no order making it compulsory for CM-PACS membership, but it is wise to keep it ready.
- 📌 Who can become a member (land limit, non-farmers) is decided by the society's bye-laws; no government source was found for claims like "5 acres".

## In the end

The door to CM-PACS membership opens through Section 16, but the real ease lies in preparation — keep Aadhaar (mobile-linked), PPP, land details and the bank account correct in advance, keep the spelling of your name the same everywhere, and confirm your exact eligibility from the society's bye-laws and the ARCS office. For the member this is a matter of a few documents; for the society it is the foundation of clean, verifiable records — on which reliable dividend, loans and audit rest. And remember, scheme details like the Farmer Registry are based on news reports — the final word is always the official source's.
