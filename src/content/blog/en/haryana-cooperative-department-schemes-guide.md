The Haryana Cooperation Department's website has a "Schemes" page where the department lists dozens of its schemes — here an interest rebate for farmers, there a subsidy for women's cooperative societies, here interest support for Scheduled Caste members, and there government shareholding to increase the capital of banks and federations. For any cooperative member, farmer, women's group or institution, this list is a kind of map of what the government has brought for them — but the most important way to understand it is to sort the schemes by their type.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** The details of every scheme have been matched against the Haryana Cooperation Department's "Schemes" page (titled "2025-26") — see "Sources" below. That page has only a short description of each scheme; most do not state eligibility or the application process. So confirm the current conditions for your case with your PACS, the District Central Cooperative Bank or the Assistant Registrar.

## Four groups — at a glance

Before going further, see the full picture in one table, so that each scheme can be placed in its correct group:

| Group | For whom | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Directly to members/farmers | Ordinary members, farmers, women's groups, SC members | Interest rebate, milk subsidy, women's subsidy |
| 2. Capital of institutions | Banks/federations (indirect benefit) | "Share Capital to…" schemes |
| 3. Computerisation/infrastructure | PACS/banks/RCS office | Computerisation (60:40), dairy infrastructure |
| 4. Sugar mills/NCDC | Sugar mills, some societies | Cane-payment loans, ethanol; NCDC-financed |

This classification makes it easy to decide which scheme's benefit will come directly into your account, and which will indirectly strengthen your society/bank.

## Group one — schemes that reach the member or farmer directly

**Interest rebate ("Interest Subvention Scheme-2014"):** According to the department's page —
- Borrower members of HSCARDB who repay instalments on time (good pay masters) get a **50% rebate** on interest.
- Members who take crop loans from PACS within the set time get a **4% interest rebate** from the state, and a **3% interest subvention** from the Central Government.
- PACS good pay masters get an **interest-free loan**.

The condition is — repaying the loan on time. How the rebate is given (automatically in the account, or on application) is not written on the page; ask your PACS.

**Deposit guarantee scheme for PACS depositors:** Under the "Deposit Guarantee Scheme for Primary Agriculture Cooperative Societies", deposits in PACS have insurance cover of **up to ₹50,000 per member**; the state government helps with its guarantee fee.

> 💡 Knowing this limit is especially useful — any member can decide how much to keep deposited in a single society only by keeping this limit in mind. Questions have been raised again and again about the financial health of PACS, so this cover is a practical safety net, even though it does not cover the entire deposit.

**Mukhyamantri Dugdh Utpadak Protsahan Yojana (Chief Minister's milk producer incentive scheme):** A subsidy of **₹5 per litre** on both cow and buffalo milk. Also, as announced by the Union Minister of Cooperation at Karnal on 02.11.2023, **Antyodaya families** get an extra **₹10 per litre** for one year for supplying milk to milk unions.

**Subsidy for women's cooperative societies:** For improving the quality of vegetables and fruit and for other activities of women's cooperative societies, central cooperative banks give loans of up to **₹5 lakh**, of which **20% or a maximum of ₹1 lakh** is given to each society by the state government as a subsidy. There is also a similar scheme for vegetable and fruit grower cooperative societies.

**Interest subsidy for Scheduled Caste members:** Scheduled Caste members of PACS, DPCARDBs, central cooperative banks and labour and construction societies who repay loans on time get a **7%** subsidy on interest. There is a separate scheme of government loans through Housefed for SC, backward class and weaker section members of urban housing cooperative societies.

**Assistance for cooperative labour and construction societies:** Societies that are **more than three years** old and have done work of **₹10 lakh or more** can get **₹80,000 share capital, an ₹80,000 working capital loan and a ₹40,000 managerial subsidy** — on the recommendation of the Assistant Registrar concerned. This is one of the few schemes where the department has clearly stated the eligibility and the route.

## Group two — schemes that reach institutions, not individuals directly

Many schemes in the department's list begin with the title "Share Capital to…" — that is, the government itself puts in capital by becoming a shareholder in that institution. These include HARCOFED, urban cooperative banks, central cooperative banks, HARCO Bank, the District Primary Cooperative Agriculture and Rural Development Banks (DPCARDBs), HSCARDB, the Housing Federation, the Cooperative Sugar Mills Federation and the Labour and Construction Federation.

> 📘 Their purpose is to keep the capital base of these institutions strong — for example, to maintain the CRAR (capital adequacy) level set by RBI for central cooperative banks, or to maintain the state's share in a federation. The share capital the state puts into HARCO Bank, HSCARDB and DPCARDBs is later returned to the state by NABARD as a loan. Seen from the ordinary member's or farmer's point of view, the benefit of these schemes does not come directly into their account; instead it indirectly helps the bank or society they belong to survive.

## Group three — computerisation and infrastructure

There are separate schemes for computerising PACS, HSCARDB, DPCARDBs and the office of the Registrar Cooperative Societies (RCS), in which the cost is shared **60% by the Centre and 40% by the state**. Under PACS computerisation, money is given for hardware, digitisation and support for the state's **801 PACS/PCCS**. The aim is to increase transparency, speed up loan disbursement, reduce transaction costs and increase members' trust. Besides this, in the dairy sector there are half a dozen schemes that strengthen milk chilling centres, bulk milk coolers, tetra pack plants and the processing infrastructure of milk unions.

## Group four — sugar mills and NCDC-financed schemes

There are two important schemes for cooperative sugar mills — one, loans to mills to clear sugarcane farmers' dues. According to the department's page, for the crushing season 2025-26 Haryana set the **State Advised Price (SAP) of sugarcane at ₹415 per quintal**, while the Centre's **FRP was ₹355 per quintal**. (Check the next season's rate in the department's latest notification.) Two, for plants that generate cogeneration power from bagasse and ethanol from molasses, so that sugar mills earn extra income. Besides this, schemes that increase the share capital of fruit and vegetable societies, marketing societies and PACS are marked "100% NCDC" on the department's page, meaning the entire money comes from NCDC (full details of NCDC schemes such as Nandini Sahakar and Yuva Sahakar are [here](/blog/ncdc-cooperative-credit-schemes-nandini-yuva-chini-mill)).

## How to take the benefit — a practical route

Since every scheme's eligibility and application route is different, a general practical method is this:

1. **Identify your category** — are you an ordinary member/farmer, a women's group, an SC member, or a society/institution? This decides which scheme applies to you.
2. **Choose the right door** — most member schemes come through the **PACS or District Central Cooperative Bank** concerned; some (such as for labour and construction societies) on the Assistant Registrar's recommendation.
3. **Repay on time:** for many interest-rebate schemes, the only condition is repaying the loan on time — so pay instalments on time.
4. **Keep documents ready** — proof of membership, loan account, category certificate (where needed).
5. **Confirm** — since the department's page does not state eligibility and the application process for most schemes, confirm the exact conditions directly with the bank/society concerned or the Assistant Registrar.

## Schemes on paper, questions on the ground

How much ground-level impact so many schemes on paper have is a fair question. Schemes such as interest rebates, share capital assistance and computerisation have run for decades, yet PACS earnings remain a challenge. We did not see a current government figure for how many PACS are in profit during this check — for that, see Haryana's budget documents or the Cooperation Department's latest report.

Another honest point is that the department's own Schemes page has only a short description of most schemes — eligibility and the application process are not written. For example, for the member education and leadership training scheme it only says that it is to help HARCOFED; where an ordinary member should go to take its benefit is not clear. If a page whose very purpose is to inform members leaves basic information like "how to apply" incomplete, that is a real gap in transparency.

## Why this information matters, and for whom

This whole list means different things to different people. A farmer who repays a crop loan on time should know about the interest-rebate scheme so that they can claim it from their PACS. A women's group that wants to form a cooperative society should keep an eye on the women's cooperative subsidy. Every member who deposits money in a PACS can decide how much to keep in one society only by knowing the limit of the deposit guarantee scheme. For Scheduled Caste members, the interest subsidy and housing loans directly reduce their financial burden. And for contractor-workers linked with labour and construction societies, the capital assistance scheme can be useful.

> 💡 For journalists, researchers and policymakers who follow the cooperative sector, this classification works as a benchmark — the next time a new announcement is made, one can check whether it is an extension of an existing scheme or really something new.

## Frequently asked questions

**Into which four groups can the Haryana Cooperation Department's schemes be divided?** — Schemes that reach members/farmers directly, "Share Capital" schemes that strengthen the capital of institutions, computerisation and infrastructure schemes, and sugar mill and NCDC-financed schemes.
**How much insurance cover is there on deposits in PACS?** — Up to ₹50,000 per member, under the Deposit Guarantee Scheme.
**How much rebate is there on PACS crop loans?** — Those who take crop loans within the set time get a 4% rebate from the state and a 3% subvention from the Centre; those who repay on time (good pay masters) get an interest-free loan.
**How much subsidy is there on milk?** — ₹5 per litre on both cow and buffalo milk; Antyodaya families get an extra ₹10 per litre for one year.
**Does the benefit of the "Share Capital to…" schemes go directly to the member?** — No; their purpose is to strengthen the capital of the bank or federation concerned, so that it survives and keeps its lending capacity — the benefit reaches the member indirectly.
**Is the application process clearly stated for all schemes?** — No; the department's page does not state eligibility and the application process for most schemes — for correct information, contact the bank/society concerned or the Assistant Registrar directly.
**What is the main condition of the interest-rebate scheme?** — Repaying the loan on time. Ask your PACS/bank whether the rebate is applied automatically or you have to apply.
**How is the cost shared in the computerisation scheme?** — Centre 60%, state 40%.
**What is the first practical step to get a benefit?** — Identify your category, then confirm the correct conditions and application route with the PACS/District Cooperative Bank concerned or the Assistant Registrar.

## Three things to remember

- 🗂️ Understand the Haryana Cooperation Department's schemes by dividing them into four groups — those that reach members directly, those that strengthen institutions' capital, computerisation/infrastructure, and sugar mill/NCDC-financed.
- 💰 The PACS deposit guarantee limit of ₹50,000 per member and the indirect benefit of the "Share Capital" schemes — both need to be understood so that expectations are right.
- 📋 Even on the department's own page, the eligibility/application process for many schemes is incomplete — for exact information, confirm directly with the bank, society or Assistant Registrar concerned.

## In the end

The Haryana Cooperation Department's schemes are a wide net — from the farmer's interest rebate to strengthening institutions' capital, computerisation and sugar mill assistance. But their full benefit goes only to those who do two things: first, sort the schemes into four groups and identify which reach them directly and which indirectly; and second, since many details are incomplete even on the official page, always confirm the exact conditions and application route directly with the bank/society concerned or the Assistant Registrar. The right understanding and the right door — this is the key to getting the real benefit of these schemes.
