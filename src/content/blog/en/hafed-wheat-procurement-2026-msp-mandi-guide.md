Every year in Haryana, during the wheat procurement season, HAFED stands out as a key link between farmers and the government. This guide explains wheat procurement — the MSP rates for RMS 2026-27, and the procurement system (mandis, the split between agencies, payment) from the figures of the last season whose government data we could open and read.

> ⚠️ **Figures checked up to 18 October 2026.** The MSP rates are for RMS 2026-27 (procurement in April-May 2026), from a Government of India (PIB) document. The procurement-system figures (mandis, agencies, total procurement) are for **RMS 2025-26 (April-May 2025)**, because we did not find the final government figures for 2026 in an open source. For the next season, see the announcements of HAFED and the Food, Civil Supplies and Consumer Affairs Department, Haryana.

## What exactly is HAFED

HAFED means **The Haryana State Cooperative Supply & Marketing Federation Limited** — the largest apex cooperative institution of the state (Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26, para 3.121). Its core work is government procurement, storage, processing and marketing of farmers' produce, along with the supply of inputs such as fertiliser and seed. Being a cooperative federation, its structure is linked to village-level societies — that is, it is the farmer's "own" institution. It holds the largest share of wheat procurement — in RMS 2025-26, HAFED bought about 47% of the state's total wheat (see below).

## MSP rate — the whole rabi basket, including wheat

The Union Cabinet approved the MSP for Rabi Marketing Season 2026-27 on 1 October 2025 (PIB, source 1). The MSP of wheat is **₹2,585 per quintal** (it was ₹2,425 in the last season, RMS 2025-26). The other rabi crops, per quintal:

| Crop | RMS 2026-27 MSP |
| --- | --- |
| Wheat | ₹2,585 |
| Barley | ₹2,150 |
| Gram | ₹5,875 |
| Lentil (masur) | ₹7,000 |
| Rapeseed/mustard | ₹6,200 |
| Safflower (kusum) | ₹6,540 |

Note: sunflower is not in the rabi MSP list; its MSP is announced with the kharif crops.

## What these figures mean

The government figures for RMS 2025-26 clearly show the scale — 418 mandis/procurement centres were opened and a total of 72.89 lakh metric tonnes of wheat was procured (Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26, para 3.107). Of this, 33.60 lakh metric tonnes, or about 47.28%, was bought by HAFED alone (para 3.122). Before the season there was news that a 40:30:20:10 split among agencies had been fixed (see below), but in actual procurement HAFED's share turned out even bigger. The meaning is clear — the cooperative sector is the backbone of the state's wheat procurement. These figures are season-specific; they change every year.

## When does procurement happen?

According to the Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26, wheat procurement in RMS 2025-26 ran from **1 April to 15 May 2025** (para 3.107). In 2026 too there was news that procurement would start from 1 April, but we did not open the government order for it. The state government announces the dates for every season.

## HAFED's share — the plan and the actual procurement

According to a news report on the RMS 2025-26 preparation meeting (The Tribune, 25 March 2025, source 4), the responsibility for wheat procurement was divided among four agencies like this (this was the **plan**; matching with the government order is pending):

| Agency | Share |
| --- | --- |
| HAFED | 40% |
| Food, Civil Supplies and Consumer Affairs Department | 30% |
| Haryana State Warehousing Corporation | 20% |
| Food Corporation of India (FCI) | 10% |

In actual procurement, HAFED bought 33.60 lakh metric tonnes of wheat, which was 47.28% of the state's total wheat procurement (Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26, para 3.122). That is, HAFED is the state's largest wheat procurement agency.

## Mandi network

In RMS 2025-26, **418 mandis and procurement centres** were opened across the state for wheat procurement (Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26, para 3.107). A news report on the preparation for the same season said 108 mandis were linked to e-NAM (source 4) — we did not open a government figure for this.

## Payment and transparency

According to the news report on RMS 2025-26 preparation (source 4), banks were told that payment should reach the farmer's account within **48-72 hours** of the exit gate pass being issued. This was a target, not a legal guarantee, and matching it with a government order is pending. Find out the payment system for each season and the gate-entry method at the mandi from your own mandi/procurement agency.

## Why split among agencies — and what it means

There is a clear logic behind dividing procurement among four agencies — so that the load of the whole state's procurement does not fall on a single institution, the risk is shared, and procurement can happen in parallel and fast in different districts. HAFED's largest share (about 47% in RMS 2025-26) shows that the cooperative sector is the backbone of the state's food procurement. From the farmer's point of view, it is useful to know which agency his mandi is allotted to, because the purchase slip and payment go through that agency's process.

## What is e-NAM and why it matters

e-NAM (Electronic National Agriculture Market) is an online mandi platform that takes the farmer's produce beyond a single local mandi to a wider network of buyers. This improves price discovery and transparency. According to the RMS 2025-26 news report, about 108 of the 418 mandis were linked to e-NAM — that is, the system is spreading, but it has not yet reached every mandi. Ask the mandi secretary whether your mandi is on e-NAM.

## The rabi basket — not just wheat

Note that MSP is not announced only for wheat — it is announced for the whole rabi basket. In RMS 2026-27, MSP was also announced for barley (₹2,150), gram (₹5,875), lentil (₹7,000), mustard (₹6,200) and safflower (₹6,540 per quintal). The state decides separately every season which agency will do the government procurement of which crop in Haryana. This means farmers who grow pulses and oilseeds along with or instead of wheat also have the option of government procurement. Promoting pulses and oilseeds is good for both crop diversification and soil health — and the MSP guarantee makes this diversification economically safe. (See: [Pulses and oilseeds MSP procurement](/blog/dalhan-tilhan-msp-procurement-nafed-nccf).)

## The procurement process — from the farmer's point of view

1. **Registration:** entering the crop, area and bank details on the designated portal.
2. **Mandi and gate pass:** bringing the produce to the mandi; the vehicle and goods entry is recorded at the gate.
3. **Quality and weighing:** checking against standards, weighing, purchase slip.
4. **Exit gate pass:** a gate pass when the goods leave — the payment clock starts from this.
5. **Payment:** directly into the bank account (in RMS 2025-26 the target was stated as 48-72 hours).

## Before going to the mandi — the farmer's checklist

- [ ] Portal registration complete and verified.
- [ ] Wheat clean, dry and of standard quality.
- [ ] Bank account active, Aadhaar-linked, name matching correctly (for DBT payment).
- [ ] Knowledge of your allotted mandi, procurement agency and date.
- [ ] Vehicle details ready (for entry at the gate).

## Quality standards and how to avoid rejection

In government procurement, produce has to meet certain quality standards (moisture, broken grains, foreign matter, etc.). If moisture or adulteration is above the set limit, the goods may be rejected or a price cut may apply. Practical ways to avoid rejection — bring the crop well dried, clean/sieve it after harvesting and threshing, and dry rain-soaked goods properly. A little extra effort saves you from both rejection and price cuts in the mandi.

## Cooperative procurement vs private sale

The farmer has two options — government/cooperative procurement at MSP, or selling directly to a private trader. The advantage of MSP is the guarantee of a fixed price and the transparency of direct bank payment. A private sale can sometimes give immediate cash, but there is no price guarantee there, and there is a risk of distress sale. That is why, when government procurement is available, registering and taking the benefit of MSP is usually in the interest of small and medium farmers. A balanced use of both depends on the farmer's judgement.

## Why payment gets delayed — and how to avoid it

Even when a payment target is fixed, payment sometimes gets stuck — the root is often technical: the bank account is inactive, Aadhaar is not linked, or the spelling of the name differs between the registration and the bank record. So the biggest precaution is to correct all three of these before the season. Keep the gate pass and purchase slip safely — these are the proofs that work in a payment enquiry.

## An example of a farmer

Suppose Suresh, a medium farmer, brings 200 quintals of wheat to the mandi this season. First he registers on the portal and checks the bank-Aadhaar link. In the mandi his goods pass the quality check, they are weighed, and a purchase slip is made at the RMS 2026-27 MSP (₹2,585/quintal) — a total of ₹5,17,000. After he gets the exit gate pass, this amount comes directly into his bank account. If Suresh's bank details or name match had been wrong, this same payment could have got stuck — so preparation is the real key. (This imaginary example is only to explain the process; the actual amount depends on the produce and rate.)

## HAFED, the cooperative structure and the society's accounts

At village level, HAFED's reach is often built through PACS and cooperative societies, which help link farmers and collect produce. For a society that takes part in procurement/commission, a separate, clear account of farmer-wise purchase, quantity, MSP value, commission income and expenses is necessary — only then do activity-wise profit and the audit stay clear. (See: [Basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics) and [DCCB Haryana credit structure](/blog/dccb-haryana-pacs-harco-bank-credit-structure).)

## After procurement — storage and distribution

The procured wheat is kept safely in godowns, and then reaches the needy through the Public Distribution System (PDS) and other schemes. That is why adequate and safe storage capacity is as important as procurement — if godowns are short, grain can rot in the open. That is why schemes that increase godown capacity (such as PEG) are important. For the farmer, the direct meaning is — his produce becomes part of a large, organised food supply chain, where both quality and records matter. (See: [Godown/storage scheme](/blog/pacs-godown-grain-storage-scheme-ami-aif-nabard).)

## In the end

Government wheat procurement is the biggest economic activity of Haryana's rabi season, and HAFED is its largest agency pillar. The lesson for the farmer is the same — timely registration, correct documents, and knowledge of your mandi/agency. And since MSP, targets and the number of mandis change every season, do not treat the figures given here (MSP: RMS 2026-27; system: RMS 2025-26) as applying to a new season — the official announcement is final.
