The Ministry of Cooperation of the central government has prepared "model bye-laws" for PACS, with the aim of taking PACS beyond being only an agricultural credit institution and making them multi-purpose institutions. According to the Ministry of Cooperation, PACS will be stronger and more financially sustainable when they become multi-purpose. This guide explains these model bye-laws in detail with sources — what they are, which new activities, changes in governance and member rights, the legal process of adoption, the impact in Haryana, and precautions.

> 📌 Opening note: the model bye-laws are a **template/suggestion**, not a law that applies automatically. They apply to a particular PACS only when it duly adopts them in its general body (see the process below). Figures and lists can change over time — for the latest information, confirm with the official documents in the [Sources](#sources) section and your Registrar's office.

## Why model bye-laws — background

For decades, most PACS stayed limited to short-term agricultural credit only. This created two problems — one, many PACS stayed financially weak and ran at a loss, because they had only one source of income; and two, to meet the village's needs (storage, inputs, services), the farmer had to go to different places. The model bye-laws are the solution to this — making PACS sustainable institutions with diverse income that give many services under one roof. This is part of the wider idea of "Sahkar se Samriddhi" (prosperity through cooperation), where strong PACS are seen as the hub of the rural economy. The thinking is that when a single village-level institution meets many needs, the farmer's trust grows and so does the society's income — and this becomes the foundation of a self-reliant rural economy.

## Which new activities can PACS now take up?

Under the model bye-laws, PACS have been allowed to work in **more than 25** business activities, the main ones being:

- **Dairy operations** and **fisheries**
- **Grain storage, godowns and warehousing**
- **Custom hiring centres** (farm equipment on rent)
- **Agro-processing** and **foodgrain procurement**
- Fertilizer/seed and farm-input distribution as a **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samriddhi Kendra (PM-KSK)**
- **Jan Aushadhi Kendra** (sale of generic medicines)
- **Water supply operations**, **petrol-diesel/LPG/CNG distribution**
- **Fair price shop (FPS)** and community irrigation
- **Banking correspondent (BC)** and **Common Service Centre (CSC)** services

That is, a PACS can now go beyond fertilizer and seeds to milk, fish, storage, fuel, medicines, banking and digital services — it can become a "multi-purpose rural service centre". Each PACS can choose from these activities according to its needs and local demand; doing all of them together is not required.

> 💡 CSC services (Aadhaar, bills, insurance etc.) are one part of this multi-purpose direction — in detail: [PACS now as CSC too](/blog/pacs-csc-haryana-common-service-centre-income).

## Changes in governance and member rights

The model bye-laws are not limited to new activities only — their second big aim is **better governance, transparency and inclusive membership**. They contain provisions that make membership wider and more inclusive — with a special emphasis on giving **women and Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes** adequate representation in management. The aim is that a PACS becomes an institution of the whole village community, not just of a few influential people. This is an effort to bring the cooperative values of "democratic control" and "equality" down to the ground.

## National adoption — 32 states/union territories

According to the Ministry of Cooperation, so far **32 states/union territories** have either adopted the model bye-laws, or their existing bye-laws are already in line with the model bye-laws. This shows that it is not a lone, local initiative — but a coordinated campaign across the country to make PACS multi-purpose, which is also linked to the wider plan of forming 2 lakh new multi-purpose PACS.

## Its impact in Haryana — the PM-KSK example

A concrete example of this multi-purpose change already exists in Haryana — about **742 PACS** of the state (as per a report) were working as Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samriddhi Kendras (PM-KSK), that is, they were giving additional services like fertilizer and farm-input distribution — this is exactly the direction the model bye-laws propose.

> ⚠️ The latest number changes over time — for any current figure, confirm with the Haryana Cooperation Department/the concerned ARCS office. The aim here is to show that the direction of multi-purpose PACS has already picked up speed in Haryana.

The PM-KSK example matters because it is not a "paper provision" but an activity running on the ground — it shows that PACS can actually go beyond agricultural credit and earn additional income and serve members through services like input distribution. The farmer gets fertilizer, seeds and farm inputs from a trusted society in their own village, and the society earns income from commission/margin — this is a "benefit for all" model, which is the core spirit of the model bye-laws.

## The difference between PACS and their existing bye-laws — formal adoption is required

> ⚠️ The model bye-laws are not a law by themselves — they are a template/suggestion, which each PACS has to formally adopt into its bye-laws through the process prescribed under [Sections 9A and 10 of the Act](/blog/haryana-cooperative-act-1984-rules-1989-guide) (2/3 majority, in the general body). The state government adopting the model alone does not automatically give any particular PACS the right to start new activities.

This distinction is very important, and confusion often happens right here. "The state has adopted the model" is a policy step; but **your PACS** cannot take up a new activity until it passes a bye-law amendment in its own general body. That is, there are two separate levels — state-level adoption, and society-level formal amendment. Without the second, the first does not give any particular PACS any new right.

## The process of adoption — step by step

A PACS adopts the model bye-laws (or their new activities) like this:

1. **Discussion in the managing committee:** a draft of the new activities and the bye-law amendment is prepared.
2. **Resolution in the general body:** the amendment is placed before the general body.
3. **Passed by 2/3 majority:** the amendment is approved by the prescribed majority (as per the process of Section 9A/10).
4. **Submitted to the Registrar:** the passed amendment is sent for registration/approval.
5. **In force after registration:** the amendment takes effect only once it is registered, and the new activity can be legally started.

> 🔑 Remember — just passing it in the general body is not enough; a bye-law amendment is treated as in force only after it is **registered** with the Registrar. (The basics of bye-laws: [What are bye-laws](/blog/cooperative-society-byelaws).)

## Before a new activity — practical preparation

Besides the bye-law amendment, before starting any new activity (such as a petrol pump, godown, dairy), a PACS also has to do practical preparation:

- **Capital and viability:** is the activity financially sustainable? Where will the start-up cost come from?
- **Required licences/approvals:** many activities (fuel, medicines, food) need separate regulatory permission.
- **Trained staff and infrastructure:** skills and equipment to run the new service.
- **Separate accounting:** a separate head for the income and expenses of each new activity, so that you know which activity is profitable.
- **Member consent and demand:** is there really demand for this service in the village? An activity without demand quickly goes into loss.

So adopting the model bye-laws is the first step, not the last — real success lies in choosing and running new activities thoughtfully, in a phased way.

## Accounting for new activities

Becoming multi-purpose means many kinds of income and expenses — and these must be shown separately, otherwise you will not even know which activity is in profit and which is in loss. So keep a **separate account/head** for the income and expenses of each activity (dairy, fuel, storage etc.), and look at the category-wise profit and loss at year-end. This discipline is what truly makes a multi-purpose PACS profitable — because then the managing committee can decide, based on data, which activity to expand and which to wind down.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The state has adopted the model, so my PACS can do it too"** — No; every PACS must pass a bye-law amendment in its own general body.
- **"The model bye-laws are a law"** — No; they are a template/suggestion, which has to be duly adopted.
- **"The activity starts as soon as it is passed in the general body"** — No; it is in force only after registration with the Registrar.
- **"Every PACS has to do all 25+ activities"** — No; these are options, choose as per need and viability.
- **"A bye-law amendment alone is enough for a new activity"** — No; the required licences, capital and staff are also needed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is adopting the model bye-laws compulsory?** — Adoption at the state level is a policy step, but to take its benefit, a particular PACS has to formally pass a bye-law amendment in its own general body.
**Can a PACS start a new activity as soon as it adopts the model bye-laws?** — No. The process prescribed under Section 9A/10 (2/3 majority, resolution in the general body) and registration must be completed; only after that can a new activity be legally started.
**What is the connection between PM-KSK and the model bye-laws?** — PM-KSK (fertilizer/input distribution centre) is a ground-level example of the multi-purpose approach of the model bye-laws — it shows how PACS can work beyond agricultural credit.
**How many new activities are allowed?** — More than 25 — dairy, fisheries, godowns, custom hiring, fuel/LPG, Jan Aushadhi, FPS, BC, CSC etc.
**Will every PACS have to do all the activities?** — No; these are options, a PACS chooses as per its need and viability.
**How many states have adopted them?** — According to the Ministry of Cooperation, 32 states/union territories (adopted or already in line).
**What changed in governance?** — More inclusive membership, emphasis on women and SC/ST representation, and better transparency.
**How should the accounts of a new activity be kept?** — A separate head for the income and expenses of each activity, so that the category-wise profit and loss is clearly visible.
**What else is needed for a new activity?** — Besides the bye-law amendment, the required licences, capital, trained staff and an assessment of local demand.

## Three things to remember

- 🔄 The model bye-laws are only a template — to take their benefit, a PACS must formally pass a bye-law amendment in its own general body.
- 🌾 About 25+ new activities are allowed — from dairy, fisheries and warehousing to petrol-diesel retail and CSC.
- 📋 The process of Sections 9A and 10 (2/3 majority + registration) cannot be skipped — the state adopting the model alone does not give any particular PACS the right.

## Conclusion

The model bye-laws are a big opportunity for PACS — to change from a single-income agricultural credit institution into a multi-purpose rural service centre with diverse income. But this opportunity bears fruit only when the PACS does two things properly: first, a **due bye-law amendment** (general body 2/3 majority + registration), and second, **thoughtful preparation and separate accounts** for every new activity. The state adopting the model alone is not enough — the real change comes at the society level, in a rule-compliant and phased way. A PACS that does this strengthens its own viability and also serves the village.
