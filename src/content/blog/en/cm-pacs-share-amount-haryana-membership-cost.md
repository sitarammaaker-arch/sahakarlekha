If you want to become a member of a CM-PACS in Haryana, the most common question is this: how much share amount will you have to pay for membership? This guide explains the provisions of the [Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984](/blog/haryana-cooperative-act-1984-rules-1989-guide) that decide membership and share amount, and also explains why a claim of any single fixed amount can be misleading. It also explains the difference between share amount, admission fee and other charges, the refund policy, and the things worth checking before you become a member.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** ₹20,000 → ₹1,000 was found only in one news report (January 2025); no government order was found. Read "refundable" together with the conditions of Rule 18(3). Sources are below.

## First — what is a CM-PACS

A short clarification is needed, because there is often confusion here: **CM-PACS does not mean "Chief Minister PACS"** — its full name is **Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activity Cooperative Society**, that is, a multi-purpose cooperative society that can carry out many kinds of activities (credit, godowns, procurement, digital services etc.). It is a Haryana state-specific initiative. Its membership and share amount, like other cooperative societies, are decided by the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 and the society's own bye-laws. (In detail: [What is CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-kya-hai).)

## Eligibility for membership — Section 16

Under **Section 16** of the Act, a person competent to contract, another cooperative society, the Government, and other classes written in the bye-laws (groups of persons, companies etc.) can become members of a cooperative society.

The time limit for deciding an application is in **Rule 17**: every society other than a producers' society must decide within **one month** of receiving the application, and if it refuses, it must give reasons. The proviso to Section 16 also has an "automatic member in 60 days" rule, but it applies only to **cooperative credit and service societies, Kisan Seva Samitis and primary agricultural development banks** — it does not automatically apply to a multi-purpose society like a CM-PACS. (The earlier version said it applied to every society.)

## Share amount — not one fixed figure, but a maximum limit

The Act itself does not fix a minimum share amount for a member — this is left to each society's own bye-laws and the rules prescribed by the Registrar. However, **Section 6** does fix an upper limit: other than the Government or another cooperative society, no member can hold shares of more than the prescribed portion of the society's total share capital (which can be at most one-fifth), or more than ₹5 lakh — whichever is less.

> 💡 That is, the law draws an upper wall, but each CM-PACS fixes the minimum entry fee or share amount itself in its bye-laws. Do not assume any other society's figure applies to your society — confirm the correct amount only from your committee's registered bye-laws.

## Why the share amount differs from society to society

This question is natural — the law is the same, so why does the share amount differ from society to society? The reason lies in the basic idea of cooperation: each society fixes its share amount according to its members' economic condition, its activities and its capital needs. A large society with many activities needs more capital, so its share amount can be higher; a small, new society can keep it low so that more members can join. That is why the Act kept the minimum amount flexible (dependent on the bye-laws) and fixed only a maximum limit (Section 6). This flexibility lets each society adapt to its own needs — but it also means that looking for "one fixed figure" is itself the wrong question.

## Share amount, admission fee and other charges — understand the difference

The "cost" of membership often mixes several things, which need to be understood separately:

- **Share amount:** the member's capital stake in the society; it stays recorded in the member's name, but its refund has strict conditions under Rule 18(3) (see below).
- **Admission fee (admission/entrance fee):** a one-time fee when becoming a member; usually it is not refunded.
- **Other charges:** some societies may also take other small charges as per the bye-laws (such as a passbook/form fee).

So when someone asks about the "cost of membership", see what it includes — only the share amount, or the admission fee and others too. The exact details always come from the society's bye-laws.

## Why the share amount is taken

The share amount is not just an "entry ticket" — it has a deeper purpose. When every member deposits some share amount, it builds the society's **initial capital (share capital)**, so that it can start its activities (giving loans, buying goods, building godowns). At the same time, this amount makes the member a co-owner of the society — that is, not just a customer, but a stakeholder directly connected to the society's profit, loss and decisions. This "member = owner" principle is the soul of cooperation, and the share amount is its concrete form. So treat the share amount not as an expense, but as an investment in your own society.

## What "refundable" share means — Rule 18(3)

The news report below calls the share amount "refundable" — but Haryana's rules are clear on this. Under **Rule 18(3)**, in a society with limited liability, a member is usually **not** allowed to withdraw shares. A refund can happen only when the society has created a **share transfer fund** out of its profits — then the managing committee, keeping the society's interest in view, may allow a share refund; and even then, total refunds at any time cannot exceed **5%** of the paid-up share capital as on the previous 31 March (excluding the Government's portion). For a society with unlimited liability, Rules 18(1)-(2) are different (refund without interest after notice and the period written in the bye-laws). That is, the share amount does not "sink" like the admission fee, but it is also not returned "whenever you want". When becoming a member, do ask whether your society has limited liability, and whether it has a share transfer fund.

## Rights only when payment is complete — Section 19

**Section 19** makes it clear that a member cannot exercise their rights in the society (such as voting or contesting elections) until they have completed the payment specified in the bye-laws — that is, the share amount and other charges. So submitting an application alone is not enough; paying the prescribed amount is the condition for getting full membership rights.

## What has been seen in practice?

Since this amount is not fixed in the law but can differ in each society's bye-laws, no single, uniform, statewide figure can be drawn from the text of the Act. A January 2025 news report (Mahendra India News, 3 January 2025) says that the share capital of CM-PACS was earlier fixed at ₹20,000 per member, which the government reduced to ₹1,000, "which is refundable". The report does not name any officer or order, and we did not find the RCS Haryana order with the CM-PACS Model Bye-laws — see in detail the [CM-PACS Model Bye-laws guide](/blog/cm-pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-guide).

> ⚠️ ₹20,000 and ₹1,000 are figures from only one news report — do not treat them as a "fixed amount" applicable to every CM-PACS, and read "refundable" together with the conditions of Rule 18(3). Always confirm your society's actual amount in writing from its registered bye-laws or the ARCS.

## Why the claim of "one fixed amount" misleads

On the internet or in rumours, a fixed figure like "the CM-PACS membership fee is this much" often circulates — but be careful with any such claim. The reason is clear: the law itself did not fix a minimum amount; it depends on each society's bye-laws. So one society's figure does not apply to another, and it can also change over time (like the report's ₹20,000 to ₹1,000). Avoid paying money or taking decisions by treating any one number as a "universal truth" — this leads to misunderstanding and sometimes to fraud. Confirming from your particular society's registered bye-laws or the ARCS is the only reliable way.

## What membership gives you

Becoming a member of a CM-PACS by paying the share amount brings several benefits that make it worthwhile: access to the multi-purpose society's services (credit, inputs, godowns, digital services); a fair share in profit/dividend (as per rules); the right to vote in the society's decisions; and a voice in the general body. That is, the share amount is not just a fee, but the door to all these rights and services. That is why it is right to see it as "a stake in your own society" more than as a "cost".

## What to check before becoming a member

A practical checklist, so that no confusion remains:

1. **Full cost:** share amount + admission fee + any other charges — how much in total.
2. **Refund policy:** which part is refundable and which is not — and whether the society has a share transfer fund (Rule 18(3)).
3. **When rights will come:** membership rights like voting/elections only after the prescribed payment is complete (Section 19).
4. **Receipt:** do take and keep a receipt for every payment (share amount, admission fee) and the share certificate.
5. **Read the bye-laws:** read the membership conditions, share amount and rights in the society's registered bye-laws.

Checking these five things first means no confusion later in your membership, and you know exactly what you are paying and what you will get in return. (Membership eligibility and KYC: [CM-PACS Membership guide](/blog/cm-pacs-membership-haryana-eligibility-kyc).)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there one fixed figure for CM-PACS share amount in Haryana?** — No. The Act fixes only the maximum limit (Section 6); each society fixes the minimum share amount itself in its bye-laws.
**Within how many days should an application be decided?** — Rule 17: within one month (other than a producers' society), with reasons if refused. The "automatic member in 60 days" rule of Section 16 applies only to credit and service societies, Kisan Seva Samitis and primary agricultural development banks.
**Can one vote without paying the share amount?** — No. Under Section 19, a member cannot exercise their rights without completing the prescribed payment.
**What is the full name of CM-PACS?** — Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activity Cooperative Society (it is not "Chief Minister PACS"); it is a Haryana state-specific, multi-purpose initiative.
**What is the difference between share amount and admission fee?** — The share amount is the member's capital stake (refund on the conditions of Rule 18(3)); the admission fee is a one-time fee (usually not refunded).
**How many shares can be held at most?** — Under Section 6, the prescribed portion (at most one-fifth) or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less (other than the Government/another society).
**Where can I find the correct amount?** — From your society's registered bye-laws or the concerned ARCS office — do not rely on another society's figure.
**Why is the share amount taken?** — It builds the society's initial capital and makes the member a co-owner of the society (not just a customer).
**Is the share amount refunded?** — Usually not in a society with limited liability; only if there is a share transfer fund, and total refunds up to 5% of the paid-up share capital as on the previous 31 March (Rule 18(3)).
**What benefits does membership give?** — Access to the society's services, a share in profit, the right to vote and a voice in the general body.

## Three things to remember

- 🔝 The Act fixes only the maximum share limit (Section 6: the prescribed portion or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less) — each society fixes the minimum share amount itself in its bye-laws, so asking for "one fixed figure" is itself the wrong question.
- 📝 An application should be decided within one month (Rule 17); the share amount is an investment, not an expense — but its refund is only on the conditions of Rule 18(3).
- 🗳️ Membership rights (voting, elections) cannot be exercised without full payment (Section 19) — an application alone is not enough.
- 🔎 Do not trust any "fixed amount" claim — confirm only from your society's bye-laws/ARCS.

## In the end

There is no single, statewide answer to "What is the CM-PACS share amount?" — and that is the most important message of this article. The Act draws only a maximum limit (Section 6); each society fixes the real, minimum amount in its registered bye-laws, according to its own needs. So do not trust another society's figure or a rumour — confirm the correct amount, refund policy and conditions only from your CM-PACS's bye-laws or the ARCS. And remember — full membership rights come only after paying in full, taking a receipt and reading the bye-laws. With this much care, no confusion remains later in your membership.
