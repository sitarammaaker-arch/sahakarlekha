Want to build a management or policy-level career in the cooperative sector? India now has an organised education structure for this — from a free introductory online course to the country's first national cooperative university. This guide brings all the main options together in one place, in simple language — eligibility, duration, who each one suits, and where you can work after studying — so that you can choose the right path for your situation.

## Tribhuvan Sahkari University — India's first national cooperative university

To give professional education in the cooperative sector a new identity, **Tribhuvan Sahkari University** has been set up in Anand, Gujarat — named after Amul's founder Tribhuvandas Kishibhai Patel, the university is built on the existing structure of the Institute of Rural Management Anand (IRMA) and has been given the status of an **"Institution of National Importance"**.

> 💡 The university works on a **"Hub-and-Spoke"** model — Anand is its centre (hub), while state-level cooperative training institutes across the country are linked to it (spokes). It offers a PhD, an MBA (with specialisations such as Cooperative Management, Cooperative Banking & Finance and Agribusiness Management), graduate, diploma and short-term certificate courses. For young people who want a career in institutions such as PACS, HAFED, NAFED, cooperative banks or NCDC, this is a new, national-level path.

## IRMA Anand — PGDM Rural Management

The Institute of Rural Management Anand (IRMA) was founded in 1979 by Dr. Verghese Kurien. Its flagship course, **PGDM Rural Management**, is a 2-year full-time programme. To apply you need at least 50% marks in your graduation (general category) or 45% marks (SC/ST/OBC category), and scores of national entrance tests such as CAT, XAT or CMAT are accepted for admission, followed by an interview.

> 📘 Study at IRMA is not limited to the classroom — it includes a large share of village fieldwork, where students work directly with rural communities and cooperative societies, building a real ground-level understanding of PACS, dairy cooperative societies and rural development institutions. IRMA's placement record has traditionally been very strong.

## VAMNICOM Pune — PGDM Agribusiness and Management

The Vaikunth Mehta National Institute of Cooperative Management (VAMNICOM), Pune is counted among the country's oldest training institutes in cooperative management. Its main programme, **PGDM (Agribusiness and Management)**, is a 24-month full-time course recognised by AICTE. To apply you need a graduate degree from any recognised university (minimum 50% for General/OBC/EWS, 45% for SC/ST), and a score in any one of CAT, XAT, CMAT, MAT or ATMA is accepted, followed by a group discussion and personal interview. Graduates go into management roles in cooperative banks, agribusiness companies, FPOs, and institutions such as NAFED and NCDC.

## HDCM — Higher Diploma in Cooperative Management

For people already working in the cooperative sector and for those looking for a new career alike, **HDCM** is a practical course, run through the country's network of Institutes of Cooperative Management (ICM) and Regional Institutes of Cooperative Management (RICM) — such as Pune, Chennai, Dehradun, Imphal and Chandigarh.

It is a 36-week course in all, split into two semesters — it includes classroom study, study tours, project work and a final exam. The minimum qualification is a graduate degree; people already working in cooperative departments/institutions get preference, but eligible private candidates can also apply (the upper age limit is generally 55 years, with relaxation possible in special cases). The course focuses on practical subjects such as cooperative law, financial management, audit and organisational behaviour — that is, the same knowledge used directly in daily work at a PACS, DCCB or state cooperative federation. Most institutes run this course in correspondence mode as well as the regular session, so it can be completed while in a job.

## A free start — the IGNOU-SWAYAM online course

If you cannot take a big course because of fees or lack of time, there is also a free, fully online option — **MNRE 015: Cooperatives and Farmers' Organizations**, available through IGNOU on the SWAYAM platform. It is a 12-week self-study (video and text based) course with no fee and no barrier such as an entrance test — anyone interested can register directly on the SWAYAM portal and begin.

> 💡 Before enrolling in a big diploma or degree course, this course is a good way to build a basic understanding of cooperative organisations and farmers' organisations.

## What these courses teach

Cooperative management courses are a little different from a regular MBA — along with general management they include cooperative-specific subjects. Areas commonly taught:

- **Cooperative law and governance** — the Act, bye-laws, managing committee, general body.
- **Cooperative accounting and audit** — member/share/loan accounts, final accounts, audit.
- **Cooperative banking and finance** — credit, recovery, NPA management.
- **Rural/agribusiness management** — supply chain, marketing, FPOs.
- **Organisational behaviour and leadership** — member-centred management.
- **Fieldwork** — practical experience with real societies/villages (especially at IRMA).

This cooperative-specific knowledge is what makes these courses directly useful in PACS, cooperative banks and federations — because the challenges there (member management, recovery, audit, multipurpose activities) differ from an ordinary company, and handling them needs special training.

## Why start with the free course first

Before putting money and time into a big course, the free IGNOU-SWAYAM course is a sensible first step. It has three benefits: first, without any risk (no fee, no entrance test) it tells you whether the cooperative sector interests you; second, it explains the basic vocabulary and concepts (cooperative principles, organisation, farmers' organisations) in advance, so the bigger course later feels easier; and third, it adds a starting qualification to your resume. So whether you are a student, working, or changing careers — starting here is the lowest-risk path.

## Where the career goes after studying

After studying cooperative management, opportunities are not limited to one place — the cooperative ecosystem is very large:

- **Cooperative banking:** state/district cooperative banks (StCB/DCCB), urban cooperative banks — management, lending and audit roles.
- **PACS and federations:** management from primary societies up to state federations.
- **Apex institutions:** development/finance/project roles in institutions such as NABARD, NCDC and NDDB.
- **Dairy and agribusiness:** milk unions, FPOs (farmer producer organisations), agribusiness companies.
- **Procurement agencies:** cooperative marketing/procurement institutions such as NAFED and HAFED.
- **Government cooperative departments:** policy, registration, audit and supervision.

In other words, a cooperative management degree/diploma opens many doors — from rural development to banking and policy.

## Which course suits whom — a simple guide

- **Just want to build understanding / a start:** the free IGNOU-SWAYAM course.
- **Already working in the cooperative sector:** HDCM (including correspondence mode, alongside the job).
- **A new, full-time management career:** the PGDM at IRMA or VAMNICOM.
- **Research/higher education/wide options:** Tribhuvan Sahkari University (from PhD to certificate).

Choose the right step for your current situation (student, working, or career change) and your time and budget — the biggest course is not necessarily the right one.

## Why a career in the cooperative sector

The cooperative sector is a growing field connected to society — especially after a separate Ministry of Cooperation was created (2021) and the growing focus on cooperative reforms. A career here means not just a job, but also a chance to make a direct impact on the rural economy and the lives of farmers and small producers. For young people who want a career that gives social meaning along with economic stability, cooperative management is an attractive path. And now, from free online courses to a national university, the ways in are more accessible than ever.

## Frequently asked questions

**How can I build a basic understanding of the cooperative sector at no cost?** — The free MNRE 015 course on IGNOU-SWAYAM is the easiest start.
**Which course suits people already working in cooperative banks/federations best?** — HDCM, because it gives preference to working candidates and is also available in correspondence mode.
**Which courses are better for a full-time, management-level career?** — IRMA's PGDM Rural Management and VAMNICOM's PGDM Agribusiness — both are 2-year full-time programmes.
**How is Tribhuvan Sahkari University different from the rest?** — It is a full university, with many levels from PhD to certificate courses available together, and it holds the status of an institution of national importance.
**Where do jobs come from after studying?** — Cooperative banks, PACS/federations, NABARD/NCDC/NDDB, dairy unions, FPOs, NAFED/HAFED and the cooperative department.
**Which course should I do first?** — For a start, the free IGNOU-SWAYAM; then HDCM/PGDM/university depending on your situation.
**What is taught in these courses?** — Cooperative law/governance, accounting/audit, banking/finance, rural and agribusiness, leadership and fieldwork.
**Can I do a course while in a job?** — Yes; HDCM is often available in correspondence mode, and IGNOU-SWAYAM is fully self-study.
**Is there fieldwork?** — Many courses (especially IRMA) include practical fieldwork with villages/societies.

## In the end

A career in the cooperative sector is now a clear, organised ladder — from a free introductory course to the country's first national cooperative university. The right path is the one that matches your current situation and goal: a free online course if you want to build understanding, HDCM if you are working, and IRMA/VAMNICOM or the university if you want a full-time management career. Just always remember one thing — fees, seats and admission processes change every year, so confirm on the official website of the institute concerned before applying.
