# How to fill a payment voucher in a cooperative society? Sample, required documents and accounting entry

As an example, suppose a payment voucher is placed before the auditor. It has a date, an amount and one signature at the bottom. But where it says "Paid to?" it only says "M/s Sharma", the reason says "miscellaneous expense", there is no bill number, no cheque number, and the payee's signature space is blank. The amount is ₹18,500. The auditor asks: "What is this voucher proof of?" The secretary's only answer is memory.

A payment voucher is the paper (and that entry in the books) which proves tomorrow that money went **to whom, how much, for what work, on what basis and how**. A good voucher speaks for itself; with a bad voucher the secretary has to explain every time. This article is every step of **filling** a payment voucher: a complete paper sample, what to write in each box, which documents go with which payment, entries for seven kinds of payment, and how to fill it in the portal.

> 📘 **In short:** A payment voucher must make **nine things** clear: **voucher number, date, paid to whom, amount (in figures and words), for what work, on the basis of which document, which account is charged (Dr), how it was paid (cash/cheque number/online UTR), and who prepared, checked and approved it and that the payee received it.** The entry is simple: **Dr expense (or supplier/advance), Cr cash/bank**; if there is a deduction (such as TDS), that is a separate Cr line. The supporting documents should be attached to the voucher. I did not find any fixed format of a payment voucher in the Haryana Act or Rules; the format below is **good practice**, not a legal template.

What a voucher is and all its types are in [What is a voucher](/blog/what-is-voucher-types-uses-accounting-entry), and the full framework for **approval** of payments (who approves up to what amount) is in [Payment approval and voucher](/blog/payment-approval-voucher-entry-secretary-chairman-treasurer). This article is the link between the two: **how to fill the voucher paper and its entry.**

## Paper sample of a payment voucher

In small societies, a **printed voucher form** is still used for every payment, and one copy goes into the voucher file. A simple format *(sample; your society's format may differ)*:

| Box | Filled *(imaginary sample)* |
| --- | --- |
| **Society name / Registration No.** | Example Village Cooperative Society Ltd. / ____ |
| **Payment voucher No.** | PV-0142 |
| **Date** | 12-10-2026 |
| **Paid to** | M/s Sharma Electricals, Main Bazaar |
| **Amount (in figures)** | ₹18,500.00 |
| **Amount (in words)** | Rupees eighteen thousand five hundred only |
| **For what work (head)** | Repair of wiring of office and godown |
| **Supporting document** | Bill No. 311 dt. 10-10-2026; managing committee resolution No. 14 dt. 02-10-2026 |
| **Expense account head (Dr)** | Repair expense |
| **Mode of payment** | Cheque No. 004517, Bank ____ , Branch ____ |
| **Deduction (if any)** | None / TDS ₹____ |
| **Net payment** | ₹18,500.00 |
| **Prepared by** | (Accountant) signature / date |
| **Checked by** | (Treasurer) signature / date |
| **Approved by** | (Chairman / as per resolution No.) signature / date |
| **Received by payee** | Name, signature/thumb impression, date |
| **Attached** | Copy of bill, copy of resolution, cheque counterfoil |

The strength of this one page is that **this voucher alone** tells the whole story of the payment: to whom, how much, why, on what basis, how, who checked it, who approved it, and whether the payee received it or not.

## What to write in each box

| Box | What to write | What not to write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Voucher number** | Serial, with no gaps or repeats; separate series for payments, receipts etc. | Skipping or repeating a number |
| **Date** | **The day the payment actually happened** | Back-dating or forward-dating to bring it into a "convenient" financial year |
| **Paid to** | **The same name as on the bill** (full name, firm name, address or member code) | Only "Sharma", "contractor", "party" |
| **Amount** | In figures **and in words** both, and both must match | Only figures; or figures and words differ |
| **For what work** | "Repair of office wiring", "Electricity bill for September 2026" | "Miscellaneous expense", "goods", "expense" |
| **Supporting document** | Bill/invoice **number and date**, resolution/order number | "Bill attached" (without number) |
| **Expense account (Dr)** | Correct head: repair, electricity, salary, purchase, **supplier** (if the purchase is already recorded) | Everything in "miscellaneous expense" |
| **Mode of payment** | Cash, or **cheque number**, or online **UTR/reference**, and which bank account | Only "from bank" |
| **Deduction** | TDS/GST/other, with amount | Forgetting the deduction and paying the full amount |
| **Signatures** | Separate persons for preparing, checking and approving | All three signatures by one person |
| **Payee's receipt** | Name, signature or thumb impression, date | Leaving it blank; someone else's signature |

**A formula for the narration,** which works for every payment:

> **To whom + for what work + on the basis of which bill/order + how paid (cheque/UTR) + (which period)**

Example: "Payment to M/s Sharma Electricals for repair of godown wiring, Bill No. 311 dt. 10-10-26, resolution No. 14, cheque No. 004517". Such a narration tells the whole story even after six months. (Details: [Voucher narration and documents](/blog/voucher-narration-and-documents).)

## Which documents with which payment

The list below is **good practice**; if the instructions of a scheme or department ask for different documents, those are final.

| Payment | Attach with it |
| --- | --- |
| **To supplier (purchase of goods)** | Bill/invoice; purchase order; goods receipt (GRN); check of rate and arithmetic; proof of payment |
| **Contractor / repair** | Quotations (often three); managing committee resolution; bill; proof of work completion; GST/TDS details |
| **Salary / wages** | Attendance or muster roll; salary sheet; details of deductions; proof of bank transfer |
| **Rent** | Rent agreement; landlord's receipt; TDS details (if applicable) |
| **Electricity, phone, water** | Copy of bill; payment receipt/UTR |
| **Travelling allowance / tour expense** | Travel order/intimation; travel details; tickets or receipts |
| **Professional fee (CA, lawyer etc.)** | Bill; appointment resolution/letter; TDS deduction |
| **Audit fee** | Bill or order of the Registrar/Auditor |
| **Advance payment** | Approval; condition of the advance and date of adjustment |
| **Share refund / deposit withdrawal to a member** | Member's application; society's resolution/bye-law basis; entry in passbook |
| **Bank charges** | Line in the bank statement |
| **Small cash expense (petty cash)** | Receipt/bill; if not available, a self-voucher with reason |

Three general rules:

- **The supporting document should exist before the payment,** not be "found" after the payment.
- **Write the voucher number on every paper** in pencil, so that if the bill and voucher get separated they can be matched again.
- **Where no document was available at all,** write the reason on the voucher and take the signature of the person who allowed it; do not pay quietly. (Payment without proof is the most common audit objection.)

## Entries for seven kinds of payment

*(All amounts and names are imaginary.)* In every row Dr and Cr are equal.

**1. Small cash expense, ₹1,200 (stationery):**

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stationery expense | ₹1,200 | — |
| Cash | — | ₹1,200 |

**2. Electricity bill, online, ₹3,200:**

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electricity expense | ₹3,200 | — |
| Bank | — | ₹3,200 |

Narration: "Electricity bill for September 2026, consumer No. ___, UTR ___".

**3. Part payment to a supplier:** first goods of ₹38,000 were bought on credit (Dr purchase, Cr supplier), then ₹25,000 was paid by cheque:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier | ₹25,000 | — |
| Bank | — | ₹25,000 |

₹13,000 remains payable in the supplier's account. **Do not charge the expense again:** the purchase entry was already made when the bill came; in the payment only the supplier's account is debited.

**4. Professional fee with TDS deduction, ₹20,000** *(the TDS rate here is taken as 10% only to explain; the real rate and threshold come from your CA/TDS article):*

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Professional fee (expense) | ₹20,000 | — |
| TDS payable (liability) | — | ₹2,000 |
| Bank | — | ₹18,000 |
| **Total** | **₹20,000** | **₹20,000** |

The full bill is ₹20,000, but the payee received **₹18,000** and ₹2,000 was held back to deposit with the government. Record this in three lines, in one voucher. (What TDS is, when it is deducted, when it is deposited: [TDS and 26Q](/blog/tds-and-26q-for-societies).)

**5. Advance to a supplier, ₹10,000:**

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advance to supplier (asset) | ₹10,000 | — |
| Bank | — | ₹10,000 |

An advance is not an expense, it is an **asset to be recovered or adjusted later**. When the bill comes, it is adjusted.

**6. Payment of salary (when salary has already been booked as "payable"), ₹20,000:**

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salary payable (liability) | ₹20,000 | — |
| Bank | — | ₹20,000 |

**7. Bank charges, from the statement, ₹354:**

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank charges (expense) | ₹354 | — |
| Bank | — | ₹354 |

Its basis is the bank's line, so the bank statement date and reference should be in the narration. (Monthly bank matching: [BRS](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide).)

> ⚠️ **Some payments go beyond even the committee's approval:** under Section 89 of the Act, purchase, sale or lease of land and construction of a building or immovable property, and under Section 90, writing off dues, require **the prior approval of the Registrar**. If such a payment is made, that approval should be attached to the voucher. (Details: [Payment approval and voucher](/blog/payment-approval-voucher-entry-secretary-chairman-treasurer).)

**On cash payments:** from the income tax point of view there are some limits on cash payments, but I did not open the text, so I am not writing any limit. Make big payments through the bank, and **confirm the limits with your CA.** (Cash precautions: [Cash handling and verification](/blog/cash-handling-and-verification).)

## How to fill a payment voucher in the portal

In SahakarLekha, a payment voucher is made from the entry tab of the **Vouchers** page. There are **two modes** here, and you can switch at any time:

**1. Easy mode:** ready templates for "Money Paid": **Salary Paid, Wages Paid, Electricity Bill, Phone Bill, Repair Expense, Cash Purchase, Audit Fee** (along with "Cash to Bank", a cash→bank transfer). When you choose a template, the accounts are already set (Dr its expense head, Cr cash); you fill the date, amount, narration and (optional) linked member.

**2. Expert mode:** choose the type (receipt/payment/journal/contra), then fill the **Dr and Cr lines**. In the payment type, Cr can be cash or any bank account ("For cash/bank payments"), and **more than one line** can be added, as for the TDS example 4: one Dr line, and two Cr lines (TDS payable and bank).

**Steps to fill (Expert mode):**

1. **Vouchers** → entry tab → **Expert mode**.
2. **Type:** Payment.
3. **Date:** the day the payment was made.
4. **Dr line:** the correct expense head (or supplier/advance) and amount.
5. **Cr line:** **cash or the correct bank account**, and if there is a deduction, a separate line for it (such as TDS payable).
6. **Narration:** as per the formula above; **write the cheque number/UTR here too** (see below).
7. **(Optional) add a member,** if the payment is linked to a member.
8. **(Optional) voucher number:** write your own; if you don't, the portal gives the next number automatically.
9. **(Optional) tick the "Submit for Approval (Maker-Checker)"** checkbox (it is in both Easy and Expert modes); the voucher will first stay "Pending" and will post to the books after approval.
10. **Save.** Until the Dr total and Cr total are equal, the save button stays disabled and the portal shows a warning, so an unbalanced voucher cannot be made.

**Payment of a supplier's bill** can also be made from **"Bill-wise Settlement → Pay Supplier"** on the same page: when you choose the supplier, their open bills are shown, the amount is split across the bills (oldest bills first, automatically too), and the voucher is made by choosing cash or bank. (Step by step: [How to pay a supplier (Help)](/help/make-payment).)

**Two warnings of the portal, which appear printed in Easy mode, and which are important to remember:**

- **The "Cash Purchase" template records only the amount:** **it neither changes stock nor adds GST.** For purchase of goods, use the **Purchases** page.
- **The "Salary Paid" template:** if that month's salary has already been processed from the **Salary/Payroll** page, **do not** use this template, otherwise the salary will be recorded **twice**. And this template **does not add deductions (PF/TDS) either.**

**Two limits, which must be stated honestly:**

1. **There is no separate box for the cheque number.** In the voucher form I found neither a separate cheque number/UTR field, nor one for uploading documents. So **write the cheque number and UTR in the narration,** and keep the bill, resolution etc. in a **paper or separate digital file** with the voucher number.
2. **Easy mode templates are built as cash payments.** If you need to pay from the bank, choose the correct bank account on the Cr side in **Expert mode**.

**A subtle point about approval:** the optional Maker–Checker system (amount limit or voucher type) applies to vouchers of this entry form. But vouchers of **Bill-wise Settlement** and **deposit/withdrawal** do not come under that hold, so take their approval first on a paper slip. (Full explanation and reason: [Payment approval and voucher](/blog/payment-approval-voucher-entry-secretary-chairman-treasurer).)

## If a mistake happens

- **Do not delete the voucher.** If the amount, account or date is wrong, **cancel** it and make a correct new voucher, so the audit trail remains. In the portal's voucher list, cancel and reverse are available by recording a reason.
- **An approved voucher** (where approval is switched on) stays locked for editing; correct it by cancelling and making a new voucher.
- **Once the financial year is locked** no correction is possible; so catch mistakes before year-end. (Monthly matching: [Monthly reconciliation](/blog/monthly-reconciliation-checklist-cashbook-bank-trial-balance).)
- **In paper vouchers:** the old voucher stays in the file with a "Cancelled" stamp and the reason, and the new voucher refers to it.

## Audit preparation: what the law says, what the practice is

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auditor's access | Section 95(3) | Free access to books, papers, **vouchers** and property |
| Access during inspection | Section 97(1) | Access to vouchers etc. for the Registrar/authorised person |
| Land-building and write-offs | Sections 89, 90 | **Prior** approval of the Registrar |
| Conflict of interest | Rule 30 *(third-party copy)* | An officer should have no direct/indirect interest in the society's dealings |
| Format of payment voucher, boxes, signatures, attached documents | — | **I did not find it in the Act/Rules**; practice, bye-laws and orders of the Registrar/higher body |

**Before the audit, check this on every payment voucher:**

- [ ] Voucher numbers serial, and none missing
- [ ] Date of the actual payment, and in the correct financial year
- [ ] Payee's full name, matching the bill
- [ ] Amount in figures and words matches
- [ ] Reason clear; not "miscellaneous"
- [ ] Supporting document attached, its number/date on the voucher
- [ ] Signatures of approval, checking and payment by different people
- [ ] Cheque/UTR number recorded, and visible in bank matching
- [ ] Payee's receipt
- [ ] Deduction (TDS etc.) correct
- [ ] Resolution number for big amounts; and where needed, the Registrar's approval
- [ ] Voucher file month-wise, stitched (full preparation: [Audit preparation checklist](/blog/audit-preparation-checklist))

> ⚠️ **For PACS and readers on a prescribed portal:** if your society's payments are recorded in a prescribed ERP/portal (such as e-PACS), DCCB or department form, then that voucher format, boxes and procedure are final. The sample in this article does not replace it; it is for understanding it and for your own records. (Background: [e-PACS and day-end](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).)

## Common mistakes

1. **A narration like "miscellaneous expense".** The most common root of audit objections.
2. **Incomplete payee name** ("Sharma", "contractor").
3. **Not writing the bill number and date.** Proof and voucher do not link.
4. **Amount differs in figures and words.**
5. **Not writing the cheque number or UTR.** Trouble every time in bank matching.
6. **The same person preparing, checking and approving.**
7. **Charging the expense again when paying a supplier.** If the purchase is already booked, Dr supplier.
8. **Forgetting to deduct TDS,** or deducting it but not showing it in a separate line.
9. **Recording, through Easy mode's "Salary Paid", salary that has already been done through payroll.** The salary gets booked twice.
10. **Recording purchase of goods through the "Cash Purchase" template.** Stock and GST are not added.
11. **Cash payment without the payee's receipt.**
12. **Deleting a voucher.** Cancel, don't delete.

## Three things to remember

- 🧾 A payment voucher must make nine things clear: number, date, to whom, amount (figures and words), for what work, on the basis of which document, which account (Dr), how paid (cheque number/UTR), and prepared-checked-approved plus the payee's receipt. Entry: Dr expense (or supplier/advance), Cr cash/bank, a separate Cr line for the deduction.
- 📎 The supporting document should exist before the payment and its number and date should be written on the voucher; the narration formula: to whom + why + on what basis + how. Do not delete a voucher; cancel it and make a new one.
- 🖥️ In the portal, Easy mode's "Cash Purchase" does not add stock/GST and "Salary Paid" can book payroll salary twice; write the cheque number in the narration, and take approval for Bill-wise Settlement/deposit-withdrawal on paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How to fill a payment voucher?** — Voucher number, date (of the actual payment), payee's full name, amount in figures and words, reason (head), number and date of the supporting document, expense account (Dr), mode of payment (cash/cheque number/UTR), deduction if any, and signatures of preparer, checker, approver and the payee.
**What is the entry in a payment voucher?** — Dr the expense account (or the supplier, if the purchase is already recorded; or advance), Cr cash or bank. If there is a deduction like TDS, a separate Cr line for it.
**Which documents should be attached with a payment voucher?** — It depends on the type of payment: for a supplier, bill, purchase order and goods receipt; for a contractor, quotations, resolution and proof of work completion; for salary, attendance and salary sheet; for electricity-phone, the bill; and so on. See the table above.
**Does the law have a fixed format for a payment voucher?** — In the text of the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989 that I read, I did not find a fixed format for a payment voucher. Under Section 95(3) the auditor has free access to vouchers. If the Registrar or a higher body has a format, follow that.
**What to write for a cheque payment?** — Cheque number, bank and branch, and in the narration to whom/for what work/on the basis of which bill. Keep the cheque counterfoil with the voucher.
**How to record a part payment to a supplier?** — Dr supplier, Cr bank/cash, for the amount paid. The remaining amount stays payable in the supplier's account. Do not charge the purchase expense again.
**Where to fill a payment voucher in the portal?** — In the entry tab of the Vouchers page: through Easy mode's "Money Paid" templates, or in Expert mode by choosing the type "Payment" and filling Dr/Cr lines.
**Does the portal have a separate box for the cheque number?** — I did not find a separate cheque number/UTR field in the voucher form. Write it in the narration.
**How to correct a wrong payment voucher?** — Do not delete it. Cancel it (or reverse it) with a reason and make a correct new voucher, so the audit trail remains.

## Finally

The payment voucher is the smallest everyday paper, and also the one most tested in audit. A voucher in which to whom, how much, why, on what basis, how and who approved are all visible at a glance needs no explanation. And in a society where every voucher is filled this way, the secretary does not have to search files for proof at year-end.
