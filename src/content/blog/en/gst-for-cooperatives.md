# GST for cooperative societies: a simple guide

As soon as a society's turnover grows, or it starts buying and selling goods, **GST** comes into the picture. Many secretaries think it is complicated, but the daily work actually rests on a few simple rules. This article makes GST simple from a cooperative society's point of view.

This article explains **GST for cooperative societies** — the basic idea, accounting entries, ITC, returns, the mutuality confusion and common mistakes — in simple language. Note — this article explains the *accounting method* for GST; exact rates, limits and rules keep changing, so always confirm the provisions that apply to your society with your CA or the GST portal.

## What GST is — in one line
When you sell something, you collect **output GST** from the customer; when you buy, you pay **input GST** to the supplier. At the end of the month: output − input = what you must deposit with the government.

In simple words, the society works here as a "collector" between the government and the customer — it collects GST from the customer and deposits it with the government, after reducing the GST paid on purchases (ITC). So GST is neither an expense nor an income of the society — it is a pass-through liability, and keeping its correct account is essential.

> 💡 GST registration is not required for every society — it depends on the turnover limit and the activity. Confirm the rule that applies to your society from the **GST portal or your CA**. This article only explains the method of accounting.

## Three terms you use every day
- **CGST + SGST** — on sales within the same state (half to the Centre, half to the state).
- **IGST** — on sales to another state.
- **ITC (input tax credit)** — the GST paid on purchases, which reduces your liability.

An easy way to remember them: if the buyer and seller are in the same state, it is CGST+SGST (two parts); if they are in different states, it is IGST (just one). The total rate stays the same in both, only the split is different. ITC can be available on both kinds of GST, provided the conditions are met. This basic understanding is enough for most day-to-day GST entries.

## Monthly work (a simple checklist)
1. Match the **sales register** with **GSTR-1** (outward).
2. Match **purchases/ITC** with **GSTR-2B** — take only the ITC that shows in 2B.
3. Deposit the **GST liability** on time and file **GSTR-3B**.

This three-step monthly habit is the real secret of keeping GST notice-free. A society that does this matching every month has no big confusion left at year-end; one that puts it off finds its small differences piling up into big trouble at the time of the annual return and audit. So treat it as "essential monthly work", not something to postpone.

## Accounting entries for GST

Showing GST correctly in the books is essential. Suppose there is ₹180 output GST on a sale of ₹1,000 (the rate is only for an example calculation):

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash/Customer | ₹1,180 | — |
| Sales (income) | — | ₹1,000 |
| Output GST (liability) | — | ₹180 |

In the same way, GST paid on purchases goes to "Input GST" (an asset/credit). At month-end, the input credit is reduced from the output liability and the net amount is deposited with the government, and that entry reduces the liability. This way GST is mixed neither into income nor into expense — it stays in separate liability/credit accounts. (Basics of voucher entry: [voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide).)

## Conditions for taking ITC

Input tax credit (ITC) is not free — it has some basic conditions:

- You must have a valid **tax invoice** from the supplier.
- The purchase must show in your **GSTR-2B** (that is, the supplier must have filed their return).
- The goods or service must actually have been **received**.
- The purchase must be for the purpose of the society's **taxable business**.

So just having a bill is not enough — it must match in 2B. This is why ITC matching every month is so important.

## Reverse charge — a glimpse

In some cases, GST is paid directly to the government not by the supplier but by the **buyer (the society)** — this is called reverse charge (RCM). Which transactions fall under it is decided by the rules and keeps changing, so confirm with your CA. Record it separately in the accounts, so that it does not get mixed with normal GST.

## Correct GST on a discounted bill
Even when selling to members at a discount, GST is charged on the **amount after the discount (taxable value)** — not on the full MRP. This is a common mistake. For example, if an item of ₹1,000 is sold for ₹900 after a ₹100 discount, GST will be charged on ₹900 (taxable value), not on ₹1,000. But keep in mind — the discount must already be shown on the bill; a discount given later has different rules, so if in doubt, confirm with your CA.

> ⚠️ Do not take ITC without a GSTR-2B match — this directly leads to audit objections or notices. Do ITC matching every month. (For more such mistakes, see [10 common mistakes](/blog/common-accounting-mistakes).)

## How a digital system makes it easier
With the right setup, GST is added or reduced automatically on every sale/purchase, an **HSN summary** is made, and reports for GSTR matching are ready — you do not have to add and subtract separately. (For the basics, see [why digital accounting is necessary](/blog/digital-accounting-for-cooperatives).)

This is the biggest difference between hand-kept and digital GST accounts — manually, adding the GST of every bill, matching ITC and preparing return figures takes hours, and the room for error is large. Digitally, all of this is built automatically from the vouchers, so the secretary's attention is not on adding and subtracting but on correct matching and timely filing. Still, the final responsibility is understanding — the system gives the figures, but checking them, filing, and confirming with your CA when in doubt remains your job.

## GST on supplies to members too? The "mutuality" confusion
Many societies believe that no GST applies when selling only to their own members (the "mutuality principle"). The **Finance Act 2021** added **Section 7(1)(aa)** to the CGST Act and limited this with retrospective effect from 1 July 2017 — now the society and the member are legally treated as two separate "persons". **In April 2025, the Kerala High Court** held this section unconstitutional in a separate case (clubs/associations), but the direct effect on cooperative societies is not yet clearly settled — until the Supreme Court gives a final decision, relying on it and not charging GST is risky.

> 💡 Agricultural produce services (loading, unloading, storage, grading, commission agent services) already have a clear exemption (Notification 12/2017-Central Tax) — this is a separate and more reliable route than the mutuality debate. But as soon as processing changes the basic form of the produce (such as milling), this exemption ends.

> ⚠️ Registration limit (general category states): sale of goods ₹40 lakh/year, services only ₹20 lakh/year — registration becomes compulsory as soon as the limit is crossed, even if the sales are only to members.

## Main GST returns — at a glance

In GST, mainly three returns come up again and again (frequency and exact rules depend on your category):

- **GSTR-1** — details of your sales (outward).
- **GSTR-3B** — monthly/quarterly summary and payment.
- **Annual return** — consolidated details for the whole year (where applicable).

Their due dates and frequency change from time to time — confirm the current deadlines for your category from the GST portal or your CA. Late filing attracts a late fee and interest. (Annual compliance: [annual statutory returns](/blog/annual-statutory-returns).)

## HSN/SAC codes

Every item of goods has an **HSN code** and every service a **SAC code**, which must be written on the bill (the number of digits depends on the size of the business). The right code keeps reporting clean and matching easy. In a digital system this code is linked to the item, so it appears automatically on every bill.

## Record-keeping for GST

A GST-registered society must keep invoices, purchase bills, ITC records and copies of returns in order — for several years (the exact period depends on the rules, confirm with your CA). Good records are your defence at the time of a notice or audit. Keep the documents of every sale/purchase in order and take a digital backup.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking ITC without a 2B match** → the most common reason for notices; always match with 2B.
- **Late returns** → late fee and interest; file before the due date.
- **Treating GST as income/expense** → it is a pass-through liability, keep it in separate accounts.
- **GST on the full MRP** → GST applies only on the taxable value after the discount.
- **Not charging GST assuming mutuality** → legally risky; see below and confirm with your CA.
- **Wrong HSN/SAC** → reporting errors; choose the right code for every item/service.
- **Not keeping records** → keep invoices, bills and copies of returns for years as per the rules.

## Frequently asked questions
**Does every society need GST registration?** — No; it depends on the turnover limit and the activity — confirm with your CA/the portal.
**When can we take ITC?** — Only when there is a valid invoice, the goods have been received, and it shows in GSTR-2B.
**Is GST income or a liability?** — It is a pass-through liability; neither income nor expense — keep it in separate accounts.
**On what is GST charged on a discounted bill?** — On the taxable value after the discount, not on the full MRP.
**Is there no GST when selling to members?** — That is the old "mutuality" idea; the position changed after the Finance Act 2021 (§7(1)(aa)) — see below, and confirm with your CA.
**Is there an exemption on agricultural produce?** — Many agricultural services are exempt under Notification 12/2017, but not when processing changes the basic form — check the details with your CA.
**Which are the main returns?** — GSTR-1 (sales), GSTR-3B (summary + payment), and the annual return (where applicable).
**Why is the HSN code necessary?** — To identify the goods/service on the bill and for correct reporting.
**What is reverse charge?** — In some transactions, GST is paid directly to the government not by the supplier but by the buyer (the society); which ones is decided by the rules — confirm with your CA.
**What if GST is not deposited?** — Risk of late fee, interest and notices; so deposit and file every month before the due date.

## Three things to remember
- 🧾 Take ITC only when it shows in GSTR-2B, not merely because you have a bill.
- 🗓️ File GSTR-3B and pay every month before the due date.
- 📊 On a discounted bill, GST applies only on the taxable value, not on the full MRP.

## In the end
GST is nothing to be afraid of — just output, input and timely filing. Understand it as a pass-through liability, match ITC every month, file returns on time, and keep records in order. With the right system and monthly matching, it stays simple and notice-free. And if there is any doubt — especially on mutuality, exemptions or rates — do not guess, confirm with your CA.
