# How to register a cooperative society

The first legal step in starting a new cooperative society is **registration**. The moment it is registered, the society gets a separate legal identity. This article explains **how to register a cooperative society**: why it is needed, where to apply, the main steps, the documents you need, Haryana's online portal, and the first steps after registration, all in simple language.

> 💡 Note: this article explains the general process. The exact rules, minimum members, fees and forms depend on your state's cooperative Act. Always confirm with your state's RCS/portal.

## Why registration is needed
After registration, the society becomes a **separate legal entity (body corporate)**. It can hold an account, property, contracts and lawsuits in its own name, and its liability is separate from its members. Without registration, you do not get these protections.

In short, registration gives you:

- **Separate identity:** the society works in its own name, independent of its members.
- **Limited liability:** a member's liability is usually limited to their share amount.
- **Continuity:** members may keep changing, but the society carries on.
- **Legal rights:** bank account, loans, property and contracts are all in the society's name.
- **Government support/schemes:** many schemes are open only to registered societies.
- **Member trust:** a registered structure guarantees transparency and accountability, so members join with confidence.

## Who can form a society

Generally, a group of people with a common economic interest (such as farmers, consumers, shopkeepers, employees) can come together to form a society. Members should be adults, connected to the society's objective, and should meet the prescribed minimum number. The objective should be in line with cooperative principles (voluntary membership, democratic control, mutual help). This is the basic spirit of the Act.

## Where to apply
The application goes to the **Registrar of Cooperative Societies (RCS)**, under the state's cooperative Act. A society that works in more than one state applies to the Central Registrar under the **Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002**.

In India, cooperation is a **state subject**, so every state has its own cooperative Act and rules, and its own RCS office. That is why the minimum members, fees and forms differ from state to state. So always treat the current information from your state's RCS/portal as final. In bigger states, below the RCS there are ARCS (Assistant Registrar) offices at district/sub-division level, where the actual application goes.

## Main steps
1. Form a group of the **chief promoter** and interested members (the minimum number of members depends on the state Act/type, often 10).
2. Decide on **name reservation** and the objective.
3. Prepare the **proposed bye-laws**. The RCS provides model bye-laws.
4. Submit the **application form** + documents (member list, share capital details, resolution of the meeting).
5. The RCS **examines** it; if everything is in order, a **registration certificate** is issued.

Let us open up these steps a little more:

- **Steps 1–2:** first make the group and the objective clear: what need will the society meet (consumer goods, farm services, housing etc.). A clear objective makes both the bye-laws and the approval easier later.
- **Step 3:** the bye-laws are the society's "rule book". Membership, shares, meetings, elections, profit distribution are all settled in them. Start from the model bye-laws and adapt them to your objective. (See: [What are bye-laws](/blog/cooperative-society-byelaws).)
- **Step 4:** submit all documents in one go, complete and correct. An incomplete application is the biggest cause of delay.
- **Step 5:** in the RCS/ARCS examination, the bye-laws, member eligibility and documents are checked; once satisfied, the certificate is given.

| Step | What |
| --- | --- |
| Promoter group | Gather the minimum members |
| Bye-laws | Proposed rule book |
| Application | To the RCS, with documents |
| Certificate | Legal identity |

> ⚠️ Note: the exact minimum members, fees and documents change according to the **state's cooperative Act and rules**. Confirm with your state's RCS office.

## Role of the chief promoter

In the registration process, one person is chosen as the **chief promoter**, who handles the application and paperwork on behalf of the group: dealing with the RCS, filling forms, submitting the bye-laws and making arrangements up to the first general body meeting. After registration, this person coordinates until a proper managing committee is formed. So give this responsibility to a trusted and active member who can handle both the paperwork and dealing with the RCS well, because the speed of registration depends a great deal on this person's promptness.

## Required documents: a list

There is some difference from state to state, but generally you need:

- The prescribed **application form** (duly signed).
- Copies of the **proposed bye-laws**.
- **List of members**: name, address, occupation, photo, signature.
- Proof of **share capital** deposit (bank slip or receipt).
- Proof of the **registered office** address (rent agreement or ownership document).
- **Resolution** of the promoters' meeting, recording the decision to form the society.

> 💡 Keep the documents complete and correct in advance. Incomplete papers are the root of the biggest delays in registration.

## Single-state vs multi-state society

| Aspect | Single-state | Multi-state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law | The state's cooperative Act | Multi-State Co-op Societies Act, 2002 |
| Application | To the state's RCS | To the Central Registrar |
| Area of operation | One state only | More than one state |

If the society's work is limited to one state, the state law is the simpler and cheaper route. Choose multi-state registration only if you plan to spread across several states. Most small and local societies (consumer stores, PACS, housing societies) start with single-state registration, and that is enough for them.

## After registration: first steps

As soon as you get the certificate, some work has to be done right away:

- The **bye-laws come into force**; call the **first general body meeting** and form the managing committee.
- A **bank account** in the society's name and the required registrations (such as PAN).
- Start **books of account and compliance**: opening balances, share register, member register.
- Regular record-keeping. This is where cooperative-specific software like SahakarLekha helps from day one (see: [How to enter opening balances](/blog/opening-balances-new-society) and [Basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics)).

> 💡 Keeping proper books from day one makes the first audit easier and keeps members' trust strong from the start.

## Responsibilities after registration

Registration is not the end, it is the beginning. A registered society has to keep doing these regularly:

- **Regular meetings:** managing committee and annual general body meeting on time.
- **Books and records:** cash book, ledger, member and share registers up to date.
- **Annual audit:** by the cooperative department; cooperate on time.
- **Annual returns:** the prescribed returns/reports to the Registrar.
- **Following the bye-laws:** elections, profit distribution, membership, all as per the bye-laws and the law.

Meeting these responsibilities is what turns a society from "registered" into "healthy and trusted". (Also: [The framework of cooperative law](/blog/cooperative-law-framework).)

## In Haryana: fully online registration
In Haryana, registration is fully online through the **cooponline.rcsharyana.gov.in** portal: self sign-up, choosing the society type, uploading the form and bye-laws, then approval after review by the concerned ARCS office (32 in total in the state) and verification by the Inspector. **Section 8** of the Act gives an important protection: if the ARCS does not decide within one month, the application is by law **deemed accepted**.

> 💡 The required documents include the form, the proposed bye-laws, a list of at least 10 members with photos, proof of share capital and proof of the registered office address. The forms to be uploaded change from time to time, so treat only the current forms on the portal as final.

> ⚠️ The most common delay: the bye-laws do not show a match between the society's objective and Section 4 of the Act (an economic interest linked to cooperative principles), or signatures/photos in the member list are incomplete. With incomplete documents, the "one month" clock resets and the application becomes pending again.

### Step-by-step on the Haryana portal

On the portal, the process broadly runs in this order:

1. **Self sign-up:** create your account on the portal.
2. **Choose the society type:** from consumer, agricultural, housing, multipurpose etc.
3. **Fill the form:** enter the details of the society and its members.
4. **Upload documents:** proposed bye-laws, member list (with photos/signatures), proof of share capital and address.
5. **Submit:** the application goes to the concerned ARCS office.
6. **Review and verification:** ARCS review + Inspector verification.
7. **Approval:** if everything is in order, the registration certificate is issued.

This online system reduces the need to visit the office again and again and keeps the whole process transparent. Every stage stays visible on the portal, so the applicant always knows which stage the application is at and what is expected next.

## After submitting the application: how to check the status
After submitting the application, there is no need to visit the office again and again. On the homepage of cooponline.rcsharyana.gov.in, a "Status Enquiry" section is available even without login. Enter your ARCS office, society type and registration number/name, and the current status (pending at ARCS level, Inspector verification in progress, approved or rejected) shows up immediately. Societies already registered can also see basic information by entering their registration number.

> ⚠️ If the application shows at the same stage without any update for longer than the one-month limit of Section 8, contact the ARCS office directly. By law, in such cases the application is deemed accepted.

## Common mistakes that cause delay

- No match between the objective in the bye-laws and the spirit of the Act.
- Photos/signatures incomplete in the member list.
- Not attaching proof of share capital or address.
- Using old/wrong forms (always take the current form from the portal).
- Minimum number of members not met.
- Proof of registered office address not clear.

If you check these in advance, the process stays quick and smooth. A good habit is to tick off the full document list one by one before submitting, and to re-check every member's photo and signature, because most rejections or delays come from these small gaps, not from some big reason.

## Frequently asked questions
**How is a cooperative society registered?** — Application to the RCS with bye-laws and documents → examination → registration certificate.
**Where to apply?** — The state's RCS (one state), or the Central Registrar (multi-state, 2002 Act).
**How many members at minimum?** — As per the state Act/type, often 10.
**What does registration give?** — A separate legal identity and limited liability.
**Who can form a society?** — A group of adults with a common economic interest, meeting the minimum number.
**How long does it take?** — It differs by state; in Haryana, under Section 8, if the ARCS does not decide within one month, the application is deemed accepted.
**First task after registration?** — First general body meeting, forming the managing committee, bank account and starting the books.
**Difference between single-state and multi-state?** — Work in one state = state law/RCS; in several states = 2002 Act/Central Registrar.
**Who is the chief promoter?** — The person who handles the application and paperwork on behalf of the group, and coordinates until the first general body meeting.

## Three things to remember
- 🏛️ Registration is with the RCS; it gives the society a separate legal identity.
- 📋 Bye-laws + members + application = the foundation of registration.
- 🧾 After the certificate, the bye-laws apply; keep accounts from day one.
