After [HAFED's wheat procurement](/blog/hafed-wheat-procurement-2026-msp-mandi-guide), it is now the turn of paddy — the biggest procurement operation of Haryana's kharif season. This guide explains HAFED's role, MSP and the mandi system on the basis of government figures for the last completed kharif season (KMS 2025-26).

> ⚠️ **Figures checked up to 18 October 2026.** The MSP is taken from a Government of India (PIB) document and the procurement quantity from the Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26 (see "Sources" below). These are for the kharif 2025-26 season; procurement for kharif 2026-27 is still going on and its figures will be different. For the latest position, see the announcements of HAFED and the Food, Civil Supplies and Consumer Affairs Department, Haryana.

## MSP rate and total procurement

In Kharif Marketing Season (KMS) 2025-26, the MSP of paddy was **₹2,369 for Common paddy** and **₹2,389 per quintal for Grade-A paddy** (source 1). According to the Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26, paddy procurement in the state ran from 23 September to 15 November 2025, and a total of **62.13 lakh metric tonnes** of paddy was procured (source 2).

## How are farmers paid?

The MSP money goes directly into the farmer's bank account. In Haryana, procurement is done only from farmers who have registered their crop on the "Meri Fasal Mera Byora" portal (news report, source 4) — so it is difficult to be included in procurement without online registration. We did not find in an open source the final government figure of how much was paid in total over the whole season, and to how many farmers, so no amount is given here.

## Procurement agencies — HAFED's role

According to the Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26 (para 3.109), the procurement agencies are: the Food, Civil Supplies and Consumer Affairs Department, HAFED, the Haryana State Warehousing Corporation (HSWC) and FCI. The Survey says that in kharif 2025-26 HAFED procured 19.03 lakh metric tonnes of paddy, which was about 30.64% of the state's total paddy procurement (para 3.122). Mandis are allotted district-wise to different agencies; we did not find in the source any fixed percentage split for paddy like the one for wheat. (For wheat, see: [HAFED wheat procurement](/blog/hafed-wheat-procurement-2026-msp-mandi-guide).)

## Quality standards and facilities

The Government of India's Uniform Specification for paddy sets maximum moisture at **17%**. We opened and read the KMS 2024-25 specification (source 3); news reports said procurement up to 17% applied in 2025-26 as well, but we could not open that year's specification. The other standards, such as broken grains, discoloured grains and foreign matter, are also in this specification. Who will pay the labour for filling, weighing, stitching and loading in the mandi is decided in each season's state procurement policy — ask your mandi or procurement agency.

## What exactly is HAFED

HAFED means **the Haryana State Cooperative Supply and Marketing Federation Limited** (The Haryana State Cooperative Supply & Marketing Federation Limited) — the state's largest apex cooperative institution, set up on 1 November 1966 (Haryana Economic Survey 2025-26, para 3.121). Its core work is government procurement, storage, processing and marketing of farmers' produce, along with supply of farm inputs such as fertilizer and seed. Since it is a cooperative federation, its structure is linked to village-level societies — so it is the farmer's "own" institution, not some outside private company. HAFED's big role in paddy procurement comes from this cooperative character: the benefit finally returns to the farmer's interest.

## Why MSP and government procurement matter

The aim of MSP (Minimum Support Price) is to save the farmer from "distress sale" — that is, being forced to sell at throwaway prices right after harvest. When the government guarantees procurement at a fixed price, the farmer gets at least a reliable floor price, and private traders also hesitate to pay below it. In a big kharif crop like paddy, this protection becomes the backbone of the income of lakhs of farmers. A cooperative agency like HAFED is the means of putting this guarantee into practice on the ground.

## What these figures mean

The kharif 2025-26 figures give an idea of the scale. If 62.13 lakh metric tonnes of paddy is valued at the MSP of common paddy (₹2,369), its value works out to roughly ₹14,700 crore. This is our own rough calculation, not the government payment figure — Grade-A paddy sells at ₹2,389, and we did not find the final payment amount in the source. HAFED alone had a share of about 30% in this procurement. These figures are season-specific, but they show how big a support MSP procurement is for the Haryana farmer's income, and how big a link the cooperative federation is in it.

## The procurement process — from the farmer's point of view

In practice, the path for a farmer to sell paddy is usually like these steps (the exact process depends on each season's guidelines):

1. **Registration:** Entering crop, area and bank details on the "Meri Fasal Mera Byora" portal.
2. **Mandi call/gate pass:** Bringing the produce to the designated mandi, vehicle and produce being recorded at the gate.
3. **Quality check:** Checking moisture (maximum 17% in the specification) and other standards.
4. **Weighing and procurement:** The produce is weighed, and a procurement slip (J-form etc., where applicable) is made.
5. **Payment:** The MSP amount directly into the farmer's bank account (DBT).

Understanding these steps lets the farmer reach the mandi without running around, with the right documents — which reduces both payment delays and rejection.

## The link between HAFED and the cooperative structure

HAFED is an apex cooperative institution, and its reach at the village level is often built through PACS and other cooperative societies — which help in connecting farmers, collecting produce and getting it to the mandi. So government procurement is not the work of just one top agency, but a shared link of the whole cooperative structure — from the apex (HAFED) down to the village society. (See: [DCCB Haryana: the three-tier credit structure](/blog/dccb-haryana-pacs-harco-bank-credit-structure).)

## If the payment does not come on time

Sometimes the DBT payment gets stuck. The most common roots are technical — bank account inactive, Aadhaar not linked, wrong IFSC, or different spelling of the name in the registration and the bank record. In such a case, first take your procurement slip and gate pass and contact the mandi secretary/procurement agency office concerned, and get the bank details corrected. That is why checking the bank account and Aadhaar link before the season itself is the best precaution — so that there is no hold-up in payment after the produce is sold.

## Special points for small and marginal farmers

The biggest benefit of MSP procurement goes to the small farmer, because he has neither storage facilities nor bargaining power. But in practice small farmers often fall behind because of confusion over the registration process or documents. This is where the village cooperative society (PACS) can help — with group registration, document help and access to the mandi. So the small farmer should connect with his local society and start preparing before the season itself, so that he gets the full benefit of MSP without running around.

## For the society — accounts of procurement

For a cooperative society that takes part in procurement/procurement commission, clean accounting is necessary — farmer-wise procurement, quantity (quintals/bags), MSP value, commission income, and expenses such as labour/transport should be recorded separately. This shows clearly at year-end how much surplus the society got from the procurement activity, and the audit also stays smooth. Good cooperative software keeps these farmer-wise and head-wise accounts automatically. (See: [Basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics).)

## The big picture of paddy procurement — food security

Government procurement of paddy is not only a matter of farmers' income — it is also the foundation of the country's **food security**. The paddy procured at MSP becomes rice after milling and reaches crores of people through the public distribution system (PDS/ration). So it plays a double role — on one side a guarantee of a fair price to the farmer, on the other side cheap grain for the consumer. A cooperative agency like HAFED is an important link in this whole chain (procurement → storage → milling → distribution). That is why the transparency and efficiency of the procurement process affect everyone, from the farmer to the ordinary consumer.

## Before going to the mandi — the farmer's checklist

- [ ] "Meri Fasal Mera Byora" registration complete and verified.
- [ ] Crop dry enough (moisture below 17%).
- [ ] Cleaning/sieving — soil, pebbles etc. at a minimum, so there is no rejection in the quality check.
- [ ] Bank account active, linked with Aadhaar, name matching correctly (for DBT).
- [ ] Identity and necessary documents with you.
- [ ] Information on your allotted mandi and procurement date.

This small preparation saves you from all three — long queues at the mandi, rejection and payment delay.

## Storage and milling — what happens after procurement

The procured paddy does not go straight to the consumer — first it is stored safely in godowns, then given to rice mills under the milling policy, from where it becomes rice and goes into the PDS. That is why enough godown capacity and an efficient milling system are as important as procurement. If godowns are short, procured grain can spoil in the open — so the state keeps stressing the increase of storage capacity. (See: [Godown/storage scheme](/blog/pacs-godown-grain-storage-scheme-ami-aif-nabard).)

## After procurement — milling and custom milling

The procured paddy does not go straight into the ration — first it has to be turned into rice. For this, procurement agencies give the paddy to rice mills, which return rice in a fixed ratio (this is called "custom milling"). This whole chain — procurement → storage → custom milling → distribution — runs smoothly only when every link works on time. From the farmer's point of view, it is useful to know that his produce is not just bought, but becomes part of a big food supply system — and that is why the quality standards are kept so strict.

## Common precautions

- **Registration on time:** Missing the deadline for portal registration can keep you out of procurement.
- **Moisture:** Bring the crop well dried — moisture above 17% is the root of rejection.
- **Bank account and name matching:** The name should be the same in the registration, the bank account and the identity document, otherwise the DBT may get stuck.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a farmer need to register for paddy procurement?** — Yes, without online registration on the "Meri Fasal Mera Byora" portal it is difficult to get the benefit of MSP payment.
**What is the maximum moisture limit fixed for paddy?** — 17% in the Government of India's Uniform Specification; paddy with more moisture than this usually does not meet the procurement standards. Check each season's specification.
**Who bears the cost of labour such as filling and weighing?** — This is decided in each season's state procurement policy. Its order was not opened for this article, so confirm with your mandi or procurement agency.
**How is the MSP payment received?** — Directly into the registered farmer's bank account (DBT); so portal registration and correct bank details are necessary.
**Does the paddy MSP stay the same every year?** — No; the central government fixes the MSP every season. The rates given here (common ₹2,369, Grade-A ₹2,389) are for kharif 2025-26 — see the rate for a new season in PIB's official announcement.
**What is custom milling?** — Procured paddy is given to rice mills, which return rice in a fixed ratio — this rice goes into the PDS/distribution.
**Why keep the procurement slip and gate pass safe?** — In a payment enquiry or any dispute, these are your proof of procurement and quantity.
**How can a small farmer get help?** — By connecting with his village-level cooperative society (PACS), he can get group registration and document help.
**What is the full name of HAFED?** — The Haryana State Cooperative Supply and Marketing Federation Limited (The Haryana State Cooperative Supply & Marketing Federation Limited) — the state's apex cooperative institution.

## In the end

Government procurement of paddy is the biggest support for the farmer's income, and HAFED is an important link in it. For the farmer, the real key lies in preparation — portal registration on time, the right moisture in the crop, and matching bank details. And since MSP and figures change every season, always confirm the exact rate and rules of a new season only from the official announcement of HAFED and the Food Supplies Department.
