In Haryana, alongside the traditional PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Society), a new category called **CM-PACS** is now also growing fast. Here it is important to understand clearly that **CM-PACS does not mean "Chief Minister PACS"** — its full name is **Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activity Cooperative Society**, that is, a cooperative society that can carry out many kinds of business activities at the same time. This guide explains the current number of CM-PACS and their administrative structure, based on figures taken from the RCS Haryana live dashboard.

## What is CM-PACS — full name and purpose

**CM-PACS = Comprehensive Multi-Purpose Activity Cooperative Society**. It is **a Haryana state-specific initiative** — that is, this "CM-PACS" name and model is currently run only in Haryana. (We did not find its start date or the official text of the RCS order with the Model Bye-laws in our check of 18 October 2026, so the "25 December 2023" date and memo number in the earlier version have been removed.)

A traditional PACS is mainly limited to agricultural credit and fertilizer-seed distribution, while the scope of a CM-PACS is much larger — it can carry out many activities at the same time, such as running a petrol pump, grain storage (godowns), crop procurement, food processing, digital/CSC services, transport and manpower services. (See in detail: [What is CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-kya-hai) and [CM-PACS Model Bye-laws](/blog/cm-pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-guide).)

## Administrative structure — 32 ARCS circles

In Haryana, registration and supervision of cooperative societies is not done directly from headquarters, but through **32 Assistant Registrar Cooperative Societies (ARCS)** offices, which divide the state geographically. Note that this number (32) is more than Haryana's 22 administrative districts, because some large districts have more than one ARCS office.

### What does the ARCS office do

The ARCS is the district/circle-level backbone of cooperative societies. The registration application of a new society (PACS or CM-PACS) goes to this office; from here an Inspector does the physical verification, and then the registration moves forward. Besides this, the ARCS also handles supervision, audit coordination and compliance of existing societies. So any work related to a society in your area — registration, documents or a complaint — usually starts with the concerned ARCS office. (The full registration process: [CM-PACS registration process](/blog/cm-pacs-registration-process-haryana).)

> 💡 For the exact district/circle-wise number, see the "Circle-wise figures" chart on the RCS Haryana portal, or contact the concerned ARCS office directly — it is not possible to extract a statewide text-form figure from this section of the portal, so this guide presents only statewide total figures.

## How to see the district-wise list

Since this dashboard is live and a full statewide district-wise text figure is not available in one place, the most reliable way to know the CM-PACS/PACS count of a particular district is:

1. Open the "Performance Dashboard" / "Circle-wise figures" on the **RCS Haryana portal** (cooponline.rcsharyana.gov.in).
2. Select your **district/ARCS circle**.
3. The category-wise count of societies for that circle will be shown there.
4. If it is not clear on the portal, ask your **ARCS office** directly — this is the local, official source.

This way you will always get the **current** and correct number for your area, not one from some old date.

## Why are CM-PACS being formed?

The concept of CM-PACS was brought in to increase the reach of cooperative credit and services in areas where there was no traditional PACS so far, or coverage was weak. A traditional PACS was one-dimensional (mainly agricultural credit), due to which many societies were financially weak and ran at a loss. The multi-purpose model of CM-PACS is the answer to this — when a society runs godowns, a petrol pump, procurement and digital services along with credit, it gets many sources of income and becomes self-reliant. This matches the multi-purpose approach of the [PACS model bye-laws](/blog/pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-explained).

## Which activities a CM-PACS can work in

Being "multi-purpose" is the biggest feature of a CM-PACS. A single society, apart from credit, can do many kinds of work — which gives it many sources of income. The activities usually mentioned include:

- **Agricultural inputs-outputs:** sale of fertilizer, seeds, pesticides, and procurement of produce.
- **Grain storage (godowns):** crop storage facility, so that the farmer gets the option to sell at the right time.
- **Crop procurement:** participation in MSP/procurement processes.
- **Petrol pump/fuel:** fuel service in rural areas.
- **Food processing:** value addition to produce.
- **Digital/CSC services:** services like bills, certificates and banking sakhi through a Common Service Centre (CSC).
- **Transport and manpower services:** carriage and other service-based work.

Exactly which activities a particular CM-PACS can do depends on its registered Model Bye-laws and approval — so confirm the list that applies to your society from its bye-laws/ARCS. (See: [CM-PACS Model Bye-laws](/blog/cm-pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-guide).)

## Why CM-PACS matters for the village

A multi-purpose society is useful to the village economy in many ways. First, the farmer and rural member get many services in one place — from credit to storage, procurement and digital services — so they do not have to run around to different places. Second, because the society has many sources of income, it becomes financially strong and self-reliant, not dependent only on loan interest. Third, this creates employment and service centres in the village itself, and money circulates locally. This idea of a "self-reliant village" is behind the CM-PACS model.

## How to read these figures

Keep one thing in mind while understanding the figures in the table above — these are separate categories, not necessarily sub-parts of one another. "752 registered PACS" are traditional credit societies, while "476 CM-PACS" is the new multi-purpose category. "45 women's CM-PACS" are the women-focused societies within those 476. "Total institutions (27,753)" includes every kind of cooperative institution (excluding dairy), and "functional (10,564)" are those that are actively running. That is, each number answers a different question — so do not add or subtract them to draw a new conclusion; read each figure in its own context, and take the exact/latest position from the portal.

## National context — but CM-PACS is Haryana's own initiative

It is important to understand that **CM-PACS is Haryana's own, state-specific initiative.** At the national level too, the Ministry of Cooperation is working in a **separate** direction — such as the plan to form new multi-purpose PACS/dairy/fisheries societies in uncovered panchayats/villages across the country (Union Cabinet, 15 February 2023) and the project to computerise about 63,000 functional PACS (2022-23 to 2026-27). These national initiatives are connected to the idea of multi-purpose and strong PACS, but **the "CM-PACS" name and model are specifically Haryana's** — do not treat it as a synonym of the national scheme. (National context: Ministry of Cooperation, Government of India.)

## CM-PACS vs traditional PACS

| Aspect | Traditional PACS | CM-PACS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main work | Agricultural credit, fertilizer-seed | Multi-purpose (credit + godown + pump + procurement + digital etc.) |
| Income sources | Limited | Many |
| Scope | Agriculture-focused | Many sectors of the rural economy |
| Identity | All-India category | Haryana-specific name/model |

This table shows that CM-PACS is an expanded, more self-reliant form of the traditional PACS — the foundation is the same (a cooperative society), but the scope is much larger.

## Place in the three-tier cooperative credit structure

In Haryana, the cooperative credit structure is three-tier — **PACS/CM-PACS** at the village level, **DCCB (District Central Cooperative Bank)** at the district level, and the apex cooperative bank (HARCO) at the state level. CM-PACS is the lowest link of this structure, directly connected to members, from where ordinary farmers and rural members get credit and services. (In detail: [DCCB–PACS–HARCO credit structure](/blog/dccb-haryana-pacs-harco-bank-credit-structure).)

How many members are needed, which documents, how much time and share amount it takes — take the exact and current information on these from your ARCS/portal, because these conditions can change from time to time.
