# Bank Payment Methods: Cheque, DD, NEFT/RTGS and UPI

Most of a society's money passes through the bank — and there are many ways to pay. Which one should you use when, how does each work, how safe is it, and how does it appear in the books? This article explains all these questions in simple words, with examples.

## Cheque

A cheque is a written instruction to the bank to pay a fixed amount from the account to someone. It is the oldest and most common bank-payment instrument, and in societies large payments are often made by cheque because it leaves a written record.

> ⚠️ The bank balance does not reduce the moment you write a cheque — the entry is made in the books, but the money leaves the bank only when the cheque is *cleared*. This time gap shows up in bank reconciliation (BRS). (See: [Bank reconciliation guide](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide).)

**Cheque precautions:** Always issue an "account payee" cheque so it goes only into the named person's account; write the amount clearly in both words and figures; record the cheque number, date and payee's name in the books; and keep the cheque book safe. Keeping two authorised signatures (joint signatories) on cheques for large payments is a good control.

## Demand Draft (DD)

A DD is a **guaranteed prepaid** instrument issued by the bank — the money is paid to the bank in advance, so the payee is sure it will not bounce. Useful for secure payments (such as to a government department/supplier, or where the payee does not trust a cheque).

> 💡 A cheque can bounce; a DD is prepaid and guaranteed — that is the main difference. The bank charges a small fee to make a DD, but in return you get a full guarantee.

## NEFT / RTGS

These are bank-to-bank electronic transfers:

- **NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer):** the common electronic transfer; settled in batches (may take a little time). Suitable for small-to-medium amounts.
- **RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement):** for large amounts, almost instant settlement. Usually for payments above a minimum amount.

Both need the beneficiary's name, account number and IFSC code. An electronic transfer creates a record automatically (transaction reference), which makes matching easy.

## UPI

UPI is an instant bank-to-bank payment from a mobile — a fast, cheap and convenient way of digital collection from members. Very useful for small and medium payments/receipts, especially when members are far away or cash is to be avoided.

**UPI precautions:** Check the payee's name/UPI ID before paying; record the reference of every UPI transaction in the books; and keep access to the society's UPI/mobile limited and secure.

## Comparison — at a glance

| Method | Feature | When suitable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cheque | Instruction; clearing takes time | Large payments, written record |
| DD | Prepaid, guaranteed | Where trust/guarantee is needed |
| NEFT | Electronic, batch | Small-to-medium amounts |
| RTGS | Electronic, instant | Large amounts |
| UPI | Instant, from mobile | Small payments/collections |

> 🔍 The key point — every method is a **bank transaction**. Whether cheque or UPI, it must be recorded in the bank book so the balance and matching stay correct.

## Which method to choose when

Choosing the right method depends on a few things — how large the amount is, how quickly it is needed, how much guarantee the payee needs, and what kind of record you need. Broadly: cheque (or RTGS) for large and formal payments; DD where a guarantee is needed; NEFT for everyday electronic payments; and UPI for small, instant payments/collections. No single method is "the best" — choose according to the occasion.

## Every payment in the bank book — why and how

Whatever the method, recording every bank payment/receipt in the bank book is compulsory — date, amount, method (cheque no./UPI ref/NEFT ref), and from whom/to whom. If a transaction is missed, the bank book balance will not match the bank statement, and there will be a difference in bank reconciliation (BRS). So along with the convenience of electronic payments, this discipline is necessary — do not forget to record in a hurry. (See: [Cash book vs bank book](/blog/cash-book-vs-bank-book).)

## Safety of digital payments

Electronic payments (NEFT/RTGS/UPI) are fast, but safety is just as important:

- **Check the beneficiary:** Recheck the name/account/IFSC or UPI ID before paying — money sent to the wrong account is hard to get back.
- **Do not share OTP/password:** The bank never asks for an OTP; do not tell anyone.
- **Avoid suspicious links:** Beware of fake payment links/QR codes.
- **Keep access limited:** Access to the society's bank app/UPI only for authorised persons; joint approval is better.
- **Keep records:** Keep the reference and screenshot/receipt of every transaction.

## The life cycle of a cheque — an example

How a cheque works becomes clear with an example. Suppose the society gives a supplier a cheque of ₹50,000 on the 5th:

1. **5th:** Cheque issued; a payment of ₹50,000 recorded in the society's bank book → the bank balance in the books went down.
2. **5th (at the bank):** Nothing has left the bank account yet — the cheque has not cleared.
3. **8th:** The supplier deposits the cheque in their bank; clearing starts.
4. **9th–10th:** The cheque clears; now ₹50,000 actually leaves the bank account.

In between (the 5th to the 10th) there will be a difference of ₹50,000 between the society's bank book and the bank statement — this is an **unpresented cheque**, which is resolved in bank reconciliation (BRS). This is why assuming "the bank went down the moment the cheque was issued" is wrong. (See: [Bank reconciliation guide](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide).)

## Reconciliation of each method

Reconciliation is slightly different for each payment method:

- **Cheque:** Issued cheques are in the books; in the statement once cleared — difference = unpresented cheques.
- **DD:** The money is deducted from the bank when the DD is made, so matching is straightforward.
- **NEFT/RTGS:** Show in the statement almost instantly; match by reference.
- **UPI:** Instant; match every UPI ref with the statement (they are small and many, so take care while recording).

Matching every method's transactions with the bank statement at month-end is the guarantee of a clean bank book.

## Digital collection from members

UPI and electronic payments have made collection from members much easier. Earlier the member had to come to the office and pay cash; now they can send an instalment/fee by UPI from anywhere. The benefits: the risk of handling cash goes down, every collection creates a record automatically, and the recovery percentage improves (the member does not have to come). Just keep the discipline that every digital collection is recorded at once in the right member's account, so who owes how much is always correct. (See: [Loan recovery](/blog/loan-recovery-before-year-end).)

## Rules for operating the bank account

The society's bank account does not belong to any one person — it belongs to the society, and it is operated as per the managing committee's resolution. Every society should have some basic rules:

- **Authorised signatories:** Who can sign cheques/payments should be decided by resolution.
- **Joint signatories:** Two signatures on large payments — full control should not be in one person's hands.
- **Limit:** Extra approval for payments above a fixed amount.
- **When staff change:** When authorised signatories change, inform the bank at once and remove old access.

These rules protect the society's money from the mistake or wrongdoing of any single person. (See: [Internal control in societies](/blog/internal-control-in-cooperatives).)

## An example — digital collection

Suppose 200 members of a credit society pay an instalment every month. Earlier each member came to the office and paid cash — crowds, the risk of handling cash, and many who came late. Now the society starts collecting by UPI: each member sends the instalment from their mobile, and every payment is recorded automatically in their account. The result — cash handling went down, the recovery percentage improved, and month-end matching became easy, because a digital record of every transaction exists. This is the practical benefit of digital payments.

## Documenting every payment

Whatever the method, every payment should have a document behind it — a bill/invoice, the managing committee's approval (for large payments), and proof of payment (cheque no., DD copy, or NEFT/UPI reference). This documentation does three jobs: it justifies the payment (what this money went for), it becomes evidence in audit, and it protects the society in any dispute. The habit of "paid now, bill later" is dangerous — keep the documents along with the payment, at the same time. In a digital system a bill/proof can be attached to every voucher, which makes this even easier.

## Common mistakes

- **Not recording the transaction in the books** → difference in bank reconciliation; record it at once.
- **Treating a cheque as a cash reduction** → the bank reduces only when the cheque clears.
- **Not checking beneficiary details** → money in the wrong account; check first.
- **Sharing OTP/password** → a path to fraud; never tell anyone.
- **Payment without documents** → keep the reference/receipt of every payment.
- **All control in one person's hands** → keep joint approval for large payments.
- **Large payments without approval** → managing committee approval is needed above a fixed limit.
- **Putting off the bill until after payment** → keep documents along with the payment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Difference between NEFT and RTGS?** — NEFT settles in batches (small-to-medium amounts); RTGS is instant, for large amounts.
**Difference between DD and cheque?** — A cheque can bounce; a DD is prepaid and bank-guaranteed.
**Is it necessary to record UPI payments?** — Yes; every UPI receipt/payment is a bank transaction, record it in the bank book.
**Does the bank balance reduce the moment a cheque is issued?** — No; the money leaves the bank when the cheque clears — this time gap shows up in BRS.
**Which method is the best?** — None in particular; choose according to the need for amount, speed, guarantee and record.
**How to keep digital payments safe?** — Check the beneficiary, do not share OTP/password, keep access limited, keep a record of every transaction.
**What controls to keep on large payments?** — Two authorised signatures/joint approval and documentation.
**What is an unpresented cheque?** — A cheque issued but not yet cleared; reduced in the books, not in the bank — resolved in BRS.
**What are the benefits of UPI collection from members?** — The cash risk goes down, a record is created automatically, the recovery percentage improves.
**Who should operate the bank account?** — Authorised signatories decided by the managing committee's resolution; joint signatures on large payments.

In short, the many bank-payment methods give a society speed and convenience — but their real benefit comes only when every transaction is recorded, every payment is safe, and every method is matched regularly. Choose the right method for the right occasion, keep a record of every rupee, and never relax on safety — that is the whole secret of clean, reliable bank accounts.

## Three things to remember
- ✍️ Cheque = instruction (clearing takes time); DD = prepaid guarantee.
- ⚡ NEFT is common, RTGS is large/instant, UPI is instant from mobile.
- 🧾 Record every payment method in the bank book, and send only after checking the beneficiary.

## In the end
Choosing the right payment method and recording each one properly — this is the path to a clean bank book and easy matching. Electronic payments have made societies' work fast and convenient, but along with them discipline (record every transaction) and safety (check the beneficiary, limited access) are just as important. With these habits every rupee of the society stays safe, recorded and matchable.

Remember — whether the method is old (cheque/DD) or new (NEFT/RTGS/UPI), the basic discipline is the same: choose the right method, check the beneficiary, record in the books at once, keep the documents, and match regularly. These five habits keep every rupee of the society safe and accountable.
