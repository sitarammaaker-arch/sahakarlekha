# How to approve a payment and record the voucher in a cooperative society? Easy examples for the secretary, chairman and treasurer

As an example, suppose a supplier has sent a bill of ₹38,000 and is saying on the phone early in the morning, "I need the cheque today itself." The secretary looked at the bill. They ask the chairman, and the answer comes, "Yes, pay it." The treasurer asks, "Whose signatures will be on the voucher?" The accountant asks, "Was the receipt of goods against the bill checked?" And three months later the auditor asks, "Who approved this payment, on what basis?" By then neither any trace of that "yes" remains, nor of anyone's check on the bill.

Payment is the most delicate point for the society's money: once money goes, it is hard to get back. And "approval" often stays verbal, while its trace in the accounts is the most important thing. This article is the **full chain** of a payment for the secretary, chairman, treasurer and accountant: checking, approval, voucher, payment and the record after payment, with three easy examples.

> 📘 **In short:** Every payment should pass through five steps: **(1)** the bill/demand comes, **(2)** it is checked (goods received? rate correct? tax?), **(3)** the designated person approves it, **(4)** the voucher is made and payment is made (**Dr expense/supplier, Cr cash/bank**), **(5)** the bill, approval and proof of payment are kept with the voucher. **The maker and the approver should be different persons** (Maker–Checker). Who approves up to what amount is set by **the bye-laws and the committee's resolution**; I did not find any approval limit for payments in the Haryana Act. But some payments require **the prior approval of the Registrar** (such as purchase or construction of land-building, Section 89).

The full basis of what a voucher is and its types is in [What is a voucher](/blog/what-is-voucher-types-uses-accounting-entry), and the method of filling a voucher is in [Voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide). This article is the **link between them**: the framework for approving payments, and how the voucher is recorded after approval.

## The payment chain: who does what

In a small society several roles are with one person, but **the principle of keeping work divided** stays the same: whoever prepares the payment should not approve it; whoever approves it should not withdraw the money.

| Step | What happens | Usually who | What should be recorded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Demand | Bill/invoice/demand letter comes | Secretary/accountant | Date of receipt of the bill |
| 2. Check | Goods/service received? Rate, quantity, tax, budget head | Secretary + treasurer | Checking signatures on the bill |
| 3. Approval | Designated person/committee as per the amount level | Chairman or managing committee | Approval slip/resolution |
| 4. Voucher and payment | Voucher is made, payment by cheque/bank/cash | Accountant (voucher); treasurer/signatory (payment) | Voucher, cheque number |
| 5. Record | Bill, approval, proof of payment together | Secretary | Voucher file |

A comparison of the roles of these posts is in [Roles of chairman, secretary and treasurer](/blog/office-bearers-chairman-secretary-treasurer), from the treasurer's view in [Treasurer's accounting work](/blog/treasurer-accounting-work-checklist), and from the secretary's view in [Secretary's work](/blog/secretary-work-meetings-records-accounts-audit).

**A basic principle:** Maker–Checker, that is, **the maker and the checker/approver are different persons.** If the person who makes the voucher also approves it and also withdraws the money, there is no one left to catch a mistake or wrongdoing. (Details: [Internal control](/blog/internal-control-in-cooperatives).)

## The approval ladder: who up to what amount

The answer to "who can approve a payment up to how many rupees?" should be in **your bye-laws and the committee's resolution**. I did not find any such fixed limit in the Haryana Act or Rules. So the ladder below is **only an example**: *(the limits are entirely imaginary; set your society's limits by your own resolution)*:

| Amount (imaginary) | Who approves | Additional condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to ₹5,000 | Secretary | Treasurer's check; receipt/bill attached |
| ₹5,001 to ₹50,000 | Chairman (on the secretary's recommendation) | Treasurer's check; signatures of both on the voucher |
| Above ₹50,000 | Managing committee resolution | Resolution number on the voucher; recorded in the minutes |
| Immovable property (land/construction) | Committee **and** prior approval of the Registrar | Section 89 |

Rules of the ladder, which are useful whatever the limits are:

- **It should be in writing:** the limits should be recorded in the bye-laws or a committee resolution, so that no secretary has to depend on a "verbal order".
- **Do not split:** dividing one big payment into small parts to keep it below the limit is serious in the auditor's eyes.
- **The chairman's limit should also be set:** how much the chairman can approve alone should also be delegated by the bye-laws/resolution. (Full topic: [Can the chairman decide alone](/blog/can-chairman-decide-alone-committee-general-body-powers).)
- **It should be bound in a resolution:** how to write the minutes for the committee-resolution row of the ladder above is in [Board meeting minutes](/blog/board-meeting-minutes-format-secretary-sample-checklist) with a sample.

## Laws that directly affect payments

As per the text of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 that I read:

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Land, building and immovable property** | Section 89 | No society can **purchase, acquire, take on lease or sell land, and cannot construct any building or other immovable property**, without **the prior approval of the Registrar** |
| **Writing off dues** | Section 90 | No society can **write off** any loan or amount due or property, fully or partly, without the prior approval of the Registrar |
| **Investment of funds** | Section 85 | A society can invest its funds only in the post office savings bank, trust securities, shares-securities of other cooperative societies, a Registrar-approved bank or other ways set in the Rules |
| **Suspension of resolution** | Section 27 | The Registrar can suspend (for at most 6 months) a committee resolution that is beyond the powers under the Act/Rules/bye-laws or against interest |
| **Conflict of interest** | Rule 30 | An officer should have no direct/indirect interest in the society's contracts, property dealings or other transactions (some exceptions like loans, investment, housing) |
| **Auditor's access** | Section 95(3) | Free access to books, papers, vouchers, stock and property |

**What was not found in the law:** any fixed approval limit for payments, which office-bearer signs for how much, how many signatories on a cheque, or a fixed form for payments. All these come from **the bye-laws, the Registrar's orders, the bank's instructions and the committee's resolution**. (Where the line between "construction" and "repair" is drawn in Section 89, I did not find from the text; for any big repair, ask the Registrar/ARCS.)

> ⚠️ **A caution:** in matters like Sections 89 and 90 the society's own approval is not enough; **the Registrar's *prior* approval** is needed, that is, before the payment or work. Taking approval later or making the payment without mentioning the approval is a serious lapse.

## Checks before payment

Before the voucher for every payment, check these eight things. If any item is "no", stop the payment:

1. **The bill/invoice is genuine and complete:** supplier's name, date, bill number, item-wise amount.
2. **Goods or service received:** goods receipt (GRN) or proof of service delivery, and the signature of the person who checked. (Payment without receiving goods is the most common wrongdoing.)
3. **Rate and arithmetic correct:** the bill rate matches the order/quotation; the total is correct.
4. **Tax:** where applicable, GST is correct and TDS is deducted. ([TDS and 26Q](/blog/tds-and-26q-for-societies), [GST](/blog/gst-for-cooperatives).)
5. **Budget/head:** which head the expense will go to, and whether there is room in that head.
6. **Approval:** the right person/committee as per the amount level; and where the Registrar's prior approval is needed (Sections 89, 90), it has been received.
7. **Conflict of interest:** does any officer have a direct/indirect interest in the supplier (Rule 30)? If so, a separate check. ([Board Resolution guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)
8. **Mode of payment and balance:** cash, cheque or online; enough balance in bank/cash; a big amount is not in cash. ([Payment methods](/blog/bank-payment-methods-cheque-dd-upi), [Cash handling](/blog/cash-handling-and-verification).)

## Three easy examples

*(All amounts and names are imaginary, only to show the process. The ladder is the same imaginary one given above.)*

### Example 1: Small cash expense, ₹1,200 (stationery)

| Step | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Demand | Stationery shop's bill ₹1,200 |
| Check | Goods received, bill correct; treasurer put checking signature on the bill |
| Approval | Secretary, within the limit of up to ₹5,000 |
| Voucher | Payment voucher, from cash (petty cash) |

Entry:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stationery expense | ₹1,200 | — |
| Cash (petty cash) | — | ₹1,200 |

Narration: "Stationery, Bill No. 212 dt. ___, M/s ___ , from petty cash". Bill with the voucher.

### Example 2: Payment to a supplier, ₹38,000 (by cheque)

First the entry for buying goods (when the bill came and goods were received), then for the payment:

| Stage | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goods received, bill recorded (credit) | Purchase | Supplier | ₹38,000 |
| Payment (by cheque) | Supplier | Bank | ₹38,000 |

| Step | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Check | Recorded in the goods receipt register; rate matched the order; treasurer checked |
| Approval | Amount in the ₹5,001–₹50,000 level, chairman approved on the secretary's recommendation |
| Payment | Cheque No. 004517; entry in cheque register; two signatories |
| Voucher | Payment voucher, narration: "Payment to M/s ___ for Bill No. 311 dt. ___, cheque No. 004517" |

**Note:** the payment is debited to the "supplier" account, not to expense, because the expense was already recorded when the goods came. That is the benefit of two stages: every bill and every payment of the supplier shows separately. (Further bank matching of this account: [BRS](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide).)

### Example 3: Big repair, ₹2,40,000 (committee resolution)

| Step | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Demand | Repair of the godown roof; three quotations |
| Check | Treasurer and secretary compared the quotations; conflict of interest check was done |
| Approval | Above ₹50,000, so **managing committee meeting**, resolution No. ___ (maximum limit ₹2,40,000) |
| Registrar? | If this were a new building/construction, prior approval of the Registrar under Section 89. *Confirm the difference between repair and construction with the Registrar/ARCS* |
| Payment | On completion of work, with bill and proof, in instalments |

Entry (on completion of work and arrival of the bill):

| Stage | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repair bill recorded | Repair expense (or added to asset, if capital) | Contractor (supplier) | ₹2,40,000 |
| Payment (bank) | Contractor | Bank | ₹2,40,000 |

Whether a repair is "revenue expense" or "capital expense" is a question of accounting policy, to be decided with your auditor. Write the resolution number and the date of the minutes on the voucher.

## Sample approval slip

In small societies, attaching a simple **payment approval slip** to the voucher is the cheapest and most effective method *(imaginary sample; your society's format may differ)*:

| Item | Filled |
| --- | --- |
| Slip No. / date | MP-0142 / ___ |
| Paid to | M/s ___ (supplier) |
| Subject | Purchase of ___ |
| Amount (in figures, in words) | ₹38,000 (Rupees thirty-eight thousand) |
| Basis (bill/resolution) | Bill No. 311 dt. ___; goods receipt No. ___ |
| Expense head | Purchase |
| Checked (treasurer) | Signature/date |
| Recommended (secretary) | Signature/date |
| **Approved** (chairman/resolution No.) | Signature/date |
| Mode of payment | Cheque No. 004517 |

The strength of this slip is that the auditor gets the answer to "who checked, who approved, on what basis" along with the voucher itself. (What else to attach with the voucher: [Voucher narration and documents](/blog/voucher-narration-and-documents).)

## Record after payment

The payment is made, but the work is not finished. These things should stay together in the voucher file:

- [ ] **Bill** and proof of goods receipt
- [ ] **Approval slip** or copy of resolution (with resolution number)
- [ ] **Voucher** with the correct narration, and signatures on it
- [ ] **Proof of payment:** cheque counterfoil/bank slip; receipt signature for cash
- [ ] In the **cheque register**: cheque date, payee, amount, and later the date of clearing
- [ ] The payment posted in the **supplier account**, and matched with the supplier's statement at month-end
- [ ] **Whether the cheque cleared in the bank or not**, in bank reconciliation (BRS)

And the **payment register:** date of every payment, to whom, how much, which voucher, who approved. This register tells you at a glance how much went out this month and with what approval.

## How approval works in the portal

In SahakarLekha, a payment voucher is made from the **Vouchers** page. For paying a supplier's bill, there is "Pay Supplier" in **Bill-wise Settlement**: when you choose the supplier, their open bills are shown, the amount can be split across the bills (oldest bills first, automatically too), and the voucher is made by choosing cash or bank. (Step by step: [How to pay a supplier (Help)](/help/make-payment).)

**The approval (Maker–Checker) system:** it is **optional** for every society, and the society itself decides in **Society Setup**:

| Option | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Approval on every manual voucher | Every hand-made voucher first stays "Pending" |
| Amount limit (≥ ₹) | Vouchers of that amount or above stay pending |
| Voucher type | Chosen types (such as **payment**, journal, contra) always pending, whatever the amount |

This means one part of the approval ladder above, **"vouchers above this amount or of this type must be checked by someone else"**, can be applied in the portal.

> ⚠️ **An important limit:** this hold in the portal applies only to vouchers made from **the direct entry form of the Vouchers page** (receipt, payment, journal, contra etc.). A voucher made from **Bill-wise Settlement** ("Pay Supplier" / "Receive from Customer") does not come under this hold. So **a payment made through Bill-wise Settlement is not kept pending, even when amount-limit or "payment"-type approval is on.** Vouchers made automatically from the portal's other entries (purchase, sale, salary etc.) are also outside the scope of this hold. If your society makes big supplier payments through Bill-wise Settlement, take their approval **outside the portal, with a written approval slip, before recording the payment**.

**What happens to a pending voucher:**

- **It is not counted in the accounts.** Until approved, a pending voucher has no accounting effect: it is not added to the cash book, ledger and reports.
- **Approval is done from the Voucher Approval page**, where vouchers show in pending, approved and rejected tabs. When rejecting, a **reason** has to be given.
- **Your own voucher cannot be approved:** the portal blocks it saying "self-approval is not allowed", that is, the maker and approver of the voucher must be different users.
- **Permission to approve is set by role.** In the portal's permission table, approval permission is with roles like society admin, secretary, manager, chairman and board member; not with accountant and cashier. "Treasurer" is not a separate role in the portal; it is a post in the society, and which role it gets in the portal is decided by the society. (How to give roles: [How to give user permission (Help)](/help/user-permissions).)
- **After approval the voucher is locked for editing**, so that it is not quietly changed after approval; if there is a mistake, it is cancelled and a new voucher is made.
- **A trace of every approval** (who, when) is recorded in the books' audit trail, and once the financial year is locked, approval also cannot happen.

**Three honest limits** (besides the warning above):

1. The portal **does not itself know** your bye-law limits (chairman up to ₹50,000, committee above it). It only applies what you tell it in Society Setup as amount limit and voucher type. The committee resolution approval and the Registrar's prior approval (Sections 89, 90) are a responsibility outside the portal; keep that paper attached with the voucher.
2. The portal's approval hold **does not apply to Bill-wise Settlement payments** (the warning above): control over approval of supplier payments there rests on your paper approval slip and the checks before payment.
3. When switching approval on, make sure there are **at least two users**: one maker and one approver. If the society has only one user, switching approval on will keep that user's own vouchers pending forever, because self-approval is not allowed.

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant/treasurer:** you can keep the "payment approval slip", voucher and payment register above on paper too, but when there are ten or twenty payments every week, finding "which voucher is stuck for approval with whom" is tiring. In SahakarLekha, **Voucher Approval** shows this very list in one place (for vouchers made from the direct entry form of Vouchers), and as soon as a voucher is approved it posts to the books. The approval decision, checking the bill and setting limits remain your job.

## Common mistakes

1. **Payment on verbal approval.** Later there is no proof of "who said so".
2. **The maker is also the approver.** When Maker–Checker breaks, control breaks.
3. **Payment without receiving goods.** The most common and most costly wrongdoing.
4. **Splitting a big payment into pieces to keep it below the limit.**
5. **Treating the committee's approval as enough in Section 89/90 matters.** The Registrar's *prior* approval is needed.
6. **Ignoring conflict of interest** (Rule 30).
7. **Keeping the bill and approval separately after payment.** The voucher file is incomplete.
8. **Not keeping a cheque register.** No idea which cheque cleared when.
9. **Dr to the wrong account (expense versus supplier).** The supplier's dues do not show correctly.
10. **Running your own system in PACS instead of the prescribed portal's procedure.**

> ⚠️ **For PACS and readers on a prescribed portal:** if your society's payments are made as per a prescribed portal (such as e-PACS), a higher body's or a bank's instructions, then that procedure, limits and forms are final. The ladder, slip and checklist in this article do not replace them; they are only for understanding and for your own records. (Background: [e-PACS and day-end](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).)

## Three things to remember

- 🧾 The chain of every payment has five steps: bill, check, approval, voucher-and-payment (Dr expense/supplier, Cr cash/bank), and a record of bill-approval-payment together. The maker and approver should be different, and approval should be in writing.
- ⚖️ Who approves how much is set in the bye-laws and the committee's resolution (no limit was found in the Act); but purchase-construction of land-building (Section 89) and writing off dues (Section 90) require the Registrar's *prior* approval.
- 🖥️ In the portal approval is optional (every manual voucher, amount limit or voucher type), a pending voucher is not counted in the accounts, you cannot approve your own voucher; but this hold does not apply to Bill-wise Settlement payments, and the committee resolution and the Registrar's approval are your responsibility outside the portal.

## Frequently asked questions

**How to take approval for a payment in a cooperative society?** — Check the bill (goods received, rate correct, tax right), then written approval of the designated person or committee as per the amount level (approval slip or resolution), then the voucher and payment, and finally the bill, approval and proof of payment together in the voucher file.
**How is a payment voucher made?** — In a payment voucher, Dr the expense account (or the supplier, if the purchase is already recorded) and Cr cash or bank; along with date, amount and a clear narration (to whom, for which bill, by which cheque/method).
**Who can approve payments up to what amount?** — This is set by the bye-laws and the committee's resolution. I did not find any fixed approval limit for payments in the Haryana Act or Rules, so keep your society's limits in writing.
**Can the chairman approve a payment alone?** — Only as much as has been delegated to them by the bye-laws or the committee's resolution. I did not find any provision in the Act giving the chairman the right to decide alone. (Details: [Can the chairman decide alone](/blog/can-chairman-decide-alone-committee-general-body-powers).)
**Which payments need the Registrar's approval?** — Under Section 89, purchase, acquisition, lease or sale of land, and construction of a building or other immovable property; under Section 90, writing off any loan or amount due. Both need *prior* approval.
**What to debit when paying a supplier?** — If the purchase entry (Dr purchase, Cr supplier) was made when the bill came, then in the payment Dr supplier and Cr bank/cash. Do not charge the expense again.
**How to set payment approval in the portal?** — Switch approval on in Society Setup: every manual voucher, or an amount limit, or chosen voucher types (such as payment). Such vouchers made from the direct entry form of the Vouchers page stay "Pending" and are approved or rejected from the Voucher Approval page. Note: a voucher made from Bill-wise Settlement ("Pay Supplier") does not come under this hold; take its approval on paper first.
**Is a pending voucher counted in reports?** — No. In the portal a pending voucher has no accounting effect until approved.
**Can the maker of a voucher approve it themselves?** — No. The portal blocks self-approval, so the maker and approver should be different users.
**What if a prescribed portal or the bank has a different instruction?** — Then follow that. This article is for your understanding and your own records, not a replacement for it.

## Finally

Payment approval is not a formal hurdle; it is the answer to the question that will surely be asked one day: "Who gave this money, on what basis, after what check?" A society that has the bill, approval slip and proof of payment with every voucher is not scared by this question. And among a secretary, treasurer and chairman whose work is clearly divided, none of them has to rely on "but I said it verbally".
