PACS are not just institutions that give loans to farmers — they also have hundreds of secretaries, clerks and other employees who run them every day, and whose recruitment, promotion and service conditions are decided by a separate legal framework. This guide explains in full detail, with sources, the "common cadre" and Service Rules system made for PACS employees under the [Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984](/blog/haryana-cooperative-act-1984-rules-1989-guide) — including recruitment, promotion, discipline, pay and dispute settlement.

> 📌 Opening note: service rules, number of posts, age limits, pay and promotion quotas keep being amended from time to time. In this guide, the legal framework (sections, the common-cadre arrangement) is based on the text of the Act, and the specific service conditions on the official PACS Service Rules — but in any real recruitment/service matter, always confirm with the latest notification and your district's Registrar office. Links to the official documents are given in the [Sources](#sources) section below.

## At a glance — how this whole system is built

To understand the service arrangement of PACS employees, three things have to be joined together:

1. **Where the basic power comes from** — Sections 37 and 38 of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984, which give the Registrar the power to create a "common cadre" and make its rules.
2. **Where the actual rules are written** — the **PACS Service Rules** issued by the Registrar (and their amendments), which contain detailed provisions on posts, qualifications, age, recruitment, promotion, discipline and pay.
3. **How it is implemented** — selection committees (chaired by the DRCS/ARCS), final approval by the managing committee, and, in case of a dispute, Registrar arbitration or the appropriate forum.

See these three not separately but as one chain — the Act gives the power, the Service Rules fill in the details, and the selection/managing committee applies it on the ground.

### Who this guide is for

- **PACS secretary/managing committee:** to carry out recruitment, promotion and discipline in a rule-compliant way — so that no decision falls when challenged later.
- **Current and future employees:** to know their rights, promotion path and dispute forum.
- **Members and researchers:** to understand how the arrangement for cooperative employees was built and how it runs.

For every reader there is one basic message — almost nothing here happens "at will"; every step is bound by some rule or section, and knowing that is strength.

## What is a common cadre?

**Section 37(1)** of the Act gives the Registrar the power to direct an apex society to create a shared (common) cadre for the employees — secretaries, managers and other posts — of the central and primary societies linked to it (which include PACS). The aim is that instead of each small PACS deciding the recruitment and service conditions of its employees separately, it works under a standardised, centralised arrangement.

The direct benefit of this is that service conditions, pay scales and discipline rules stay the same across the whole cadre — that is, the secretary of one village's PACS and the secretary of another village's PACS come under the same framework, not each society's own arbitrariness. This increases both transparency and fairness, and saves small societies from the burden of making HR rules separately.

> 💡 "Common cadre" does not mean that every employee can automatically be transferred to every society — its basic meaning is **centralised, standardised regulation** of recruitment and service conditions. Transfers and other details are decided by the relevant Service Rules.

## Why the common cadre was created — a little background

The idea of a common cadre did not come suddenly. An old pain of the cooperative movement has been that thousands of small PACS kept their employees on separate, irregular terms — low pay in some places, favouritism in recruitment in others, no service security somewhere else. This gave neither stability to the employee nor professional, trained staff to the society. The common cadre is the solution to this problem — all employees under one standard, one rule book and one monitoring system.

Its second big aim is **professionalisation**. As the work of PACS has grown — beyond agricultural credit to insurance, CSC services and computerisation — the need for trained, accountable staff has also grown. Centralised recruitment rules ensure that qualified people are chosen through a fair process, and that they get a clear career path (promotion) and service security. This is the foundation of a healthy, sustainable cooperative structure.

## Who decides recruitment and service conditions?

Under **Section 37(2)**, when a common cadre is created, the Registrar — in consultation with the concerned cadre society — makes rules regulating the **recruitment, service conditions and number (strength)** of such employees. Under the proviso to the same sub-section, the Registrar, in consultation with the cadre society, can also **add or remove** a class of employees to or from the common cadre.

In practice, this means that the recruitment and service conditions of posts like PACS secretary are decided not at the will of an individual society, but according to these centralised rules made by the Registrar (PACS Service Rules). That is why an official "PACS (Primary Agriculture Cooperative Societies) Service Rules" document exists in Haryana, issued by the office of the Registrar, Cooperative Societies, Haryana (see [Sources](#sources)).

> ⚖️ Note: this "centralised regulation" does not mean that the Registrar makes every appointment himself. The Registrar makes the rules, but the actual selection and appointment process (next section) happens through the selection committee and the managing committee.

## Annual monitoring — Section 38

Under **Section 38(1)**, every cadre society — as prescribed — must submit an **annual review** of its own working and that of its member societies to the Registrar, in the format fixed by the Registrar, **within six months** of the end of every cooperative year. Under **Section 38(2)**, the Registrar can issue the directions necessary for the efficient running of the cadre society and its member societies — which can also include issues related to staff management.

This annual review arrangement is the "monitoring backbone" of the common cadre — through it, the Registrar gets a picture every year of how the cadre and member societies are running, and can give corrective directions when needed. That is, the common cadre is not just "recruitment rules", but a continuous accountability cycle — rules are made (Section 37), and their compliance is checked every year (Section 38).

## The service security aspect

The service security (job security) of PACS employees has been a separate, often debated subject — because they are not government employees, but not fully unorganised private employees either. The common cadre and Service Rules give them a middle-path security: rule-based appointment, process-based discipline, and a fixed forum for disputes. This makes arbitrary dismissal difficult. Which service-security arrangement applies to which employees, and to what extent, is a detailed subject — see: [Job Security Act and cooperative society employees](/blog/haryana-pacs-job-security-act-eligibility).

## Sanctioned posts in a PACS and the selection structure

According to the official PACS Service Rules, a PACS/PCCS basically has four sanctioned posts — **Manager (Prabandhak), Clerk-cum-Cashier, Salesman and Peon-cum-Chowkidar**. It is not necessary that all posts are sanctioned in every society — it depends on the society's class (Class A/B/C, based on outstanding loans/deposits), according to which the number of sanctioned posts goes up or down.

Recruitment has two separate roles — **selection** and **final appointment**:

- **Manager post:** the selection recommendation is made by a committee chaired by the Deputy Registrar.
- **Clerk-cum-Cashier, Salesman, Peon-cum-Chowkidar:** the selection recommendation is made by a committee chaired by the Assistant Registrar.
- **Final appointment:** in both cases, made by the **Managing Committee**, on the basis of the selection committee's recommendation.

Vacancies are usually advertised in English and Hindi newspapers with at least **30 days' notice**, followed by scrutiny of applications, the selection committee's merit-based recommendation, and finally the managing committee's approval. Keeping a written record of every stage (advertisement, applications, merit list, minutes) is necessary.

> 🔑 This arrangement of "selection with one, appointment with the other" has been made deliberately — so that neither the Registrar machinery alone can impose anyone, nor can the society choose anyone arbitrarily. Recruitment is complete only with the consent of both.

### The role of each post — in brief

The work of the four sanctioned posts is different, and the qualifications for recruitment/promotion are also linked to this role:

- **Manager (Prabandhak):** the executive head of the society — day-to-day running, loan management, records, reporting and compliance. The most responsible post, so the qualifications and selection process are also the strictest (committee chaired by the Deputy Registrar).
- **Clerk-cum-Cashier:** cash transactions, books, vouchers, member records — that is, the backbone of accounting. Honesty and accounting skill matter most here.
- **Salesman:** where the society sells fertilizer-seed/consumer goods, sales, stock and customer service.
- **Peon-cum-Chowkidar:** office support and security of the premises; a lower but necessary support post.

Understanding these roles is important because the promotion channel also runs in their order, and the accountability of each post is different.

## PACS vs CMS — the staffing difference

Although both are cooperative institutions and the service arrangement of both runs on Registrar rules, there is a clear difference in the staffing structure of a PACS and a CMS (cooperative marketing society):

| Aspect | PACS/PCCS | CMS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic sanctioned posts | 4 (Manager, Clerk-cum-Cashier, Salesman, Peon-cum-Chowkidar) | 10 (Manager, Assistant Manager, Accountant, Cashier etc.) |
| Basis of classification | Loans/deposits (Class A/B/C) | Turnover (Category A/B/C/D) |
| Nature of work | Agricultural-credit focused + expansion (CSC etc.) | Marketing/processing, large trading work |

That is why the CMS structure is larger and more specialised — it also has posts like Accountant, Store Keeper and Driver, which are linked to trade and logistics. A detailed comparison of PACS/CMS and the recruitment conditions of CMS are in a separate guide: [PACS/CMS service rules, recruitment and dispute settlement](/blog/haryana-pacs-cms-service-rules-recruitment-guide).

## One recruitment cycle — in order

A PACS recruitment usually runs in this sequence (exact periods as per rules):

1. **Identifying the vacancy:** deciding which post is sanctioned and vacant in the society's class.
2. **Advertisement:** in English and Hindi newspapers with at least 30 days' notice.
3. **Applications and scrutiny:** checking eligibility (age, qualification).
4. **Selection:** for Manager, a committee chaired by the DRCS, and for the rest a committee chaired by the ARCS, makes a merit-based recommendation.
5. **Appointment:** the managing committee gives final approval on the selection recommendation.
6. **Probation and confirmation:** confirmation on satisfactory service after the probation period.

Keeping the document of every stage (advertisement copy, applications, merit list, minutes, appointment letter) is necessary — this becomes the society's strongest defence in any later challenge, as the Gaurav Mehla case showed.

## Age, qualification and retirement

For direct recruitment in a PACS/PCCS, the age limit is generally 18 to 40 years, and the retirement age has usually been kept at 58 years (60 years for some lower posts like Peon-cum-Chowkidar). The educational qualification is different for each post — such as generally graduation and accounting/computer qualification for Manager/Clerk-cum-Cashier, and less for lower posts.

> ⚠️ These age, qualification and retirement limits have been amended from time to time — for the exact conditions, always see the latest PACS Service Rules and their amendments. The figures in this article show the general framework, not the final authority.

## Promotion — the path to move up from within

Every post is not filled only by outside (direct) recruitment — some part is filled by **promotion**, that is, by moving the society's existing employees up to a higher post. This gives experienced employees a chance to move forward and keeps institutional experience intact.

- **Direct recruitment vs promotion quota:** the Service Rules fix this ratio for each post — for example, the Manager post is usually filled partly by direct recruitment and partly by promotion (the exact ratio can change as per the rules/amendments, so see the latest rules).
- **The general channel of promotion:** usually from a lower post upwards — such as beyond clerk/salesman, and up to Manager level on the basis of experience and qualification.
- **Seniority:** promotion usually happens on the basis of seniority or seniority-cum-merit, as the rules decide.
- **Eligibility:** a minimum length of service, the required qualification and a satisfactory service record are necessary for promotion.

> 💡 For promotion, it is necessary to keep the **seniority list** clear and up to date — most promotion disputes arise from a wrong or outdated seniority list. Keep each employee's date of appointment, post and service record accurate.

### Beyond PACS — the promotion exam towards DCCB/bank

Promotion is not limited to within the PACS — some paths also take PACS employees towards the district/state cooperative bank (DCCB/HARCO Bank), often through a prescribed exam/process. This is a separate, detailed subject — for full information see: [The exam for PACS employees to become a DCCB/HARCO Bank clerk](/blog/pacs-employee-dccb-clerk-promotion-haryana-exam).

## Probation, confirmation, discipline and pay

- **Probation:** a new appointment is usually on a fixed probation period; **confirmation** in the post comes only after satisfactory service.
- **Disciplinary action:** action against an employee (such as suspension, withholding of pay, termination of service) cannot be taken without the fixed process — charge sheet, a chance to reply, inquiry and hearing. This is the basic rule of **natural justice**; if it is violated, the action can be quashed by the Registrar/court.
- **Pay and allowances:** pay scales and allowances are decided by the Service Rules/Registrar's approval; the society cannot reduce or increase them at will. Check the exact pay scale from the latest rules/notification.

The aim of all this is the same — fair and rule-compliant treatment of the employee, so that the society does not act arbitrarily and the employee does not stay insecure.

### Disciplinary action — step by step

If action against an employee is necessary, it is done not arbitrarily, but in a fixed, just sequence:

1. **Charge sheet:** the employee is told in writing what the charges are.
2. **A chance to reply:** he gets reasonable time to give an explanation/reply.
3. **Inquiry:** where needed, an inquiry by a neutral inquiry officer, in which the employee can present his side and evidence.
4. **Show cause:** a last chance to the employee before the proposed penalty.
5. **Order:** a reasoned, written decision of the competent authority.
6. **Appeal:** the employee's right to appeal to a higher level as per rules.

The root of this whole sequence is **natural justice** — "no one should be punished without hearing his side". If the society skips this sequence (such as dismissing directly without an inquiry), the action can be quashed by the Registrar/court, even if the charge is true. So following the process protects not just the employee, but the society too.

> ⚠️ Despite the legal framework being clear, there have long been complaints among PACS employees about its implementation, such as pay-scale anomalies — having a common cadre system does not mean every practical dispute automatically gets resolved. Any service dispute should be raised only through the Registrar's office or the [appropriate forum](/blog/haryana-pacs-cms-service-rules-recruitment-guide).

## When there is a procedural lapse in recruitment — the Gaurav Mehla judgment

Sometimes a procedural lapse happens in the recruitment process — such as a mandatory officer not being present at the board meeting for final approval. Does this cancel the whole recruitment? The Supreme Court answered this in **Gaurav Mehla and others vs State of Haryana (2026 INSC 641, 11 June 2026)**.

In this case, some candidates had been appointed under a 2014 recruitment in the Thanesar Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Society (Thanesar CMS); later it was challenged on the ground that the board gave approval in the absence of some mandatory officers. The bench of Justice Sanjay Karol and Justice N. Kotiswar Singh divided the recruitment into three stages — advertisement, selection and final approval — and held that if the advertisement and selection were correct, such a lapse in final approval is **"curable"**, not a ground to cancel the whole recruitment. The court directed the society to call a new board meeting within one month in the presence of all three mandatory officers; if the candidates are found eligible, they are to be reappointed and their past service counted.

> 📘 This judgment gives balance to both sides — an employee's job does not go just because of an administrative lapse, but the society must correct the lapse and complete the due process. In detail: [Gaurav Mehla Supreme Court judgment](/blog/gaurav-mehla-supreme-court-cooperative-recruitment-case).

### The wider impact of this judgment

Gaurav Mehla is not just one society's case — its principle applies to every cooperative recruitment, and it gives a two-way message:

- **For employees:** if you have served for years after a fair advertisement and selection, your job cannot automatically be taken away only because of an administrative lapse like the absence of an officer — the court clearly said that candidates should not be punished for the officers' lapse.
- **For societies/administration:** this does not "forgive" the lapse — the court ordered the lapse to be **corrected**, not ignored. That is, the due process (presence of all mandatory officers) must be completed, just without bringing down the whole recruitment.

The practical lesson is that between "process vs justice", the court chose balance — priority to **substantive** fairness, and not treating a curable procedural lapse as fatal. In future, whenever a cooperative recruitment is challenged only on some technical/procedural ground, this judgment will remain a strong precedent — provided the basic stages of advertisement and selection were clean.

## Link with professionalisation and computerisation

The service arrangement of PACS cannot be seen in isolation today — it is linked to the fast-changing form of PACS. At the national level, computerisation of PACS, a shared ERP, CSC services and new activities are being added, which has also raised the skills expected from employees. Now a PACS secretary should know not just bookkeeping, but also digital transactions, online reporting and managing new services.

For this reason, the importance of educational and computer qualifications in recruitment has grown, and training has become a continuous need. The common cadre and standardised Service Rules help handle this change — because they give a uniform standard and career structure on which training and professional development can rest. A society that keeps its employees trained in new technology is the one that can later take full advantage of opportunities like CSC services and computerisation.

## Promotion — a practical scenario

Suppose a person is recruited as a Clerk-cum-Cashier in a PACS. After some years of satisfactory service and the required qualification, when a promotion-quota vacancy comes up for the Manager post, his promotion can be considered on the basis of seniority/merit — that is, instead of competing with outside direct-recruitment candidates, he gets the benefit of internal promotion.

But this does not happen automatically — for this, (a) there should be a promotion-quota vacancy, (b) he should meet the minimum length of service and qualification, and (c) his service record and seniority should be in his favour. That is why every employee should keep an eye on his seniority position and promotion eligibility, and the society should keep the seniority list fair and up to date. Where these records are clear, promotion is smooth; where they are hazy, there are disputes.

## Where to go for service disputes

For service-related disputes, the general rule is **Section 102(1)** — first arbitration with the Registrar, not directly the civil court (full process: [Where to go if there is a dispute in the society](/blog/cooperative-society-dispute-resolution-haryana)). But there is also a settled exception — if an employee comes within the definition of "workman" under the **Industrial Disputes Act, 1947**, he can also knock on the door of the Labour Court (as decided in *Sonepat Co-operative Sugar Mills Ltd. vs Presiding Officer, Labour Court*, Punjab and Haryana High Court, 1986).

| Situation | General forum |
| --- | --- |
| Most service disputes | Registrar arbitration (Section 102) |
| Employee in the "workman" category | Labour Court (Industrial Disputes Act) as well |
| Fundamental right/writ-worthy matter | High Court (limited) |

### Dispute resolution — the practical sequence

A service dispute usually moves forward in this sequence:

1. **Internal level:** first, an attempt at a solution at the society/managing committee level (appeal, representation).
2. **Registrar arbitration (Section 102):** if not resolved, the dispute goes to the Registrar/nominated arbitrator; this is the main legal route for cooperative disputes.
3. **Labour Court (where applicable):** if the employee is a "workman", the Labour Court under the Industrial Disputes Act as well.
4. **High Court (limited):** the scope of a writ petition is limited — a society does not become a "government body" just by registration, so not every service matter is writ-worthy.

The right forum depends on what the employee's category is and what the nature of the dispute is — choosing the wrong forum wastes both time and effort, so take advice from someone who knows cooperative law when needed.

## Compassionate appointment

If a regular PACS/PCCS employee dies or goes missing during service, the family can get financial assistance or an appointment under the **PACS/PCCS Employees (Compassionate Financial Assistance or Appointment) Policy, 2022**. For CMS there is a separate, detailed 2024 policy — full information including eligibility, order of priority and process: [CMS ex-gratia and compassionate assistance policy](/blog/haryana-cms-ex-gratia-compassionate-policy).

## Employee and society — practical checklist

**For the society (employer):**

- Before recruitment, check that the post is sanctioned in the society's class.
- Advertise in English and Hindi newspapers with 30 days' notice.
- Managing committee approval only after the recommendation of the selection committee (chaired by the DRCS/ARCS).
- Keep a written record and minutes of every stage.
- Keep the seniority list up to date; follow the rule process and natural justice in promotion and discipline.
- The annual review of Section 38 on time (where applicable).

**For the employee:**

- Keep copies of your appointment, post, seniority and service record.
- Check your promotion eligibility and the seniority list from time to time.
- In a service dispute, first Registrar arbitration; if you are a "workman", the Labour Court is also an option.
- If any action is taken without a charge sheet/inquiry/hearing, it can be challenged.

## Common practical complaints — and their right route

Despite the legal framework being clear, there have been some common complaints of PACS employees on the ground. Knowing them is useful for both sides:

- **Pay-scale anomaly:** complaints of different pay for the same work or of old pay scales. The right route — a collective representation at the cadre/Registrar level, not individual discontent.
- **Delay/dispute in promotion:** no promotion despite a vacancy, or a dispute over the seniority list. The right route — a written objection on the seniority list and an appeal as per rules.
- **Irregular appointment/termination:** action without process. The right route — Registrar arbitration (Section 102), or the Labour Court if a "workman".
- **Lack of records:** appointment/service documents not available. The right route — keep a copy of every document (appointment letter, pay slips, seniority list, service record) yourself from the start; later these become the biggest evidence.

The main point — whatever the complaint, raising it at the right forum, with documents, is the key to a solution; not rumours or pressure.

## Common mistakes of the employer (society)

- **Recruiting beyond the sanctioned number** → first check that the post is sanctioned in the society's class.
- **Keeping the advertisement/notice period short** → follow the fixed 30-day notice.
- **Bypassing the selection committee** → the recommendation of a committee with the right chair (DRCS/ARCS) is necessary.
- **Not keeping minutes/records** → every stage should be documented, otherwise the recruitment is weak when challenged.
- **Disciplinary action without inquiry** → follow natural justice.
- **Not keeping the seniority list up to date** → the root of promotion disputes.

## Common misconceptions

- **"A PACS employee is a government employee"** — No; the society is an independent body corporate, and employees are not of the state government but the society's/cadre's own employees.
- **"Every PACS recruits at its own will"** — No; recruitment and service conditions are controlled by the common-cadre Service Rules made by the Registrar, not by an individual society's wishes.
- **"Procedural lapse = whole recruitment cancelled"** — Not necessarily; as per the Gaurav Mehla judgment, a curable lapse in final approval does not bring down the whole recruitment, provided the advertisement and selection were correct.
- **"Every service dispute goes directly to the High Court"** — No; the normal route is Registrar arbitration under Section 102, and in some cases the Labour Court too.
- **"Any new central HR policy automatically replaces the state's rules"** — No; until a formal notification comes at the state level, the existing Service Rules continue to apply.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why was the common cadre created?** — So that the recruitment and service conditions of employees of institutions like PACS/CMS are decided through a standardised, centralised arrangement instead of each society deciding them separately.
**Who recruits the PACS secretary?** — Under the common cadre, the Registrar regulates recruitment as per rules made in consultation with the concerned cadre society; the actual selection is made by the selection committee and the final appointment by the managing committee.
**What is the responsibility of the cadre society?** — Under Section 38, submitting an annual review of its own working and that of its member societies to the Registrar within six months of each financial year.
**How many basic posts are there in a PACS?** — Four: Manager, Clerk-cum-Cashier, Salesman, Peon-cum-Chowkidar (the sanctioned number depends on the society's class).
**How does promotion happen?** — On the basis of the quota and seniority/merit fixed in the Service Rules; some paths also lead towards the DCCB/bank (through a separate exam).
**Who gives the final appointment?** — The Managing Committee, after the selection committee's recommendation.
**What is the retirement age?** — Generally 58 years (60 for some posts); confirm from the latest rules.
**Can an employee be removed without an inquiry?** — No; disciplinary action requires a charge sheet, a reply and an inquiry (natural justice).
**The first step in a service dispute?** — Usually Registrar arbitration under Section 102; the Labour Court too for a "workman".
**The difference in staffing between PACS and CMS?** — A PACS has 4 basic posts, a CMS 10; the CMS structure is larger and specialised (Accountant, Store Keeper etc.).
**Can a PACS employee be promoted into a bank (DCCB)?** — Some paths exist through a prescribed exam/process (see the separate guide).
**Under which section is the common cadre created?** — Section 37; and Section 38 ensures its annual review and monitoring.
**Where to complain about a pay-scale anomaly?** — Through a collective representation at the cadre/Registrar level, with documents.
**How are promotion disputes prevented?** — With a fair and up-to-date seniority list and following the quota as per rules.

## Three things to remember

- 👥 The aim of the common cadre (Section 37) is that the recruitment and service conditions of PACS employees are decided through a centralised, standardised framework instead of each society deciding them separately, which increases both fairness and professionalism.
- 📝 Recruitment happens under rules made by the Registrar (Service Rules); the actual selection is made by the selection committee (DRCS/ARCS) and the final appointment by the managing committee — the roles of both are deliberately separate.
- 📅 Submitting the annual review under Section 38 is mandatory — it is the main part of the common cadre's monitoring arrangement, and also the basis for the Registrar to give corrective directions.

## An employee's service journey — the full cycle at a glance

If you join the whole arrangement from an employee's point of view, the picture becomes clear: **advertisement → application → selection (DRCS/ARCS committee) → appointment (managing committee) → probation → confirmation → regular service → promotion (quota/seniority) → retirement**. Throughout this journey, rules, process and documents go along at every stage — and if there is a dispute anywhere, the route of Registrar arbitration or the appropriate forum stays open. In an unfortunate situation like death/going missing, there is also a compassionate policy for the family.

This "from start to retirement" framework is what the common cadre and Service Rules build together — and this is what gives a PACS employee security, clarity and a career path.

## Conclusion

In Haryana, the service arrangement of PACS employees rests on a clear, centralised framework — Section 37 of the Act gives the power to create a common cadre and make rules, Section 38 ensures annual monitoring, and the PACS Service Rules made under these decide the detailed provisions of recruitment, promotion, discipline and pay. Two principles are the key to the whole system: first, **recruitment and service conditions come from centralised rules, not from an individual society**; and second, **process is necessary, but a curable lapse does not bring down the whole recruitment** (Gaurav Mehla). The lesson for the society — keep every stage as per rules and documented; for the employee — know your records, seniority and rights, and choose the right forum. This knowledge reduces disputes and gives security to both sides.

Finally, one important caution must be repeated — this guide is meant to explain the legal framework and the general arrangement, not to give a final decision on any specific case. Specific numbers like the number of posts, age, promotion quota and pay keep changing with amendments to the Service Rules, and every real recruitment/service dispute has its own circumstances. So before any concrete step — whether recruitment, promotion, disciplinary action or a dispute — confirming with the latest official PACS Service Rules and your district's Registrar office, and taking advice from someone who knows cooperative law when needed, is the safest route. Right information + right process = fewer disputes, more trust — this is the mark of a healthy cooperative institution.
