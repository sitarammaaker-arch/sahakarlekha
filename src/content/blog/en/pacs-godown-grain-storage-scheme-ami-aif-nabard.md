In the grain storage scheme of the cooperative sector, godowns are built at the level of PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies) or other cooperative societies — that is, the village society itself becomes the owner of the godown. If your PACS or CM-PACS is considering a godown/warehouse project, it is necessary to understand this scheme's subsidy, the interest maths and the selection process. This guide brings together the scheme, the sources of money, FCI's hiring assurance, the selection process and practical steps — all in one place.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** All figures have been matched with the Ministry of Cooperation's PIB releases (replies given in Parliament) — see "Sources" below. We have not seen the full text of the scheme's guidelines (guidebook/SOP). Before deciding, confirm the latest conditions with your district's DCDC, the cooperation department or the DCCB.

## What the scheme is

On **31 May 2023** the government approved the "World's Largest Grain Storage Plan in Cooperative Sector", and it was started as a pilot project. The aim is simple — not to keep grain storage limited to big central godowns but to take it to the village level, that is, directly to the PACS, so that the farmer is not forced to sell the crop at throwaway prices right after harvest (distress sale), post-harvest losses fall, and transport cost is saved.

The scheme is not limited to godowns — it also covers facilities at PACS level such as custom hiring centres, processing units, and Fair Price Shops. All this is done by joining together (convergence) schemes of the Government of India that are already running.

In the pilot phase **11 godowns were built in 11 PACS**, creating a total storage capacity of **9,750 metric tonnes**. Position as of 20 January 2026 (PIB, 18 March 2026): **560** cooperative societies identified in the country, DPRs submitted for **426**, and godowns completed in **120** societies with **72,702 metric tonnes** of capacity ready.

> 📍 **Haryana's position (as of 20.01.2026, PIB):** 30 societies identified, DPRs submitted for 11, but not a single godown had been completed by then. Ask your district's DCDC for the latest position.

> 💡 The target of "2 lakh new multipurpose PACS, dairy and fisheries societies" is not of this storage scheme, but of a **separate scheme** approved on 15 February 2023. Do not read the two together.

## Why a godown is beneficial for a PACS

A godown strengthens a PACS in many ways, so it is not just a "building" but an income-earning asset. First, **protection of the farmer-member from distress sale** — he can keep the crop in the godown, take money against it as a pledge (pledge financing), and sell at the right time. Second, **new income for the society** — storage rent and interest on advances given against pledged grain. Third, **trust in membership** — when the society gives members a concrete facility, both its business and membership grow.

## The money maths — several schemes together

According to PIB, the scheme runs by joining schemes such as the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF), Agricultural Marketing Infrastructure (AMI), SMAM and PMFME. The most useful points for a godown:

| Facility | What is available (according to PIB) |
| --- | --- |
| AMI subsidy | Raised from 25% to **33.33%** for PACS (in plain areas ₹875 to ₹2,333 per tonne; in the North-East ₹1,333.33 to ₹2,666 per tonne) |
| AMI construction-cost norm | In plain areas raised from ₹3,000–3,500 to **₹7,000 per tonne**; in the North-East ₹4,000 to ₹8,000 per tonne |
| Margin money | Society's own share **cut from 20% to 10%** |
| Supporting infrastructure | **1/3 additional** of the total admissible subsidy for things like internal roads, weighbridge, boundary wall |
| AIF | **3% interest subvention** on the godown loan; for PACS the repayment period raised from 2+5 to **2+8 years** |
| NABARD's special refinance scheme | Combined with AIF's 3% subvention, **effective interest rate of about 1%** for PACS |

> 💡 The key to the real benefit is using these facilities "together", not "separately" — so get the DPR made from the start keeping AMI, AIF and NABARD refinance in mind. According to the 2023 PIB release, the rate of around 1% is stated for projects up to ₹2 crore.

## FCI's hiring assurance — 9 years

Just building a godown does not ensure earnings — grain must also come in and the godown must stay full. According to PIB (18 March 2026), **the Food Corporation of India (FCI) has agreed to give a uniform 9-year hiring assurance for PACS/cooperative godowns built under the scheme.** After DCDC approval, FCI, NAFED, NCCF, the state food department or another central/state agency issues a **Hiring Assurance Letter**, so that the project can go ahead.

> ⚠️ The PACS can build the godown itself, or through the state's nominated agency/Project Management Consultant (PMC) — but in both cases it is necessary to follow the scheme's **fixed design, logo and quality standards**. Do not compromise on construction quality.

## How a PACS is selected — the role of the DCDC

Not every PACS gets this benefit automatically. The monitoring structure is as follows (PIB, 19 December 2023): at the national level an **Inter-Ministerial Committee (IMC)**, which can change the guidelines of the converged schemes; a **National Level Coordination Committee (NLCC)**, which sets the direction of the whole implementation; at the state level a **State Cooperative Development Committee (SCDC)**; and in every district a **District Cooperative Development Committee (DCDC)**. The pilot is being implemented by **NCDC** with the help of NABARD, FCI, CWC, NABCONS, NBCC and others.

Steps as per the guidebook (SOP) (PIB, 18 March 2026):

1. The state cooperation department identifies capable PACS/societies with enough land.
2. The **DCDC** approves them.
3. FCI/NAFED/NCCF/state food department or another agency issues a Hiring Assurance Letter.
4. A DPR is made according to the site, and the society applies to the **DCCB** for loan and subsidy under schemes like AIF/AMI.
5. The bank examines and sanctions the loan, and construction has to be completed **within six months**.

That is, for the secretary or committee of any PACS, the first practical step is — to find out from your district's DCDC and the cooperation department how far the selection has reached.

## Land conditions

According to PIB, the first step is identifying societies "with enough land". Whether the land must be owned or a lease will also do, and for how many years the lease should be — this condition will be in the scheme's guidebook, whose text we have not seen. So confirm the land position (ownership, lease period, waterlogging) with the DCDC and DCCB beforehand.

## How to keep accounts of godown income

A godown is a new income stream for a PACS, so it needs separate, accurate accounts so that its real earnings and loan repayment are clearly visible. Main heads: storage rent income, advances against pledged grain and interest income on them, godown loan instalments and interest, subsidy received, depreciation of the godown, and maintenance (O&M) expenses. Along with this, a member-wise record of pledged grain (who kept how much, how much advance was taken, when it was withdrawn) is very important — because large quantities of farmers' grain and money are handled here. (See: [Inventory and stock management](/blog/inventory-and-stock-management).)

## Practical steps — for PACS that are considering this

1. **Contact your district's DCDC and the cooperation department** — how far the identification of PACS has reached.
2. **Clear up the land position** — whether owned or on lease, and ask the DCDC/DCCB about the lease condition.
3. **Get the DPR made combining all facilities from the start** — AMI subsidy, AIF interest subvention and NABARD refinance.
4. **Secure a Hiring Assurance Letter** — from FCI or another agency, so that the godown stays full.
5. **Build to the fixed design and quality standards** — keep the six-month deadline in mind.

> 💡 Understand this as separate from HAFED or the state's big godown network — that is a state-level procurement-storage structure, while in this scheme the village society itself becomes the owner and operator.

## Common mistakes

- **Looking at the facilities separately** → only by combining them does the effective interest cost of about 1% come about.
- **Starting without a Hiring Assurance** → if the godown stays empty, the loan instalment becomes a heavy burden.
- **Compromising on construction quality** → the fixed design and standards are necessary.
- **Not contacting the DCDC** → approval comes from the DCDC.
- **Not keeping separate accounts of godown income** → earnings and audit become blurred; keep separate project accounts.
- **Relying on old figures** → progress and conditions change; confirm from the latest PIB or the DCDC.

## Frequently asked questions

**When was this scheme approved?** — The government approved it on 31 May 2023, and it was started as a pilot project.
**How many godowns were built in the pilot?** — 11 godowns in 11 PACS, total capacity 9,750 metric tonnes.
**How much subsidy is there in AMI?** — According to PIB, 33.33% for PACS (earlier 25%), and margin money 10% (earlier 20%).
**Will every PACS get the effective interest rate of about 1%?** — According to PIB, combining NABARD's special refinance scheme and AIF's 3% subvention, the effective rate for PACS comes to about 1%. Whether your PACS will get it depends on project sanction and the bank's appraisal.
**Will FCI hire the godown?** — FCI has agreed to a 9-year hiring assurance for the scheme's godowns; after DCDC approval an agency issues a Hiring Assurance Letter.
**Who does the selection?** — The state cooperation department identifies PACS and the district DCDC approves.
**Where does the loan application go?** — To the DCCB, after the DPR is made.

**What income does a PACS get from a godown?** — Mainly storage rent and interest on advances against pledged grain.

## Three things to remember

- 💰 AMI subsidy (33.33%), margin money 10%, AIF's 3% subvention and NABARD refinance — combined, the effective interest is about 1%.
- 🏗️ There is FCI's 9-year hiring assurance, but a Hiring Assurance Letter and construction to fixed standards are necessary.
- 🏛️ Selection happens through the cooperation department and the DCDC, not automatically.
- 🧾 A godown is a new income-earning asset — keep separate, member-wise and transparent accounts for it.

## Finally

The grain storage scheme of the cooperative sector is a big opportunity for PACS — the village society builds its own godown and becomes the owner, earns new income from rent and interest, and saves the farmer from distress sale. But work is moving slowly (120 godowns completed in the country by January 2026), and the benefit comes only when all four are in place — DCDC approval, a Hiring Assurance Letter, a correct DPR and quality construction. For an interested PACS the first step is today — contacting your district's DCDC.
