# What is NABARD and its role in cooperative societies

When you run a village cooperative society, a PACS or a cooperative bank, the name "NABARD" comes up again and again — in refinance, in inspections, in schemes. This article explains plainly what NABARD is, its history and structure, and how it is connected to your society.

## What NABARD is

NABARD stands for the **National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development**. It was set up on **12 July 1982** by an Act of Parliament. It is the country's **apex development bank** for agriculture and rural development. Its head office is in **Mumbai**, and it is wholly **owned by the Government of India**.

When NABARD was set up, the agricultural credit functions of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and the refinance functions of the then **Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation (ARDC)** were transferred to NABARD — that is, NABARD was created to bring these two streams of rural credit under one roof. This is what makes it the central institution for rural and agricultural finance.

## Why NABARD was created

After Independence, rural credit was scattered across many institutions, and getting institutional credit to the village was a big challenge. The need was felt for an apex institution that could handle coordination, refinance and development of credit for agriculture and rural development together — and NABARD filled that need. Its core aim is **integrated rural development** — that is, not just loans, but also promoting rural infrastructure, cottage industries, handicrafts and other rural economic activities.

## What NABARD does for a society

- **Refinance:** It gives low-cost funds to cooperative banks and Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), which they use to lend to PACS and farmers.
- **Supervision:** It inspects State/District Cooperative Banks and RRBs.
- **Rural infrastructure:** Through RIDF (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund) it funds states for roads, godowns, irrigation and so on.
- **SHG-Bank Linkage:** A large programme to link self-help groups with banks.
- **Computerisation and reform:** Support for computerising PACS and strengthening the cooperative structure.

| --- | --- |
| Funds | Refinance to cooperative banks/RRBs |
| Oversight | Supervision of cooperative banks and RRBs |
| Infrastructure | Rural projects through RIDF |
| Inclusion | SHG-Bank Linkage, micro-finance |

## What refinance actually is

The word "refinance" often causes confusion, so let us understand it with a simple example. When your PACS gives a crop loan to a farmer, the money for it comes from the District Central Cooperative Bank (DCCB). The DCCB does not always have all of this money of its own — it borrows part of it from NABARD (at a low interest rate). This is "refinance" — that is, "lending to the lenders." In this way NABARD's money reaches the ground by the route DCCB → PACS → farmer, even though NABARD itself does not deal directly with the farmer. This is why NABARD does not lend directly to the farmer, yet it is present at the root of every farmer's credit.

## NABARD and the cooperative credit structure

NABARD's deepest link is with the three-tier cooperative credit structure — **PACS** in the village, the **DCCB** in the district, and the **State Cooperative Bank (StCB)** in the state. NABARD supports this whole structure in two ways: **refinance** (funds) and **supervision** (oversight). That is, it not only puts money into this structure, it also checks that the structure runs in a healthy way and according to the rules. That is why anyone running a PACS or a cooperative bank needs to understand NABARD's standards and reporting expectations. (See: [The three-tier cooperative structure](/blog/cooperative-society-tiers-primary-central-state-apex).)

## NABARD's three big roles

All of NABARD's work can broadly be understood as three roles:

1. **Financial role:** Refinance — funds to cooperative banks, RRBs and other institutions, so that they can lend to farmers/societies.
2. **Developmental role:** Rural infrastructure (RIDF), SHG-Bank Linkage, skill development, and strengthening cooperative institutions.
3. **Supervisory role:** Inspection of State/District Cooperative Banks and RRBs, so that the rural credit system stays healthy.

Together these three make NABARD not just a "bank" but an all-round institution for rural development — one that gives funds, does development work, and also keeps watch.

## NABARD's main programmes

NABARD reaches the village through many programmes. Some main streams:

- **RIDF (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund):** Funds to states for rural projects such as roads, bridges, irrigation and godowns.
- **SHG-Bank Linkage:** Linking self-help groups (especially women's groups) with banking — a major channel of financial inclusion.
- **PACS computerisation and strengthening:** Making the cooperative credit structure modern and transparent.
- **Storage and infrastructure finance:** Support for godowns, cold chains and agricultural infrastructure.
- **Skills and capacity building:** Strengthening rural entrepreneurship and cooperative management.

The details and eligibility of these programmes change from time to time — so before taking the benefit of any scheme, confirm the current guidelines with NABARD/the bank concerned.

## How a society connects with NABARD

An ordinary PACS/cooperative society does not deal directly with NABARD — it connects through its District/State Cooperative Bank. Still, a few points are useful for the society: keep your relationship and reporting with your DCCB/StCB in order (because that is where the refinance comes from); get information on NABARD-supported schemes (SHG linkage, infrastructure, computerisation) from your bank/department; and keep your accounts, recovery and NPA position healthy, because these form the basis of further support. In other words, NABARD's benefit reaches every well-run society indirectly, but in a real way.

## Link with accounting

Societies connected with NABARD often have to submit reports/returns in a set format and meet the conditions of refinance. That is why clean books and timely reports are essential. Especially for cooperative banks, NABARD supervision means their accounts, recovery and NPA position must be accurate and transparent. A society/bank whose accounts are clean finds it easier to get refinance and further support. (See: [NABARD and federation returns](/blog/nabard-and-federation-returns) and [KCC and crop loan accounting](/blog/kcc-crop-loan-accounting).)

## An example — how money reaches the village

Let us see the whole chain with an example. Suppose a farmer needs a crop loan of ₹50,000:

1. The farmer asks for a loan from the village **PACS**.
2. The PACS has limited capital of its own, so it takes funds from its **DCCB (District Central Cooperative Bank)**.
3. The DCCB raises part of this from **NABARD refinance** (at a low interest rate).
4. In this way NABARD's low-cost funds reach the farmer through DCCB → PACS → farmer.

This example makes it clear that NABARD, while staying "behind the curtain", makes the farmer's credit cheaper and possible. This indirect but decisive role is what makes NABARD the backbone of rural finance. (See: [KCC crop loan accounting](/blog/kcc-crop-loan-accounting).)

## A role that has grown over time

Since it was set up (1982), NABARD's role has not stayed limited to refinance — it has spread to financial inclusion (SHG linkage), rural infrastructure (RIDF), climate and sustainable agriculture, and recently to cooperative reform (such as PACS computerisation). That is, NABARD has kept adapting itself to the changing needs of rural India. From a society's point of view this means — NABARD is a partner not only for funds but also for modernisation and strengthening.

## Common misconceptions

- **"NABARD lends directly to farmers"** → No; it gives refinance to banks/societies.
- **"NABARD and NCDC are the same"** → No; NABARD is focused on banking/refinance (Ministry of Finance), NCDC on cooperative project finance (Ministry of Cooperation). (See: [The role of NCDC](/blog/ncdc-role-in-cooperatives).)
- **"NABARD only gives loans"** → No; it also does supervision, infrastructure (RIDF), SHG linkage and reform.
- **"NABARD and RBI are the same"** → No; RBI looks after overall banking regulation, NABARD is the apex development bank for agricultural and rural credit.
- **"An ordinary society borrows directly from NABARD"** → No; it connects through its District/State Cooperative Bank.

## NABARD, RBI and cooperative banks — who does what

There are many institutions in rural credit, so confusion is natural. The simple difference:

- **RBI (Reserve Bank):** The country's central bank — monetary policy and overall banking regulation. Some regulatory aspects of cooperative banks also fall within its scope.
- **NABARD:** The apex bank for agricultural and rural development — refinance, supervision of rural banks, and development.
- **State/District Cooperative Banks (StCB/DCCB):** The links that actually carry loans to the village.
- **PACS:** The village-level society dealing directly with the farmer.

That is, RBI looks after overall regulation, NABARD gives funds and direction to rural credit, and cooperative banks/PACS carry it to the farmer — each has its own role.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does NABARD lend directly to farmers?** — Generally no; it gives refinance through banks/societies.
**Who does NABARD come under?** — The Government of India (Ministry of Finance); it works together with RBI, and is wholly owned by the government.
**What is the link between PACS and NABARD?** — PACS gets funds from the District/State Cooperative Bank, and NABARD gives refinance to that bank.
**When was NABARD set up?** — On 12 July 1982, by an Act of Parliament; head office in Mumbai.
**What is refinance?** — "Lending to the lenders" — NABARD gives funds to banks, which in turn lend to farmers/societies.
**What is RIDF?** — The Rural Infrastructure Development Fund, through which NABARD funds rural projects such as roads, godowns and irrigation.
**Difference between NABARD and NCDC?** — NABARD is focused on banking/refinance (Ministry of Finance); NCDC on cooperative project finance (Ministry of Cooperation).
**What are NABARD's three roles?** — Financial (refinance), developmental (infrastructure/SHG), and supervisory (bank inspection).
**What does NABARD expect from a society?** — Clean accounts, good recovery, low NPA and correct reports on time — this is the basis of refinance/support.
**Difference between RBI and NABARD?** — RBI does overall banking regulation; NABARD does refinance, supervision and development for agricultural and rural credit.
**Does an ordinary society connect directly with NABARD?** — Generally no; it connects through its District/State Cooperative Bank.
**Through which programmes does NABARD reach the village?** — RIDF (infrastructure), SHG-Bank Linkage, PACS computerisation, storage finance and capacity building.
**Who owns NABARD?** — It is wholly owned by the Government of India.

## Three things to remember
- 🏦 NABARD is the apex bank for agricultural and rural development (set up 12 July 1982, Mumbai).
- 🔁 It gives **refinance** to banks/societies, not directly to farmers — but it is present at the root of every farmer's credit.
- 🧾 Clean books and transparent accounts are needed for the conditions of refinance and reporting — this is the key to further support.

## In the end
NABARD is the backbone of India's rural and agricultural finance — an apex institution that, without dealing directly with the farmer, is present at the root of every farmer's credit. Through refinance, supervision, infrastructure and financial inclusion — these four — it both funds the cooperative credit structure and keeps it healthy. For anyone running a PACS or a cooperative bank, this much is enough to understand: the most direct way to win NABARD's support and trust is — transparent accounts, good recovery, and correct reports on time.

In short, the simplest way to remember NABARD is — it is the "bankers' bank" for rural and agricultural credit. It does not go directly to the farmer, but it is present at the root of every low-cost instalment; it gives funds, builds infrastructure, and keeps watch too. For anyone running a society or a cooperative bank, there is just one mantra for a better relationship with it — keep your own house (accounts, recovery, reports) clean, and the rest of the support becomes easy to get.
