# Opening Balances: The Right Start for a New Society or a New Year

Whether you are putting the society's accounts on computer for the first time, or starting a new financial year — everything rests on one thing: **correct opening balances**. One slip here spoils the Balance Sheet for the whole year and follows you until year-end. This article makes it easy, step by step, with examples.

## What an opening balance is

It is the **starting balance** that already existed in each account when work begins — such as cash, bank, stock, furniture and assets (these are the debit side), and members' share capital, reserve fund, loans taken and member deposits (these are the credit side). In other words, the opening balance is the society's "starting picture" — how much was already in which account.

It is important to understand that an opening balance is not a transaction but a **starting position**. So it is recorded not like an ordinary voucher, but as the starting balance of each account.

## The most important rule: Dr = Cr

The **total debit = total credit** of all accounts' opening balances must be equal. If they are not equal, the Balance Sheet will not tally from day one — and will not tally all year.

> 📘 **Assets = Liabilities + Capital** — this accounting equation must hold at the opening too. (See: [The accounting equation](/blog/accounting-equation-explained).)

The reason is simple: in the double-entry system, behind every asset there is some source (capital or a liability). So the total of assets (Dr) equals the total of capital and liabilities (Cr) — both are equal.

## Typical opening balances (example)

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash | ₹20,000 | — |
| Bank | ₹1,50,000 | — |
| Stock | ₹80,000 | — |
| Furniture | ₹50,000 | — |
| Share capital | — | ₹2,00,000 |
| Reserve fund | — | ₹40,000 |
| Member deposits | — | ₹60,000 |
| **Total** | **₹3,00,000** | **₹3,00,000** |

Dr = Cr → a correct start. Note that here the assets (cash + bank + stock + furniture = ₹3,00,000) exactly equal capital and liabilities (share + reserve + deposits = ₹3,00,000) — this balance is needed in every opening.

## What goes on the debit side, what on the credit side

The most common confusion when entering openings is which account goes on which side. A simple rule:

- **Debit (Dr) side:** what the society has — cash, bank balance, stock, furniture/equipment, building/land, loans given (which are to come back), dues to be recovered.
- **Credit (Cr) side:** what the society owes / the sources — share capital, reserve and other funds, member deposits, loans taken, dues to be paid.

One way to remember: "What is ours or is to come to us → Dr; where it came from or what we have to pay → Cr."

## Two situations

Opening balances have to be entered on two occasions, and the method differs in each:

- **New society / going digital for the first time:** enter each account's balance here from the old registers or the last final accounts (Balance Sheet). That is, the position just before the day you start going digital.
- **New financial year:** last year's **closing balance** is this year's opening — it should come automatically, with no need to enter it again by hand. In good software, the moment the year changes, last year's balances become next year's openings. (See: [Year-end and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).)

> ⚠️ The most common root of "Why isn't the Balance Sheet tallying?" — a wrong or incomplete opening balance. Check here first. (See: [10 common mistakes](/blog/common-accounting-mistakes).)

## Where to get the opening balances

When going digital for the first time, the sources of opening balances are:

1. **The last final Balance Sheet:** the most reliable source — it has the exact balance of every account.
2. **Old books/registers:** where there are separate accounts (cash book, bank book, member register), take the balances from them.
3. **Bank statement:** confirm the bank balance from the statement.
4. **Physical count:** count the actual cash and stock and match.

Take the figures from these sources, make a table, tally Dr = Cr, and then enter them in the software.

## A step-by-step example

Suppose a society is going digital from 1 April. The secretary copies each account's balance from last year's Balance Sheet onto a sheet of paper: cash ₹20,000, bank ₹1,50,000, stock ₹80,000, furniture ₹50,000 (total Dr ₹3,00,000); share capital ₹2,00,000, reserve ₹40,000, member deposits ₹60,000 (total Cr ₹3,00,000). Dr = Cr tallied → he enters these in the software as opening balances and confirms by taking out the Trial Balance. Now the society's accounts stand on the right foundation, and every report will tally all year.

## Care with some special accounts

Some accounts need special care at the opening:

- **Loans given (to members):** this is the society's asset (it is to come back) → Dr side. Enter each member's outstanding principal correctly.
- **Loans taken (from the bank/apex institution):** this is a liability → Cr side; enter the outstanding principal.
- **Assets (furniture/building):** enter the net value (book value) after deducting depreciation — not the original cost. (See: [Depreciation](/blog/depreciation-explained).)
- **Reserve and other funds:** a separate opening balance for each fund (reserve, building fund, education fund etc.) → Cr side.
- **Member deposits (with interest):** principal in the opening; if there is accrued interest outstanding, make proper provision for it too.
- **Dues to be recovered/paid:** what is to be recovered → Dr; what is to be paid → Cr.

Entering these correctly is essential, otherwise the picture of assets/liabilities will be wrong.

## Going digital in the middle of the year

The digital start need not be from the first day of the year — many societies start in mid-year. In that case there are two ways:

1. **Enter from the start of the year:** treat the previous year-end balances as the opening, then enter all transactions from that date until now. This makes the whole year's reports correct — this is the better way.
2. **Enter from the start date:** treat the position on the day you start going digital as the opening. This is easier but does not give detailed reports for the early part of that year.

If possible, follow the first way, so that the whole financial year's accounts stay in one place.

## How to find an imbalance (Dr ≠ Cr)

If the opening Dr and Cr are not tallying, don't panic — check in order:

1. **Add up the totals again** — a simple addition error is the most common.
2. **Was an account left out?** — is the difference equal to one missed account?
3. **Wrong side?** — if the difference is exactly double some balance, it is probably on the wrong side (Cr instead of Dr).
4. **Match with the source** — match the balance of every big account with the last Balance Sheet.

Example: if the difference is exactly ₹40,000 and you forgot to enter the reserve fund of ₹40,000, that is the root. In this way the amount of the difference often points to the mistake itself.

## Closing → opening in the new year

In a new financial year there should be no need to enter openings by hand. The right way is that after closing last year (year-end closing), each account's closing balance automatically becomes next year's opening. That is why doing the year-end closing correctly matters — whatever closing is wrong will go into next year as the opening. In good software this whole process is automatic, which keeps continuity year after year and saves both double work and errors. (See: [Year-end and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).)

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving Dr ≠ Cr** → the Balance Sheet will never tally; balance it here first.
- **Missing an account** → every asset/liability must be included; if even one is missed, a difference will appear.
- **Entering on the wrong side** → putting an asset in Cr or a liability in Dr; remember the rule above.
- **Entering again by hand in the new year** → the new year's opening comes automatically from the previous closing.
- **Not matching with the source** → always confirm against the actual position of cash/bank/stock.
- **Entering figures by guesswork** → always take them from the last Balance Sheet/registers, not by estimate.
- **Entering assets at original cost** → enter net value after depreciation, otherwise assets will look inflated.
- **Not matching with audited figures** → the opening must always match the last audited Balance Sheet, otherwise the next audit will raise questions.

## Opening balances and audit

Opening balances are closely tied to audit. When a society goes digital for the first time, its opening balances should match the **last audited Balance Sheet** — because that is the society's official, certified financial position. If the opening balances differ from the audited figures, the next audit will raise questions and reconciliation will be difficult. So when entering openings, always take the last audited Balance Sheet as the base, not estimates or incomplete registers. Where there is a difference between the audited figures and the actual position (such as a different physical count of cash/stock), first resolve it with the auditor/managing committee, then enter the correct value. This way the opening balances keep continuity with the society's official record.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an opening balance?** — The starting balance of each account that already existed when work began (cash, bank, stock, share capital etc.).
**Why is Dr = Cr necessary?** — In double entry, total assets = total capital + liabilities; if they are not equal, the Balance Sheet will not tally.
**Where do we get opening balances from?** — From the last final Balance Sheet, old registers, bank statements and physical count.
**How does the opening come in a new financial year?** — Last year's closing balance is next year's opening; automatic in good software.
**Which account goes in Dr, which in Cr?** — What the society has/is to receive → Dr; what is a source/is to be paid → Cr.
**If the Balance Sheet doesn't tally, what to check first?** — The opening balances first — this is the most common root.
**Is an opening balance a voucher?** — No; it is a starting position, recorded as the starting balance of each account.
**Assets at original cost or after depreciation at the opening?** — The net value (book value) after depreciation, not the original cost.
**What should opening balances be matched with?** — The last audited Balance Sheet; that is the society's official, certified position.
**What if we go digital in mid-year?** — If possible, enter from the start of the year (previous closing = opening, then transactions until now), so the whole year's reports are correct.

Always remember one thing — opening balances are the foundation of the society's entire accounts. If the foundation is right, the whole building above (every report, every Balance Sheet) stands right; if one brick in the foundation is crooked, cracks show all year. So patience and care at this one step is the most worthwhile investment.

## Three things to remember
- ⚖️ Always tally Dr = Cr in the opening.
- 📋 The correct balance for every account — none left out, entered on the right side.
- 🔁 In the new year the opening comes automatically (from the closing) — don't enter it again.

## In closing
Correct opening balances = correct accounts all year. It takes a little effort at the start — copying figures from the last Balance Sheet, entering them on the right side, tallying Dr = Cr — but once it is right, everything keeps tallying on its own, and the Balance Sheet stays balanced every day. So don't rush; enter the openings patiently, matched with the source — this is the foundation of correct accounts for the whole year.

In short, there are three golden rules for entering opening balances — **include every account, enter it on the right side (Dr/Cr), and tally total Dr = total Cr.** The source should always be the last audited Balance Sheet, not an estimate. And in the new year it should come automatically from the previous closing — no need to enter it again. A society that follows these rules has balanced accounts from day one, and every report tallies with confidence all year.
