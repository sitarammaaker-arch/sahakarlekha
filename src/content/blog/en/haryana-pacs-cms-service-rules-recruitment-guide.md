Who recruits the clerk-cum-cashier in a PACS? Through which committee is the manager of a CMS selected? If there is a procedural lapse in an appointment, is the whole recruitment cancelled? What is the promotion quota? And if an employee has a service dispute, where should they go — to the Registrar, the Labour Court or straight to the High Court? This guide brings together in one place, with sources, the whole framework of service rules, recruitment authority, promotion and dispute resolution in Haryana's PACS and CMS.

> 📌 Opening note: service rules, number of posts, age limits, pay and quotas are amended from time to time. The specific figures/sections in this guide are based on the official documents given in the [Sources](#sources) section below — but in any actual recruitment/service matter, always confirm with your district's Assistant/Deputy Registrar office and the latest notification.

## A three-level legal framework

The service conditions of PACS/CMS employees are set not by one but by three levels of documents — the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 (the basic legal framework), the Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules 1989 (which, through **Rule 29**, make it mandatory for every society to have Registrar-approved model service rules), and above these, special **Staff Service Rules** made for each type of society — the rules in force since 2014 for PACS/PCCS (and later amendments), and the 2003 Staff Service Rules for CMS (and later amendments).

It is important to understand these "three layers", because the answer to any question is found in one of these layers:

| Layer | Document | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic law | Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 | Powers, authorities, the dispute-resolution framework |
| Rules | Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules, 1989 | Mandatory model service rules (Rule 29), recruitment roles |
| Service rules | PACS/PCCS 2014 · CMS 2003 (+ amendments) | Posts, qualifications, age, recruitment/promotion, discipline |

> ⚠️ One more important limit — under **Rule 27(i)**, no salaried employee can become a member of the Managing Committee (there are some exceptions for producer societies). That is, the roles of employee and governance have been deliberately kept separate.

## How many posts are sanctioned

PACS/PCCS have **only 4 sanctioned posts** — Manager (Prabandhak), Clerk-cum-Cashier, Salesman and Peon-cum-Chowkidar. The CMS structure is bigger — **10 sanctioned posts** — Manager, Assistant Manager, Accountant, Cashier/Assistant Accountant, Store Keeper, Salesman-cum-Clerk, Peon-cum-Chowkidar, Truck Cleaner, Driver and Mistri (mechanic).

> 💡 The "Manager" post of a CM-PACS is completely different from these — it is not filled by outside recruitment; this post can be given only to a member of the society (see: [What is CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-kya-hai)).

## Number of sanctioned posts by size of the society

Not all posts are automatically sanctioned in every society — it depends on the society's size (loans/deposits or turnover):

| Society | Basis of classification | Limit of sanctioned posts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PACS/PCCS | Class A/B/C based on outstanding loans/deposits | Up to 7 posts for Class A, 6 for Class B, 4 for Class C |
| CMS | Category A/B/C/D based on annual turnover | Up to 14 posts for Category A, decreasing down to 4 for D |

The practical meaning is that a small PACS may have only the manager and one or two other posts sanctioned, while a large society with more business may have a full team. So before making any new recruitment, it is necessary to check whether that post is sanctioned at all in your society's class — recruitment beyond the sanctioned number is treated as illegal and becomes an audit/Registrar objection.

## Who recruits — selection vs final appointment

The recruitment process has two separate roles — **selection** and **final appointment** — and the two are with different authorities:

- **Manager post**: the selection recommendation is made by a committee chaired by the Deputy Registrar.
- **Clerk-cum-Cashier, Salesman, Peon-cum-Chowkidar**: the selection recommendation is made by a committee chaired by the Assistant Registrar.
- **Final appointment** — in both cases it is always made by the **Managing Committee**, based on the recommendation of the selection committee (Rule 10, Rule 11).

This "selection with one, appointment with the other" arrangement has been made deliberately — so that neither the Registrar's machinery alone can impose someone, nor the society can pick someone arbitrarily. Recruitment is completed only with the agreement of both, which gives transparency and balance.

### The practical recruitment process

As per the official PACS/PCCS rules, vacancies are advertised in English and Hindi newspapers giving at least **30 days' notice**; then scrutiny of applications, the selection committee's recommendation, and finally the Managing Committee's approval. It is necessary to keep a written record of every stage (advertisement, applications, merit list, minutes) — this is what becomes the defence later in any dispute or audit.

In CMS there is one more condition — under **Rule 3**, added to the original 2003 rules by the **amendment of 31 January 2011**, the presence of three officers — the Assistant Registrar, the Inspector Cooperative Societies and the District Manager HAFED — is mandatory at the board meeting that approves the appointment.

## When an officer is absent from the meeting — the Gaurav Mehla case

This three-officer condition has often caused disputes in practice — if one of these officers is not present at a board meeting, will the whole recruitment be treated as cancelled? The Supreme Court answered this in **Gaurav Mehla & Ors. v. State of Haryana (2026 INSC 641)**.

**Summary of the case:** Under a recruitment started in 2014 in the Thanesar Cooperative Marketing-cum-Processing Society, some candidates were appointed as Clerk-cum-Salesman and Peon-cum-Chowkidar. Vacancies were advertised, interviews were held, and the board approved the appointments. Later these appointments were challenged on the ground that the board had given approval in the absence of some officers mandatory under Rule 3.

**Judgment:** On 11 June 2026, the bench of Justice Sanjay Karol and Justice N. Kotiswar Singh divided the recruitment process into three stages — advertisement, selection and final approval. The court held that if the advertisement and selection stages were done properly, a procedural lapse such as an officer's absence only at the final-approval meeting will be treated as **"curable"**, and not a ground to cancel the whole recruitment.

> 📘 The court's reasoning was clear — candidates should not suffer for the lapses of administrative officers. Reversing the Punjab and Haryana High Court's judgment, the court directed the society to call a fresh board meeting within one month in the presence of all three mandatory officers (Assistant Registrar, Inspector and District Manager HAFED). If on reconsideration the candidates are found eligible and not disqualified, they are to be appointed again, and their past service (of more than a decade) is to be counted for all purposes.

**What this means:** This judgment gives balance to both sides — an employee does not lose their job merely because of an administrative lapse (curable irregularity), but the society must correct the lapse and complete the due process. That is, "process is necessary, but one lapse in the process is not the end". (This case is so important that a separate detailed analysis is also available — see: [Gaurav Mehla Supreme Court judgment](/blog/gaurav-mehla-supreme-court-cooperative-recruitment-case).)

## Age and qualifications

For direct recruitment in PACS/PCCS, the age limit is generally 18 to 40 years, and the retirement age is set at 58 years (60 years for Peon-cum-Chowkidar). The educational qualification is set separately for each post — for example, generally graduation and a computer/accounts qualification for Manager/Clerk-cum-Cashier, and less for lower posts. For exact conditions it is necessary to see the relevant Staff Service Rules and their amendments, because these have changed from time to time.

## Promotion — the route to grow from within

Not every post is filled only by outside (direct) recruitment — a part is filled by **promotion**, that is, by moving existing employees of the society up to a higher post. This gives experienced employees a chance to move ahead and keeps institutional experience intact.

- **Direct recruitment vs promotion quota:** the Staff Service Rules set this ratio for each post — for example, for the Manager post usually about 70% by direct recruitment and 30% by promotion (the exact ratio can change as per rules/amendments, so see the latest rules).
- **Seniority:** promotion is usually on the basis of seniority and/or seniority-cum-merit, as the rules decide.
- **Eligibility:** promotion requires a minimum length of service, the required qualification and a satisfactory service record.

> 💡 For promotion it is necessary to keep the seniority list clear and up to date — most promotion disputes arise from a wrong or outdated seniority list. Keep every employee's date of appointment, post and service record accurate.

## Probation, confirmation and discipline

- **Probation:** a new appointment is usually on a fixed probation period; **confirmation** on the post comes only after satisfactory service.
- **Disciplinary action:** action against an employee (such as suspension, termination) cannot be taken without the set procedure — charge-sheet, chance to reply, inquiry and hearing. Following "natural justice" is necessary, otherwise the action may be set aside by the court/Registrar.
- **Pay and allowances:** pay scales and allowances are set by the rules/Registrar's approval; the society cannot cut or raise them at will. Check the exact pay scale in the latest rules/notification.

The purpose of all this is the same — fair and rule-based treatment of the employee, so that neither the society acts arbitrarily nor the employee stays insecure.

## Common cadre and centralised service rules

**Section 37** of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act gives the Registrar the power to direct an apex society to form a shared "common cadre" for the employees of its member central and **primary societies — including PACS**. Under **Section 37(2)**, the Registrar, in consultation with the cadre society, makes rules regulating the recruitment, service conditions and number of such employees (and under the proviso can add/remove any class to/from the cadre). This is why the recruitment and service conditions of PACS employees are set by the official **PACS Service Rules** issued by the Registrar — that is, by a centralised, standardised framework — and not by each society's own arbitrary rules.

> ✅ Note: this "centralised regulation" does not mean that the Registrar himself makes every appointment — the Registrar makes the rules, but the actual selection is made by the selection committee (DRCS/ARCS) and the final appointment by the Managing Committee. Transfers and other details are set by the relevant Service Rules. For the full picture of this common-cadre arrangement and the annual review under Section 38, see: [PACS Service Rules: recruitment, promotion and common cadre](/blog/pacs-service-rules-haryana-recruitment-promotion).

## Government-appointed Managing Director in large societies

If the government's share capital in a large society or federation is ₹10 lakh or more, then under **Section 31** the Registrar/government can appoint a Managing Director for that society — usually an IAS/HCS officer or a Class I/II officer of the Cooperation Department. This is a special situation, separate from the normal PACS/CMS recruitment process, and does not apply to ordinary staff recruitment.

## Where to take disputes

The general rule for service-related disputes is **Section 102(1)** — first arbitration before the Registrar, not straight to the civil court (the full process on this topic — [Where to go when there is a dispute in a society](/blog/cooperative-society-dispute-resolution-haryana)). But there is also a settled, old exception — in **Sonepat Co-operative Sugar Mills Ltd. v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court** (Punjab and Haryana High Court, 14 August 1986), the court held that if an employee falls within the definition of "workman" under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, they can also approach the Labour Court — Registrar arbitration is not the only route.

So the right forum depends on whether the employee is a "workman" or not (this is decided by the nature of the post and duties, not just the designation):

| Situation | Usual forum |
| --- | --- |
| Most service disputes | Registrar arbitration (Section 102) |
| Employee in the "workman" category | Labour Court (Industrial Disputes Act) as well |
| Fundamental rights/writ-worthy matter | High Court (limited — see below) |

The scope for a writ petition directly in the High Court (Article 226) is limited — a society does not become a "government body" merely by registration; read the full discussion on this here: [Can the High Court hear a writ against a cooperative society?](/blog/cooperative-society-writ-jurisdiction-article-226)

## Compassionate appointment on death or going missing

If a regular PACS/PCCS employee dies or goes missing during service, the family can get financial assistance or an appointment separately under the **PACS/PCCS Employees (Compassionate Financial Assistance or Appointment) Policy, 2022**. For CMS there is a separate, more detailed 2024 policy — read the full details, including eligibility, order of priority, calculation of assistance and procedure, here: [Full guide to the CMS Ex-gratia and compassionate assistance policy](/blog/haryana-cms-ex-gratia-compassionate-policy).

## A practical checklist for both employee and society

**For the society (employer):**

- Before recruitment, check that the post is sanctioned in the society's class.
- Advertisement in English and Hindi newspapers with 30-day notice.
- Managing Committee approval only after the recommendation of the selection committee (chaired by DRCS/ARCS).
- In CMS, ensure the presence of all three mandatory officers.
- Keep a written record and minutes of every stage.
- Keep the seniority list up to date; follow natural justice in disciplinary action.

**For the employee:**

- Keep copies of your appointment, post and service records.
- Check the seniority list and promotion eligibility from time to time.
- In a service dispute, first Registrar arbitration; if you are a "workman", the Labour Court is also an option.
- If any action is taken without inquiry/hearing, it can be challenged.

## Common misconceptions

- **"A PACS employee is a government employee"** — No; the society is an independent body corporate under Section 39, and the employees are not the state government's but the society's own employees.
- **"Every service dispute goes straight to the High Court"** — No; the usual route is Registrar arbitration under Section 102, and in some cases the Labour Court too.
- **"PACS and CM-PACS run under the same rules"** — No; their recruitment, post structure and service rules are completely different (see: [Common mistakes in CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-common-mistakes)).
- **"Procedural lapse = whole recruitment cancelled"** — Not necessarily; as per the Gaurav Mehla judgment, a curable lapse at final approval does not bring down the whole recruitment, provided the advertisement and selection were proper.
- **"Any new central HR policy automatically replaces the state's rules"** — No; until a formal notification comes at the state level, the existing Staff Service Rules remain in force.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many posts can be sanctioned in a PACS?** — Up to a maximum of 7 posts as per the society's classification (Class A/B/C); the basic sanctioned posts are 4 (Manager, Clerk-cum-Cashier, Salesman, Peon-cum-Chowkidar).
**Who gives final approval to an appointment?** — Always the Managing Committee, after the selection committee's recommendation.
**Who chairs the selection committee?** — For the Manager post, the Deputy Registrar (DRCS); for other posts, the Assistant Registrar (ARCS).
**What is the ratio of promotion to direct recruitment?** — It differs by post (for example, for Manager about 70% direct, 30% promotion); check the exact ratio in the latest Staff Service Rules.
**If a mandatory officer is not present at a CMS board meeting, will the whole recruitment be cancelled?** — Not necessarily; as per Gaurav Mehla (2026 INSC 641), if the advertisement and selection were proper, this lapse can be treated as curable.
**What is the first step for a service dispute?** — Usually arbitration before the Registrar under Section 102; for employees in the "workman" category, the Labour Court is also an option.
**Who decides the recruitment/service conditions of PACS employees?** — The common-cadre Service Rules made by the Registrar under Section 37; details such as transfers are set by those rules, not by each society at will.
**What is the retirement age?** — In PACS/PCCS generally 58 years (60 for Peon-cum-Chowkidar), but confirm from the latest rules.
**Can a society remove an employee without an inquiry?** — No; disciplinary action requires a charge-sheet, a chance to reply and an inquiry (natural justice).

## Three things to remember

- 👥 Selection and final appointment are with different authorities — the selection committee recommends, the Managing Committee appoints.
- ⚖️ Under Section 37, the recruitment and service conditions of PACS employees are set by centralised common-cadre Service Rules made by the Registrar, not by each society's own arbitrary rules.
- 📘 A procedural lapse (such as an officer's absence) does not always cancel the whole recruitment — the Gaurav Mehla judgment treats it as "curable", provided the rest of the process was proper.

## Conclusion

The service system of PACS/CMS in Haryana rests on three layers (Act → Rules → Staff Service Rules), and every big question about it — how many posts, who recruits, how promotion happens, where disputes go — is decided in one of these layers. Two principles are the key to understanding the whole framework: first, **the roles of selection and appointment are kept separate** (for balance); and second, **process is necessary, but one curable lapse does not bring down the whole recruitment** (Gaurav Mehla). The lesson for the society — keep every stage as per rules and documented; for the employee — know your records and rights, and choose the right forum. This knowledge reduces disputes and gives security to both sides.
