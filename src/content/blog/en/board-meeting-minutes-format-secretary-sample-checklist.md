# How to write the minutes of a cooperative society board meeting? Sample and checklist for the secretary

For example, suppose the auditor asked about a godown repair expense of ₹2.4 lakh: "Where is the approval for this?" The secretary opened the minutes book. It says, "Repairs were discussed, everyone agreed." Who was present? Was the quorum complete? Agreement on what amount? Which contractor got the work? At what rate? Nothing is recorded. The decision was certainly taken, but on paper it cannot be proved.

This is the real test of minutes: **what happened in the meeting should be understood six months later, by an unknown reader, without asking the secretary.** This article is for the secretary: the structure of the minutes, a full sample, writing rules and a checklist for before, during and after the meeting.

> 📘 **In short:** The minutes are the **written, permanent record** of the meeting: who was present, whether the quorum was complete, what was decided on which subject, on what basis, by what majority, and now which task is to be done by whom and by when. It is not a word-for-word account of the discussion; it is **a summary of the decisions and their reasons**. **The Haryana Act does not prescribe any format for minutes**; but as per Section 9A, the manner of calling meetings, the quorum, the number of meetings in a year and the venue are fixed in the **bye-laws**, so the minutes should record confirmation of those. Always give priority to your bye-laws.

A separate question, "How to write a resolution and when is a Certified True Copy needed", is answered in the [Board Resolution guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide). This article is the **view from above** it, that is, the minutes of the whole meeting. One set of minutes can contain several resolutions.

## What minutes are, and what they are not

| They are | They are not |
| --- | --- |
| A summary of decisions, their reasons and responsibilities | A word-by-word account of the discussion |
| Proof of attendance and quorum | A collection of someone's personal opinions |
| A permanent record that can be read and understood later | A note that depends on the secretary's memory |
| A document confirmed in the next meeting | The decision of any single office-bearer |

**The relation between minutes and a resolution:** the minutes are the original record of the whole meeting; a resolution is the formal form of one decision from it, whose certified copy can be given to a bank or department. The resolution number should be the same in both. (This difference in detail: [Minutes Book vs Resolution](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)

## What the law says, what depends on the bye-laws

According to the texts of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 and Rules, 1989 that I read:

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the bye-laws should contain | Section 9A | The society's bye-laws include, among other things, **the manner of calling the general body meeting and its quorum**, **the procedure of notice and voting**, and **the manner of calling board/managing committee meetings, the quorum, the number of meetings in a year and the venue** |
| Calling a meeting | Section 26(1); Rule 23 | A general body or committee meeting must be called within one month of a written demand by the Registrar/members; as per Rule 23, the meeting is called by the chief executive officer or an officer authorised by him |
| Casting vote | Section 20(a) | When votes are equal, the chairman has a casting vote |
| Suspension of a resolution | Section 27 | The Registrar can suspend (for up to 6 months) a resolution that is beyond the powers under the Act/Rules/bye-laws or against the interest |
| Persistent lack of quorum | Section 34(1) | The Registrar can remove the committee of a PACS in situations like "persistent lack of quorum" |
| Conflict of interest | Rule 30 | An officer should not have a direct/indirect interest in the society's transactions, except for some exceptions |

**What was not found in the law:** any fixed format for minutes, within how many days of the meeting they must be written, whose signatures should be on them, or what the minutes book should be like. Rule 23 is about the officer who calls the meeting, not about minutes. And in the text I read, the quorum number was also not fixed in the Act/Rules; it is in the **bye-laws** (Section 9A). So all the checklists and the sample in this article are **good practice**, not a legal format. If there is anything in your bye-laws, an order of the Registrar or an instruction of a higher body, that comes first.

> 💡 The mention of "persistent lack of quorum" in Section 34(1) is exactly why **confirmation of quorum** in the minutes is essential: a clear mention of the number present and the quorum at every meeting, so that it is proved that meetings had a full quorum.

## Preparation before the meeting

Good minutes start being made before the meeting.

- [ ] **Notice of the meeting** as per the bye-laws: how many days in advance, by what mode, to whom. Keep a copy of the notice and proof of sending.
- [ ] **Agenda** with serial numbers, with a mention of the related papers (quotations, bills, reports) against each subject, and the subjects needing a decision marked separately.
- [ ] **The agenda paper should reach members** in time, so that they come informed.
- [ ] **Attendance sheet:** name, post, signature, and time of arrival and leaving.
- [ ] **Quorum number:** check it from the bye-laws and write it down in advance.
- [ ] **Keep the minutes of the last meeting** and its **action points** (what was to be done, who did it) with you.
- [ ] **Conflicts of interest:** if a member may have an interest in any subject, find out in advance.

## Structure of the minutes: twelve parts

| # | Part | What to write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Heading** | Society's name, registration number, registered office |
| 2 | **Meeting details** | Meeting number/type (managing committee), date, time, venue |
| 3 | **Notice** | On what date, by whom, by what mode it was sent |
| 4 | **Attendance** | Names and posts of members present; absent (and whether leave was taken); invitees |
| 5 | **Quorum** | Number required as per the bye-laws; number present; "quorum complete" |
| 6 | **Chair and start** | Who chaired, when the meeting started |
| 7 | **Confirmation of the previous minutes** | Read, whether amended or not, confirmed |
| 8 | **Each agenda subject** | Subject, short summary of discussion, **decision**, basis, voting, action |
| 9 | **Conflict of interest** | Who declared an interest, whether he took part or not |
| 10 | **Other subjects** | Subjects raised with the chairman's permission |
| 11 | **Action points** | What, by whom, by when |
| 12 | **Close and signatures** | When the meeting ended, date of the next meeting, signatures of the chairman and secretary |

For every **decision**, answers to five questions should be recorded: **what** was decided, on **what basis**, up to **what amount/limit**, **who** has to do it, and by **what majority**.

## A full sample

*(The sample below is **hypothetical**: society, names, amounts, dates — everything. It only shows the structure; the wording and quorum will change according to your society's bye-laws.)*

---

**Example Village Cooperative Society Limited**
Registration number: _______ · Registered office: _______

**Minutes of managing committee meeting number 7 (year 2026-27)**

**Date and time:** ___ October 2026, 4:00 pm · **Venue:** Society office

**Notice:** The notice and agenda of the meeting were given in writing to all members on ___ (receipt signatures attached).

**Present:** Mr A (Chairman), Mrs B (Vice-chairman), Mr C, Mr D, Mrs E, Mr F, Mr G (members), Mr H (Secretary, invited).
**Absent (with leave intimation):** Mr J.

**Quorum:** As per the bye-laws the quorum is 5 members. Members present 7. Quorum complete. The meeting started at 4:05 under the chairmanship of Mr A.

**Subject 1: Confirmation of the minutes of the last meeting (number 6)**
The minutes were read. In subject 3, the expense amount had been written as ₹1,800 instead of ₹18,000; it was corrected. The minutes, with the amendment, were confirmed unanimously.

**Subject 2: Review of cash and bank balances**
The secretary presented the cash balance and the bank BRS up to ___. The difference of ₹45,000 in the bank balance was explained in the BRS (cheques issued but not yet cleared ₹60,000, cheques deposited but not yet credited by the bank ₹1,05,000). The committee noted the BRS.
**Decision:** From the next meeting, the BRS should be presented by the 7th of every month. **Responsible:** Secretary.

**Subject 3: Approval of ₹2,40,000 for repair of the godown**
*Background:* The godown roof leaks in the rains; there is a risk of goods getting wet. The secretary placed three quotations: ₹2,40,000, ₹2,75,000 and ₹2,90,000.
*Conflict of interest:* Mr D said that the contractor with the lowest quotation belongs to his relative. He did not take part in the discussion and voting on this subject (he stayed outside during the discussion).
*Summary of discussion:* Members compared the quotations. Mrs E suggested the work be done after the rains; other members said that, given the risk to the goods, it is right to get it done immediately.
**Decision:** The repair of the godown roof should be got done up to a maximum limit of ₹2,40,000. The selection of the contractor should be decided separately by the meeting in such a way that there is no conflict of interest (in the context of Rule 30, the chairman and secretary should check and report to the next meeting). Payment on completion of the work, with bill and proof.
**Voting:** For 4, against 1, neutral 1 (Mr D did not take part). **Passed by majority.**
**Responsible:** Secretary (re-check of quotations) and Chairman. **Time limit:** By the next meeting.

**Subject 4: Settling the audit objection "two payments without voucher"**
The secretary said that the vouchers and bills for both payments have now been found. The committee examined them.
**Decision:** A compliance letter for the objection should be prepared and sent to the Registrar. **Responsible:** Secretary. **Time limit:** ___ date.

**Subject 5: Fixing the date of the annual general meeting** *(example of equal votes)*
Two proposals came: ___ November and ___ December. In the voting one member stayed neutral and the remaining votes were equal (3–3), so the chairman gave the casting vote and ___ November was fixed.
**Responsible:** Secretary (notice and agenda).

**Action points (summary):**

| What | By whom | By when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BRS by the 7th of every month | Secretary | From the next meeting |
| Re-check of quotations and report | Secretary/Chairman | Next meeting |
| Compliance letter for the audit objection | Secretary | ___ |
| AGM notice and agenda | Secretary | ___ |

**Other subjects with the chairman's permission:** None.
**Close:** With the chairman's vote of thanks, the meeting ended at 5:30 pm. Next meeting on ___.

**Signatures:** ______ (Secretary) · ______ (Chairman) · Date: ______
**Confirmation:** These minutes were read and confirmed in meeting number 8. ______ (Chairman)

---

Special points of this sample: subject 3 has **background, comparison, conflict of interest, limit of the decision, voting and responsibility** — all of it; subject 5 has an example of the casting vote when votes are equal (Section 20(a)); and subject 2 mentions the BRS but not its details. The BRS details are in its own paper, not in the minutes.

## Writing rules

**Language:**

- **Past tense and neutral:** "members discussed", "the committee decided": no language of praise or criticism of anyone.
- **Clear decision:** instead of "agreement was given", "it was decided to get the repairs done up to a maximum limit of ₹2,40,000".
- **Amount in figures and words**, and for which head.
- **Give references:** date of the quotation, bill number, number of the previous resolution.

**What to write and what not:**

- **Write:** the decision, its reason, amount, voting, responsible person, time limit, mention of interest.
- **With care:** the summary of discussion that forms the basis of the decision. No long speech of anyone.
- **Do not write:** personal comments, matters outside the meeting, things on which no decision was taken.

**Dissent:** if a member disagrees and wants his dissent recorded, write his name and his short reason. Hiding dissent increases disputes later.

**How to make corrections:**

- **Do not erase or overwrite** in the minutes book.
- If there is a mistake, **take a decision to correct it in the next meeting** and record it in that meeting's minutes (as in subject 1 of the sample).
- **Strike through** blank space and sign it, so that nothing can be added to it later.
- Pages should have **serial numbers** and the book should stay **bound (stitched)**. (This care is good practice; I did not find a reference to any rule.)

## After the meeting

- [ ] **Write the draft minutes quickly**, while things are fresh. (I did not find any statutory time for how many days; writing within a few days of the meeting is good practice.)
- [ ] **Send the draft to members,** so that any mistake is known before the next meeting.
- [ ] **Read and confirm in the next meeting** and get the chairman's signature.
- [ ] **Resolution numbers** the same in the minutes and the resolution register.
- [ ] Keep a **list of action points** separately and ask about their status in the next meeting.
- [ ] Where a **certified copy** must be given to a bank or department, make it from the original minutes. (When and how: [Board Resolution guide](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-board-resolution-guide).)
- [ ] **All records safe:** notice, attendance sheet, original minutes, certified copies. (Custody of records: [the secretary's work](/blog/secretary-work-meetings-records-accounts-audit).)

## Checklist

**Before the meeting**
- [ ] Notice and agenda sent on time as per the bye-laws, with proof
- [ ] Papers for every subject ready
- [ ] Attendance sheet and quorum number ready
- [ ] Previous minutes and action points at hand
- [ ] Conflicts of interest thought about in advance

**During the meeting**
- [ ] Attendance and quorum recorded, and the quorum stayed throughout
- [ ] Previous minutes read and confirmed
- [ ] Basis, amount, voting and responsible person of every decision noted
- [ ] Mention of interest and that member's participation recorded
- [ ] Dissent recorded, if anyone wants it

**After the meeting**
- [ ] Minutes written quickly, draft sent
- [ ] Confirmation and signatures in the next meeting
- [ ] Watch on action points
- [ ] Records in one place, safe

## A special point: minutes of the general body meeting (AGM)

The structure of the general body meeting minutes is the same, but it must record the points of Section 25(3): approval of the work plan, consideration of the audit report and annual report, the list of defaulters, funds, the list of employees who are relatives of office-bearers, elections (when due) and bye-law amendments. For a bye-law amendment, as per Section 10, a two-thirds majority of the members present and voting and 15 clear days' notice are necessary, which should be recorded in the minutes. (Full AGM preparation: [AGM checklist](/blog/agm-preparation-checklist).)

## A separate note for PACS

If your society is a PACS of Haryana:

- **Bye-laws:** for meetings, quorum and minutes, the PACS's own (model) bye-laws apply. See the exact text in your registered bye-laws. (Background: [PACS model bye-laws](/blog/pacs-model-bye-laws-haryana-explained).)
- **Prescribed portal and higher body:** if ARCS/DCCB or the prescribed portal (e-PACS) asks for any format of minutes or resolutions, that is final. The sample in this article does not replace it; it is only for understanding. (e-PACS: [e-PACS and day-end](/blog/e-pacs-haryana-erp-day-end-audit-explained).)

## Common mistakes

1. **A vague decision like "discussed, all agreed".** Without amount, head and responsible person, this is not a decision.
2. **Not writing attendance and quorum.** Later the validity of the meeting is questioned.
3. **Not keeping proof of notice and agenda.**
4. **Not writing voting and dissent.**
5. **Leaving out the mention of interest.** A risk from the point of view of Rule 30.
6. **Writing minutes months later, from memory.** Mistakes in dates, amounts, names.
7. **Correcting by erasing or overwriting.** Take a decision to correct in the next meeting.
8. **Leaving blank space** in which anything can be added later.
9. **Not writing action points.** In the next meeting nobody remembers what was to be done.
10. **Running your own format instead of the prescribed one in a PACS.**

## Three things to remember

- 📝 Minutes are a summary of decisions and their reasons: who was present, quorum complete, what was decided, on what basis and amount, by what majority, who has to do what and by when.
- ⚖️ The Act does not prescribe a format for minutes; notice of meetings, quorum and the number of meetings are subjects of the bye-laws (Section 9A). Follow your bye-laws, and in a PACS the prescribed format or portal is final.
- ✍️ Do not erase; take a decision to correct in the next meeting; record mention of interest and dissent; write the minutes quickly and get them confirmed and signed in the next meeting.

## Frequently asked questions

**How to write board meeting minutes?** — Heading, meeting details, notice, attendance, quorum, confirmation of the previous minutes, summary of discussion and decision on each agenda subject (basis, amount, voting, responsible person), conflict of interest, action points and signatures. A full sample is given above.
**What is the difference between minutes and a resolution?** — The minutes are the original record of the whole meeting; a resolution is the formal form of one decision from it, whose certified copy can be given to a third party.
**Is there any fixed format for minutes in the law?** — In the text of the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989 that I read, I did not find a format for minutes. As per Section 9A, notice of meetings, quorum, the number of meetings and the venue are subjects of the bye-laws.
**What should the quorum be?** — It is fixed in your bye-laws (Section 9A). I did not find any single quorum number in the Act or Rules. In the minutes, write both the number required as per the bye-laws and the number present.
**Whose signatures should be on the minutes?** — Usually the chairman's and the secretary's, but this depends on the bye-laws and the society's practice. I did not find any fixed rule on this in the law.
**Within how many days should minutes be written?** — I did not find a time limit in the law. Writing within a few days of the meeting, while things are fresh, is good practice, and get them confirmed in the next meeting.
**What to do if there is a mistake in the minutes?** — Do not erase or overwrite. Take a decision to correct it in the next meeting and record it in that meeting's minutes.
**If a member has an interest, what should be written in the minutes?** — What interest he declared, and whether he took part in the discussion and voting or not. As per Rule 30, an officer should not have a direct or indirect interest in the society's transactions; examine such a transaction separately.
**What if the votes are equal?** — As per Section 20(a), the chairman has a casting vote. Write in the minutes that the votes were equal and the chairman gave the casting vote.
**How to write minutes in a PACS?** — As per your PACS's registered bye-laws and the instructions of the higher body/prescribed portal (e-PACS). This article's sample is for understanding, not in its place.

## In the end

Minutes are the paper that keeps speaking even after the meeting. A secretary who has written them clearly, on time and completely does not have to depend on his memory in an audit, before the Registrar or in any dispute. And in a society whose minutes show the basis, majority and responsible person of every decision, the question "who decided this" arises much less.
