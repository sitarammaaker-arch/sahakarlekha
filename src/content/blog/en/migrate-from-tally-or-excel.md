# Move from Tally or Excel to SahakarLekha: without losing data

Many societies want to go digital, but one fear holds them back — "our old data will be lost" or "we will have to type everything again". The good news: there is a clear, tried path for migration. This article explains in detail how to move ahead from Tally or Excel, step by step, without losing data.

## What is migration

Migration means moving the society's existing records — masters (accounts and members), opening balances, and the history of transactions — from the old tool to the new software **once**. Usually this is done by exporting data from the old system and bringing it into the new system by **bulk import** — that is, hundreds of entries at one go, not one by one by hand.

> 💡 With this you move ahead not from zero, but from where you were — the old hard work does not go to waste.

## Why there is no need to fear

The fear of migration mostly rests on misunderstanding. In fact:

- **Data is not lost** — the old Tally/Excel record stays exactly as it is; migration brings a copy of it into the new system and deletes nothing.
- **No re-typing** — with bulk import, hundreds of entries come in together.
- **A one-time job** — migration happens once in a lifetime; after that everything is in the new system.
- **Keep the old backup safe** — even after migration keep the old file safely, so it is available for any matching.

## Universal Importer — the end of re-typing

The **Universal Importer** brings existing data (members, accounts, opening balances, transactions) from a spreadsheet or the old system all at once — so that nothing has to be entered again by hand. This is the tool that turns migration into a job of very little time instead of hours or days.

## The right order

The order of migration matters a lot — first the foundation, then the walls:

| Step | What to bring |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Masters — accounts (chart) and members |
| 2 | Opening balances (Dr=Cr must match) |
| 3 | History/transactions (as much as needed) |

> ⚠️ Migration does not need to be "perfect" in one go — first the core data (masters + opening), then the other details.

### About each step

- **Step 1 — Masters:** First bring the list of accounts (chart of accounts) and the list of members. This is the foundation — everything else links to these. So keep them clean and complete. (See: [Structure of accounts](/blog/chart-of-accounts-groups-heads).)
- **Step 2 — Opening balances:** Enter the starting balance of every account, and match total Dr = total Cr. This is the most important step; a slip here spoils the whole year's Balance Sheet. (See: [Opening balance guide](/blog/opening-balances-new-society).)
- **Step 3 — History:** Bring as much transaction history as is needed. Many societies bring only the current year's history and leave old years in the old file for reference — that is fine too.

## Preparation — before migration

Migration is easy only when the old data is clean. Before you start:

1. **Take a backup of the old file** — keep a copy of the original Tally/Excel file safe.
2. **Clean the data** — fix duplicate members, wrong names, incomplete accounts; garbage in means garbage out.
3. **Fix a cut-off date** — confirm which date's balances are to be treated as opening (usually the closing of the last year-end).
4. **Note the figures for matching** — write down the old system's Trial Balance, total member count and main balances, so you can check later.

## What to keep ready for migration

If these things are in hand before starting work, migration goes without a halt:

- **List of accounts** (chart of accounts) — from the old system.
- **List of members** — name, membership number and basic details.
- **Last Balance Sheet / Trial Balance** — for opening balances and matching.
- **Bank statement** — to confirm the bank balance.
- **Cut-off date** — which date's balances are to be treated as opening.
- **Current year's transactions** — as much history as is to be brought.

With all this in one place, migration becomes a smooth, orderly job.

## What changes after going digital

Migration is not just a transfer of data — it is an improvement in how the society works. After it: Trial Balance, Income & Expenditure and Balance Sheet come in one click (earlier it took hours); every report is made automatically and error-free; at audit time everything is ready; and the data stays in a safe backup. That is, the real benefit of migration keeps coming for years afterwards — so this one-time effort is very worthwhile. (See: [Why digital accounting](/blog/digital-accounting-for-cooperatives).)

## Checking after migration

The most important part of migration — matching:

- **Is the Trial Balance matching?** (Dr = Cr) — the first and biggest test.
- **Did all members and accounts come in?** — match the count with the old system; has anything been missed?
- **Do opening balances match the last closing position?** — match the balance of every main account with the old closing.
- **Check some sample transactions** — open a few entries and see that they came in correctly.

If the Trial Balance does not match, check the opening balances first — that is the most common root cause. (See: [Opening balance guide](/blog/opening-balances-new-society).)

## An example — step by step

Suppose a society has kept its accounts in Excel until now. Its migration will go like this: first it prepares the member list and the list of accounts from Excel in one sheet and imports them (step 1). Then it puts the last year-end balances in a sheet, imports the opening balances and checks Dr = Cr (step 2). Then it imports the current year's transactions (step 3). At the end it takes out the Trial Balance and matches it with the old Excel — if it matches, the migration is complete. This whole job, including preparation, is often done in just a few hours.

## Common data problems and their fixes

Old data often has some hidden problems that come up at the time of migration. Fix them beforehand:

- **Duplicate members/accounts:** The same member twice with different spellings — merge them into one before import.
- **Different spellings of names:** "Ram Kumar" and "Ramkumar" — fix one standard.
- **Blank or incomplete balances:** Accounts that have no balance — give them zero or the correct value.
- **Wrong date format:** Dates in Excel in many formats — make them uniform.
- **Mixed currency/unit:** Rupees in some places, thousands in others — bring them to one unit.
- **Opening Dr≠Cr:** If the old record itself is out of balance, resolve that first.

Remember the rule — "garbage in, garbage out." The cleaner the data that goes in, the more correct the result.

## By your starting point

Every society starts from a different place; the method changes a little:

- **From Tally:** Export masters and transactions from Tally (Excel/CSV), then bring them in through the Universal Importer. Tally's Trial Balance is useful for matching.
- **From Excel:** Clean the sheet, arrange it in the right columns (name, account, balance, date), then bulk import.
- **From paper registers:** Here the data first has to be copied into an Excel sheet (at least masters and opening), then imported. Instead of entering the full old history, entering just the opening and the current year is often enough.

In all three the last step is the same — confirm by matching the Trial Balance.

## Running side by side (parallel run)

For a big or careful society there is a safe method — **run both the old and the new system together** for a few weeks/one month. That is, enter the same transactions in both and match the figures of both at the month-end. When you are confident the new system is giving correct figures, drop the old one. This brings the risk of the change down to almost zero. A small society can go straight across if it wants, but a parallel run is the safest.

## After migration — actually starting (go-live)

After migration and matching are complete, real use begins. For this: give the secretary/clerk basic training (entering vouchers, taking out reports), fix a date from which "everything is in the new system from now on", and keep the old file safely only for reference. Within a few days the team becomes comfortable, and then every report comes faster and more accurately than before. (See: [Getting started with SahakarLekha](/blog/getting-started-sahakarlekha).)

## Common mistakes

- **Starting without a backup** → first keep a copy of the old file safe.
- **Bringing in dirty data** → clean it first; fix duplicate/incomplete records.
- **Breaking the order** → first masters, then opening, then history.
- **Leaving Dr≠Cr in the opening** → the Balance Sheet will never match; fix it right here first.
- **Not checking after migration** → always match the Trial Balance and the counts.
- **Deleting old data at once** → keep the old file safely for some time.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I migrate from Tally?** — Yes; it is exported from Tally and brought in through the Universal Importer.
**Will the old data be lost?** — No; masters, opening balances and history come along, and the old file also stays exactly as it is.
**Can we bring it from Excel too?** — Yes; bulk import from a spreadsheet is supported.
**How much time does migration take?** — With clean data, often just a few hours including preparation.
**Is it necessary to bring the full history?** — No; many societies bring only the current year and leave old years in the old file.
**What to check after migration?** — Trial Balance (Dr=Cr), the count of members/accounts, and the main opening balances.
**What if the Trial Balance does not match?** — Check the opening balances first; that is the most common root cause.
**Can we migrate from paper registers too?** — Yes; first copy the data (at least masters and opening) into an Excel sheet, then import.
**Can we run the old and the new together?** — Yes; running them side by side for a few weeks and matching the figures is the safest method.
**What is needed for migration?** — The list of accounts and members, the last Balance Sheet, bank statement, cut-off date and the current year's transactions.

Do not think of migration as a big, scary job — it is just an orderly, one-time step. Get the preparation right, get the order right, and get the matching done at the end — do just this much and the society goes digital without losing anything, and then the hassle of old re-typing and hand-made reports ends for good.

## Three things to remember
- 📥 Existing data comes in bulk — no re-typing.
- 🔢 Order: masters → opening (Dr=Cr) → history.
- ✅ Always check the Trial Balance after migration, and keep the old file safely.

## Where to get help with migration

If migration feels hard to do yourself, do not worry — a good cooperative-focused platform helps with it: guides in Hindi, videos, ready import templates, and direct support when needed. So when choosing, also see how much the tool helps with migration. With the right support, even a secretary without technical knowledge can easily bring in their society's data — it is not a job only for an expert.

## In the end
The fear that "data will be lost" is no longer a barrier. A little preparation — backup, cleaning, the right order — and migration becomes a smooth job of a few hours. With a clean migration your society can go digital without a halt and without losing anything — and after that every report, every audit becomes much easier.

In short, the whole secret of migration lies in three things — **preparation, order and matching.** First clean the old data and take a backup (preparation); then bring it in the order masters → opening → history (order); and at the end confirm by matching the Trial Balance and the counts (matching). Every society that follows these three goes digital without losing anything and without stress. The fear that is stopping the change today is really just a small, organised job — and its fruit comes for years.
