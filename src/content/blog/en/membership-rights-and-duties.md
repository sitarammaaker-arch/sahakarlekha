# Membership: who is a member, and their rights and duties

At the centre of a cooperative society is its **member** — who is both an owner and a user. But membership is not only about rights; it is also a responsibility. This article explains **the rights and duties of membership** — who a member is, the types, admission, the balance of rights and duties, ending of membership and the nominee — all in simple language.

This "member at the centre" idea is what sets a cooperative apart from a company — here the owners are not outside investors, but the very people who use the society's services. (Basics: [Cooperative principles and values](/blog/cooperative-principles-and-values).)

## Who is a member
A member is a person whom the society has admitted under its rules (bye-laws). They hold shares, use the services, vote on decisions, and share in both the benefits and the responsibilities.

> 💡 Just using a service does not make someone a member — proper admission is required.

It is important to understand this difference, because only a member gets the rights to vote, receive dividend and take part in decisions. A customer who only buys from the society is a "consumer", not a "member" — until they formally take membership. So the society too should keep it clear who is a member and who is only a customer, so that rights and benefits reach the right people.

## The process of becoming a member

Membership is not automatic — there is a set path for it:

1. **Application:** The person applies on the prescribed form, along with the required share amount and admission fee.
2. **Eligibility check:** Do they meet the bye-law conditions (area, occupation, age etc.)?
3. **Managing committee approval:** The committee considers the application and approves it.
4. **Entry in the register:** Only after approval is the name added to the member register and the shares allotted.

Treating someone as a member, or adding them to the register, before approval is wrong — it is a common audit objection. (See: [What are bye-laws](/blog/cooperative-society-byelaws).)

## Types of membership
- **Ordinary member** — full rights (including the vote).
- **Nominal member** — for a limited purpose, usually no vote.
- **Associate member** — limited rights, often along with a main member.

> ⚠️ The rights of each type are set by the society's **bye-laws** — do not mix them up.

It is important to understand the practical difference between these types. The **ordinary member** is the real "owner" of the society — the vote, dividend and all rights rest with them. A **nominal member** is often made when a person wants a limited dealing with the society (such as a one-time loan or service) but does not want to become a full member — they usually have no right to vote. An **associate member** is linked to a main member (such as in a joint account). So decide carefully, and as per the bye-laws, which category to put a person in, because it changes their voting and benefit rights.

> 🔍 From the accounting point of view too this difference matters — who gets dividend and who goes on the voters' list depends on the member type.

## Rights and duties — the balance
| Rights | Duties |
| --- | --- |
| Use of services | Paying dues on time |
| Vote and participation | Attending meetings |
| Share in the benefits | Following the rules |

> 🔍 A society runs well only when members do their duties — paying on time, attending meetings, following the rules.

### Main rights of a member — in a little more detail

- **Right to vote:** One member, one vote in the general body; a part in electing the managing committee and setting policy. (See: [Society elections](/blog/cooperative-society-elections).)
- **Right to services:** The benefit, as per rules, of the services the society offers (loans, sales, storage).
- **Dividend/bonus:** A share in proportion to share capital and to business done with the society.
- **Right to information:** The right to see or ask for the accounts, audit report and decisions.
- **Right to ask questions:** To ask questions and seek explanations about the accounts in the general body.

### Main duties of a member — in a little more detail

- Paying all dues (loan instalments, fees etc.) on time.
- Taking an active part in the general body, meetings and elections.
- Following the bye-laws and the society's decisions.
- Honest cooperation in the society's interest, not misuse for personal gain.

Rights and duties are two sides of the same coin — a member who wants only rights but does not do their duties weakens the society; and where all members do both, the society flourishes.

## Why a nominee is necessary
Every member should register a **nominee** — so that on the member's death their shares/deposits go to the right person without dispute. The society keeps a nomination register for this.

Without a nominee, after the member's death the family has to go through a long and complicated legal succession process, while with a nominee the settlement stays simple. So register the nominee at the time of adding the member, and update the register at once when the nominee changes (such as on marriage or a change in the family). This small precaution prevents big family and legal disputes later — and it is also a responsible step by the society in the member's interest.

## The right to information and transparency

An important part of being a member is the right to get information about how the society works. A member can see the accounts and audit report in the general body, ask questions about decisions, and ask for information about certain records as per rules. This right is what keeps the society transparent, because when office-bearers know members can ask questions, accountability stays in place. So members too should actively use this right — attend the general body, read the reports, and ask questions when needed. This is the real strength of cooperative democracy.

## Some concrete rights under the Haryana Act

Besides the general rights and duties described above, the state Act (such as the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984, Chapter III) also gives members some clear, section-based rights:

- **"One member, one vote"** — however many shares a member holds, they have the right to only one vote in the society's affairs. This gives the cooperative principle a legal basis, and sets it apart from the "one share, one vote" model of corporate companies.
- **Personal voting, no proxy** — voting has to be done in person; no one can be authorised to vote in your place, except in cases where a cooperative society is itself a member of another society.
- **Right to bring a no-confidence motion** — if members are unhappy with the working of the president or vice-president, the elected members can remove them by a no-confidence motion with a 2/3 majority (provided at least 1 year has passed since the election), and after that an election for the new appointment is compulsory within 2 months.

> ⚠️ Even after leaving membership or after a member's death, liability can remain for the next 2 years for the dues existing up to that date — leaving membership does not automatically wipe out old dues.

If a member feels their rights have been violated, the dispute does not go straight to court but to the Registrar or an arbitrator — [read the full process here](/blog/cooperative-society-dispute-resolution-haryana).

This "one member, one vote" system is the soul of cooperation — it ensures that control of the society rests with people, not with capital. So even if a member holds more shares, they cannot dominate the society. This system keeps equality between small and big members, and this is why a cooperative society becomes a safe and fair platform even for poor and small members.

## How membership ends

Membership can end in several ways:

- **Voluntary withdrawal:** The member leaves membership as per rules; their share capital is returned (after adjusting any dues).
- **Death:** The shares and deposits are transferred to the nominee or the legal heir.
- **Expulsion:** For a serious violation or acts against the society's interest, through the set process and with proper approval.
- **Disqualification:** When an eligibility condition is no longer met (such as leaving the area of operation or breaking a condition).

In every case record the "date of cessation" in the member register and do not delete the record. And remember — leaving membership does not automatically wipe out old dues (see above).

Expulsion is the most serious step, so it cannot be done arbitrarily — it needs the set process, a chance for the member to present their side, and proper approval. This is because taking away a member's rights is a big matter, and in cooperation every step should be fair and as per rules. So before any expulsion, always check the process in the bye-laws and the Act.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Taking a service made me a member"** — no; proper admission and approval are required.
- **"More money = more votes"** — no; one member, one vote.
- **"Left membership, dues gone"** — no; liability can remain for a set period.
- **"No need for a nominee"** — very much needed; without one the family faces difficulty.
- **"Only rights, no duties"** — both go together; without duties the society weakens.

## How membership connects to accounting

A correct record of every member is directly linked to the accounts — share capital, deposits, loans and dividend are all recorded member-wise. So a clean member register is the guarantee of correct share capital, correct dividend distribution and a correct voters' list. The register total must always match the ledger. (See: [Member and share register accounting](/blog/member-and-share-accounting).)

## Frequently asked questions
**Who is a member?** — A shareholder admitted as per rules, who is both owner and user.
**What is the difference between a nominal and an associate member?** — Both are limited-rights categories, but the bye-laws define them differently.
**What is a nominee?** — A person chosen in advance by the member, who receives the amount due on the member's death.
**How does one become a member?** — Application → eligibility check → managing committee approval → register entry and share allotment.
**Do more shares mean more votes?** — No; one member, one vote, however many shares they hold.
**What happens to dues on leaving membership?** — Old dues do not end automatically; liability can remain for a set period.
**What are a member's main duties?** — Paying dues on time, attending meetings, following the rules and honest cooperation.
**Where to go if rights are violated?** — Not straight to court; the dispute goes to the Registrar/arbitrator.
**How does membership end?** — By voluntary withdrawal, death, expulsion or disqualification; in every case record the date of cessation in the register.
**Can the nominee be changed?** — Yes; update the nomination register at once on a change.
**Can a member see the accounts?** — Yes; seeing the accounts and audit report in the general body and asking questions is a member's right.
**How does expulsion happen?** — Not arbitrarily; only after the set process, hearing the member's side and proper approval.

## Three things to remember
- 👤 Member = both owner + user, through proper admission and approval.
- ⚖️ Duties come with rights — balancing both is a condition of the society's health.
- 📝 Always register every member's nominee and keep it updated on any change.
- 🗳️ One member, one vote — control rests with people, not with capital.

## In the end
The member record is the backbone of the society. Only a clean, complete member register keeps shares, dividend and voting correct. Treat membership not just as rights but as duties too — only when every member knows their rights and does their duties does the society truly become "of the members, for the members". And always keep every member's nominee and a correct record, so there is no dispute later.
