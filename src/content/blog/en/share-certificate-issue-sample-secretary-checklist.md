# How to issue a share certificate in a cooperative society? A sample and checklist for the secretary

For example, suppose a new member, Kamla (imaginary), joined the society two months ago. She deposited ₹5,000 as share money, took the receipt, and today she has come to ask for "the society's share certificate" so that she can open an account in a bank. The secretary wonders: has this ever been issued here? Whose signatures will be on it? Which number should go on it? And if after a year she buys more shares, or the certificate is lost, what then?

A share certificate is a small piece of paper, but in the member's hand it is the most solid proof of her stake in the society, and in audit it is checked against the share register. This article explains from the secretary's point of view: what a certificate is and what it is not, what to check before issuing it, what to write on it (sample), how to number and sign it, and checklists for reissue, transfer and cancellation.

> 📘 **In short:** A share certificate is **documentary proof** that so many shares stand in the member's name and so much money has been paid. Issuing it makes **no money entry**: the money entry was already made when the share money was received. In the texts of the Haryana Act and Rules we read, **we found no direct provision or format for a share certificate**; so its form, signatures and time limit will be decided by your **registered bye-laws**. Before issuing it, the member's admission, share money, share register entry and nomination must all be complete.

## What a share certificate is, and what it is not

Three things often get mixed up with each other, so first make the difference clear:

| Item | What it is | Who keeps it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Receipt** | Proof that money was received (date, amount) | With the member; copy in the society's receipt book |
| **Share register** | The society's own record: which member, how many shares, how much money | The society (secretary) |
| **Share certificate** | A **certified document** of so many shares and so much paid-up amount in the member's name | With the member; copy with the society |

A receipt is for one payment; a certificate is for the **total stake**. The register is part of the society's books; the certificate is the "outgoing" form of that same register. The two must match: what is written on the certificate must be in the register, and the same in the member's share account. The full method of this matching is in [member and share register](/blog/member-and-share-accounting).

One more thing: cooperative shares are not a stock-market item. The certificate is "proof of stake", **not a slip for selling**. Shares can be transferred only subject to the conditions of Section 22 of the Act (below).

## What the law says, and what it does not say

In the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 (text amended up to 2007) and the Rules, 1989 (third-party copy), the term "share certificate" or a format for it was **not found**. That means the source texts of this article do not decide who signs the certificate, within how many days it is given, or what kind of paper it is. This is usually a matter for the **bye-laws**. So look in your registered bye-laws for:

- The **provision** for issuing a certificate (must every member be given one, or only on request).
- **What is written** on the certificate (name, number of shares, paid-up amount, sometimes the distinctive numbers of the shares).
- **Whose signatures** (chairman, secretary/CEO, or another authorised member).
- **Time limit** (within how many days of share allotment).

In this article we have **not given** any clause number or time limit from any state's bye-laws: the model bye-laws seen in the available material were a commentary from another state, not Haryana's registered bye-laws. Your own society's bye-laws are the authority.

There are some provisions of the Act and Rules worth remembering while issuing a certificate:

- **Section 6:** no member (other than the government or a cooperative society) can hold an interest in shares above the prescribed part of the capital (at most one-fifth) or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less. So check the holding limit before giving a large number of shares.
- **Section 22:** shares can be transferred only when the member has held them for **at least two years**.
- **Section 23 and Rule 21:** on a member's death the shares are transferred to the nominee or their value is paid; every member must name a nominee, and the number of nominees cannot be more than the number of shares the member holds.
- **Rule 22:** every society keeps a **register of members**, which records the name, address, occupation, details of shares held, date of entry of the name, date of ceasing to be a member, and the nominee.
- **Section 55:** the society has a charge on the member's shares. So the shares of a member who owes dues to the society are "tied"; the certificate is no proof that the member is free of dues.

## Checklist before issuing

All of this must be complete before printing the certificate:

1. ☐ **The member's admission is approved**: the managing committee has decided on the application and the resolution is recorded. (Haryana Rule 17: an admission application is disposed of within one month; the process is in [membership application in Haryana](/blog/haryana-cooperative-society-membership-application-guide).)
2. ☐ **The share money has been received** and a receipt issued. We treat this as a rule of caution (not a direct sentence of the law): only when the "paid-up amount" is complete, write that amount on the certificate.
3. ☐ **The share register entry** is done: member number, number of shares, face value, amount, date.
4. ☐ **The entry in the books** is done: *Dr Cash/Bank, Cr Share Capital* (normal accounting practice).
5. ☐ Within the **holding limit** (Section 6).
6. ☐ **Nomination** is done (Rule 21) and the number of nominees is not more than the shares.
7. ☐ The member's **name, father's/husband's name and address** are correct and the same as in the application form (down to the spelling).
8. ☐ The **signatories** named in the bye-laws are available and the society has their specimen signatures.

## Sample: what to write on a share certificate

Below is a simple sample. Adapt it to your bye-laws. All names and figures are **imaginary**.

> **[Name of society]**
> Registration number: [number] · Address: [village, district, state]
>
> **Share Certificate**
>
> Certificate number: **SC-014**  ·  Member number: **M-0231**
>
> This is to certify that **Smt. Kamla Devi**, wife/daughter of **Shri [name]**, resident of **[village, address]**, is a registered member of this society and is the holder of **10 (ten)** shares, each of face value **₹500**, total paid-up amount **₹5,000 (Rupees five thousand only)**, subject to the Act, the Rules and the bye-laws of the society.
>
> Date of issue: **[date]**
>
> Secretary: ______________   Chairman: ______________
> (Name and seal)

Always keep the six fields of the sample: **name and registration number of the society**, **certificate number**, **member's name, father's/husband's name, address**, **number of shares, face value and total paid-up amount**, **date of issue**, **signatures/seal**. Write the amount both in figures and in words, so there is less room for alteration.

## How to number them: the certificate register

Every certificate should have **its own separate serial number** and be given to only one member. For this, keep a simple **certificate register** (a column in the share register can also work):

| Cert. no. | Member no. / name | Shares | Value | Amount | Issue date | Status | Remarks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SC-013 | M-0230 / Ramkumar | 5 | ₹500 | ₹2,500 | 02-09 | Issued | |
| SC-014 | M-0231 / Kamla Devi | 10 | ₹500 | ₹5,000 | 10-10 | Issued | |
| SC-009 | M-0188 / Suresh | 4 | ₹500 | ₹2,000 | 12-03 | Reissued | Lost |

Some habits help a lot at audit time: **never reuse** a serial number (cancelled numbers also stay in the register); keep one numbering system through the year (e.g. from SC-001); and **do not keep blank certificates signed in advance**.

## A simple example: Kamla Devi's full sequence

> **Member "Kamla Devi" (imaginary).** Admission approved; 10 shares × ₹500 = ₹5,000 deposited in cash.

1. **Money entry (as soon as the share money is received):**

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash | ₹5,000 | |
| Share Capital | | ₹5,000 |

2. **Share register:** member M-0231, 10 shares, ₹500, ₹5,000, date.
3. **Certificate issued:** serial number SC-014, as per the sample above; secretary and chairman sign; one copy in the society's file.
4. Add a row in the **certificate register**.

In the third step there is **no entry**: issuing a certificate changes neither cash nor share capital. It is a task of record and proof, not of accounts. The mistake happens when someone thinks a voucher must also be made with the certificate.

## When something changes: reissue, and shares going up or down

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Certificate lost / torn** | Written application from the member; approval of the managing committee; old number recorded as cancelled; issue with a new number or a "reissued (duplicate)" mark; reason in the register |
| **Member took more shares** | Money entry first; then cancel the old certificate and issue a new one for the total shares, or a separate certificate for the extra shares (see bye-laws) |
| **Shares transferred (Section 22)** | Cancel the transferor's old certificate; new certificate for his remaining shares (if any remain); new certificate to the transferee |
| **Shares refunded / member left** | Cancel the old certificate after the refund entry; record the reason; refund conditions from Rule 18 and the bye-laws (in limited liability there is normally no refund) |
| **Member's death (Section 23)** | Transfer or payment after the consent of the nominee/heir; cancel the old certificate; if transferred, a new one to the nominee |

One thread runs through all of these: **first the correct money or register entry, then the change in the certificate, and never a new one without cancelling the old one.** Otherwise two certificates for the same stake keep going around.

## How this works in the SahakarLekha portal

This part covers only what was checked in the portal's code (as per the version of 21 October 2026).

**What the portal does:**

- On the **Members page**, in the member's row, there is a **"Share certificate (issue/reissue/cancel)"** icon. In it you fill the **certificate number**, **status** (issued / reissued / cancelled), **number of shares** and **face value (₹)**. For "reissued" or "cancelled", filling a **reason** is mandatory. This icon is shown only to the roles allowed to change members, and only for a member who has no approval action pending.
- **A certificate number can be in the name of only one member in the society**: if the same number has been given to another member, the portal does not let you save and tells you whom it was given to.
- As soon as you save with the status "issued", **the certificate PDF is made automatically** (A5 landscape paper, with a border): the society's name, address, registration number, "SHARE CERTIFICATE", certificate number, member number, member's name, father's/husband's name and address, number of shares, face value, paid-up amount, issue date, and signature lines for the secretary and chairman (names from the society's settings). For "reissued", the PDF shows **"DUPLICATE / RE-ISSUED"**.
- In the **Share Register**, each member's row has a **certificate number** column and (if not cancelled) a **certificate PDF** button. The whole share register can also be taken out as a PDF, which keeps the certificate number column. The certificate number and nomination can be corrected from the share register; **the number of shares/amount does not change from there**, it changes only through "Add Shares / Refund / Transfer", which make vouchers.

**What the portal does not do (know this clearly):**

- **The certificate number is not generated automatically.** You write it yourself (e.g. SC-014); keeping the sequence is your job.
- **The PDF carries no signatures.** It will be printed and the secretary and chairman will sign by hand; the portal adds no security paper, hologram or stamp.
- **The "issue date" is recorded as the day of saving.** The date cannot be back-dated.
- **The PDF labels are in English** (the member's name and address can print in Hindi).
- **On the certificate you fill the number of shares and the face value separately, while the "paid-up amount" comes from the member's share capital.** We did not find in the code any automatic check that these three agree with each other (number × value = amount). Match them yourself before printing.
- **The certificate does not change or get cancelled automatically when shares go up, go down or are transferred.** The share voucher entry is separate, and recording a new/cancelled certificate is something you must do separately.
- No PDF is made in the cancelled status.

## Common mistakes

- **Issuing a certificate without receiving the full share money**, and writing the full amount on the certificate.
- **Spelling of the name or address differing from the register.** Trouble later in bank or legal work.
- **The same serial number to two members**, or giving a cancelled number again.
- **Keeping blank certificates signed in advance.**
- **Giving a new certificate on increased shares without cancelling the old one.**
- **A duplicate of a lost certificate without managing committee approval.**
- **Treating the certificate as proof of being free of dues.** Because of Section 55, the society keeps a charge on the shares.

## Three things to remember
- 📜 The certificate is proof, not an entry: the money entry was already made when the share money was received.
- 🔢 Every certificate has its own serial number, recorded in the certificate register; and what is written on the certificate must be in the share register and the books.
- 🔁 If something changes, cancel the old certificate, record the reason, then issue the new one; signatures, time limit and form come from your bye-laws.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it legally necessary to give every member a share certificate?** — In the texts of the Haryana Act and Rules we read, no direct provision for this was found. It is usually a matter for the bye-laws, so see your registered bye-laws.

**Whose signatures go on a share certificate?** — The bye-laws decide this (usually the chairman and the secretary/CEO). We did not find any state-specific rule on this, so see the clause in the bye-laws.

**What is the time limit for issuing a certificate?** — Not found in the Act and Rules we read; see the bye-laws.

**Is there any entry for issuing a certificate?** — No. The entry is made when the share money is received (Dr Cash/Bank, Cr Share Capital). The certificate is documentary proof; it has no voucher.

**What if the certificate is lost?** — Take a written application from the member, record the old number as cancelled with the approval of the managing committee and issue a new one with a "reissued (duplicate)" mark; write the reason in the register.

**What if the member buys more shares?** — After the money entry, cancel the old certificate and give a new one for the total shares (or a separate certificate, if the bye-laws allow).

**What happens to the certificate when shares are given to someone else?** — If the condition of Section 22 (shares held for at least two years) is met, the transferor's certificate is cancelled, a new certificate is given for his remaining shares, and a new one to the transferee.

**Can a loan be taken from a bank by showing the certificate?** — That is the bank's policy, about which we have no information. And the society keeps a charge on the shares (Section 55), so the certificate is not proof of being free of dues.

**Can a certificate be printed from the portal?** — Yes: the PDF is made as soon as you save "issued" on the Members page, and it can also be taken out from the Share Register. Signatures are by hand, and you fill the serial number yourself.

**What if a certificate number is repeated in the portal?** — If the same number is given to another member, the portal stops it and tells you whom it was already given to.

## In the end

The work of a share certificate is split into four small things: the checklist complete before issuing, all fields of the sample filled, a separate serial number and register for every certificate, and on every change, cancel the old one and then issue the new one. Once these are done, the member does not have to wait for the certificate and in audit the match with the register falls into place directly.
