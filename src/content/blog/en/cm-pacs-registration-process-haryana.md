Once you know what a CM-PACS is and how many kinds of business it can run, the real question comes — to set up a CM-PACS in your village or panchayat cluster, where do you start the application, how many people are needed, which documents should be kept ready, and how much time and money does it actually take? This guide puts the whole process — from the legal conditions to practical preparation — in one place.

So the CM-PACS-specific figures (11 members, ₹1,000 share/admission fee, 5 acres) are no longer written as "legal rules" — only what was matched against the text of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 and Rules 1989 is stated as a rule. ## How many people are needed at the minimum

Under **Section 5** of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 (for a society none of whose members is another cooperative society), registration needs **at least 10 adult (above 18 years) individuals** — or a higher number that the Registrar may fix from time to time. Under **Section 7(2)(a)**, the application is signed by at least 10 such qualified individuals. **Rule 4** adds one more condition: these people must come from at least **5 families**. In Rule 2(dd), "family" means — the individual himself, the husband/wife, unmarried sons and daughters, and dependent unmarried brothers and sisters. That is, 10 people of the same household cannot form a society together.

Where did the number 11 for CM-PACS come from? A news report of January 2025 (Mahendra India News, 3 January 2025) says that "a CM-PACS will have at least 11 members". The report does not name any officer or order, and we did not find that order of the Registrar. So do not treat 11 as a firm legal rule — Section 5 gives the Registrar the right to fix a higher number, so ask your ARCS how many members are being asked for a CM-PACS right now.

> ⚠️ Collecting just 10-11 signatures is not enough — the applicants must come from at least 5 different families (Rule 4). Every time this condition is forgotten, the application gets delayed.

## Where the application is filed — everything is online now

The whole process now happens online through the Haryana Cooperation Department's portal **cooponline.rcsharyana.gov.in**. The applicants first register themselves on the portal, then apply online to the **ARCS (Assistant Registrar, Cooperative Societies)** of their area — filling the form, taking out its PDF and getting it signed, and uploading a scanned copy of the bye-laws. Under **Rule 5**, the application is made in **"Form I"**, and it must give the name and address of one applicant so that the Registrar can carry on further correspondence with him.

Understand one thing clearly about the bye-laws. Under Section 9A, every society makes its own bye-laws, with the Registrar's approval. Rule 2(g) defines "Model Bye-laws" — bye-laws approved or proposed by the Registrar, which all societies of one type can adopt. Such Model Bye-laws are said to exist for CM-PACS, but we did not find their official text. So before applying, do get a copy of the current CM-PACS Model Bye-laws from the ARCS office — the name, area of operation, share amount and membership conditions will be in it. Under **Rule 6**, **3 copies** of the bye-laws go with the application, and each copy is signed by at least 2 applicants authorised by the members.

## What happens after the application is filed

The application is not approved automatically. Under **Rule 7(1)**, before the final order, the Registrar may ask the applicants for more information or get an inquiry done himself. In practice, this inquiry is a **physical verification** by an inspector on the spot — whether the members named really exist, and whether they belong to the area stated. After this, the ARCS sends the file forward with his recommendation.

**Section 8(1)** sets out what the Registrar must be satisfied about before registering the society — the application is in line with the Act and Rules, the object is as per Section 4, the bye-laws are not against the Act, and the proposed society has a "reasonable chance" of success.

The same section also has an important time limit. **Section 8(2)** (after the 2006 amendment) says that if the Registrar refuses registration, the refusal order with reasons has to be sent **within one month** of receiving the application, and a reasonable opportunity of hearing has to be given. If the application is not disposed of, or the refusal order is not sent, within one month, the application is **deemed to be accepted** and the Registrar has to issue the certificate. Under **Rule 8**, this order is sent by registered post (A.D.).

On approval, under **Rule 7(2)** the society gets the registration certificate (**Form II**) and a copy of the registered bye-laws. Under **Section 9**, this certificate is conclusive evidence that the society has been registered, and under **Section 39** a registered society becomes a body corporate — after this, the society can start opening a bank account, holding property and entering into contracts in its own name. If the application is rejected, under **Rule 9** an appeal needs the signatures of all the applicants (if there are more than 10 applicants, of at least 2/3).

## Document checklist — what to keep ready before applying

| What to keep ready | Why it is needed |
| --- | --- |
| At least 10 adult applicants (or the higher number fixed by the Registrar), from 5 different families | Section 5, Section 7(2), Rule 4 |
| Application in Form I, address of one applicant | Rule 5 |
| 3 copies of the bye-laws, each signed by 2 authorised applicants | Rule 6 |
| Proof of identity and address (Aadhaar/Voter ID) | This is the first thing checked in verification |

| Contact mobile and email | Portal-based process, all correspondence happens on these |

## How much time and money it actually takes

In the law book, the picture is clear — under Section 8(2), a decision within one month, otherwise the application is deemed accepted. But across the state, the pace at which new societies were formed has been different. According to a news report of January 2025, the target for the first phase was to open CM-PACS in 500 villages by 31 March; The Tribune (6 July 2025) quoted the minister as saying that 141 CM-PACS had been formed by then; and on 18 October 2026, the RCS Haryana dashboard was showing 546 registered CM-PACS.

Most delays are not because of the law, but in the preparation stages — the time taken for verification in remote villages, the file coming back due to incomplete documents, and the time it takes to reach mutual agreement on the area of operation/managing committee. This last part is decided entirely at the local level.

> ⚠️ We did not find any registration-fee amount in the Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules 1989. (Rule 80 is about audit fees and Rule 82 about arbitration fees, not registration — the earlier version wrongly linked these.) The most reliable way to know the current fee, if any, is to ask your ARCS office directly.

How much initial membership capital will be needed depends on the share amount and admission fee in the CM-PACS bye-laws. The same January 2025 news report says that share capital was first fixed at ₹20,000 per member and later made ₹1,000 (refundable) — but we did not find the official order, and the ₹1,000 admission fee was not mentioned in any source. Do not accept "refundable" blindly either: under **Rule 18(3)**, in a society with limited liability, a member is generally not allowed to withdraw shares; refund is allowed only if the society has created a share transfer fund out of profits, and even then the total refund at any time cannot exceed 5% of the paid-up share capital (excluding the government's portion) as on the previous 31 March. So get the correct amount and the refund condition in writing from your ARCS.

## Managing committee and representation

Along with registration, the society has to constitute its managing committee; the number of members is fixed in the bye-laws. Under the proviso to **Section 28(1)**, the committee of every society must have at least one Scheduled Caste member and (after the 2013 amendment) **two women members** coming through election; if Backward Class members are 10% or more of the total membership, at least one of them too. Under **Section 28(4)**, the committee holds office for **5 years** from the date of election. That is, the managing committee is not just a group of a few influential people, but the voice of different sections of the village. It is better to settle the names of the first managing committee at the time of applying itself, so that there is no hurry later.

## After registration — the first necessary steps

As soon as the certificate (Form II) is received, the society becomes a separate legal body, but to become ready for work some first steps are necessary:

1. **Open a bank account** — in the society's name, so that all transactions go through it (never from a personal account).
2. **Collect share amount and admission fee** — the amount written in the bye-laws from every member, with a receipt, and an entry in the member register. Under Section 19, until a member makes the payment written in the bye-laws, he cannot exercise the rights of a member.
3. **First general body and managing committee meeting** — choose office-bearers, pass the initial resolutions, record the minutes.
4. **Start the accounts** — opening balances, chart of accounts and daily entries — keep them digital from day one and the audit is easy.
5. **Start activities** — begin only with the activities written in the bye-laws; a new activity needs a bye-law amendment (in the general body under Rule 10, with at least two-thirds of members present) and then registration by the Registrar (Section 10).

Doing these steps in an orderly way makes the society disciplined and compliance-ready from the start.

## A practical timeline

Broadly, the path looks like this — gathering members and documents (a few days to weeks, depending on local agreement) → online application on the portal → ARCS acceptance and verification → the Registrar's decision (Section 8(2): one month, otherwise deemed accepted) → certificate. The most time is often spent before applying (agreement and documents), less in the legal decision stage. So the fuller the preparation, the faster the process.

## Common mistakes that cause delay

In practice, applications get stuck for these reasons — members were gathered but the condition of 5 different families was forgotten; documents incomplete; mutual disagreement on the proposed name/area of operation; or relying on wrong information about the fee/share amount. The best way to avoid all of these — confirm with the ARCS office before applying. (In detail: [Common mistakes in CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-common-mistakes).)

## Frequently asked questions

**How many members are needed at the minimum to register a CM-PACS?** — Under the Act (Section 5), at least 10 adults, or the higher number fixed by the Registrar; under Rule 4, from at least 5 families. A news report says 11 for CM-PACS — confirm the current number with the ARCS.
**How long does registration take legally?** — Under Section 8(2), if it is to be refused, an order with reasons within one month; if none comes, the application is deemed accepted.
**Do we have to write the bye-laws ourselves?** — Model Bye-laws are said to exist for CM-PACS; get their current copy from the ARCS and fill in the name and area of operation in it.
**What is the registration fee?** — We found no amount in the Rules 1989 — confirm with your ARCS office.
**Where is the application filed?** — On the Haryana Cooperation Department's portal cooponline.rcsharyana.gov.in, to the ARCS of your area (Form I, Rule 5).
**How many copies of the bye-laws?** — 3 copies (Rule 6), each signed by at least 2 authorised applicants.
**What if the application is rejected?** — Under Rule 9, an appeal can be made with the signatures of all applicants (or, if more than 10, of at least 2/3).
**How much initial capital is needed?** — Share amount written in the bye-laws × members, plus admission fee and documentation. The ₹1,000 figure is only in a news report — confirm with the ARCS.
**What to do right after registration?** — Open a bank account, collect the share amount, hold the first general body and committee meeting, and start the accounts from day one.
**Who must be on the managing committee?** — Section 28(1): at least one SC and two women members; if Backward Class is 10% or more, one BC member. The total number is in the bye-laws.
**Where does the most delay happen?** — Not in the legal decision, but in pre-application preparation (agreement, documents) and verification — so keep the preparation complete.

## In the end

The legal path for CM-PACS registration is clear — application under Section 7, decision within one month under Section 8 (otherwise deemed accepted), certificate under Section 9. But the real work is in the preparation — at least 10 adult members (from 5 different families), complete documents, a copy of the Model Bye-laws from the ARCS, and mutual agreement. A group that does this preparation in advance sees its application move forward quickly. For points like the number of members, share amount and fees, the ARCS office is the final source — not figures going around on the internet.
