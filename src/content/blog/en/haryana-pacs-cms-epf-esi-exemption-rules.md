"We are a cooperative society, so EPF-ESI does not apply" — in Haryana's PACS, CM-PACS, CMS and other cooperative societies, this is a very common but incomplete answer. The truth is that EPF, ESI and EHS each run under separate laws, and the conditions for a cooperative society's exemption are different in each of the three. This guide explains the three by clearly separating them.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** All four Labour Codes are in force from 21 November 2025 (PIB), so the sections of the Code on Social Security, 2020 are given alongside the sections of the old EPF/ESI Acts. The EPF wage ceiling has become ₹25,000 from 17 September 2026. Sources are given below.

## First understand — EPF, ESI and EHS are three different things

| Subject | Main purpose | Legal basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EPF/PF | Provident fund, pension and linked insurance | EPF Act, 1952 / now the Code on Social Security, 2020 (in force from 21 November 2025) |
| ESI | Social security such as sickness, treatment, maternity, employment injury | ESI Act, 1948 / now the Code on Social Security, 2020 (in force from 21 November 2025) |
| EHS | If a state/institution has a special Employees Health Scheme | The state's/institution's own separate order/scheme |

"PF is not being deducted" from an employee's salary does not mean "ESI will not be deducted either" — the eligibility for each of the three has to be checked separately.

## The special exemption for cooperative societies in EPF

This is the most important part of the whole subject. Under **Section 16(1)(a)** of the old EPF Act, 1952, the Act does not apply to an establishment that is registered under any cooperative societies law, employs **fewer than 50 persons**, and works **without the aid of power**. The same words are now also in **Section 20(1)(a)** of the Code on Social Security, 2020, and this Code is in force from 21 November 2025. (Section 20(1)(d) of the Code has one more exemption — for employees who are already getting PF under some other law.)

> ⚠️ All three conditions must be met together — just saying "we are a cooperative society and we have fewer than 50 employees" is not enough. It must also be seen whether the society actually works without the aid of power (electricity/machinery) — this check is especially important for a CMS with a warehouse, processing unit or machinery.

Understand with an example — this exemption may apply to a registered cooperative society with 12 or 45 employees that works without power. But a society with 55 employees does not meet the "fewer than 50" condition, and even a society with 30 employees, if it works with the aid of machinery/electricity, would be wrong to assume the exemption just by looking at the number of employees.

## No contradiction between the general EPF limit (20 employees) and the cooperative exception (50)

General EPF coverage starts at 20 or more employees. But for cooperative societies, Section 20(1)(a) gives a separate, special exception — so for a society with 20 to 49 employees that works without power, this special exception becomes the deciding factor. At 50 or more employees, this special exception is no longer available.

## Once coverage comes, it does not go back

An important principle of EPF law is that once the Act has applied to an establishment, coverage does not end automatically just because the number of employees later falls below the limit (Section 1(5) of the EPF Act; Section 1(8) of the Code applies the same rule to every chapter). So a society should not start and stop PF just by looking at each month's headcount — it is necessary first to see whether the society is already under statutory coverage.

## Does a PACS get the EPF exemption automatically?

**No.** There is no general rule like "it is a PACS, so PF will not apply". Every PACS will have to check each of these separately — its registration, number of employees, use of power, existing EPFO registration history and PF contributions already being deposited. The same care applies to a CMS too, especially if it has activities like marketing, warehousing, processing or electrically driven machinery.

## ESI has no such special exemption for a cooperative society

Unlike EPF, ESI has no special 50-employee exemption on the ground of being a cooperative society. To decide ESI coverage, the nature of the establishment, the number of employees and the applicable notification have to be seen — the general coverage limit is **10 or more employees** (ESIC). Under the Code, ESI applies to a unit doing hazardous work even if it has 1 employee, and according to PIB a unit with fewer than 10 employees can join voluntarily. So just saying "we are a cooperative society" does not get an exemption from ESI.

## Three things for ESI coverage

1. **Category of establishment** — does it fall in a category covered under the ESI notification?
2. **Number of employees** — is the applicable threshold being met or not?
3. **Wage ceiling** — the current notified ceiling is ₹21,000 per month; for employees with disabilities (Persons with Disabilities) this ceiling is ₹25,000.

## Rate of ESI contribution

Current rate — employee's share **0.75%**, employer's share **3.25%**, total **4%**. The employee's share rate is in force from 1 July 2019 (ESIC). The employer (principal employer) first deposits both shares and can recover only the employee's share from his salary — the employer's own share cannot be deducted from the employee's salary (ESI Act Section 40; Code Section 31). For those whose average daily wage is up to ₹176, the employee's share is not deducted, but the employer pays its own share (ESIC).

> 💡 The old ESI Act has provisions for exemption in special circumstances under Sections 87, 88, 90 and 91 (and now under Sections 46 and 143 of the Code), but this needs a formal government notification — Sections 90/143 also have the condition that the benefits to employees must be similar to or better than ESI — it is not correct to simply assume "we have our own medical fund, so ESI will not apply". Facilities given by the society such as medical reimbursement, group insurance or an employee welfare fund do not automatically end the statutory ESI liability unless there is a formal exemption order.

## EHS needs the most care

EPF and ESI are central, statutory social-security frameworks. Any deduction named "EHS" seen on a salary slip should not be assumed to be automatically statutory like them — an EHS deduction should have a basis only when there is a clear state government/department/society service rule/approved scheme or order for it. We did not find any such general, state-wide official order applicable to all PACS/CMS in Haryana — so it would be wrong to assume that some other state's "EHS" scheme applies to Haryana. If an EHS deduction is being made in a particular society, its concrete basis (the name of the order/rule) should be asked for and checked.

## EPF vs ESI — the difference at a glance

| Subject | EPF | ESI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Special provision for a cooperative society | Yes (fewer than 50 + without power) | No such special cooperative exemption |
| General establishment threshold | 20 employees | 10 employees |
| Wage ceiling | ₹25,000 per month (from 17 September 2026; earlier ₹15,000) | ₹21,000 (₹25,000 for PwD) |
| Main benefit | PF / pension / EDLI insurance | Medical and cash benefits |
| Employee contribution | As per the applicable scheme/rules | 0.75% |
| Employer contribution | As per the applicable allocation rules | 3.25% |

> ⚠️ The mandatory EPF wage ceiling was ₹15,000 per month from September 2014. According to the Labour Ministry's PIB release (23 September 2026), by notification S.O. 5109(E) it has been raised to **₹25,000 per month from 17 September 2026**; the maximum contribution to EPS is 8.33% × ₹25,000 = ₹2,083. Salary for September 2026 is calculated in two parts by days. We have not opened the Gazette text ourselves, so before the new calculation do check the FAQ/notification on the EPFO site.

## Why a wrong assumption costs a lot

"We are a cooperative society, it does not apply to us" — if this assumption turns out wrong, it costs a lot. For the period in which the contributions due were not deposited, arrears, interest and penalty can all come together later, and they can be added from a back date. Even bigger — the employee is deprived of his statutory benefits (PF/pension, medical), which is a moral failure too. So instead of assuming "there is an exemption", checking properly — whether all the conditions of the exemption are really met — is far cheaper. This is the most practical lesson of this whole subject.

## When to take expert help

Since EPF/ESI eligibility depends on several factors together (registration, number of employees, use of power, establishment category, wage ceiling, past registration history), deciding it alone, by guesswork, is risky. In these situations especially, confirmation from an expert (labour-law adviser/CA) is necessary — when the society has processing/warehouse/machinery; when the number of employees is near the limit; when the society is already registered with EPFO/ESIC; or when a deduction like EHS is being made whose basis is not clear. A little spent on the right advice saves you from big arrears/penalties later.

## Practical checklist — every society should do it once a year

**Society profile:** under which Act it is registered, what the main business is, whether there is processing/warehouse activity, whether machinery and electricity are used.

**Employee profile:** separate numbers of regular, temporary, casual, contract, outsourced, daily-wage and apprentice employees, and their monthly pay.

**EPF status:** whether there is EPFO registration, whether there is a PF code, how many employees are members, whether the monthly ECR is filed, whether there is any exemption order.

**ESI status:** whether the establishment is in a covered category, number of employees and wage ceiling, employer registration. (After the Code came into force, according to PIB ESIC applies across the whole country, so the old check of "whether the area is notified or not" now matters less.)

**EHS/medical benefits:** what the exact legal basis of EHS is — which government order, which bye-laws/service-rule provision.

> ⚠️ This information is for general understanding. To decide the actual EPF/ESI liability of a particular PACS/CMS, its registration documents, employee register, machinery/power details, EPFO/ESIC registration history and the applicable service rules/government orders must be looked at separately — especially if the society is already registered with EPFO/ESIC, a decision to stop contributions should not be taken just on the ground that the number of employees has fallen.

## In short — three different things, three checks

Let us repeat it once at the end: **EPF, ESI and EHS are three different things**, and the eligibility for each has to be checked separately. EPF has a special, conditional exception for a cooperative society (fewer than 50 + without power); ESI has no such cooperative-specific exemption; and EHS is not part of any statutory central framework but depends on a particular order/scheme. So an exemption in one does not mean an exemption in the other two. Checking the status of all three once a year with the checklist above is the safest way.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does just being a cooperative society give exemption from both EPF and ESI?** — No; EPF has a special, conditional exception (fewer than 50 employees + without power), but ESI has no such special exemption for a cooperative society.
**Will EPF apply to a cooperative society with 20 employees?** — It depends on the "fewer than 50 + without power" condition; the figure of 20 alone is not enough.
**Does ESI apply automatically at 10 or more employees?** — The category of establishment, the notification and the wage ceiling — all three have to be seen together, not just the number.
**Does making our own separate medical scheme end ESI?** — Generally no, unless there is a formal statutory exemption order.
**Why is power (electricity/machinery) important?** — One mandatory condition of the EPF cooperative exemption is "working without power"; a society with a warehouse/processing/machinery does not meet this condition.
**What if coverage has come once?** — Coverage does not end automatically just because the number of employees falls; first check the statutory position, and only then decide on contributions.
**What to do if an EHS deduction is being made?** — Ask for its concrete, written basis (which order/rule); without a clear basis, do not treat it as statutory like EPF/ESI.
