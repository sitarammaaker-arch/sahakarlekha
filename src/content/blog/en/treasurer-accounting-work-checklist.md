# The accounting work of a cooperative society's treasurer: a checklist for cash, bank, vouchers and reports

As an example, suppose that on the last day of the month your society's Cash Book shows a cash balance of ₹2,47,300, but when you open the safe and count, it comes to ₹2,45,800. The bank is in a similar state — ₹8,40,000 in the Bank Book and ₹7,95,000 in the bank statement. If the auditor or an inspecting officer arrives the next morning, who will be asked the first question? Usually the treasurer.

Most people know that the treasurer "looks after the money accounts". But what this means in daily work — what to check every day, what every month, what to match in which register, and which record to keep ready before the audit — is rarely written down clearly. This article is a **practical checklist of exactly this accounting work**: cash, bank, voucher checks, reports and audit records.

> 📘 **In short:** The treasurer (Koshadhyaksh) is the office-bearer of the society who watches over the safety of the funds and the truth of the accounts. Daily — match the physical count of cash with the Cash Book. Monthly — bank reconciliation (BRS) and checking vouchers. Quarterly/yearly — place reports before the managing committee and keep records organised for the audit. **The law does not give a list of the treasurer's duties** — these are set by the bye-laws, RCS orders and good practice; but the law says clear things about cash, records and audit, which are given below.

This article is about **the treasurer's accounts work**. The roles of all three office-bearers — who does what — are covered separately in [Roles of the chairman, secretary and treasurer](/blog/office-bearers-chairman-secretary-treasurer); they are not repeated here.

## Who is a treasurer? (Koshadhyaksh in Hindi)

The English form of **koshadhyaksh** is **Treasurer** — "kosh" means the society's treasury (cash and bank), "adhyaksh" means the person in charge of it. In Hindi some people also call this person "khazanchi", and in English script it is written Koshadhyaksh.

The definition of "officer" in Section 2(m) of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984 includes the chairman, secretary, managing director, manager, committee member and **treasurer** — all of them. So in the eyes of the law the treasurer is an *officer* of the society, and every responsibility of the treasurer is the responsibility of an accountable post.

In small societies the treasurer may be an elected member and the secretary or accountant a salaried employee; in many societies both roles are with the same person. Who holds which post and how they are chosen depends on the state Act and the bye-laws.

## What the law says — and what it does not say

First let us be clear about what is *law* and what is *practice*, because the two often get mixed up. The provisions below are from the text of the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989:

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The treasurer is an "officer" | Section 2(m) | Chairman, secretary, manager, committee member and treasurer — all are officers |
| Who holds records/property | Section 49 | The society must keep a list of the employees/committee members who have custody of books, records, securities and property |
| List of books/records and custodian's signature | Rule 36 | At the start of the cooperative year a list of books, records and securities is made; the custodian signs it; when the custodian changes, a new list is made |
| Form of account books | Rule 78 | Books are to be kept in the form and manner specified by the Registrar |
| Time limit for the Balance Sheet | Rule 79 | Within 3 months of the end of the cooperative year |
| Checking cash in the audit | Section 95(2),(3) | Audit includes verification of cash balance and securities; the auditor has open access to books, vouchers and stock |
| All office-bearers must give information | Section 95(4) | Committee members, the manager, any officer/employee must give the auditor the information asked for |
| Incomplete accounts | Section 95(6),(7) | If the accounts are incomplete at the time of audit, the Registrar can get them written up; the cost can be recovered from the society or the person responsible |
| Cash verification in inspection | Section 97(1) | The Registrar/an authorised person can verify books, vouchers and cash |
| Producing/handing over records | Sections 50, 51; 117(5) | A custodian who wilfully does not produce or hand over books, records, cash or securities faces a penalty (imprisonment up to 6 months or a fine up to ₹1,000 or both) |

**What was not found in the law:** In the text of the Act and Rules that I read, there is no list of the treasurer's duties, no fixed limit on keeping cash in the safe, and no rule on the treasurer's co-signature on cheques/payments. These things come from **the bye-laws, Registrar/RCS orders, central cooperative bank instructions and the society's own internal policy**. So below, wherever something like a "cash limit" or "co-signature" appears, read it as *good control*, not a *statutory order* — confirm your society's exact limit from your bye-laws or the order of your higher institution.

## 1. Cash checklist

Cash is the most delicate thing, because mistakes or fraud happen most easily and most quickly here — and it is the first thing looked at in an audit (Section 95(3) gives the auditor the right to verify cash).

**Daily work**

- [ ] All of the day's cash transactions are entered in the **Cash Book** the same day; there is a receipt for every receipt of money and a voucher for every payment.
- [ ] At the end of the day, **work out the balance** of the Cash Book and match it with the **physical count** of the safe — counting notes and coins separately.
- [ ] If there is a **difference** between the count and the Cash Book, find it the same day; "we'll look tomorrow" makes the difference grow. A difference that cannot be found should be recorded in writing and reported to the secretary/chairman — do not hide it.
- [ ] Any cash above the limit of the bye-laws/higher institution should be **deposited in the bank on the next convenient date**, and keep the deposit slip.
- [ ] If there is **petty cash**, keep its fixed amount, a separate register and bills/vouchers. (More on handling cash: [Cash handling and verification](/blog/cash-handling-and-verification).)

**Weekly/monthly work**

- [ ] Get a **surprise cash check** done in the presence of the chairman or another office-bearer, and keep a note of it.
- [ ] Check the serial numbers of receipt books — no receipt is missing, torn or blank.
- [ ] Who holds the keys to the safe should be fixed and in writing. (On division of work and control: [Internal control](/blog/internal-control-in-cooperatives).)

> 💡 **For the secretary/accountant/treasurer:** In the daily work of "work out the Cash Book balance, match it with the count", most of the time goes into adding up by hand. In SahakarLekha the **Cash Book** and **Day Book** pages are built directly from the vouchers entered, so the balance comes out automatically every time and you only have to match it with the count of the safe. It is also possible to give a role like cashier limited rights and split cash entry and review between different people.

## 2. Bank checklist

The bank account runs in two places — in the society's **Bank Book**, and in the bank's **statement/passbook**. The balances of the two almost never match exactly, because some cheques have been issued but the bank has not yet passed them, and some have been deposited but the bank has not yet credited them. The process of matching these is **bank reconciliation (BRS)** — the full method is in [How to do a bank reconciliation (BRS)](/blog/bank-reconciliation-guide), and the difference between the cash book and bank book is [here](/blog/cash-book-vs-bank-book).

- [ ] Prepare a BRS **every month** — Bank Book versus bank statement on the last date.
- [ ] Find **every entry** of the bank statement in the Bank Book — interest, bank charges, ECS/NEFT receipts, cheque returns, which are often missed in the society's books.
- [ ] Keep a **register of cheques issued**: cheque number, date, payee, amount, when it was passed. For a cheque more than 3 months old, check its status (confirm the validity period of a cheque with your bank).
- [ ] Keep **old unpassed cheques** and **cheques deposited but not credited by the bank** in a separate list; write the reasons for them in every monthly report.
- [ ] Who the **signatories in the bank** are should be kept up to date as per the resolution; inform the bank when office-bearers change.
- [ ] Keep a list of **FDRs/fixed deposits** — amount, interest rate, maturity date — in a separate register.

### A BRS example (imaginary figures)

Let us take the bank case from the start of the article. *(All figures are imaginary, only for explanation.)* Balance in the Bank Book ₹8,40,000, in the bank statement ₹7,95,000 — a difference of ₹45,000. Is ₹45,000 missing? Step by step:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Balance as per Bank Book | ₹8,40,000 |
| (+) Cheques issued but not yet presented in the bank (unpresented) | ₹60,000 |
| (−) Cheques deposited but not yet credited by the bank (deposit in transit) | ₹1,05,000 |
| **Balance as per bank statement** | **₹7,95,000** |

8,40,000 + 60,000 − 1,05,000 = 7,95,000. So the difference is explained; the money is not missing. If a difference still remains after the calculation, that is the "unexplained difference" which the treasurer must first find and then report.

> 💡 **For the accountant/treasurer:** Re-doing this matching by hand every month is exactly the work that gets put off. SahakarLekha's **Bank Reconciliation** page works out and shows the difference between the bank balance and the books, and lets you save a **sign-off record** of the reconciliation — so the auditor finds the answer to "when was the BRS made, and by whom" in the books themselves. The *decision* in the matching (what the reason for each entry is) still remains yours. Step-by-step help: [How to do Bank Reconciliation (Help)](/help/bank-reconciliation).

## 3. Voucher check checklist

A voucher is the "proof" of every transaction. The treasurer does not make every voucher personally, but **checking** it before payment or at the end of the month is a large part of the treasurer's responsibility. Check these eight things on every voucher:

1. **Serial number and date** — no number missed or repeated.
2. **Name of the party** — who is paid, from whom received — clearly written.
3. **Amount in figures and words** — both should match.
4. **Supporting document** — bill/invoice/resolution/receipt attached. (What to attach: [Voucher narration and documents](/blog/voucher-narration-and-documents).)
5. **Approval** — signature of the designated person, and a managing committee resolution for large amounts.
6. **Correct account head (ledger head)** — the expense is posted to the right head, keeping in mind the difference between capital and revenue expenditure.
7. **Tax side** — GST/TDS where applicable (more: [TDS and 26Q](/blog/tds-and-26q-for-societies), [GST](/blog/gst-for-cooperatives)).
8. **Mode of payment** — cash, cheque or online; a large amount is not paid in cash. (Modes: [Bank payment methods](/blog/bank-payment-methods-cheque-dd-upi).)

**One rule that helps the most:** *the person who makes and the person who approves should be different* (Maker–Checker). If the person who makes the voucher also withdraws the money and writes the books, there is no one left to catch a mistake or fraud.

> 💡 **For the secretary/treasurer:** On paper, getting a signature on every voucher and finding "which vouchers stayed without approval" is the most tiring part. SahakarLekha's **Voucher Approval** page shows pending, approved and rejected vouchers in separate tabs, so that the reviewer can see all pending vouchers in one place and approve or reject them. (Full method of making vouchers: [Voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide).)

## 4. Reports — what, when and to whom

The treasurer's work is not only checking, but also **placing the true picture before the managing committee**. These reports, made regularly, are enough:

| Report | When | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily cash balance | Daily/weekly | Cash position and matching with the count |
| Monthly BRS | Once a month | Matching the bank and the books |
| Trial Balance | Once a month | Arithmetical accuracy of the books — [Trial Balance](/blog/trial-balance-explained) |
| Receipts & Payments | Monthly/quarterly | Picture of the cash flow |
| Overdue/outstanding list | Monthly | Watch on recovery — [MIS reports](/blog/mis-reports-for-management) |
| Annual financial statements | Year-end | Within 3 months as per Rule 79 |

Reports should not be just figures — every report should have a note of two or three lines: **what changed since last month, which difference is unresolved, and what decision is needed.** This is what people expect to hear from the treasurer in the meeting.

> 💡 **For the treasurer/secretary:** Before a meeting, most of the time in making reports goes into collecting figures. In SahakarLekha the **Trial Balance**, **Receipts & Payments** and **Reports** pages are built from the same books you fill in every day — so before the meeting you do not have to add up separate registers. (To learn how to read them: [How to read financial reports](/blog/how-to-read-financial-reports).)

## 5. How to keep records organised for the audit

Under Section 95(3) the auditor has *open access* to books, papers, vouchers, stock and other property, and under Section 95(4) every office-bearer must give the information asked for. So "searching for records before the audit" is the costliest habit. Keep this instead:

- [ ] **Voucher file** — month by month, in serial order, with supporting documents.
- [ ] **Bank file** — monthly statements, BRS, remaining cheque-book leaves, FDR receipts.
- [ ] **Receipt books** — complete, in order, with cancelled receipts pasted in.
- [ ] **Resolution register** — managing committee resolutions for large expenses, loans and investments.
- [ ] **Custody list** — as per Rule 36 and Section 49, who has which register/property, and the custodian's signature; **a new list when the post changes** and a complete handover.
- [ ] **Compliance on objections** of the previous audit — what action was taken on which, in writing.

Pay special attention to handover of posts: Section 117(5) provides a penalty when a custodian wilfully fails to hand over records/cash. So whenever the treasurer changes, there should be a **written handover** of the cash count, bank balance, keys, cheque books and registers — with the signatures of both. (Full audit preparation: [Audit preparation checklist](/blog/audit-preparation-checklist), and for year-end [Year-end and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).)

> 💡 **For the secretary:** You will still have to keep paper files, but in SahakarLekha the digital record of every voucher, its narration and approval status stay in one place, so when the auditor says "show me the voucher and approval for this payment", you can open that very page from the books in a few clicks.

## A small imaginary example

*(Imaginary — not of any real society.)* Suppose in a small society the secretary takes cash every day and also writes the books, and the treasurer comes once a month and signs. At the year-end audit the cash count comes out ₹4,200 short. Receipts are incomplete, two vouchers have no approval, and the BRS has not been made for the last four months.

If the treasurer had done just three things every week — a surprise count, a check of pending vouchers, and matching the BRS at the end of the month — the difference would have shown up in the first week, when it is easy to find. This is the essence of this whole article: **the treasurer's work is not big, it is regular.**

## Common mistakes

1. **Only signing, not checking.** A signature means you have looked; if you signed without looking, the responsibility is still yours.
2. **Not counting cash for months.** The difference keeps growing and finding the reason becomes impossible.
3. **Putting off the BRS.** Bank charges, interest and cheque returns get missed.
4. **The maker is also the approver.** If Maker–Checker breaks, control breaks.
5. **Keeping extra cash in the safe.** The most common cause of theft, counting errors and audit objections.
6. **Payment without a supporting document.** "We'll give the bill later" — later it often never comes.
7. **Handover of post without a handover letter.** The new treasurer becomes accountable for old irregularities.
8. **Everything verbal.** If differences, decisions and warnings are not in writing, you have no protection yourself.
9. **Mixing personal and society money.** Not under any circumstances.
10. **Treating an audit objection as "just the auditor's view".** Take written action on every objection.

## Three things to remember

- 🔢 The treasurer's work is not big, it is regular: daily cash count, monthly BRS and voucher check, quarterly/yearly reports and audit records.
- ⚖️ The law sets cash verification, custody of records and audit access; the treasurer's duties, cash limit and co-signature come from the bye-laws/RCS orders/internal policy.
- 🧾 The maker and approver should be different, every difference should be in writing, and when the post changes the handover should be in writing.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a koshadhyaksh?** — The office-bearer of the society who watches over the safety of the funds (cash and bank) and the truth of the accounts; in English, Treasurer, and in Roman script, Koshadhyaksh.
**What does Koshadhyaksh in Hindi mean?** — "Kosh + adhyaksh": the person in charge of the treasury, that is, the Treasurer. In small societies the person is also called khazanchi.
**What is the main accounting work of the treasurer?** — Matching the physical count of cash, bank reconciliation (BRS), checking vouchers, reporting to the managing committee, and keeping records organised for the audit.
**Are the treasurer's duties written in the law?** — In the text of the Haryana Act 1984 and Rules 1989 that I read, no list of the treasurer's duties was found; they are set by the bye-laws, RCS orders and practice. Under Section 2(m) the treasurer is an "officer".
**How often should cash be counted?** — Ideally every day at the end of the day, plus a surprise check weekly/monthly. How often also depends on the bye-laws/instructions of the higher institution.
**How much cash can be kept in the safe?** — I found no fixed limit in the law; it is set by the bye-laws, bank/RCS instructions or the society's policy.
**What if there is a difference between the cash count and the Cash Book?** — Find the reason the same day; if not found, record it in writing and report it to the secretary/chairman, and do not hide it.
**Who should make the BRS?** — Usually the accountant or secretary makes it and the treasurer checks/signs it; the exact arrangement depends on the bye-laws and the society's practice.
**What has to be handed over when the post changes?** — Cash, bank balance, cheque books, receipt books, registers and keys — with a written handover. Under Rule 36, a new list is made when the custodian changes.
**What should the treasurer do in a PACS?** — Follow the procedure of your PACS's prescribed portal (e-PACS) — day-end, Maker-Checker etc.; this article does not replace it, it supplements it.

## In the end

The post of treasurer is one of honour, but in reality it is a post of *trust*. Everything comes out clean in the audit only when the small daily habits — counting, matching, checking, written records — hold firm. A society that builds these habits is not afraid of the audit.
