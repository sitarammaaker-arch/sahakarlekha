# Before year-end: 10 essential jobs (preparing for FY closing)

As March comes closer, every society has to prepare to "close" the financial year. If these jobs are done **in advance**, both the year-end and the audit become smooth. Here is a 10-job readiness checklist — with the "why" of each job and practical tips.

## Make preparation a "process", not an "emergency"

In many societies the year-end arrives like an "emergency" — suddenly someone remembers that March has come, and then there is a rush. But in fact the year-end comes on the same date every year, so it should be treated not as an emergency but as a fixed yearly process. When this process is fixed in advance — who will do what, and by when — the year-end becomes smooth. This checklist is the framework of that process.

## Why prepare early

The biggest problem at year-end is not "work" but "time" — trying to do everything together in the last week of March brings stress, haste and mistakes. The solution is simple: start preparing from February. Finish the jobs that can be done earlier (recovery, stock count, bank reconciliation, GST/TDS filing) first, so that at the end of March only the final entries, Trial Balance and final accounts are left. This "advance preparation" turns the year-end from a rush into a smooth process.

## 10 essential jobs before year-end
1. **All transactions recorded** — no voucher should be missed.
2. **Bank reconciliation (BRS)** of every account up to date.
3. Push on **recovery of dues** — reduce NPA. (See [loan recovery](/blog/loan-recovery-before-year-end).)
4. **Physical count of stock** + matching with the system.
5. A single calculation of **closing stock** fixed.
6. Calculation of **depreciation** ready. (See [how to calculate depreciation](/blog/depreciation-explained).)
7. **Outstanding adjustments** — expenses payable, income receivable.
8. Tally the **Trial Balance** (total Dr = total Cr).
9. All pending **GST/TDS** filings complete.
10. **Bills/receipts** attached in order with every large voucher.

> 💡 Do all this in good time — you will avoid the rush of the last week of March, and the final accounts will be made easily.

### Split these jobs into two parts

An easy way to understand the preparation — split the jobs into "can be done earlier" and "to be done at the end":

**Start from February (time-consuming jobs):**
- Recovery of dues and reducing NPA — this takes the most time.
- Physical count of stock and matching.
- Completing pending bank reconciliations.
- Clearing missed GST/TDS filings.
- Collecting missing bills/receipts and attaching them to vouchers.

**Do at the end of March (final jobs):**
- All transactions up to the last day recorded.
- Closing stock value fixed.
- Entries for depreciation and outstanding adjustments.
- Tallying the Trial Balance.

This split shows that most of the preparation can actually be done earlier — only the closing is left for the end of March.

## Printable readiness checklist

Print this, put it up in the office and tick each job when it is done:

- ☐ All vouchers up to the last day recorded.
- ☐ BRS of every bank account complete, balance matched.
- ☐ Outstanding list made, recovery drive run, NPA reduced.
- ☐ Physical count of stock, matched with the system.
- ☐ Closing stock value fixed (single).
- ☐ Depreciation on every asset.
- ☐ Adjustments for expenses/income payable/receivable and prepaid items.
- ☐ Trial Balance tallied (Dr = Cr).
- ☐ GST/TDS filing complete (if applicable).
- ☐ Bill/receipt with every large voucher.
- ☐ Previous audit objections resolved.
- ☐ Member/share register up to date and matched with the books.

This one-page list alone avoids most year-end confusion — because what is written down and ticked does not get missed.

## The "why" of each job

- **Transactions recorded:** Even one missed voucher spoils the whole Balance Sheet, so make sure every entry is in.
- **BRS:** The difference between the books and the bank is caught here; missed bank charges/interest and direct deposits get recorded in the books.
- **Recovery:** High dues not only block cash but also lower the audit grade, so give this priority.
- **Stock count:** The only reliable way to catch the difference between actual goods and book stock.
- **Depreciation/adjustments:** Without these, profit looks inflated (wrong), and a dividend distributed on it reduces capital.
- **Trial Balance:** The first and compulsory condition for final accounts — total Dr = total Cr.
- **GST/TDS:** Risk of interest/penalty on late filing (where applicable); clear them on time.
- **Documents:** The bill for every large voucher is the first thing asked for in audit; if it is missing, an objection is raised.

## A practical scenario

Suppose a PACS starts preparing in February. First it pulls out the list of defaulters and runs a recovery drive (the most time-consuming job); side by side it does a physical count of stock in the godown and matches it with the system; it completes the pending bank reconciliation of three months; and it clears any missed GST/TDS filing. All this is done by the third week of March. Then, at the end of March, it records vouchers up to the last day, fixes the closing stock value, puts through depreciation and expenses-payable adjustments, and tallies the Trial Balance.

The result — while other societies are rushing at the end of March, this society calmly prepares its final accounts, because 80% of the work was already done. This is the real benefit of "advance preparation".

## The cost of not preparing

If preparation is put off, many problems come together at year-end: no time is left for recovery (dues stay high, the grade falls); in the hurry the stock count is skipped or goes wrong; bank reconciliation stays incomplete, so the Trial Balance does not tally; and missing bills cannot be found at the last moment, which become audit objections. So putting off preparation is not just stress — it directly affects the grade and the audit. That is why "a little at a time, early" is always better than "everything at the end".

## Then close the year
When everything is confirmed, make the final accounts and **FY-lock** — so that the figures stay fixed during the audit and next year's opening links correctly. (Full process: [year-end and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).)

## A simple timeline

- **February:** Recovery drive, planning the stock count, pending reconciliations.
- **March (first 3 weeks):** Bank reconciliations complete, GST/TDS filing, documents collected.
- **End of March / start of April:** Final entries, adjustments, Trial Balance, final accounts.
- **Approval and lock:** FY-lock after approval by the general body.

Each society can move this timeline a little forward or back as it suits them — the main point is not to leave "everything for the last week".

## Recovery — first and most important

Of all the preparation jobs, **recovery of dues** takes the most time and effort, so start it first (from February). High dues do harm in two ways — the society's cash gets stuck, and the audit grade falls. To improve recovery: pull out the list of defaulters, send them reminders/notices, give incentives to those who pay on time (where the rules allow), and follow up continuously. A society that focuses on recovery before year-end sees its dues fall and its grade improve. (See: [loan recovery](/blog/loan-recovery-before-year-end).)

## Stock count — a job that is often put off

For societies that sell goods (consumer stores, PACS), a physical count of stock is very important, but it is often put off. Without a count the "book stock" stays unreliable, and wrong closing stock directly makes the profit wrong. So fix a day before year-end, count the whole stock, match it with the system, and if there is a difference, make an adjustment with reasons. This one day of work makes the whole year's profit figure reliable. (See: [inventory and stock management](/blog/inventory-and-stock-management).)

## Common mistakes

- **Putting off preparation till the end of March** → rush, haste and mistakes in the last week.
- **Starting recovery at the end** → this is the most time-consuming job, start it from February itself.
- **Not counting stock** → book stock stays unreliable, and with wrong closing stock the profit is also wrong.
- **Leaving documents for later** → finding missing bills at year-end is hard; attach them to vouchers in good time.
- **Making accounts without tallying the Trial Balance** → the final accounts will not tally; first make Dr=Cr.
- **Delaying GST/TDS filing** → amounts due/penalties may remain outstanding (if applicable).
- **Treating preparation as an emergency every year** → make it a fixed yearly process.

## Who does what in the preparation

Year-end preparation is not the secretary's job alone — sharing the roles makes it faster and more accurate:

- **Secretary/accountant:** Completing entries, bank reconciliation, adjustments and Trial Balance.
- **Treasurer:** Cash counting, checking large payments, looking at financial reports.
- **Managing committee:** Monitoring the recovery drive, policy decisions, reviewing the final accounts.
- **Members:** Helping by paying their dues on time — recovery becomes easier.

When everyone does their part on time, the burden of preparation does not fall on any one person, and the year-end runs smoothly.

## The foundation of next year is also laid here

One point is often forgotten — good year-end preparation does not only make this year's audit easy, it also lays the foundation for next year. Because this year's correct, locked closing is what becomes next year's correct opening. So the effort you put in now pays off for two years — clean final accounts this year, and a correct start next year. (See: [new financial year and budget](/blog/new-financial-year-and-budget).)

## Frequently asked questions
**When should year-end preparation start?** — Do not wait for March; start recovery of dues, stock count and bank reconciliation from February itself.
**When should I FY-lock?** — When the Trial Balance tallies, the final accounts are made and the audit figures are fixed — only then lock.
**How do I fix closing stock?** — Match the physical count with the system and fix a single value, so that the year's profit comes out right.
**Which job comes first?** — Recovery, because it takes the most time; start it from February.
**Do all these have to be done together?** — No; most can be done earlier, only the final entries and accounts are left for the end of March.
**Why collect documents early?** — Finding missing bills at year-end is hard; attach them to vouchers in good time.
**Why link GST/TDS filing to year-end?** — So that no amount due/filing is left pending and the final accounts come out clean.
**Who has what role in the preparation?** — Secretary (entries/reconciliation), treasurer (cash/reports), managing committee (monitoring), members (dues on time).
**Why print the checklist?** — What is written down and ticked does not get missed; a one-page list avoids most confusion.
**What is the long-term benefit of good preparation?** — An easy audit this year, and a correct start next year from a correct closing.
**Do I have to repeat this every year?** — Yes; make it a yearly process, not an emergency — then it will be smooth every year.

## Three things to remember
- 🗓️ Prepare **early** — do not wait for the end of March.
- ⚖️ Do not make final accounts without tallying the Trial Balance.
- 🔒 FY-lock only when everything is correct.
- 👥 Share the roles — secretary, treasurer, managing committee and members, everyone contributes.

## In the end
Year-end preparation is a checklist job. Split these 10 jobs into two parts (February's and end-of-March's) and finish them in good time — and the year-end will become not stress but just a smooth, orderly process. A society that adopts this discipline has an easy audit and a better grade too. And the best part — it needs no expertise, just time management and a little planning. Once this becomes a yearly habit, the year-end every year becomes not stress but a smooth and satisfying close — where you can proudly place clean accounts before the members.
