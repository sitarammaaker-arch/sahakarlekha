# Annual and statutory returns of a cooperative society: the essential jobs after the year closes

Even after the financial year closes and the audit is done, one important job of the society is still left — filing the **annual and statutory returns of the cooperative society** on time. This is not just a formality but legal compliance; a lapse brings penalties, interest, and affects even the society's credit standing and audit grading. The trouble is that these returns do not go to one place but to **four different departments** — the cooperative department, income tax, TDS and (where applicable) GST. This article explains each one in simple language, with examples, dates and sources.

## Annual returns of a cooperative society — four layers at a glance

The same society may have to file these returns, depending on its activity and registration:

1. **To the cooperative department (Registrar)** — final accounts + audit + annual general meeting (AGM).
2. **To the income tax department** — ITR-5, and the 44AB audit report where applicable.
3. **TDS** — quarterly returns (26Q/24Q) and certificates, where the society has deducted tax.
4. **GST** — GSTR-9 / GSTR-9C, where the society is registered under GST.

> ⚠️ It is not necessary that all four apply to every society. Which ones apply is decided by your activity, turnover and registration. Confirm your list and due dates with the cooperative department / your CA.

## 1) To the cooperative department (Registrar) — accounts, audit and AGM

This is the basic accountability of every cooperative society:

- Preparing the **final accounts** (income and expenditure, profit and loss, Balance Sheet, Receipts & Payments) after 31 March.
- **Annual audit** — by an auditor on the Registrar's panel; the report goes to both the Registrar and the society. The time limit depends on the state — for example, under **Section 81** of the Maharashtra Cooperative Societies Act, 1960, **within 4 months** of the end of the year (by 31 July). (See: [Section 81, Maharashtra Cooperative Act](https://sahakarayukta.maharashtra.gov.in/site/upload/documents/Relevant_extract_of_Section_81_of_Maharashtra_Co-Operative_Societies_Act_1960.pdf))
- **Annual general meeting (AGM)** — within the set time, in which the audited accounts and audit report are placed before the members and the distribution of profit is passed.

**What generally goes into the annual return:** the number of members and the growth during the year, paid-up share capital and reserve fund, the year's business and working results (profit/loss), loan disbursement and recovery, and details of the managing committee. All these figures come directly from your final accounts and registers.

> 💡 In most states, missing the audit and AGM dates can lead to action against office-bearers, and against societies that default repeatedly / are inactive the Registrar can take action (even cancellation of registration). (See: [how a cooperative society audit works](/blog/how-cooperative-society-audit-works) and [year-end and FY lock](/blog/year-end-closing-and-fy-lock).)

## 2) Income tax — ITR-5, Section 80P and 44AB audit

For income tax, a cooperative society is a separate taxpayer and has to file its return in **ITR-5**.

- **Section 80P deduction:** Cooperative societies get a special deduction on certain income (such as on activities like agricultural credit, processing etc.). Under Section **80P(2)(c)** there is also a deduction of up to ₹50,000 in some cases. Note — if the society chooses the concessional rate of **Section 115BAD/115BAE**, the 80P deduction is not available. (See: [Section 80P — cooperative society deduction](https://cleartax.in/s/section-80p))
- **44AB (tax audit):** If the society does business and its turnover is more than the set limit (generally **₹1 crore**, ₹10 crore under some conditions), a separate **44AB tax audit by a CA** is compulsory — even if the cooperative department's audit has already been done.
- **Due date:** If 44AB does not apply, generally **31 July**; in an audit case, usually **31 October**. But income tax dates keep changing/being extended every year — confirm the current year's date from [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/).

**Which activities 80P covers (broadly):** Under Section 80P(2)(a) come societies giving credit/banking to members, marketing of members' agricultural produce, cottage industries, supply of agricultural inputs (seeds, implements) to members, and processing of agricultural produce without the aid of power, among other activities. Note — **cooperative banks** (except some exceptions) are outside the benefit of 80P. Check exact eligibility with your CA. (See: [Section 80P — cooperative society deduction](https://cleartax.in/s/section-80p))

**Example:** Suppose a credit society's taxable income is ₹2,00,000 and its activity comes within the scope of 80P — then after the eligible deduction, the tax liability can come down a lot. But the deduction is available **only** when the ITR is filed on time and the accounts are correct.

## 3) TDS — quarterly return (26Q) and certificates

If the society has **deducted TDS** on salary, contractors, rent, interest etc., it has to file quarterly TDS returns:

- **24Q** for salary, **26Q** for other payments.
- The return for the fourth quarter (January–March) is generally due by **31 May**. (See: [TDS return forms](https://tax2win.in/guide/tds-return-form))
- After the return, issuing the deduction certificate (**Form 16/16A**).

**Example:** Suppose the society paid a contractor ₹1,00,000 in the year and deducted TDS on it at the applicable rate. This deduction is deposited every quarter, and at the end of the quarter it is entered in **26Q** and goes into the return; then the contractor is given **Form 16A**, so that he can take his credit.

> ⚠️ A late TDS return can attract a late fee of ₹200/day (Section 234E) — so do include Q4 in the year-end work. Correct rates and limits depend on the applicable situation. (See: [TDS and 26Q for cooperative societies](/blog/tds-and-26q-for-societies).)

## 4) GST — GSTR-9 / GSTR-9C (where registered)

If the society is registered under GST (such as a consumer/marketing society that sells goods):

- **GSTR-9 (annual return)** — compulsory when the total turnover is **more than ₹2 crore**.
- **GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement)** — when the turnover is **more than ₹5 crore**.
- The due date for both is **31 December** of the next financial year. (See: [GSTR-9 annual return](https://cleartax.in/s/gstr-9-annual-return))

GSTR-9 is really a consolidated summary of the whole year's **GSTR-1 (sales) and GSTR-3B (tax payment)** — total outward supplies, input tax credit (ITC) taken, and tax paid, all in one place. So if the monthly/quarterly GST returns were filed correctly all year, GSTR-9 is left as just a matching job.

**Example:** A consumer society with a turnover of ₹3 crore will have to file GSTR-9, but not GSTR-9C (because it is below ₹5 crore). (See: [GST in cooperative societies](/blog/gst-for-cooperatives).)

## Which return for which society — a quick decision

Not all four layers apply to every society. Broadly:

- **Every cooperative society** — the cooperative department (audit + annual return + AGM) and income tax (ITR-5) generally apply.
- **One that has deducted tax on salary/contract/rent/interest** — TDS returns (24Q/26Q) apply.
- **One that sells goods/services and is registered under GST** (consumer, marketing, some multipurpose) — GSTR-9/9C apply as per turnover.
- **Credit society** — is often eligible for the 80P deduction; cooperative banks are the exception.

> 💡 The first step is this: sit down and decide which of these four apply to you — half the confusion ends right here.

## A year-end returns calendar (example)

Every society's calendar depends on its activity, but a practical framework:

- **April–May:** Final accounts closed; **Q4 TDS return (~31 May)**.
- **June–July:** Cooperative department audit (as per state, e.g. by 31 July); AGM preparation.
- **July–October:** Income tax ITR-5 (date as per audit status).
- **By December:** GSTR-9/9C (if applicable).

> 📌 Write all these dates in one list — this "due-date calendar" halves the stress of the whole year.

## How to make them easy — with clean accounts

All these returns are built from your **final accounts and clean records**:

- Final accounts (income and expenditure, Balance Sheet, Receipts & Payments) ready → the foundation of both the department and income tax returns.
- Correct member/share/loan numbers → the annual return directly.
- TDS/GST entries correct all year → quarterly and annual returns are just a matter of "pulling out". (See: [Trial Balance](/blog/trial-balance-explained).)

> 💡 If the accounts were kept clean all year, these returns become **as easy as pulling out a report** — no separate adding and subtracting. SahakarLekha is built on this idea.

## Consequences of delay and default

Each layer has its own penalty, so do not take the dates lightly:

- **Late TDS return** — a late fee of **₹200/day** under Section 234E (limited to the tax amount), along with the risk of a penalty for wrong/late filing.
- **Late income tax return** — the late fee under Section 234F, and interest under Section 234A/B/C on tax due; if not filed on time, some deductions/losses are not carried forward.
- **Late GSTR-9** — a late fee per day (CGST+SGST), limited to a percentage of total turnover.
- **Late to the cooperative department** — falling grade, effect on future borrowing capacity, and action against office-bearers.

> ⚠️ Most dates and fees are changed/extended from time to time — always confirm the current year's exact date and amount from the relevant portal / your CA.

## Filing for the first time? A simple order

1. **Close the accounts** — final accounts ready right after 31 March.
2. **Make the applicability list** — decide which returns apply to you (department always; income tax generally; TDS/GST depending on the situation).
3. **Due-date calendar** — write down the date of every return in one place.
4. **Gather documents** — audit report, TDS/GST records, member/share register.
5. **File and keep the receipt safe** — file the acknowledgement of every return filed.

## Checklist

- ✅ Final accounts and audit report ready.
- ✅ **Due date** of every return (department/income tax/TDS/GST) noted.
- ✅ Whether the 80P deduction and 44AB apply — confirmed with the CA.
- ✅ **Receipt/acknowledgement** of every return filed kept safe.
- ✅ Compliance on previous audit objections recorded.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Assuming that the department's audit alone = everything is done** — income tax/TDS/GST returns are separate. Avoid it: check all four layers separately.
- **Forgetting the 44AB audit** — it is compulsory once the business turnover crosses the limit. Avoid it: keep an eye on turnover.
- **Late Q4 TDS** — ₹200/day fee. Avoid it: file it at the start of May itself.
- **Not keeping the receipt** — no proof later. Avoid it: file a copy of every return.

## Three things to remember

- 🗓️ Due dates of all four layers (department/income tax/TDS/GST) in one list — delay brings penalties and hurts credit standing.
- 🧾 Keep the receipt/copy of every return filed safe.
- 📊 All figures should match the final accounts — no separate estimates.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every cooperative society have to file an income tax return?** — Generally yes, the society files its return in ITR-5; if eligible, it gets the Section 80P deduction. Confirm the date and applicability with your CA.
**The department's audit is done, so is a 44AB audit also needed?** — Yes, if the business turnover is more than the set limit — the two are separate. The cooperative department audit does not take the place of 44AB.
**By when is the Q4 TDS return due?** — Generally 31 May (for the January–March quarter). A fee of ₹200/day on delay.
**Who has to file GSTR-9?** — A GST-registered society, if its annual turnover is more than ₹2 crore; above ₹5 crore, GSTR-9C as well.
**How do I make returns easy?** — Keep clean accounts all year; then every return is made directly from the final accounts.
**Is the AGM necessary?** — Yes; the audited accounts and distribution of profit have to be passed by the members in the AGM, within the set time limit (as per the state Act).
**What is the benefit of filing returns on time?** — Avoiding penalties/interest, better audit grading and credit standing, easier future borrowing, and the trust of members.
**Does a dormant society also have to file returns?** — As long as the registration is active, generally yes — even with zero business the status has to be reported to the department; on repeated default the Registrar can take action. Confirm the correct position with the department.
**Is an ITR needed even when the tax liability is zero?** — As per eligibility and rules, often yes (especially, the return has to be filed to claim the 80P deduction) — confirm with your CA.

## In the end

The annual and statutory returns of a cooperative society are the last, but most essential, link in the year's work. Look at them in four layers — department, income tax, TDS, GST — keep the due date of each in one list, and keep the accounts clean all year. Then these returns become not a burden but a job as easy as pulling out a report.
