# AGM (Annual General Meeting) preparation: a complete checklist

Every cooperative society has to hold an **Annual General Meeting (AGM)** once a year — where last year's accounts, the audit report and the profit distribution are placed before the members. It is the biggest platform for the society's transparency, and the foundation of members' trust. This article makes AGM preparation simple, step by step — why it matters, what to keep ready, what to do on the day of the meeting, and how to avoid common mistakes.

## What is an AGM and why is it necessary

The AGM is the once-a-year meeting of all members of the society, where the society places an account of its work and finances before the members and gets necessary decisions approved. It is the soul of democratic cooperation — this is where the members, who are the real owners of the society, have their say and take part in decisions.

Main purposes of the AGM:

- Placing the **accounts + audit report** before the members.
- Getting the **profit distribution** (dividend/reserve) approved.
- Next year's direction, budget and necessary decisions.
- Decisions about the board/directors (where applicable).

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** The AGM time limit, notice period and quorum differ according to each state's cooperative Act/bye-laws. The Haryana part below has been matched with the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 (including the 2013 amendment) and the Rules 1989 (see "Sources"). The notice period and quorum are set by your society's bye-laws — confirm them from there.

## Preparation checklist (before the AGM)

The success of an AGM lies in its preparation. Keep these documents and tasks ready before the meeting:

- ✅ Last year's **final accounts** ready — Income & Expenditure, Balance Sheet, Receipts & Payments.
- ✅ **Audit report** and compliance on past objections.
- ✅ **Profit distribution** proposal (reserve, dividend, bonus). (See [Profit distribution](/blog/profit-distribution-and-reserves).)
- ✅ **Member list** updated and correct.
- ✅ AGM **notice** sent on time (with agenda).
- ✅ **Minutes** of the last meeting.
- ✅ Next year's **budget** proposal.

> 💡 If the accounts have been clean all year, all AGM documents are ready **in one click** — no rush in the last week.

### Importance of each document

- **Final accounts:** These are the centre of the AGM — from them members learn how the society did over the year. So they should be ready and reconciled before the audit.
- **Audit report:** The auditor's independent opinion — it gives members confidence that the accounts are correct. Keep the compliance on past objections with it too. (See: [Audit preparation](/blog/audit-preparation-checklist).)
- **Profit distribution proposal:** How much of the profit goes to reserve, how much to dividend — only the AGM approves this; the proposal should be ready in advance.
- **Member list:** A correct, updated list is necessary for voting and quorum.
- **Notice:** Sending the notice on time, with the agenda, is compulsory — a late or incomplete notice can make the meeting invalid.
- **Minutes and budget:** The record of the last meeting's decisions, and next year's plan.

## AGM notice

Sending the AGM notice on time, in the right way and with the agenda is among the most important steps. The notice usually states clearly the date, time and place of the meeting, and the agenda (subjects for discussion). Members should get enough time to prepare and attend. Also keep proof of sending the notice (register/receipt), because it is useful in an audit or a dispute. The notice period and method are set by the bye-laws/Act — confirm them first.

## On the day of the AGM

- First check whether the quorum (minimum attendance) is complete.
- Take the agenda in order; record the approval on each proposal.
- Write the **minutes** carefully — they become the evidence in the next audit.

### Running the meeting smoothly

On the day of the meeting, the chairman's job is to keep the meeting orderly: start with confirming the quorum, take the agenda in order, give members a fair chance to speak on each subject, and record the result after taking a clear vote/consent on each proposal. Answer members' questions respectfully — the AGM is the members' platform, so encourage their participation. Calm, transparent conduct builds trust, and the meeting stays free of disputes.

## Minutes — why they matter so much

The AGM minutes are not just a formality — they are the society's permanent, legal record. They should record: the date/place of the meeting, the number of members present (quorum), a summary of the discussion on each agenda item, and the decision taken on each proposal (approved/rejected, by how many votes). These minutes become the evidence in the next audit, and protect the society in any dispute. So write them carefully, in the same meeting or right after, and authenticate them with the chairman's signature.

## A sample AGM agenda

A typical AGM agenda can follow an order like this (adjust it as per your bye-laws/Act):

1. Confirmation of quorum and the chairman's welcome.
2. Confirmation of the minutes of the last general meeting.
3. Consideration and approval of last year's final accounts (Income & Expenditure, Balance Sheet).
4. Consideration of the audit report and compliance on objections.
5. Decision on the profit distribution (reserve/dividend/bonus) proposal.
6. Consideration of next year's budget and plans.
7. Other statutory subjects (such as the defaulters list, creation of funds, bye-law amendment — where applicable).
8. Any other subject with the chairman's permission.

This order keeps the meeting organised and ensures no necessary subject is missed.

## Role of members in the AGM

The AGM is the platform of the members, not just of the management — and only the active participation of members makes it meaningful. Here members can ask questions on the accounts, give suggestions, vote on proposals, and hold the management accountable. So the society should give members the chance to prepare by giving them notice and documents on time, and answer their questions openly in the meeting. In a society where members stay active, both transparency and trust stay strong — and that is the real strength of cooperation.

## AGM vs Special General Meeting (SGM)

The AGM is the regular once-a-year meeting, which is compulsory. Besides this, when needed, a **Special General Meeting** can also be called (in Haryana, under Section 26(1), on a written demand from the Registrar or from the number of members set in the bye-laws, the society has to call a general meeting **within one month**; if it does not, the Registrar can call it himself — Section 26(2)) — for a specific, urgent subject (such as a big decision, bye-law amendment, or an extraordinary situation). The difference between the two is that the AGM's agenda and timing are fixed, while an SGM is called in between for a special reason. The rules of notice, quorum and minutes apply to both. Which meeting is needed when is set by the bye-laws/Act.

## How a digital system makes the AGM easier

The biggest effort for the AGM goes into preparing documents — final accounts, audit-ready reports, member list, profit distribution calculation. When the society uses a digital system, all of this comes ready automatically, in one click, from the year's clean accounts. The member list stays updated, the Balance Sheet and Income & Expenditure come out instantly, and the profit distribution calculation is also accurate. This makes AGM preparation not a last-week rush but a smooth job — and the secretary's stress goes down a lot.

## AGM in Haryana: who calls it, what is compulsory on the agenda
**By when:** After the 2013 amendment, **Section 25(3)** of the Haryana Act says the general meeting shall be held **within 6 months of the close of the financial year** (earlier it said "once a year"). That means the AGM for the year ending 31 March by 30 September.

**Compulsory agenda (Section 25(3)):** approving next year's programme; considering the audit report and annual report; reviewing the past year's work; reviewing the society's condition, including the list of defaulters (with amounts due); creating special reserves and funds and their use; considering the list of employees who are relatives of office-bearers; election of the managing committee if needed; bye-laws amendment; and from 2013, **appointment of an auditor or auditing firm from the panel**.

**Who calls it:** Under **Rule 23**, a general meeting or committee meeting is called by the society's Chief Executive Officer (or an officer authorised by him), on the direction of the authority set in the bye-laws.

> ⚠️ **Consequence of not holding the AGM — Section 26(3):** If the general meeting is not held within the period of Section 25(3), the Registrar, after giving an opportunity of hearing, can declare **the members of the managing committee disqualified for 5 years from continuing on that committee and from contesting the election of the committee of any society**; and if the default is of an office-bearer or employee, a **fine of up to ₹10,000** can be imposed on him. So do not take the AGM date lightly.

**Two more important points:**
- Under Rule 16, no new member can be admitted within **14 days before** the AGM date.
- After 2013, under Section 48, every society has to file a return with the Registrar within 6 months of the close of the year, which also includes the **declaration of the general meeting date**.

> ⚠️ If the managing committee election is also to be held in the same AGM, the normal AGM notice is not enough — the separate election process and notice conditions of Appendix-A must be met separately. (See: [Society elections](/blog/cooperative-society-elections).)

## After the AGM

The work does not end when the meeting ends. After the AGM: finalise the minutes and keep them on record, implement the approved profit distribution, start acting on the decisions taken in the meeting, and where necessary (such as under the Act), inform the cooperative department/Registrar about the meeting and its decisions. This way the AGM's decisions do not stay only on paper but are carried out on the ground.

## Preparation timeline

AGM preparation should start well in advance, not in the last week. A rough outline:

- **Right after year-end:** Close and prepare the final accounts (closing stock, depreciation, adjustments).
- **After this:** Get the audit done and comply with the objections.
- **Before the AGM (as per the notice period):** Send the notice and agenda, prepare the profit distribution proposal and budget, update the member list.
- **On the day of the meeting:** Check the quorum, run the agenda in order, write the minutes.
- **After the meeting:** Finalise the minutes, implement decisions, inform the department where necessary.

Following this timeline spreads out the AGM preparation and gets it done without stress. Confirm exact dates and periods from your bye-laws/Act.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending the notice late/incomplete** → notice on time, with agenda; keep the proof.
- **Not checking the quorum** → decisions of a meeting without quorum can be invalid.
- **Writing minutes carelessly** → record every decision clearly and authenticated.
- **Preparing documents at the last moment** → keep clean accounts all year, and everything is ready.
- **Avoiding members' questions** → the AGM is the members' platform; answer with transparency.
- **Not acting after the AGM** → implement the approved decisions and keep records.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an AGM?** — The once-a-year meeting of all members of the society, where the accounts, audit report and profit distribution are placed and approved.
**Why is the AGM necessary?** — For transparency, approval of profit distribution, and decisions with members' participation.
**By when must the AGM be held in Haryana?** — Within 6 months of the close of the financial year (Section 25(3), 2013 amendment); if not held, committee members can be disqualified for 5 years under Section 26(3).
**What is the AGM time limit and notice period?** — Different as per the state's Act/bye-laws; confirm the rules that apply to your society from the cooperative department/bye-laws.
**What is a quorum?** — The minimum attendance needed for a meeting to be valid; check it before starting the meeting.
**What should be kept ready before the AGM?** — Final accounts, audit report, profit distribution proposal, member list, notice, past minutes and budget.
**Why are the minutes important?** — They are the society's permanent, legal record and become the evidence in the next audit.
**What if there is also an election in the AGM?** — In Haryana the normal notice is not enough; the separate election process and notice conditions of Appendix-A must be met.

## Three things to remember
- 🗓️ Hold the AGM within the time limit; send the notice on time.
- 🧾 Record every proposal and approval in the minutes.
- 📊 Keep the final accounts + audit report ready in advance; act on the decisions after the meeting.

## In the end
The AGM is not a burden but a chance to strengthen members' trust. Clean accounts and ready documents make it smooth. The rule is simple — keep the accounts correct all year, send the notice on time, run the meeting transparently, record every decision in the minutes, and act on them afterwards. For a society that adopts this discipline, the AGM every year is smooth, dignified and trust-building.

In short — the formula for a successful AGM is **preparation + transparency + records.** Keep the documents ready in advance, run the meeting openly and respectfully, and record every decision properly. For a society that follows all three, the AGM becomes not a formality or burden but a platform to strengthen its relationship with members and lay a strong foundation for the next year.
