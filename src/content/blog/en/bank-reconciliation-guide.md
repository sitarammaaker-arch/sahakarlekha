# How to do bank reconciliation (BRS): a guide for cooperative societies

At month-end the same question often comes up — "Our books show one bank balance, and the bank statement shows another. Which one is right?" The answer is the **Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)**. It is the process that explains and removes the difference between the two balances. This guide explains **how to do bank reconciliation** — the causes, step by step, examples in both directions, and how to handle common items — in simple language.

## What a BRS is, and why it is necessary

A BRS is not a new account or book — it is just a **reconciliation statement** that shows how the balance in your bank book and the balance in the bank's statement connect with each other. Neither of the two is necessarily "wrong"; often there is only a timing difference. By preparing a BRS you certify that every difference has a reason — and that no entry was quietly missed or went wrong. (Basis: [Cash book vs bank book](/blog/cash-book-vs-bank-book).)

> 💡 Bank book = the society's own record; passbook/statement = the bank's record. The BRS places these two side by side and reconciles them.

## Why does a difference arise?

Your books and the bank statement — both can be correct, and still the balances look different, because some transactions are recorded in one but not in the other. This happens because the two sides record the same transaction at different times — for example, you reduce the balance in your books as soon as you write a cheque, but the bank reduces it only when the cheque comes to it for payment. This delay is what becomes the difference. The main causes:

- **Cheques issued but not presented** — you wrote a cheque and reduced it in the books, but it has not yet been cashed at the bank.
- **Cheques deposited but not collected** — you deposited a cheque and increased it in the books, but it has not yet been credited in the bank.
- **Bank charges/interest** — the bank deducted a charge or added interest, which has not yet come into your books.
- **Direct deposits/payments** — some amount came into/went out of the bank directly, which you came to know about later.

> 💡 A difference is not a mistake — it is a difference of timing. The job of reconciliation is to identify every difference and give its reason.

### Handling common difference items — a table

| Item | In the books? | In the statement? | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cheque issued but not presented | Yes (reduced) | No | Adjust in the BRS; the books are correct |
| Cheque deposited but not collected | Yes (increased) | No | Adjust in the BRS; the books are correct |
| Bank charges | No | Yes | Make an entry in the books |
| Bank interest | No | Yes | Make an entry in the books |
| Direct deposit (NEFT/UPI) | No | Yes | Make an entry in the books |
| Standing instruction/ECS | No | Yes | Make an entry in the books |

> 🔑 Simple rule: what is only a **timing difference** (cheque not yet cleared) is adjusted in the BRS and settles on its own in the next period. What has really been **missed in the books** (charges, interest, direct deposits) needs an actual voucher entry in the books.

Understanding just this one distinction removes all the confusion about the BRS — because every remaining difference is one of these two.

## Step-by-step reconciliation

1. **Starting point** — take the bank balance as per the books (or as per the bank statement, either one of the two).
2. **Matching** — tick every transaction of the two against each other. What is in both is "matched".
3. **Sort out what's left** — list what is only in the books or only in the statement.
4. **Adjustment** — add/subtract the cheques not presented, record bank charges and interest in the books.
5. **Final check** — after adjustment both balances should come out equal.

The heart of this whole process is step 2 (matching) — this is where you must patiently tick every transaction. Most "big tangles" actually arise right here from haste. If you match one by one, the remaining differences will start showing clearly on their own.

> 🔑 Tip: start with the large amounts. Often one or two big items (one uncollected cheque, one big charge) are the cause of the whole difference; catching them first makes the small remaining bits settle quickly.

> 🔍 Entries that were really missed in the books (bank charges, interest, direct deposits) must be recorded in the books with an actual voucher — don't leave them just written in the BRS.

### Preparation before you start

To make reconciliation easy, gather these first:

- The balance of that month's **bank book** (your record).
- The **statement/passbook** for the same period from the bank.
- Last month's BRS (so you remember the earlier items not yet presented).
- A pen/tick mark — to mark every matched item (even easier if digital).

Tick off last month's items that have now cleared; those still not presented will stay on this month's list too.

## The right way to handle each item

While reconciling, put every remaining difference into one of these three categories:

1. **Timing difference (will settle on its own):** cheque not presented/not collected — just adjust these in the BRS, do nothing in the books.
2. **Missed in the books (make an entry):** bank charges, interest, direct deposits/payments — make their actual voucher entry in the books.
3. **Mistake (correct it):** if some amount is entered wrongly in the books (such as ₹500 instead of ₹5,000), correct it with the right voucher.

Just dividing them into these three categories clears half the work — the rest is only arithmetic.

## A short example

| Particulars | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Bank balance as per books | ₹50,000 |
| (+) Cheques deposited but not collected | ₹8,000 |
| (−) Cheques issued but not presented | ₹6,000 |
| (−) Bank charges (to be recorded in the books) | ₹200 |
| **Adjusted balance = statement balance** | **₹51,800** |

## A complete example — as a story

Suppose on 31 August your bank book shows ₹50,000, but the statement shows ₹51,800. On matching you find:

- You deposited a cheque of ₹8,000 and increased it in the books, but the bank has not credited it yet (not collected).
- You wrote a cheque of ₹6,000 and reduced it in the books, but the payee has not yet put it in the bank (not presented).
- The bank deducted a bank charge of ₹200, which is not in your books.

Now the adjustment: books ₹50,000 + not collected ₹8,000 − not presented ₹6,000 − charge ₹200 = ₹51,800 — exactly the statement balance. Reconciled. After this the ₹200 charge must be entered in the books (Bank Charges Dr, Bank Cr); the ₹8,000 and ₹6,000 cheques will clear on their own in September. This is the whole BRS — no complexity, no magic.

## The other direction — start from the statement

Above we started from the books. You can also start in the opposite direction, from the statement — the result should be the same:

| Particulars | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Balance as per statement | ₹51,800 |
| (−) Cheques deposited but not collected | ₹8,000 |
| (+) Cheques issued but not presented | ₹6,000 |
| (+) Bank charges (to be recorded in the books) | ₹200 |
| **Adjusted balance = book balance** | **₹50,000** |

Both directions are two routes to the same truth. Whichever end you start from, in the end both balances must match — only then is the reconciliation complete.

## Entries in the books after reconciliation

Adjusting in the BRS is not enough — for items that were really missed in the books, always make the entry:

- **Bank charges:** Bank Charges (expense) Dr, Bank Cr.
- **Bank interest:** Bank Dr, Interest Income Cr.
- **Direct deposit (NEFT/UPI):** Bank Dr, the relevant income/member Cr.

There is no new entry for cheques not presented/not collected — they clear on their own in the next period. Just check them again in next month's BRS to see whether they have cleared; if a cheque stays unpresented for a very long time, contact the payee — the cheque may have been lost.

## Signs of fraud and mistakes

A regular BRS is not just reconciliation, it is also a security check. Stop at these:

- A withdrawal in the statement that is not in the books and whose reason is unknown.
- The same cheque lying "not presented" for a very long time.
- The same small difference coming back again and again.
- Unknown charges/debits in the statement.
- A cheque's amount differing between the books and the statement.
- The same person repeatedly both handling cash and doing the reconciliation (lack of segregation of duties).

If you see anything like this, check at once — a problem caught early does the least damage. (Also: [10 common mistakes](/blog/common-accounting-mistakes).)

## Why do it every month?

- Mistakes and fraud are caught early.
- You get a correct picture of cash flow, which makes payment planning better.
- At audit time the bank balance "tallies at once" — no objection.
- The correct cash position can be told to members and the managing committee.
- Missed income (such as bank interest) comes into the books on time.

> ⚠️ Not reconciling for months is the costliest habit — small differences pile up and become one big, tangled knot at year-end.

## Three things to remember

- 🔍 A difference is not a mistake, it is often a timing difference — find the reason for every item.
- ✍️ Adjust timing differences in the BRS; record items missed in the books (charges/interest/direct deposits) with actual entries.
- 🗓️ Do the BRS every month — a small regular habit prevents a big tangle.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a BRS?** — A reconciliation statement of the bank book and bank statement balances, which shows the reason for every difference.
**Why are the books and the statement different?** — Because of timing differences like cheques not presented/not collected, bank charges/interest and direct deposits.
**Which items should I enter in the books?** — Those really missed in the books (charges, interest, direct deposits); not cheques not presented.
**Which end should I start from — books or statement?** — Either; done correctly, both directions give the same reconciliation.
**How often should it be done?** — Every month; a large society can even do it weekly.
**Do I need to make an entry for cheques not presented?** — No; they clear on their own in the next period, just adjust them in the BRS.
**What is the harm in not doing a BRS?** — Small differences pile up into a big tangle at year-end, and fraud can stay hidden for months.
**What if there are several bank accounts?** — Prepare a separate BRS for each account; don't mix one account's reconciliation with another.
**Is a BRS easier in a digital system?** — Yes; the bank book is made automatically, you only have to match it with the statement, which cuts the time a lot.
**Are the passbook and the statement the same?** — Both are the bank's own record (one a booklet, one a statement); either of the two will do for the BRS.

## Conclusion

Bank reconciliation is not a complicated job — matching every transaction, finding the reason for what is left, and recording missed entries in the books. Spending 30 minutes on this every month keeps the accounts clean all year and keeps the audit easy. Don't put it off — a regular BRS is the cheapest protection. At first it may feel a little slow, but with two or three months of practice it becomes a job of minutes, and every month you keep the confidence of knowing your society's correct bank position.
