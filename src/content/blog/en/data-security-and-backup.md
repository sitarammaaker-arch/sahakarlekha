# Data Security and Backup: Never Lose Your Society's Accounts

A society's complete accounts — members, shares, loans, ledgers — are its most valuable asset today. A register can burn, a laptop can fail, a file can go missing or be deleted by mistake. And losing the accounts is not just an inconvenience — members' trust, audit preparation and years of hard work are all at stake. That is why **data security and backup** is not something "for later"; it is a need today. This article explains it in detail, with practical steps.

## The biggest danger: "saved locally, nowhere else"

If the data sits on only one computer or one register, it can be wiped out completely in **a single accident** — fire, theft, hard-disk failure, a virus, or an accidental delete. This is the most common and the most costly mistake. The cure is simple — **cloud backup**, meaning the data is not in one place only, but also kept safely outside.

> 💡 Cloud means — the data also stays on a secure server, can be reached from anywhere, and is backed up automatically. Even if the computer breaks down, the accounts stay safe. (See: [Why digital accounting matters](/blog/digital-accounting-for-cooperatives).)

A paper register is the weakest in this respect — it has no copy, and once destroyed it does not come back. Desktop software is a little better, but there too the data stays on that same machine. The cloud almost removes this risk.

## Threats to data — what to guard against

- **Physical accidents:** fire, water, theft, power surges.
- **Technical failure:** hard-disk failure, a file getting corrupted.
- **Human error:** accidental delete, overwrite, or saving in the wrong place.
- **Malice:** virus/ransomware, or damage by a disgruntled person.
- **Misuse of access:** an unauthorised person viewing/changing data.

A good security setup builds a shield against all of these — not one single measure, but several layers.

## Three layers of security

1. **Access control (roles):** secretary, accountant, auditor — each person gets only as much authority as needed. With **Maker–Checker**, one person creates and another approves. This way no one can change everything alone, and no one makes a big mistake unknowingly.
2. **Cloud + regular backup:** the data is not in one place, but also kept safely outside — so it can be recovered after any accident.
3. **Export:** you can take out your data in Excel/PDF at any time — no lock-in. The data always belongs to the society, not held hostage by any company.

Together these three layers make strong security — limited access, a safe copy, and data always under your control.

## Role-based access — in depth

Giving every person authority over everything is a risk. Instead give **role-based access**: full management to the secretary, entry to the accountant, read-only (view-only) to the auditor, only the cash module to the cashier. Everyone can do their own work, but no one has more authority than needed. This is the principle of "least privilege" (minimum necessary authority) — the backbone of security. And **Maker–Checker** makes it stronger: one person creates large transactions and another checks and approves them — which stops a single person's error or wrongdoing. (See: [Internal control in a society](/blog/internal-control-in-cooperatives).)

## Password hygiene

- Keep **strong, separate passwords** (long, letters + numbers + symbols); don't use the same password everywhere.
- **Don't share with anyone** — if needed, create a separate login; don't tell the password.
- **When staff change**, remove old access immediately — this is the most overlooked mistake.
- **Beware of suspicious links/emails** — banks/portals never ask for your password or OTP.
- Turn on **two-factor authentication (2FA)** if available — protection even if the password is stolen.

> ⚠️ "We'll take a backup later" is the most expensive thinking — accidents don't come with a warning. Regular, automatic backup is the only real protection.

## A simple backup routine

Make backup a habit, not a one-time event:

1. **Do daily work in cloud-saved software** — so every entry is saved automatically.
2. **Once a month, export the full data to Excel/PDF** and keep it in a separate safe place — an extra copy besides the cloud.
3. **When staff change, remove old access immediately.**
4. **Check from time to time that the backup actually works** — having a backup is not enough; it must be recoverable.

This small, regular routine saves the entire accounts in a big accident.

## The golden rule of backup (3-2-1)

A widely accepted rule is **3-2-1**: keep at least **3 copies** of the data, on **2 different places/media**, of which **1 copy is outside (off-site)**. For a society this simply means: the original data in cloud software (1), its automatic backup on the cloud server (2), and a monthly export kept in a separate safe place (3, off-site). This way the failure of any one place does not sink the entire accounts.

## A scenario — an accident that was avoided

Suppose there are two societies. The first keeps its entire accounts on one old computer, with no backup. One day that computer breaks down — the hard disk won't open. Years of accounts, member records, loan details — all gone. Now the society must rebuild its accounts from scraps of paper and memory, and the audit is stuck.

The second society uses cloud software. Its computer also breaks down — but there is no worry. The secretary logs in from another computer/mobile, and the full accounts are there exactly as they were. Work restarts in a few minutes. This is the difference between having a backup and not having one — and this is why it is not something "for later".

## Cloud vs local — a security comparison

| Aspect | Local only (computer/register) | Cloud (SaaS) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data in an accident | Can be lost | Stays safe |
| Backup | By hand, often forgotten | Automatic |
| Access | Only from that place | From anywhere |
| Access control | Difficult | Role-based, easy |
| Updates/security patches | By hand | Automatic |

The comparison is clear — the cloud is generally safer for a small society, because it gets regular backup and security even without a separate IT expert.

## Audit trail — who changed what

Good software has an **audit trail** — a record of who created or changed which entry, and when. This is an important layer of security: if something goes wrong, you can find out where and at whose level it happened. In a paper register this is nearly impossible (you can't tell who made the cuttings and corrections), while in a digital system every change is recorded. This transparency builds trust in the society and prevents wrongdoing.

## If data is lost — recovery

A backup has real value only if it can be recovered. So: (1) check from time to time that the backup/export file actually opens and is correct; (2) in cloud software the data is available immediately from another device after an accident; (3) essential information can also be recovered from the export copy (Excel/PDF). Saying "we have a backup" is not enough — making sure "we can recover from the backup" is the real security.

## Protecting paper records

Even after going digital, some paper documents remain — registration certificate, bye-laws, audit reports, resolutions, large contracts. Keep these safe too: an organised filing system, a cupboard safe from fire/water, and scanned copies of important documents in the cloud. This way both physical and digital records stay safe.

## Data security and members' trust

Data security is not just a technical matter — it is directly tied to members' trust. Members have handed the society the accounts of their savings, shares and loans; if those accounts are lost or fall into the wrong hands, trust breaks, and it is hard to rebuild. On the other hand, a society that keeps its data secure, backed up and access-controlled assures members that their accounts are in safe hands. The whole cooperative structure stands on trust — and data security is the modern backbone of that trust. So see it not as a technical hassle, but as a duty towards members.

## Common mistakes

- **Keeping data in one place only** → one accident wipes out everything; cloud backup is essential.
- **Putting off backup "for later"** → accidents don't come with a warning; choose automatic backup.
- **Giving everyone full authority** → keep role-based access.
- **Not removing access when staff leave** → old access is a big risk.
- **Sharing passwords / keeping weak ones** → strong, separate, confidential passwords.
- **Not checking the backup** → test once in a while that it can be recovered.
- **Not being able to export data** → choose a tool that doesn't lock you in.
- **Keeping important paper documents unprotected** → registration/bye-laws/audit reports in a safe cupboard + scanned copies in the cloud.

## A simple start for a small society

Don't think of data security as complicated — even a small society can become very secure with a few simple steps: (1) choose cloud-based software that backs up automatically (this is the biggest protection); (2) give every person a separate login, don't share passwords; (3) once a month export the full data and keep it in a separate place; (4) when staff change, remove old access immediately. Doing just this protects the society's accounts from most of the accidents that actually cause data loss. A bigger setup can be added later; these basic steps can be taken today.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is data backup necessary?** — In one accident (fire/theft/failure/delete) the entire accounts can be lost; a backup saves them.
**Is the cloud safe?** — Yes; the data is on a secure server, backed up automatically, and safe even if the computer breaks down.
**How often should we back up?** — Daily work goes to the cloud automatically; a separate export copy once a month.
**What is the 3-2-1 rule?** — 3 copies, 2 media, 1 outside — a widely accepted backup rule.
**What is role-based access?** — Each person gets only as much authority as needed (least privilege) — the backbone of security.
**How should passwords be kept?** — Strong, separate, confidential; remove old access immediately when staff change; turn on 2FA.
**Can I take out my data at any time?** — Yes, in a trustworthy tool; the data always belongs to the society and can be exported to Excel/PDF.
**What is an audit trail?** — A record of who created/changed which entry, and when — an important layer for catching wrongdoing and for transparency.
**What about paper documents?** — Keep registration/bye-laws/audit reports in a safe cupboard, and scanned copies of the important ones in the cloud.
**If data is lost, how do we get it back?** — In the cloud, log in from another device; essential information can also be recovered from the export copy; so check the backup in advance.

One last point — data security is not a one-time job but a continuous habit. Choosing the right software is the first and biggest step, but password discipline, access control and a regular export copy are equally necessary. Together these protect the society's most valuable asset — its accounts — from every accident, and keep the foundation of members' trust strong.

## Three things to remember
- ☁️ Data not in one place only — cloud backup is essential (3-2-1 rule).
- 🔑 Role-based access + Maker–Checker; remove access when staff leave.
- 📤 Be able to export your data at any time — no lock-in.

## In closing
Data security is the backbone of cooperative trust. Members have handed their savings and trust to the society — keeping those accounts safe is the society's first duty. Cloud backup, role-based control, password hygiene and an export facility — with these habits the society's accounts are never lost, whatever the accident. This is not an expensive or complicated setup; it is just a matter of choosing the right software and the right habits once — and then years of peace of mind.

The whole summary in one line — data not in one place (cloud backup, 3-2-1), access limited (role-based + Maker–Checker), and the data always yours (exportable). A society that follows these three rules keeps its accounts safe in any accident.
