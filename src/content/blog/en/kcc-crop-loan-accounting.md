# KCC and crop loan accounting: a guide for PACS

Many PACS give farmers crop loans under the **KCC (Kisan Credit Card)** — this is their main service. The Kisan Credit Card scheme is very large: according to government figures, operative KCC loans have crossed **₹10 lakh crore** and reach about **7.72 crore farmers**. So only correct **KCC and crop loan accounting** keeps a society sustainable — with wrong accounts, neither recovery nor the real profit shows. This article explains KCC loan accounts, interest subvention, limits and recovery/NPA — all in simple language, with examples and sources. (See: [PIB — Kisan Credit Card](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2106230))

## What is KCC — a quick look

KCC is a card-based short-term loan facility through which a farmer gets easy credit for needs from sowing to harvest. It is now not limited to crops — KCC is also available for allied activities like **animal husbandry and fisheries**. The farmer can draw and repay again and again up to a sanctioned **limit** (revolving credit) — so its accounting is a little different from an ordinary term loan.

**Eligibility and facility (broadly):** farmers who cultivate land, sharecroppers, and those doing animal husbandry/fisheries are eligible for KCC. A KCC is usually sanctioned for a fixed period (such as five years) and comes with a RuPay debit card, so the farmer can draw as needed. A PACS usually brings this loan to farmers through the DCCB. Confirm the exact eligibility and terms with your DCCB/NABARD.

## Why KCC matters — for both the farmer and the society

The aim of KCC is to give the farmer **timely, cheap and formal credit** — so that he does not have to depend on a moneylender's high interest. The benefit for the farmer: a concessional rate, drawing as needed, and even lower interest on prompt repayment. The benefit for the society (PACS): it is its main income-earning service and strengthens its bond with members. But it is sustainable only if **recovery discipline** holds — that is why correct accounting is not just record-keeping, but the backbone of the society's health.

## A KCC loan creates an asset

When the society gives a farmer an amount under KCC, cash/bank goes down but a new **asset** is created — "loan recoverable from farmer".

| Account | Dr | Cr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KCC loan (asset) | ₹50,000 | — |
| Bank/cash | — | ₹50,000 |

> 💡 The sanctioned **limit** and the actual **drawing** are different — the record is always made for the actual drawing, not the limit. Suppose the limit is ₹1,00,000 but the farmer has drawn only ₹50,000 so far — then the books will show a loan of only ₹50,000.

## Interest, interest subvention (MISS) and the concessional rate

Here a farmer-friendly scheme comes in — the **Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS)**:

- The upper rate of interest on short-term crop loans is usually kept at **7%**, for which the government gives banks/societies **interest subvention**.
- A **farmer who repays on time** gets a **3% prompt repayment incentive (PRI)** — so his effective rate comes down to about **4%**.
- KCC loans taken only for **animal husbandry/fisheries** also get the interest benefit (up to a fixed limit).
- The central government has continued MISS for **financial year 2025-26**. (See: [PIB — continuation of MISS](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2131989) and [NABARD — interest subvention scheme](https://www.nabard.org/content1.aspx?id=602&catid=23&mid=23))

**What this means in the accounts:**
- Interest is the society's **income** — when charged: interest income Cr, farmer account/receivable Dr.
- Record **interest subvention/incentive** **separately** (as and when received) — do not mix it with the interest taken from the farmer, or the picture of both income and subsidy gets distorted.
- Charge the farmer the rate that applies to him (concessional on timely repayment) — **using the correct rate** is essential.

> ⚠️ The rate and terms of interest subvention keep changing (for example, the subvention given to banks was earlier 2%, which has been changed). Confirm the **current rate and terms** that apply to your society with the cooperative department/DCCB/NABARD — this article explains the method.

**Example:** suppose a farmer took a ₹1,00,000 KCC crop loan and repaid on time. Under the scheme his effective interest came to ~4% (7% minus the 3% prompt repayment incentive). In the society's books, interest income will be recorded separately, and the government subvention/incentive separately.

## How interest subvention reaches the PACS

The burden of giving the farmer a concessional rate does not fall fully on the society — this is the core idea of MISS. Broadly, the flow is like this: the farmer pays interest to the society at the concessional rate; the society/bank gets the remaining difference from the government as **interest subvention** (through NABARD/DCCB). So two things should show separately in the society's books — (1) the interest income taken from the farmer, and (2) the subvention receivable/received from the government.

> 💡 If you mix the two, you will know neither the real interest income nor how much subvention is still due from the government. Keep the subvention as a "receivable" until it is received. Confirm the exact accounting method and claim process with your DCCB.

## Loan limit and collateral-free loans

These limits have gone up recently, so keep an eye on them:

- **KCC loan limit:** in the Union Budget 2025, the limit under MISS was raised **from ₹3 lakh to ₹5 lakh**.
- **Collateral-free limit:** RBI raised the collateral-free limit for agricultural loans **from ₹1.6 lakh to ₹2 lakh** per farmer (effective from 1 January 2025).

(See: [PIB — KCC ecosystem/limit](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246855))

> ⚠️ These limits and dates keep changing with policy — before giving a new loan, confirm the current limit with RBI/NABARD/DCCB.

## Keep a close watch on recovery % and NPA

Giving a loan is half the job; the real test is **recovery**.

- **Recovery %** = amount recovered ÷ amount due × 100. Example: ₹8,50,000 recovered out of ₹10,00,000 due = **85%**.
- Under RBI's income recognition and asset classification (IRAC) norms, an account is classified as **NPA** when interest/principal stays overdue beyond a fixed period (usually **90 days**, with special crop-season-based rules for agricultural loans).
- On NPAs, record income only **on a recovery basis** — not on accrual.

**Example:** the due date of an instalment in a KCC account has passed and nothing was recovered within the set period — then treat it as NPA as per the rules and do not count unearned interest on it as income. Rising NPA lowers the society's grading and its future borrowing capacity. (See: [Loan and interest accounting](/blog/loan-and-interest-accounting) and [NABARD/federation reporting](/blog/nabard-and-federation-returns).)

> 💡 Special NPA/period rules on crop loans depend on the crop cycle — confirm the exact rules that apply to you with the DCCB/NABARD.

## The life cycle of a KCC loan — a full example

See through a running example how to record a KCC account over a year. Suppose farmer "Ramprasad" was sanctioned a KCC limit of ₹1,00,000:

1. **Drawing (at sowing):** Ramprasad drew ₹60,000.
   - KCC loan (asset) Dr ₹60,000 · Bank/cash Cr ₹60,000. (The limit of ₹1,00,000 is not recorded, only ₹60,000.)
2. **Interest charged (quarterly/half-yearly):** interest added at the applicable rate.
   - Farmer receivable Dr · Interest income Cr. (Government subvention in a separate account.)
3. **Part repayment (after harvest):** Ramprasad paid back ₹40,000.
   - Bank/cash Dr ₹40,000 · KCC loan/interest Cr ₹40,000.
4. **Prompt repayment incentive:** because he repaid on time, he gets the benefit of the concessional rate (≈4%); the incentive/subvention is recorded separately.
5. **Renewal (next season):** the limit becomes available again; the outstanding balance carries forward.

> 💡 If you record this cycle cleanly, at year-end the recovery %, outstanding and NPA come out right on their own — no separate adding and subtracting. (See: [Trial Balance](/blog/trial-balance-explained).)

## At a glance — the main KCC entries

| Event | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| KCC loan given (drawing) | Asset (loan) up, cash/bank down |
| Interest charged | Interest income up, receivable up |
| Instalment/repayment received | Cash up, loan/interest down |
| Interest subvention/incentive received | Recorded as separate income/adjustment |
| Account turned NPA | Income accrual stops, recovery basis |

## Practical tips for PACS

- **Record the drawing, not the limit** — the books should be based on the amount actually drawn.
- **Keep subvention separate** — never mix the farmer's interest and the government subsidy.
- **Watch recovery all year** — act early on accounts nearing 90 days.
- **The right rate for the right farmer** — make sure the farmer who repays on time gets the concessional rate.
- **Renew the KCC limit** on time so the farmer faces no disruption.
- **Treat government subvention as a "receivable"** — until it is received, keep it in receivables, do not count it as income.
- **A separate account for every farmer** — with mixing, neither recovery stays clear nor does the NPA picture stay correct.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Treating the limit as the loan** — if ₹60 thousand is drawn out of a ₹1 lakh limit, the books should show ₹60 thousand, not ₹1 lakh.
- **Mixing subsidy into interest income** — this shows income inflated and the subsidy hidden; keep both separate.
- **Counting interest on NPAs as income** — treating unearned interest as income is paper profit; follow the recovery basis.
- **Going by an old rate/limit** — MISS rates and limits change; confirm the current rules every cycle.
- **Delay in renewal** — if the KCC is not renewed on time, the farmer cannot get a loan in the season.

## Three things to remember

- 💳 KCC loan = asset (on drawing), interest = income, subsidy = separate.
- 📉 Watch recovery % and NPA all year — grading and borrowing capacity depend on them.
- 🧾 Always confirm the current rate/limit (MISS, collateral-free) with NABARD/DCCB.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I record a KCC loan in the books?** — On drawing, "KCC loan (asset)" Dr and cash/bank Cr; the record is made for the actual drawing, not the sanctioned limit.
**What rate does a farmer pay on timely repayment?** — Under the scheme, after the prompt repayment incentive (3%), the effective rate is about 4% (out of the 7% upper rate) — confirm the current rate.
**Should interest subvention be added to interest income?** — No; record the subsidy/incentive separately so that both the real income and the government support show clearly.
**What is the current KCC loan limit?** — In Budget 2025 the MISS limit was raised from ₹3 lakh to ₹5 lakh; the collateral-free limit from ₹1.6 lakh to ₹2 lakh (2025). Confirm the current limit with RBI/NABARD.
**When does a KCC account become NPA?** — When it stays overdue beyond a fixed period (usually 90 days, with special crop-season-based rules for crop loans).
**Is KCC also available for animal husbandry/fisheries?** — Yes; KCC and the interest benefit are also available for these allied activities (up to a fixed limit).
**For how long is a KCC valid?** — Usually for a fixed period (such as five years), during which the limit becomes available again each season; get it renewed on time.
**Why record only what was drawn out of the limit?** — Because interest and recovery apply to the actual drawing, not the sanctioned limit; so the books show the actual drawing.
**How does rising NPA hurt the society?** — Rising NPA lowers the society's audit grading, which makes further borrowing and refinance from DCCB/NABARD harder/costlier — so the next loan cycle is also affected.

## In the end

The core of KCC accounting is simple — treat the loan as an asset, interest as income, government subvention as separate, and keep a close watch on recovery/NPA. Record the actual drawing, not the sanctioned limit, keep a separate account for every farmer, and renew on time. Scheme rates and limits (MISS, collateral-free) change from time to time, so confirm the current rules with the DCCB/NABARD in every new loan cycle. Only correct accounts keep the society — and its farmers — strong.
