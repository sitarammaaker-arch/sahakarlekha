# Office-bearers of a society: the roles of the chairman, secretary and treasurer

> **Short answer:** The chairman presides over meetings and represents the society before the bank, the department and the federation. The secretary handles day-to-day administration, records and minutes. The treasurer keeps watch over cash, payments and bank transactions. All three are answerable to the members.

Within the managing committee, certain special responsibilities are given to a few people — these are the **office-bearers**: the chairman, the secretary and the treasurer. Each has a different role, and together the three run the society smoothly and transparently. This article explains the **roles, responsibilities, accountability and mutual balance** of these three in simple language — so that both new office-bearers and members know who does what.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** This article mostly describes good practice, which is roughly the same in every state. Where the law is cited, it has been matched with the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 (including the 2013 amendment) — see "Sources" below. In other states, check your own Act and your society's bye-laws.

## Office-bearers and the managing committee — the difference

First let us clear up a basic point: the **managing committee** is the group of all directors elected by the members, while the **office-bearers** are the few people chosen from within that same committee who are given special responsibilities (chairman, secretary, treasurer). So every office-bearer is a member of the managing committee, but not every committee member is an office-bearer. (See: [Role of the managing committee](/blog/managing-committee-role).)

## Chairman (Chairman / President)
- **Presides** over meetings and keeps the discussion orderly.
- **Represents** the society outside (bank, department, federation).
- Gives **guidance** in decisions; a casting vote when votes are equal.
- Is not the "boss" — but the leader of collective decision-making. In cooperation, leadership does not mean giving orders, but taking everyone along — this is the spirit of cooperative values.

The chairman's job is not to give orders, but to run the meeting so that every member is heard and decisions are made democratically. A good chairman stays impartial, moves the meeting forward as per the agenda, and makes sure decisions are taken by majority, not at the whim of one person. Before outside bodies (bank, cooperative department, federation) the chairman is the face of the society, so the chairman's conduct is tied to the society's reputation.

### Main functions of the chairman — a list

- Presiding over meetings and running them in an orderly way as per the agenda.
- Giving the deciding (casting) vote when votes are equal — proviso (a) to Section 20 of the Haryana Act says that when votes are equal, the chairman shall have a second or casting vote.
- Representing the society before outside bodies (bank, department, federation).
- Seeing that decisions are democratic and as per the rules.
- (In many societies) co-signing important documents/payments.

## Secretary (Secretary)
Often the society's **executive** — this is where the real daily work happens:
- **Records and minutes**, correspondence, filing.
- **Day-to-day administration** — coordinating membership, vouchers, reports and compliance (GST/TDS/audit).
- Calling meetings, preparing the agenda, carrying out decisions.

### Main functions of the secretary — a list

- Keeping membership records, the share and member registers always up to date.
- Preparing daily vouchers and looking after all the books. (See: [Voucher entry guide](/blog/voucher-entry-guide).)
- Preparing meeting notices, the agenda and minutes.
- Coordinating annual returns, audit support and compliance.
- Correspondence with the Registrar/department and carrying out decisions.

The secretary is the "backbone" of the society — from calling meetings to carrying out decisions, and from membership records to annual compliance, most of the practical work is the secretary's responsibility. So the secretary being organised, punctual and honest matters most for the society's health. In many societies the secretary is a salaried employee, while the chairman and treasurer are elected members — this arrangement depends on the state and the bye-laws.

> 💡 In most cooperative societies **the secretary is the main user of software like SahakarLekha** — because the accounts and reports are the secretary's responsibility. (For the secretary's accounting skills: [Basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics).)

## Treasurer (Treasurer)
- Looks after the society's **funds and cash**.
- Keeps an eye on payments, recoveries and bank transactions; shares financial accountability with the chairman/secretary.
- (In small societies this role is often combined with the secretary's.)

The treasurer's main job is to make sure the society's money stays safe and every transaction is accounted for. The treasurer checks the cash balance regularly, approves large payments, and keeps an eye on the bank reconciliation. Where there is a separate treasurer, the work of handling cash and writing the books is split between different people — this segregation of duties is the first wall against wrongdoing. (Cash control: [Cash handling and verification](/blog/cash-handling-and-verification).)

> 🧾 For a full checklist of the treasurer's day-to-day accounts work — cash count, BRS, voucher checks, reports and audit records — read [The treasurer's accounts work: a checklist for cash, bank, vouchers and reports](/blog/treasurer-accounting-work-checklist).

### Main functions of the treasurer — a list

- Regular (daily or weekly) checking and physical counting of the cash balance.
- Co-approval and monitoring of large payments.
- Keeping an eye on bank transactions and the monthly bank reconciliation (BRS).
- Placing financial reports before the managing committee from time to time.
- Sharing the society's financial accountability with the chairman and secretary.

| Office-bearer | Main responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Chairman | Presiding, representation, guidance |
| Secretary | Records, administration, day-to-day running, compliance |
| Treasurer | Oversight of funds, cash and bank |

> ⚠️ Note: how office-bearers are chosen and their exact powers are decided by the **state Act and bye-laws**. So the roles given in this article are a general framework; in your society some details may differ as per the bye-laws. (See: [What bye-laws are](/blog/cooperative-society-byelaws).)

## Who is senior, the chairman or the secretary?

In a meeting the chairman ranks higher: the chairman presides and has the casting vote when votes are tied. But the chairman is not the secretary's "boss". The real day-to-day work is in the secretary's hands, and both are answerable to the managing committee and the members.

## Roles in a small vs a large society

In a small society the secretary often handles most of the work, and the treasurer's role is added to it — because keeping a separate person is not possible. In such a case the lack of segregation of duties is made up for by **regular audit, surprise cash checks and the chairman's oversight**. In a large society the three roles are split among different people, and there are often salaried employees too (such as a secretary/accountant). Whatever the structure, the basic principle is the same — no single person should hold full financial power alone.

## How the three work together — an example

Suppose the society has to buy new godown equipment worth ₹1,00,000 (a hypothetical example). See the role of each:

1. The **secretary** prepares the proposal and puts it on the meeting agenda.
2. The **chairman** presides over the meeting; the managing committee discusses and decides by majority.
3. The **treasurer** checks that the money is available and that the payment will be made as per the rules.
4. When the payment is made, the **secretary** prepares the voucher, and because the amount is large the **chairman/treasurer** approve it.
5. The **secretary** records the full decision in the minutes.

So in one single piece of work, each of the three has a different role — and this balance stops anyone acting on their own whim and brings transparency.

## Qualities of a good office-bearer

Whatever the post, some qualities are needed in every office-bearer:

- **Honesty and transparency** — protecting both the society's money and its trust.
- **Punctuality** — meetings, compliance and records on time.
- **Basic understanding of accounting** — especially for the secretary and treasurer.
- **Impartiality** — decisions in the society's interest, not personal interest.
- **Team spirit** — the three work together, without conflict.

Office-bearers with these qualities keep the society healthy, free of disputes and audit-ready.

## How office-bearers are chosen

Office-bearers are usually chosen from among the elected members of the managing committee — the procedure is set by the state Act, Rules and bye-laws (in Haryana, elections are held under Rule 25 and Appendix "A"). **In Haryana**, after the 2013 amendment, Section 28(4) says that the managing committee, unless dissolved earlier by the Registrar, shall continue for **5 years** from the date of election, and **the term of the office-bearers also ends with the term of the committee**. If a seat falls vacant in between, it is filled from members of the same category — by election if more than half the committee's term is left, and by co-option if less than half is left. Timely and fair elections are part of cooperation's "democratic control" principle. (See: [Society elections](/blog/cooperative-society-elections).)

## Accountability — to whom

Office-bearers are ultimately accountable **to the members**, through the general body. Beyond this, they are also answerable to the state Cooperative Societies Act, the bye-laws and the Registrar. This means — no office-bearer can use the society's money or make decisions at their own will; every major decision must come from the managing committee/general body and be as per the rules. This accountability is what makes the society an institution people trust.

The most practical way to meet this accountability is — **a written record of everything**. The minutes of the meeting, the voucher and approval for every payment, the resolution for every decision. When the record is clear, any member or auditor can get to the root of any decision, and baseless allegations are not made against the office-bearers either. So a good record is also the office-bearers' own protection.

## Why segregation of duties matters

One big reason for having three separate office-bearers is **segregation of duties (separation of duties)** — so that no single person takes all the power into their own hands. For example: the secretary makes the entries, the treasurer handles the cash, and the chairman/treasurer approve large payments. When roles are divided, wrongdoing becomes hard and catching it becomes easy. In small societies where roles are combined, this gap is filled by regular audit and surprise checks.

## The link with internal control
In a good system **one prepares, another approves** (Maker–Checker) — for example the secretary makes the entry, and the chairman/treasurer approve large payments. This reduces the room for wrongdoing and keeps the audit easy. In a digital system this Maker–Checker arrangement becomes even stronger with role-based access — each office-bearer gets only as much access as their role needs. (See: [Data security and role control](/blog/data-security-and-backup).)

## Common mistakes and conflicts

- **All power in one person** → follow segregation of duties; no one should handle everything alone.
- **Large payments without approval** → above a set limit, always co-approval by two office-bearers.
- **Not keeping minutes** → every meeting and decision should be properly written and signed.
- **Putting off compliance** → always do the audit, returns and AGM on time; do not wait for the last moment.
- **Confusion over roles** → keep who does what clear through the bye-laws.
- **Mixing personal and society money** → never; the society's account and cash always stay separate.
- **Not handing over records when posts change** → hand over the full records and passwords properly to the new office-bearer.

Clear roles and written records prevent most internal conflicts. Where roles are vague, conflicts like "that was not my job" are common — so it is best to write down and settle responsibilities right at the start.

## Frequently asked questions
**What is the secretary's role?** — Records, minutes, day-to-day administration and compliance — often the society's executive.
**What is the chairman's role?** — Presiding over meetings, representation and guidance.
**What does the treasurer do?** — Oversees funds, cash and bank transactions.
**Can the secretary take decisions alone?** — No; major decisions are made by the managing committee/with approval.
**What is the difference between office-bearers and the managing committee?** — The managing committee is all the elected directors; office-bearers are a few of them with special responsibilities.
**To whom are office-bearers accountable?** — Mainly to the members (general body), and to the Act/bye-laws/Registrar.
**How are office-bearers chosen?** — From among the members of the managing committee, as per the procedure in the Act and bye-laws.
**Can the same person be secretary and treasurer?** — Sometimes in small societies, but then regular audit and checks become even more necessary.
**Is the secretary salaried?** — Yes in many societies (an employee), while the chairman and treasurer are often elected members; this depends on the state/bye-laws.
**Who should approve large payments?** — Above a set limit, co-approval by the chairman/treasurer, so that no one can do it alone.
**How long is an office-bearer's term?** — Along with the committee's term; in Haryana the committee's term is 5 years from the election and the office-bearers' term ends with it (Section 28(4), the 2013 amendment).
**What happens when votes are equal?** — In Haryana the chairman gets a second or casting vote (Section 20).

## Three things to remember
- 🪑 The chairman presides and represents; the secretary handles daily work and records; the treasurer handles funds.
- 🧑‍💻 The secretary is usually the main user of the accounting software.
- 🔒 Keep internal control strong with segregation of duties and Maker–Checker.

## In the end

Chairman, secretary and treasurer — the three roles are different, but the aim is one: to run the society in a transparent, rule-abiding way in the members' interest. Only when every office-bearer knows their responsibility, duties are divided, and every decision and transaction has a written record — does the society become an institution people trust. Do match their exact powers with your state Act and bye-laws.
