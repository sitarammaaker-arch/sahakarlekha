# How should a cooperative society keep accounts of a government grant? From sanction and spending to the utilization certificate

As an example, suppose a society has been sanctioned a grant of ₹5 lakh under some scheme, for repair of the godown and some equipment. The first instalment has come into the bank. The secretary is happy, but then three questions come up: should this money be booked as "income" in the books? ₹1.2 lakh of it went into repairs, what to do with the rest? And a letter has come from the department: "Send the utilization certificate (UC)." The secretary has never filled such a certificate before.

A government grant is the most different kind of money a society receives. It is **not the society's earning, and not members' deposits either.** It is **money given with conditions**, which can be spent only on the set work, whose accounts must be kept separately, and for which **proof** must be given to the government that it was used in the right place. This article is the full sequence for this: reading the sanction letter, identifying the type of grant, the accounting entries, separate records, the utilization certificate and the audit.

> 📘 **In short:** First **identify** the grant (not every rupee from the government is a grant: contribution to share capital, loans and guarantees are different), then read **the conditions of the sanction letter**, then do the accounting **according to the type**: **reimbursement of expense** (revenue grant), **for an asset** (capital grant), or **for members** (pass-through). Keep a **separate record** of every grant, spend the money **only on the approved work**, handle leftover money and the interest earned on it as per the condition of the sanction letter, and give the **utilization certificate** in the same form and time limit that the sanction letter or department asks for. Section 46 of the Haryana Act is the basis for the government's "financial assistance including subsidy"; but I did not find any separate Haryana rule for grant accounting or the UC.

> ⚠️ **The most important point, which will come again and again in this article:** the **conditions of every grant are in its own sanction letter**. This article gives a general framework and precautions, but your scheme's form, time limit and spending conditions are what your **sanction letter or the department's order** says. If the two differ, the sanction letter comes first.

## Government money, but not a grant: know the difference

Under **Section 46 ("State aid to co-operative societies")** of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984, the government can help a society in these ways: contribution to share capital; giving loans or advances; guarantee of principal and interest on debentures or loans; guarantee of share capital and dividend on it; and **"financial assistance in any other form, including subsidy."** The accounting of each of these is different:

| Received from government | Nature | In the accounts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Contribution to share capital** | Capital | Share capital (government as a member); not a grant |
| **Loan or advance** | Liability | To be repaid, interest (if any) is an expense |
| **Guarantee** | No money comes | Note in the agreement/details; no entry in the accounts |
| **Grant or subsidy** | Money with conditions | The subject of this article |

This difference matters because in three of the four above the money must be returned or becomes a member's stake, and only in the fourth is it "no refund, but conditions". ICAI's accounting standard AS 12 (Government Grants) is also said to keep **government participation in ownership** (that is, contribution to share capital) outside the scope of grants. That is, the government's share contribution is recorded like capital, not like a grant.

**One more point, about the government nominee member:** under Section 29(1)(a)(iv), where the government has assisted the society with loans and **grants** of **one lakh rupees or more**, the government or its authorised person has the right to keep a nominee member on the managing committee. But as per the proviso to the same section, **there is no government nominee member on the committee of a primary agricultural cooperative society (PACS)**, whether or not the government has contributed to share capital.

## Sanction letter: what to read, what to do

The most important document of a grant is its **sanction letter**. Read it before the money comes into the account, place it before the committee and hand over each of its conditions to someone.

| Thing to ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Purpose and head** | For what work; how much under which head (repair, equipment, training). No spending outside this |
| **Amount and instalments** | Total amount; which instalment when, on what condition |
| **Time limit** | By when the work and spending must be complete; process to extend the period |
| **Bank account** | Must the grant come into a separate account, or can it run in a common account |
| **Interest** | Whose is the interest on the grant balance (returned to government or added to the scheme) |
| **Society's contribution** | Does the society have to put in its own share somewhere (matching) |
| **Ownership of asset** | Register, insurance and conditions of use for any asset created |
| **Purchase procedure** | Condition of quotation/tender, approved suppliers |
| **Records and audit** | Which records, for how many years, checked by whom |
| **UC form, time and signatory** | When, in which form, signed by whom |
| **Leftover money** | To be returned, or adjusted in the next instalment |
| **On violation** | If a condition is broken, refund or action |

**Starting tasks:**

- [ ] **Place the sanction letter in the committee meeting** and pass a resolution accepting it (how to write minutes: [Board meeting minutes](/blog/board-meeting-minutes-format-secretary-sample-checklist)).
- [ ] **Fix responsibilities:** scheme in-charge, approver of expenses, keeper of records, preparer of the UC. (The GFR 12-A form itself asks whether the responsibilities of the main functionaries of the scheme are clearly assigned.)
- [ ] **Grant register:** the first line with the number and date of the sanction letter.
- [ ] **Bank:** if it is a condition, open a separate account; even if not, keep the grant accounts separate in the books.

## Three forms of grant and their accounting

Whether a grant is income, a liability or capital depends on its **type**. Based on ICAI's accounting standard **AS 12 (Accounting for Government Grants)** *(from the material of ICAI's Board of Studies; I did not open the full text of the original standard, so confirm with your auditor)*, the principles are:

**Condition for recognition:** a grant is **not treated as final** in the books until there is **reasonable assurance** that (a) the society will comply with the conditions attached to it and (b) the grant will be received. The arrival of the sanction letter is not conclusive proof of this.

| Type of grant | When | How recorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Revenue grant** (reimbursement of expense) | For expenses like training, service, maintenance | Into income matched with the expense (**"other income"**) or deducted from the related expense |
| **Capital grant** (for an asset) | For buying/building an asset like equipment, machine, building | **Two methods:** deduct from the cost of the asset, **or** treat as deferred income and take it into income gradually over the life of the asset, like depreciation; for a non-depreciable asset (like land) the grant goes to **capital reserve** |
| **Grant in the nature of capital contribution** | Like a contribution to the society's total capital cost, whose refund is generally not expected | In **capital reserve**; no dividend can be distributed from it |

Three points are special:

- **"Matching" a revenue grant with the expense:** the grant becomes income in the same period in which the expense it reimburses was incurred. Booking the whole money as income at once makes that year's profit look higher, while the expense will happen next year.
- **Capital grant and depreciation:** if the asset is depreciated, the deferred income part of the grant comes into income at the same speed; so look at the asset and the grant together. (Depreciation: [How to do depreciation](/blog/depreciation-explained).)
- **No dividend from capital reserve:** a grant that went into capital reserve is not distributable profit. (Dividend: [Member dividend guide](/blog/cooperative-society-member-dividend-guide).)

**Pass-through grant (for members):** in many schemes the society receives the grant for members, such as a subsidy to be given to members, and the society is only a distributor. Then that money is not the society's income but a **liability to pay members**: **Dr bank, Cr grant payable to members**, and on paying the member **Dr grant payable, Cr bank**. The general accounting principle here is that money the society does not own but handles as a channel for others is a liability.

**How binding is the standard (AS 12) on cooperative societies?** I have not checked this. Generally ICAI's accounting standards are used as guiding practice for societies, but whether they are binding depends on Haryana's accounts formats, the Registrar's orders and the auditor's instructions. So ask **your auditor**, settle on one method and keep it the same every year.

## From receipt to spending: solved examples

*(All amounts are imaginary; the conditions of a real scheme will differ.)*

### Example 1: Grant for reimbursement of expense (revenue), ₹2,00,000

The society received ₹2,00,000 for a members' training programme; ₹1,80,000 was spent on the programme, ₹20,000 was left.

| Step | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grant received (condition not yet met) | Bank | **Grant received (conditional / advance), liability** | ₹2,00,000 |
| Programme expense | Training expense | Bank | ₹1,80,000 |
| Grant equal to the expense into income | Grant received (liability) | **Government grant (income)** | ₹1,80,000 |
| Leftover ₹20,000: refund as per sanction letter | Grant received (liability) | Bank | ₹20,000 |

**Note:** only ₹1,80,000 came into income, that is, equal to the expense. The leftover ₹20,000 did not become income, because it either has to be returned or will be used in the next period as per the condition. If the sanction letter allows the leftover money to be carried forward, it stays in the liability; if not returned, it does not go into income.

### Example 2: Grant for an asset (capital), ₹3,00,000

A grant of ₹3,00,000 to buy equipment, life of the equipment 10 years, depreciation 10% per year (simple example).

| Step | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grant received | Bank | Deferred grant (capital) | ₹3,00,000 |
| Equipment bought | Equipment (asset) | Bank | ₹3,00,000 |
| Year-end: depreciation | Depreciation expense | Equipment | ₹30,000 |
| Year-end: share of grant into income | Deferred grant | Government grant (income) | ₹30,000 |

In the first year depreciation is an expense of ₹30,000 and the grant income of ₹30,000 balances it; so an asset bought from a grant puts no expense burden on the society's profit. **The second method:** deduct the grant from the cost of the asset (₹3,00,000 deducted from equipment of ₹3,00,000, making it zero) and charge depreciation on the reduced cost. Both methods are said to be acceptable; the society should choose one and keep it the same, and the asset should stay marked **in the register as bought from a grant**.

### Example 3: Subsidy to be given to members (pass-through), ₹1,00,000

| Step | Dr | Cr | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subsidy received (for members) | Bank | Grant payable to members | ₹1,00,000 |
| Payment as per member list | Grant payable to members | Bank | ₹1,00,000 |

Nothing went into the society's income. If the scheme also gives the society a service charge or administrative allowance, that amount will come into income separately.

## Separate records and accounts of spending

**A separate account for every grant:** for every sanction letter create a separate ledger head in the books, so that it shows at a glance: how much came, how much was spent, how much is left. The money of one grant should not mix with the spending of another.

**Grant register (sample):**

| Column | What |
| --- | --- |
| Scheme/sanction letter No. and date | Basis |
| Sanctioned amount | Total |
| Date and amount of instalment | When it came |
| Spending (head-wise) | Where it was used, through which voucher |
| Interest (if earned) | As per condition |
| Balance left | Remaining |
| UC status | Sent/pending and date |

**Statement of Expenditure:** government forms usually ask for a simple statement which records **head-wise** (such as staff, contingency, travel, equipment): amount received from the department this year, unspent balance of last year, interest, total, spending this year, and balance. This statement comes straight from the grant register.

**Voucher habits:**

- [ ] **Write the number of the sanction letter on every expense voucher**, and attach the bill/invoice. (What to attach: [Voucher narration and documents](/blog/voucher-narration-and-documents).)
- [ ] **Spending must stay within the approved head;** before moving leftover money of one head to another, permission from the sanction letter or department.
- [ ] **Approval of payment** should be in writing, and the maker and approver should be different. (The full chain: [Payment approval and voucher](/blog/payment-approval-voucher-entry-secretary-chairman-treasurer).)
- [ ] **The purchase condition** (quotation/tender) should have been followed, and its paper should stay with the voucher.
- [ ] **In the asset register**, an asset bought from a grant should be marked separately.

## Leftover money and interest on it

This is where most disputes happen. The GFR form (below) has proper columns: **"unspent balance"**, **"interest earned on it"** and **"interest refunded to government"**. That is, in government schemes the bank interest on the grant balance is generally treated as **belonging to the scheme, not the society**. But all this depends on **the condition of your sanction letter**, so:

- [ ] Check the sanction letter on whether **the grant balance** may be kept in a savings/fixed deposit account.
- [ ] If **interest** comes, record it separately, and add it to the scheme or return it as per the condition.
- [ ] Whether **unspent money** will be adjusted in the next instalment or returned, the condition tells; do not take it into your income without knowing the condition.
- [ ] If leftover money has to be returned **after the time limit**, the refund entry: Dr grant received (liability), Cr bank.

## Utilization certificate (UC)

A **utilization certificate** is the written proof through which the society tells the government how much of the grant received was spent, on what work, as per the conditions of sanction. The next grant is often linked to it.

**In Central Government grants**, this form is **GFR 12-A** (reference to Rule 238(1) of the General Financial Rules, 2017). As per its form kept on a government department's website, it has these parts:

| Part | What to fill |
| --- | --- |
| Scheme and type | Name of scheme; whether the grant is recurring or non-recurring |
| Position at the start of the year | Balance in cash/bank; unadjusted advances; total |
| Details of grant (actual) | Unspent balance of previous years; interest earned on it; interest refunded to government; grant received this year (sanction No., date, amount); total available; expenditure incurred; closing balance |
| Component-wise utilization | General grant, salary grant, grant for creation of assets |
| Position at the end of the year | Cash/bank, unadjusted advances, total |
| **Certification** | Below |
| Signatures | Head of finance and head of the organisation |

**In the certification, the points the organisation checks before saying "we have satisfied ourselves"** (as per the form):

1. The main and subsidiary accounts and registers (**including the assets register**) have been kept as per the prescribed rules and **have been checked by the designated auditor; the figures above tally with the audited financial statements.**
2. There are **internal controls** for safeguarding public funds and assets, watching outcomes and quality, and they are checked from time to time.
3. No transaction has been made **in violation of any rule or scheme guideline**.
4. The **responsibilities** for executing the scheme are clearly assigned among the main functionaries.
5. The benefits reached the **intended beneficiaries** and only in the areas where the scheme was meant to run.
6. Spending on the components of the scheme was in the **approved proportion**.
7. The physical and financial performance of the scheme was as per the prescribed guidelines, and **details of outcomes are attached**.

A direct lesson comes from this list: **before signing the UC, you must have all this ready:** spending that tallies with the audited accounts, the asset register, the head-wise statement of spending and the beneficiary list. That is why preparation of the UC starts **from year-end**, not on the same day.

**The limits of my information, which must be stated honestly:**

- **GFR are Central Government rules.** The UC form of a scheme of the Haryana Government or its departments is set by its own financial rules or the sanction order; many state schemes use a form just like GFR 12-A, but I did not open any such Haryana rule.
- **Time limit:** in some central schemes the UC is asked for "within 12 months of the end of the financial year", as I found in secondary sources I read; but I could not verify this from the official text. **The time limit of your scheme is written in your sanction letter; follow that.**
- **Who signs:** the form has the signatures of the "head of finance" and the "head of the organisation". Who this will be in the society (such as secretary/accountant and chairman) should be decided by the bye-laws and the department's instruction; some schemes also ask for a chartered accountant's certification.
- **Risk of false information:** under Section 117(3) of the Act there is a provision of fine (up to ₹5,000) for knowingly giving a false return or information; in the case of grants there may also be liability under other laws, which I have not studied. So sign only after matching every figure of the UC with the books and vouchers.

## Audit and refund

Under Section 95(3) the auditor has free access to books, papers, vouchers and property. For a grant, the auditor will generally look at this:

- [ ] **The grant is recorded separately**, and receipt, spending and balance tally in the books.
- [ ] **Spending is within the approved head and conditions**, and every voucher refers to the sanction letter.
- [ ] **The figures of the utilization certificate tally with the audited figures.**
- [ ] **The asset** is in the register, with insurance and conditions of use.
- [ ] **Interest and unspent balance** were handled as per the condition.
- [ ] **The purchase procedure** condition was followed.
- [ ] **The UC was sent** and its copy is in the file.

**Diversion of a grant:** spending outside the approved head, or putting the money into other work, is the most serious lapse, because its result can be **refund, stopping of further instalments and action**. This depends on the "on violation" condition of the sanction letter. If for some reason the head really must change, take written permission **first**, not later. (Full audit preparation: [Audit preparation checklist](/blog/audit-preparation-checklist), [How an audit happens](/blog/how-cooperative-society-audit-works).)

## Law versus practice

| Point | Source | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forms of government aid | Section 46 | Share capital, loan/advance, guarantee, and **"financial assistance in any other form, including subsidy"** |
| Society taking grants from outside sources | Section 63(1) | Can take deposits, loans and **grants** up to the limit set in the bye-laws (the ten-times limit is on deposits and loans) |
| Government nominee member | Section 29(1)(a)(iv) | Right to a nominee member if loans and grants are ₹1 lakh or more; no nominee member in PACS |
| Auditor's access | Section 95(3) | To books, vouchers and property |
| False return | Section 117(3) | Fine for knowingly false information |
| Accounting of grants (revenue/capital/deferred income/capital reserve) | **ICAI's AS 12** | Accounting standard *(confirm whether binding on cooperative societies with the auditor/Registrar)* |
| Form of utilization certificate | **GFR 12-A (Centre)** | For central grants; in a state scheme, the sanction letter/state financial rules |
| Separate bank account for grant, UC time limit, whose interest | Sanction letter | **I did not find it in the Act/Rules** |
| Separate rule for grant accounts or UC of Haryana cooperative societies | — | **I did not find it** |

## Calendar

| When | Task |
| --- | --- |
| **As soon as sanction is received** | Reading the sanction letter, the committee's acceptance resolution, responsibilities, grant register and a separate head in the books |
| **On every instalment** | Receipt entry, check of conditions, entry in the register |
| **On every expense** | Check of approved head, approval, voucher with reference to the sanction letter |
| **Every month** | Grant account balance versus register; bank matching; new assets in the asset register |
| **Quarterly** | Progress report (if the scheme asks); status of leftover money and interest |
| **Year-end** | Year-end statement of expenditure, tally with audited figures, UC preparation, head-wise utilization |
| **Before the UC date** | Sending the UC and keeping its copy in the file |

## Common mistakes

1. **Booking the whole grant as income at once.** If the expense happens next year, profit rises falsely.
2. **Treating a grant received for members as the society's income.** It is a liability.
3. **Counting the government's share contribution or loan as a "grant".**
4. **Not creating a separate ledger head:** the money of one grant mixes with another.
5. **Spending outside the approved head,** or changing the head without permission.
6. **Not writing the sanction letter reference on vouchers.**
7. **Treating leftover money and interest as your own income** without checking the condition of the sanction letter.
8. **Not marking an asset bought from a grant in the asset register.**
9. **Signing the UC without matching its figures with the books and audited accounts.**
10. **Missing the UC time limit,** which can stop the next instalment.

## Grant accounts in SahakarLekha

Plainly: **SahakarLekha does not yet have a separate page for grants or the utilization certificate.** There is also no GFR 12-A form or separate grant register yet. But the general tools of the books are enough for this work, if you follow the framework above:

| Task | In SahakarLekha |
| --- | --- |
| Separate ledger head for each grant | Create new accounts in **Ledger Heads** (revenue grant liability, capital deferred grant, grant payable to members) |
| Receipt of grant | Receipt voucher in **Vouchers**: Dr bank, Cr that grant's head |
| Spending of grant | Payment voucher, sanction letter number in the narration |
| Taking into income (equal to expense) | **Journal voucher:** Dr grant (liability), Cr government grant (income) |
| Asset bought from grant, depreciation | **Asset Register** and **Depreciation Schedule** |
| Balance and activity of grant account | Ledger and **Trial Balance** for each head |
| Bank matching | **Bank Reconciliation** |
| UC form (GFR 12-A or the department's) | Your job: the portal does not make this form; take the figures above from the ledger and fill the form |
| Grant register, statement of expenditure form | Your job: make them from the Ledger and Trial Balance |
| Deciding the type of grant (income/deferred/capital reserve/liability) | Your and your auditor's decision |
| Whose interest, where the leftover money goes | The condition of the sanction letter and your decision |
| The department's prescribed portal/form | Final; the portal does not replace it |

**A caution about the chart of accounts:** the standard chart has some grant-related accounts: such as "Government Grant" (income, under the "Scheme Income" group) and in some charts "Grant Receivable" (asset). But **account numbers change according to the chart** (for example 3305 is "Grant Receivable" in one chart and some other head in another), and the income account "Government Grant" is not right for a capital or pass-through grant, because it turns it straight into income. So for capital grants and grants for members **create your own separate accounts**, and check your chart first.

That is, the portal carries the burden of books, spending, assets and matching, and the UC figures come straight out of your ledger; but the decision on type, compliance with the sanction letter's conditions and the signature on the utilization certificate are yours.

## Three things to remember

- 📜 A grant is money with conditions: first identify that it is a grant (not share contribution, loan or guarantee), then read the sanction letter; its conditions, time limit and form are final.
- 🧮 Accounting by type: reimbursement of expense into income **equal to the expense**; a grant for an asset into **deferred income or capital reserve**; money received for members is a **liability**. A separate head for every grant, and money only on the approved head.
- ✍️ Before signing the utilization certificate, every figure should tally with the ledger and the audited accounts; GFR 12-A is the form for central grants, in a state scheme the sanction letter and the department's form will apply; and the portal has no UC page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How to keep accounts of a government grant?** — After reading the sanction letter, create a separate ledger head and a grant register for every grant; make entries according to the type (revenue, capital, for members); spend only on the approved head and with reference to the sanction letter; handle leftover money and interest as per the condition; and finally give the utilization certificate.
**Is a grant income or a liability?** — It depends on the type. A revenue grant becomes income equal to the expense and stays a liability until the condition is met; a grant for an asset becomes deferred income or capital reserve; a grant received for members stays a liability. Booking the whole money as income at once is not right.
**What is a utilization certificate (UC)?** — The written proof in which the society tells the government how much of the grant was spent, on what work, as per the conditions of sanction. For central grants it is in the GFR 12-A form.
**By when must the UC be given?** — It is written in your sanction letter or the department's order. In some central schemes it is asked for within 12 months of the end of the financial year, but I have not verified this from the official text; follow your own scheme's time limit.
**Whose is the interest earned on the grant balance?** — GFR 12-A has columns for "interest earned" and "interest refunded to government", that is, in central schemes interest on the grant balance is generally treated as belonging to the scheme. But in your scheme it depends on the condition of the sanction letter.
**What to do with the leftover grant?** — Return it or adjust it in the next instalment/period as per the sanction letter. Do not take it into the society's income without knowing the condition.
**Is the government's contribution to share capital also a grant?** — No. In Section 46(a) it is a contribution to share capital, that is, capital; and AS 12 is also said to keep government ownership participation outside the scope of grants.
**Is AS 12 binding on a cooperative society?** — I have not seen a conclusive source for this. Generally ICAI's accounting standards run as guiding practice, but whether binding depends on Haryana's accounts formats, the Registrar's orders and the auditor; confirm with your auditor.
**Does SahakarLekha make the UC?** — No. The portal has no separate page for the UC or a grant register. The full accounts of a grant can be kept with Ledger Heads, Vouchers, Asset Register and Trial Balance, and the UC figures come from there.
**How will a grant be taxed?** — I did not open the text of income tax and GST, so I am not giving any limit or section. Confirm with your CA.

## Finally

Getting a grant looks easy, but giving its account is just as detailed, because here the money, even though it is the society's, is not fully the society's. In a society that has a separate account for every grant, vouchers that show the sanction letter reference, and figures that tally with the audited accounts, the utilization certificate is left as just a form to fill. And a secretary who read every condition of the sanction letter on the first day and handed it over to someone does not have to search at year-end for the answer to "where did this money go".
