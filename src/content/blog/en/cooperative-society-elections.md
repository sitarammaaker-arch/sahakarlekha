# How elections are held in a cooperative society

"Who decides the people who run the society?" The answer is **elections**. The biggest strength of a cooperative society is that the members themselves choose, democratically, the people who run it. This article explains **how elections are held in a cooperative society**: the principle, who elects whom, the full procedure, the election authority, term and disqualification, all in simple language.

## Why elections — the life of cooperative democracy

A cooperative society is "of the members, for the members", so the right to run it must also stay with the members. Regular, fair elections ensure exactly this: that no one person or group takes control for ever, and that every few years members get a chance to choose representatives they want. That is why timely elections are not just a formality; they are the real test of the cooperative principle of "democratic control". Where elections are held on time and fairly, the leadership stays accountable and members' voices count; where they are put off, the society slowly becomes the private estate of a few people, which goes against the basic spirit of cooperation. (See: [Principles of cooperation](/blog/cooperative-principles-and-values).)

## The biggest principle: one member, one vote
In a company, more shares mean more votes. **Not so in a cooperative society** — every member has **only one vote**, no matter how much capital they have put in. This is the core principle of "control by people, not by money".

This arrangement keeps equality between small and big members: a poor farmer's vote carries the same weight as the biggest shareholder's. That is why a cooperative society becomes a fair platform even for ordinary people, where control is decided not by the power of capital but by the collective will.

It has one more meaning: voting is personal. The member has to vote in person; usually they cannot authorise someone else to vote in their place (except in some cases, such as when a cooperative society is itself a member of another society). This rule ensures that every vote truly reflects that member's own wish, and is not the result of pressure or a deal.

## Who elects whom
- **General Body** = all members of the society — this is the supreme body.
- The general body elects the **Managing Committee (board of directors)**, which runs the society's day-to-day work.
- The managing committee (or the general body) then decides the **office-bearers** — chairman, secretary, treasurer.

| Body | Who | Work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General body | All members | Elect the managing committee, approve policy and budget |
| Managing committee | Elected members | Day-to-day running |

So it is a two-level system: members elect the managing committee, and the managing committee elects office-bearers from among itself. In this way, authority flows not from the top down, but from the bottom (the members) up. (See: [Role of the managing committee](/blog/managing-committee-role) and [Roles of office-bearers](/blog/office-bearers-chairman-secretary-treasurer).)

## The procedure — broadly
1. A **voter list** is prepared (eligible members).
2. **Nomination** — interested members file papers for the managing committee.
3. **Voting** — by secret ballot or show of hands (as per the bye-laws).
4. **Result** and formation of the new managing committee; then office-bearers are elected.

> ⚠️ Note: the details change according to the **state cooperative Act and the society's bye-laws**. In many states (after the 97th Constitutional Amendment), elections are conducted by the **State Cooperative Election Authority**, and the term is usually 5 years.

### Each step in a little more depth

- **Voter list:** only eligible members can vote — those whose membership is valid and who meet the conditions of the bye-laws (such as having no dues). The list is published in advance, so that no eligible member is left out and no ineligible person is added.
- **Nomination and scrutiny:** interested members file papers; then eligibility is checked (scrutiny). Ineligible nominations are rejected, and there is also a chance to withdraw if one wishes.
- **Voting:** a secret ballot increases fairness, because there is no pressure. Some small societies also vote by show of hands, as the bye-laws decide.
- **Counting and result:** votes are counted, winners are declared, and office-bearers are elected in the first meeting of the new managing committee.

Keeping a written record of every step is essential; this is what helps later in any dispute or audit. If a member objects to the election process, they do not go straight to court; such disputes go to the Registrar/election authority or the forum laid down. So keeping every step as per rules and documented avoids many later complaints by itself.

## An election cycle — in order

An election usually runs in this time order (the exact period as per the bye-laws/Act):

1. **Announcement:** notice of the election date and programme.
2. **Voter list:** the list of eligible members is published; time for objections and corrections.
3. **Nomination:** interested members file papers, then time for eligibility scrutiny and withdrawal.
4. **Campaign and voting:** on the fixed day, votes are cast by secret ballot or show of hands.
5. **Counting and result:** votes are counted and winners are declared.
6. **Formation:** the first meeting of the new managing committee is called; office-bearers are elected in it (within 60 days in Haryana).

This whole sequence is fixed and published in advance, so that every member knows what will happen when. This transparency is what makes the election fair.

## Elections of unions/federations

The cooperative structure is three-tier: primary society → district/central union → state/apex union. The managing committee of the upper-level unions is often elected by representatives of the societies below (not directly by ordinary members). So a member of a primary society directly elects their own society, and indirectly also takes part in forming the unions. This arrangement keeps the whole cooperative movement democratically connected.

## Reservation — everyone's participation

In many states, some seats on the managing committee are reserved for certain groups (such as Scheduled Castes/Tribes, women), to ensure everyone's participation in the society. This is the practical form of the cooperative values of "equality" and "democratic control". The exact reservation rules differ by state, so check your bye-laws and the state Act.

## Role of the election authority

After the 97th Constitutional Amendment, in many states the responsibility for holding cooperative elections has been given to an independent **State Cooperative Election Authority**. Its purpose is to keep elections fair and on time, so that no society can put off or influence its own election. Where this authority applies, the process from the voter list to the result is under its supervision. (Legal framework: [The framework of cooperative law](/blog/cooperative-law-framework).)

## Link with accounting/records
Elections, meetings and decisions must be **entered in the records**: voter list, minutes, list of office-bearers. Transparent records are essential for both audit and member trust. The audit checks that elections were held on time and as per rules, so keep the full documents of every election safely. A correct member register is the foundation of a correct voter list, so both must stay up to date. (See: [How a cooperative audit works](/blog/how-cooperative-society-audit-works).)

## Elections in Haryana: the time limits of Section 28
Under **Section 28** of the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984, the term of the managing committee is **5 years** from the date of election (Section 28(4)), and representation of at least one Scheduled Caste member and one woman member is compulsory. If the existing committee does not hold the election on time, the Registrar can hold the election itself within **90 days** (Section 28(3)). The election of office-bearers (chairman/secretary etc.) must be held within **60 days** of the committee's formation, and it cannot be put off under any circumstances.

> ⚠️ The strictest provision: members of an outgoing committee that fails to hold the election on time **can be barred from contesting for up to 5 years** in the next election.

## No-confidence motion — change in between

Even after the election, if members are unhappy with the working of the managing committee or office-bearers, they can be removed as per rules through a **no-confidence motion** (with the set conditions and majority). This ensures that elected representatives stay accountable for the whole term, rather than relaxing after winning. (In detail: [Membership: rights and duties](/blog/membership-rights-and-duties).)

## Common problems and how to avoid them

- **Not holding elections on time** → start the process before the term ends; delay brings strict results like Registrar intervention and disqualification.
- **Errors in the voter list** → publish the list in advance and keep it correct; give members a chance to object and correct it.
- **Not keeping records** → keep documents of every step (list, nomination, counting, result, minutes).
- **Ignoring reservation** → be sure to fill reserved seats (such as SC, women) as per rules.
- **Letting defaulters vote/contest** → check eligibility conditions as per the bye-laws.
- **Compromising secrecy/fairness** → ensure a secret ballot and fair counting.
- **Breaking the order of the procedure** → follow the fixed time order and notice period.

## Frequently asked questions
**How are cooperative elections held?** — The general body elects the managing committee on one member, one vote; then office-bearers are decided.
**Do more shares give more votes?** — No, every member has only one vote.
**How long is the term?** — Usually 5 years, but it depends on the state Act/bye-laws.
**Who conducts the election?** — In many states, the State Cooperative Election Authority; otherwise as per the bye-laws.
**Who can be a voter?** — A member with valid membership who meets the eligibility conditions (such as having no dues).
**What if elections are not held on time?** — The Registrar/authority can hold them itself; the outgoing committee can face results like disqualification.
**Why reservation?** — To ensure participation of all groups (such as SC, women) in the managing committee.
**What if there is dissatisfaction after the election?** — Change is possible through a no-confidence motion as per rules.
**How are union/federation elections held?** — The committee of an upper union is often elected by representatives of the societies below, not directly by ordinary members.
**Can someone else vote in my place?** — Usually not; voting is personal (except in some cases).
**When is the office-bearer election?** — Within the set time after the new managing committee is formed (within 60 days in Haryana).
**Why are election records needed?** — For both audit and dispute settlement; keep the voter list, minutes and result.

## Conclusion

Cooperative elections are the backbone of the society's democratic character: one member one vote, a timely and fair process, and a clear record of every step. A society that holds elections on time and transparently keeps members' trust strong and its leadership accountable. So treat elections not as a burden but as the soul of cooperation; this is what decides that the society truly stays "of the members".
