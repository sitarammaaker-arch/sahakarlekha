Today India is the world's largest milk-producing country — and the biggest contribution behind reaching this position comes from one institution: the National Dairy Development Board, or **NDDB**. It is important to know what NDDB is, what its history is, and what its role in the cooperative sector is — because its decisions directly affect the livelihood of lakhs of small milk producers.

## Founding and background

The National Dairy Development Board was set up in **1965**, and the name behind it is Dr. Verghese Kurien — the man called the father of the **"White Revolution"** in India. Seeing the success of the Amul cooperative model that began in Anand, Gujarat, the government decided that milk producers' cooperative societies should be set up across the whole country on the same lines, and a national-level institution was needed to lead this whole effort. NDDB is the result of that thinking. (The Amul model in detail: [Amul and the Anand Pattern](/blog/amul-anand-pattern-dairy-cooperative).)

> 💡 NDDB does not work like an ordinary government department, but as a **statutory body** created by an Act of Parliament, and even today its headquarters is not in a big city like Delhi or Mumbai, but in Anand, Gujarat itself — as if it were a sign of staying connected to its roots.

## Repeating the Amul model across the country

NDDB's basic idea was simple but revolutionary — the model that worked for the milk producers of one district in Anand (Gujarat) should be repeated across the whole of India. Amul's "Anand Pattern" had three tiers: a dairy cooperative society in the village, a milk union in the district, and a federation in the state. This structure removed middlemen and took the full benefit of milk directly to the farmer. NDDB's main job was to set up this proven structure in every corner of the country, so that every small milk producer could get organised and connect with the big market. (See: [The three-tier cooperative structure](/blog/cooperative-society-tiers-primary-central-state-apex).)

## Operation Flood — the world's largest dairy development project

To understand NDDB's work, the most important thing is to know **"Operation Flood"**, which began in 1970. Its aim was to form milk producers' cooperative societies in village after village, link them with a union and a state-level federation, so that the farmer's milk reached the big market directly and the role of middlemen was reduced.

> 📘 During this campaign, which ran in three phases, about 81,000 village-level dairy cooperative societies were set up. The result was that India, which once depended on outside help for milk, became the world's largest milk-producing country by 1998 — and it still holds that position today.

### The three phases of Operation Flood

This huge campaign ran broadly in three phases:

- **Phase-1 (about 1970–1980):** initial capital was raised from foreign aid (the sale of skimmed milk powder and butter-oil donated by the European Community); big cities were linked with village milk-sheds.
- **Phase-2 (about 1981–1985):** a large expansion of milk-sheds and urban markets.
- **Phase-3:** making the cooperative dairy network stronger and self-reliant.

Together these phases built a nationwide cooperative dairy system that even today carries crores of litres of milk from the farmer to the consumer. (The full story of the White Revolution: [Cooperatives in the Green and White Revolutions](/blog/cooperatives-in-green-and-white-revolution).)

## NDDB's subsidiary units

Today NDDB's work is not limited to history — several of its subsidiary companies and units work on different fronts:

| Unit | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Mother Dairy | A well-known brand that takes milk and dairy products to urban consumers |
| IDMC | Manufacture of dairy machinery and equipment |
| Indian Immunologicals Limited | Animal vaccines and medicines |
| NDDB Dairy Services | Running dairy services |
| NDDB Mrida | Work related to rural infrastructure |
| NDDB CALF Limited | Allied work |

That is, NDDB is present in some form or other in the whole chain from milking to the packet — from animal vaccines and machinery to the brand that reaches the urban consumer.

## In which directions NDDB works today

NDDB's present work is divided into several directions — scientific work on breed improvement and breeding of animals, so that cows and buffaloes give more and better-quality milk; research and advice on animal feed and nutrition, animal health and disease prevention; training and organisational support to strengthen cooperative societies; dairy engineering, that is, technology for chilling, processing and packaging milk; and a separate arrangement for taking financial services and credit to small milk producers.

> 💡 The idea with which this institution was set up sixty years ago — a fair price to the farmer for his produce, and reliable milk to the consumer — is just as relevant today; only the challenges are now of a different kind: rising demand, changing weather, and keeping pace with new technologies.

## Through the eyes of a milk producer

To understand NDDB's impact, look at an ordinary milk producer. Before the White Revolution he had to sell his milk to a local middleman at an arbitrary, low price, and payment too was uncertain. After Operation Flood a dairy cooperative society was formed in his village — now he gives his milk there every morning and evening, its quantity and fat are measured and the account is made immediately, and payment is regular and transparent. This change — from uncertain, exploitative selling to a reliable, organised system — came into the lives of crores of families. And NDDB laid the foundation of this change.

## How NDDB is different from NABARD/NCDC

All three apex institutions are connected with the cooperative sector, but their roles are different — it is useful to understand this difference:

- **NDDB** — specifically for **dairy**; setting up dairy cooperative societies, breed improvement, processing technology and dairy development.
- **NABARD** — the apex bank for agricultural-rural **credit**; refinance and supervision of cooperative banks. (See: [The role of NABARD](/blog/nabard-role-in-cooperatives).)
- **NCDC** — direct finance for cooperative **projects** (warehouses, processing etc.). (See: [The role of NCDC](/blog/ncdc-role-in-cooperatives).)

That is, when it comes to milk and dairy development, NDDB has the central role; NABARD for credit, and NCDC for project finance.

## Today's challenges in the dairy sector

Despite the success of the White Revolution, the dairy cooperative sector still faces challenges, on which NDDB and cooperative societies work: raising productivity to match rising demand; animal health and breed improvement; milk quality and the cold chain; changing weather and the cost of fodder; and competition from private dairy companies. The cooperative way of facing all this is the same as it has always been — keep the farmer organised, give him the right price and services, and maintain trust through transparent working. Dairy cooperative societies that stick to these basics stay strong in competition too.

## The importance of accounts in a dairy cooperative

The life of a milk cooperative society is its daily, accurate accounts — because here it has to handle every farmer's milk twice a day, payment according to its quantity and fat/quality, and member-wise accounts. Even a small mistake hurts the farmer's trust. That is why successful dairy cooperative societies put great stress on transparent, fast and reliable accounting. A good digital system makes this whole job — payment by quantity-fat-rate, member accounts and reports — much easier. (See: [Dairy cooperative accounting](/blog/dairy-cooperative-accounting-basics).)

## The impact of the White Revolution — in a few points

The impact of NDDB and Operation Flood can be summed up in a few points:

- **Self-reliance:** from a milk-importing country, India became the world's largest milk producer.
- **Farmer empowerment:** crores of small producers got a regular, transparent income.
- **Women's participation:** large participation of women in village dairy societies, so that household income came directly into their hands.
- **Rural economy:** milk became a reliable source of daily cash in the village.
- **Spread of the model:** the Anand Pattern was repeated across the country, and it also became an inspiration for other sectors.

This many-sided impact makes the White Revolution not just a "milk story", but a broad rural-social change.

## In closing

NDDB is one of India's most inspiring institutional successes — an institution that spread one district's proven cooperative model (Amul) across the whole country, changed the lives of crores of small milk producers, and made India self-reliant and a world leader in milk. The biggest lesson of its story is this — when small producers get organised, a transparent system is built, and technology and training reach them, any sector can move towards a revolution. This lesson is just as true today for every village dairy cooperative society.

The biggest lesson for a small dairy society is this — big success comes not from big capital, but from organisation, transparency and trust. Amul too started with a few farmers; NDDB turned that small seed into a banyan tree for the whole country. If your village society too keeps correct accounts of every member, pays regularly and transparently, and builds collective trust — it too can bring the same change in its area.
