A dispute can arise in a society at any time — an allegation of rigging in an election, an arbitrary decision by the committee, a tussle over dues, or a quarrel over expelling a member. Most members' first question is: **Can I go straight to court?** The Haryana Cooperative Societies Act 1984 answers — in most cases, no. Such disputes go first to the Registrar. This article explains in simple language where a dispute goes, what each section says, and what a member should do in practice.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** All sections have been matched with the official text of the Act (as amended up to October 2007) and the 2013 Amendment Act; see "Sources" below. We have not seen any amendment after 2013. This is general information, not legal advice — confirm for your own case with the original Act, the Registrar's office or a lawyer.

## First rule: a dispute goes to the Registrar, not to court

**Section 102** of the Act says that any dispute touching the constitution, management or business of a society, if it is between the following, shall go to **arbitration by the Registrar**:

- members, past members or persons claiming through them, among themselves;
- a member/past member and the society, its committee, any officer, employee or liquidator;
- the society and a past committee, past officers or employees (or their heirs);
- one society and another society (or their liquidators).

**No court shall entertain a suit** on such a dispute — this bar is written in Section 102(1) itself. Section 102(2) also makes clear that these matters too fall within this scope: a society's claim to recover dues from a member, a surety's claim against the principal debtor, and **a dispute relating to the election of any office-bearer of the society**.

> ⚠️ **Two important exceptions:** (1) Disciplinary action or service matters of a society's **paid servant** are outside Section 102 — they do not run through this route. (2) Proceedings under Section 101 (surcharge) are also not treated as a "dispute".

Whether a matter falls within the scope of Section 102 or not — on this, the Registrar's decision is final (Section 102(3)).

## Election disputes: the 30-day limit

The Registrar will hear a dispute about the election of a committee member or office-bearer only if it is referred to him **within 30 days from the date of declaration of the result** (Section 102(4)). This is the time limit most often missed — if you are unhappy with an election, don't delay.

## What the Registrar does with a dispute — Section 103

On receiving a dispute, the Registrar has three routes (Section 103(1)):

1. Decide it himself;
2. Transfer it to an officer to whom the government has given this power;
3. Refer it to another person appointed as arbitrator.

The Registrar or the arbitrator can also pass an interlocutory order until the decision comes (Section 103(4)). There is also a separate, faster route for recovering dues: the society or a financial institution can apply to the Registrar with a statement of account for a **recovery certificate** (Section 104).

## How the decision is enforced — Section 110

A decision (award) given under Section 103 **is treated as a decree of a civil court** and is enforced in the same way — or the Registrar (or an officer authorised by him) can enforce it through attachment and sale of property (Section 110). The recovery certificate of Section 104 is also recovered under Section 110.

## Why this system was made

Why does a dispute not go straight to court? There are three practical reasons behind it. First, **speed** — cases drag on for years in civil courts, while disputes over dues, shares or elections need quick settlement, otherwise the society's work stops. Second, **expertise** — the Registrar's office deals with cooperative law and working every day. Third, **cooperative spirit** — disputes should be settled "within the house", so that the relationship between the member and the institution does not break.

## Section 128: how far the civil court bar goes

Besides Section 102, **Section 128** of the Act imposes a general bar: except in the manner provided in the Act, no civil or revenue court shall entertain these matters — registration/amendment of a society or its bye-laws, removal of a committee, disputes under Section 102 or recovery under Section 104, and winding up of a society. Section 128(3) also says that an order, decision or award made under the Act cannot be challenged in any court (other than in the manner provided in the Act).

> ⚠️ This bar is on civil/revenue courts. The High Court's writ jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution is a separate question, and a writ against a cooperative society does not lie every time — it depends on how deep the government's control over the society is (in detail in [this article](/blog/cooperative-society-writ-jurisdiction-article-226)).

## Section 27: when the Registrar can stop a committee resolution

If, in the Registrar's opinion, a resolution of the committee (or sub-committee) is beyond the powers given by the Act, rules or bye-laws, **or** carrying it out would be against the interest of the society or its members, **or** it is likely to lead to waste or loss of the society's funds — the Registrar can **suspend** it by a written order. This suspension cannot be **for more than 6 months** (Section 27(1)).

But the matter does not end with suspension. After giving the committee an opportunity of hearing, the Registrar can also **rescind** the resolution, or allow it to continue with or without changes (Section 27(2)). A resolution that has already been carried out can also be rescinded or changed. However, **6 years after** the resolution is passed, no new proceeding can be started under this section — this keeps old decisions stable.

Section 27 is often mixed up with Sections 34 and 35, but the three are different:

| Provision | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| Section 27 | Suspending **a single resolution** (maximum 6 months) and rescinding or changing it after a hearing — the committee stays |
| Section 34 | Superseding **the whole committee**, if it makes persistent default, is negligent, acts against the society's interest, does not hold elections, or causes a deadlock in working |
| Section 35 | Removing **a single member** of the committee for default, negligence or acts against the society's interest; a removed member can be barred from contesting elections for up to 5 years |

> 💡 **The 2013 change to Section 34:** The 2013 amendment rewrote Section 34(1) entirely. Now, when a committee is superseded, a fresh election will be held, or administrators will be appointed for **a maximum of 6 months** (1 year in a society doing banking). And the committee of a society in which **there is no government share, loan, financial assistance or guarantee** can neither be superseded nor suspended. The old text had separate conditions for PACS such as "losses for 3 consecutive years"; the new 2013 text does not have them.

These powers are not exercised only by the state Registrar himself. Under Section 3(2), the **government** can give the Registrar's powers to officers appointed to assist the Registrar (such as the Deputy Registrar or Assistant Registrar of a district), and since 2013 the Act's definition of "Registrar" also includes such officers (Section 2(r)). So in practice many proceedings start at the district or sub-division level itself.

## The ladder of appeal — Section 114

If you are not satisfied with a decision, there is the route of **appeal** under Section 114. The orders that can be appealed include: the decision/award under Section 103, the recovery certificate under Section 104, rescinding a resolution under Section 27, removing a committee or member under Sections 34/35, expelling a member from the society, and (except in a producer society) refusing membership to an eligible person.

**Time limit: 60 days from the date of the decision or order.** To whom the appeal goes depends on who passed the order (Section 114(2)):

| Who passed the order | Appeal goes to |
| --- | --- |
| Assistant Registrar | Deputy Registrar |
| Deputy Registrar or Joint Registrar | Registrar, or an Additional Registrar authorised by the Registrar |
| Additional Registrar, Registrar, or an officer given power under Section 3(2) (not covered in the rows above) | State Government |
| Any other person (such as an arbitrator appointed by the Registrar) | Registrar or an officer authorised by him |

There is **no second appeal** against a decision given in appeal (Section 114(3)). Where an appeal does not lie to the government, the government can, on its own or on the application of an aggrieved party, call for the record and carry out a **revision** (Section 115). An interim order such as a stay can also be obtained during an appeal or revision (Section 116).

## Where to take a complaint — practical guide

| Situation | First route |
| --- | --- |
| A dispute touching the society's constitution, management or business (shares, dues, membership) | To the Registrar under Section 102 — in practice, the Registrar of Cooperative Societies office of your district/sub-division |
| Election of the committee or an office-bearer | Under Section 102, **within 30 days of the result** |
| A committee resolution beyond its powers or against the society's interest | A written complaint at the Registrar's office, so that action under Section 27 can be taken |
| Not satisfied with the decision of the Registrar/arbitrator | Appeal under Section 114 **within 60 days** |
| Service matter of a paid employee of the society | Outside Section 102 — a separate route; ask a lawyer about your case |
| Delay/negligence of a government office | **CM Window** ([cmharyanacell.nic.in](https://cmharyanacell.nic.in/)) — the Haryana government's complaint portal, with a tracking number |

> 💡 CM Window is for complaints about office slackness; it does not replace a Section 102 dispute or a Section 114 appeal. In matters with a time limit, take only the route given in the Act.

## How to make a complaint strong

When going to the Registrar's office, the preparation of the complaint decides its success:

- **Written and clear:** what happened, when, with whom — with dates, in plain language.
- **Attach documents:** copy of the resolution, meeting proceedings, correspondence, receipts.
- **Choose the right section:** is it a dispute (Section 102), an objection to a resolution (Section 27), or an appeal against an order (Section 114).
- **Remember the time limits:** election dispute 30 days, appeal 60 days.
- **Keep records:** keep a copy of the complaint and its acknowledgement.

## An example

Suppose a member feels the committee has passed a resolution against the rules that will waste the society's money. His first step is not the civil court but a written complaint at the Registrar's office. If the Registrar agrees, the resolution can be suspended for up to 6 months under Section 27, and also rescinded after a hearing. If the same member has a quarrel with the society over share money, it will go to the Registrar's arbitration under Section 102. And if the decision goes against him, he can appeal under Section 114 within 60 days. The Act has a route for every situation — you just have to identify the right one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can members go straight to civil court in a society-member dispute?** — Usually not. Disputes touching the society's constitution, management or business go to the Registrar's arbitration under Section 102, and Sections 102 and 128 bar civil courts from hearing such suits.
**What is the time limit for an election dispute?** — 30 days from the declaration of the result (Section 102(4)).
**For how long can the Registrar stop a committee's decision?** — Suspension for a maximum of 6 months (Section 27(1)); after a hearing the resolution can also be rescinded (Section 27(2)). Proceedings cannot be started 6 years after the resolution is passed.
**What if you are not satisfied with the decision of the Registrar/arbitrator?** — Appeal under Section 114 within 60 days; no second appeal on an appeal, but in some cases the government can carry out a revision under Section 115.
**How is the arbitrator's decision enforced?** — Under Section 110 it is treated as a decree of a civil court, or the Registrar enforces it through attachment and sale.
**Will a service matter of a society employee also go to the Registrar?** — No. Disciplinary action and service matters of paid employees are kept outside Section 102.
**Difference between Sections 27, 34 and 35?** — 27 is about a resolution, 34 about the whole committee, 35 about one member of the committee.
**Where to complain about delay in a government office?** — On the CM Window portal; but it does not replace a dispute or appeal under the Act.
**What is the fast route for recovering dues?** — The society can ask the Registrar for a recovery certificate under Section 104, which is recovered under Section 110.
**How to make a complaint strong?** — Written, with dates and documents, under the right section, within the time limit.

## Three things to remember

- 🧭 A dispute about the society's constitution, management or business goes first to the Registrar (Section 102) — not the civil court (Sections 102, 128).
- ⏱️ Two time limits: election dispute 30 days from the result (Section 102(4)), and appeal against any order 60 days (Section 114).
- ⏸️ Section 27 stops a resolution for up to 6 months and can also rescind it after a hearing; removing the whole committee is Section 34.
- 📄 Give the complaint in writing, with documents — and service matters of paid employees do not run through this route.

## In the end

The whole system of cooperative dispute resolution rests on one simple principle — disputes should first be settled "within the house" (with the Registrar or the arbitrator he appoints), and the appeal against the decision should also be within the department. This is good news for the member, because the route is fast and cheap — provided he identifies the right section and does not miss the time limit. So don't panic when a dispute arises: first identify whether it is a dispute, an objection to a resolution or an appeal, then give a written complaint within time.
