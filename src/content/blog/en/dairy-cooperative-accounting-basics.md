# Dairy cooperative society: understand the basics of its accounts

Dairy cooperative societies are among the best-known cooperative institutions in India. Their accounts are a little different from an ordinary society — daily milk collection, quality, and payment to members. This article gives a basic understanding of a dairy society (with advice to get expert validation on specific accounting treatment).

## What is a dairy cooperative society

It is a society of milk producers that collects members' milk, tests its quality (fat/SNF), pays for it and sells it on (through the district/state milk union). This gives small dairy farmers a fair price and an assured market.

> 💡 This is the village-level part of the "Amul model" — members pour milk every day and get paid according to quality and quantity. (See: [Amul and the Anand pattern](/blog/amul-anand-pattern-dairy-cooperative).)

A village dairy cooperative society (VDCS) usually collects milk twice a day — morning and evening. In each shift the member brings their milk, its quantity is weighed and its quality is tested. These two things — quantity and quality — are the basis of the member's payment. The society sends this milk to the district/state union, and the amount received from there pays the members and runs the society's expenses.

## What is special in the accounts

- **Daily collection** — record of quantity and quality.
- **Member-wise payment** — usually based on quantity and quality (fat/SNF).
- **Member accounts** — separate transactions and balance for each member.
- Often the supply of cattle feed/inputs too.

> ⚠️ The exact **accounting treatment** of fat/SNF-based payment rates depends on the state/union arrangement — confirm it with your CA/auditor or the milk union. **Needs Expert Validation**

## Fat and SNF — the basis of payment

The price of milk is set not just by the litre but also by quality. There are two main measures:

- **Fat:** the percentage of fat in the milk. It is higher in buffalo milk than in cow milk.
- **SNF (Solids-Not-Fat):** the other solids apart from fat (protein, lactose, minerals).

The higher the fat/SNF, the better the milk, and the higher the rate. This system rewards honest, good-quality milk and discourages wrongdoing like adding water. The rate chart (what price at which fat/SNF) is different for every milk union/state and changes from time to time — always take it from your union.

## A simple example — quantity and quality

Suppose two members bring milk on the same morning:

| Member | Quantity | Fat | Quality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 8 litres | 6.5% | High |
| B | 8 litres | 4.5% | Low |

Even though both have the same quantity (8 litres), member A's payment will be higher, because the quality (fat) of their milk is higher. In the accounts, this entry (quantity + fat/SNF + amount payable) should be recorded for every member, for every shift. At the end of the month or the ten-day cycle, each member's total payment is worked out from these same entries.

> ⚠️ The rates above are only to explain the principle. Confirm the real rate chart and its accounting treatment with your milk union/auditor. **Needs Expert Validation**

## Same foundation, different focus

The same foundation works in a dairy society too — double entry, member accounts, cash/bank book, reports. Only the focus stays on milk collection and member payments.

| Ordinary society | Focus in a dairy society |
| --- | --- |
| Member register | Members + daily collection |
| Sales/purchases | Milk collection and payment |
| Reports | The same, with member-wise detail |

So if you know the basics of general cooperative accounting (see: [Basics of cooperative accounting](/blog/cooperative-accounting-basics)), in a dairy society you only need to add that daily collection and fat/SNF-based payment are also recorded with the same discipline.

## Ten-day/monthly cycle and deductions

Most dairy societies do not pay in a lump sum every day, but in a cycle (such as ten-day or monthly). In this cycle each member's total milk and amount payable are added up, and some deductions (where applicable) are subtracted from it:

- **Cattle-feed advance:** if the society gave the member cattle feed on credit, it is recovered from the payment.
- **Other advances/services:** veterinary care, medicine etc., where the society provides such services.

That is why each member's **account (ledger)** is necessary — with their milk payment (payable) on one side and their deductions (advance recovery) on the other, so the net amount payable comes out clearly. This member-wise transparency is the root of the society's trust.

## Reconciliation with the union

The society sends its milk to the district/state milk union and gets an amount in return. An important task here is **reconciliation**: the milk the society sent (quantity and quality) and what the union paid for should match. If there is a difference, it should be caught in time and clarified with the union. Not doing this reconciliation can become the root of wrongly recorded income and wrong payments to members.

## Structure of the milk-collection register

The heart of a dairy society is its **collection register**. It has an entry for every member for every shift (morning/evening). A good collection record should have at least this much:

| Column | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Date and shift | Day + morning/evening |
| Member code/name | Which member |
| Quantity (litres) | How much milk |
| Fat % | Quality |
| SNF % | Quality |
| Rate | From the rate chart |
| Amount payable | Quantity × rate (quality-based) |

This record later becomes the basis of the member's cycle payment. If it is filled correctly every day, the end-of-cycle accounts come out by themselves; if it is slack, the whole payment cycle falters.

## Where the society's income comes from

A common misunderstanding is that the society earns a "profit" by selling milk. In fact a village-level dairy society often runs on a **commission/margin model** — that is, the fixed difference between the price the union gives and the payment made to members (or a per-litre commission) makes up the society's running costs and surplus. This surplus pays for the society's staff costs, electricity, transport etc., and as per the rules members may also get a dividend/bonus.

> ⚠️ The exact structure of income/commission and its accounting treatment differs in every state/union — confirm it with your milk union/auditor. **Needs Expert Validation**

So the society's accounts should clearly show not just "milk sales" but also the related expenses (transport, testing, staff) and the surplus, so that it is clear whether the society is financially sustainable or not.

## The role of technology

Today many dairy societies use machines like the **automatic milk collection unit (AMCU)**, which weigh the quantity of milk, measure fat/SNF, and instantly create a member-wise slip and record. This reduces human error and disputes and increases transparency. Where there is such a machine, its data must be matched regularly with the society's accounting records, so that the machine and the books both tell the same story. (The bigger picture of digital adoption: [Why digital accounting is needed](/blog/digital-accounting-for-cooperatives).)

## Practical tips for a small VDCS

- **Today's work today:** record the two shifts' collection on the same day — don't leave it pending.
- **One member, one code:** give every member a permanent code, so the records never get mixed up.
- **Always write the quality:** not just litres, but fat/SNF too — only then is the payment correct and fair.
- **Inputs separate:** keep credit for cattle feed/medicine in a separate account; don't mix it up with the milk payment.
- **End-of-cycle slip:** give every member a clear slip of their net amount payable.
- **Reconcile in two places:** with the union (sent vs paid) and with the bank (deposits vs books).

These simple habits keep even a small society organised and trustworthy without a large staff.

## Common mistakes

- **Recording collection late:** if each day's milk is not recorded the same day, the accounts go wrong at the end of the cycle.
- **Not writing fat/SNF:** recording only litres — this means a correct quality-based payment cannot be made.
- **Forgetting deductions:** if the recovery of the cattle-feed advance is missed, the society's money gets stuck.
- **Not reconciling with the union:** not checking that milk sent and milk paid for match.

## Accounts of cattle feed and inputs

Many dairy societies do not just take milk, they also give members **cattle feed, fodder, medicine** and other inputs — often on credit, which is later recovered from the milk payment. From the accounting point of view, this means the society on one side does milk purchases (from members) and milk sales (to the union), and on the other side also input sales (to members). So both sides should be recorded in each member's account — how much milk payment they earned, and how much input (on credit) they took. At the end of the cycle, net amount payable = milk payment − input recovery. If this input account is not kept separate and clear, the society's money gets stuck and there is confusion in the accounts with the member.

## Annual accounts and audit

Besides daily collection and cycle payments, a dairy society also has to prepare its **annual accounts** every year — income and expenditure, profit and loss and balance sheet — and get them **audited** as per the rules. For this, the whole year's collection, payments, deductions, receipts from the union and the society's expenses must all be correctly recorded. If the daily records are clean, the year-end accounts and audit stay easy; if not, there is a lot of confusion at year-end. That is why, in a dairy society, "daily discipline" is the price of annual peace. (See: [Audit preparation](/blog/audit-preparation-checklist).)

## What the member should see — transparency

A dairy society's biggest trust lies with its members, and this trust lasts through transparency. Every member should clearly see — how much milk they gave in this cycle, of what quality, at what rate, how much it came to in total, and after which deductions how much they got net. When all this is clear in front of the member, there are no disputes and no doubts. In digital accounts this slip is created automatically, which reduces the secretary's work and also increases the member's trust.

## Frequently asked questions

**How does a dairy cooperative society work?** — It collects members' milk, tests it, pays for it, and sells it on.
**Does it only sell milk?** — It often provides cattle feed and services too.
**Do all members get the same rate?** — Usually it depends on quantity and quality (the exact treatment needs expert validation).
**What is the difference between fat and SNF?** — Fat = % of fat in milk; SNF = the other solids apart from fat. Together they decide quality and price.
**When is payment made?** — Usually in a cycle (such as ten-day/monthly), after subtracting deductions.
**Why is reconciliation with the union needed?** — So that any difference between milk sent and milk paid for is caught in time and members are paid correctly.

## Three things to remember

- 🥛 Milk collection + quality + member payment = the centre.
- 🧾 The foundation is the same (double entry, member accounts, reports).
- ⚠️ The fat/SNF payment treatment needs expert validation.

## In the end

Once you understand the basics of a dairy society, its digital accounts become easy — members, collection and payments all in one place. The real key is discipline: each day's milk recorded the same day, with quality (fat/SNF); every member's account kept clean; and reconciliation with the union done on time. These three habits take even a small VDCS to the same transparency on which the whole Amul model stands.
