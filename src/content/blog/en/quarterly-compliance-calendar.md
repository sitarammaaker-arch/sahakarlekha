# Quarterly compliance calendar: GST, TDS and return due-dates

In a cooperative society, most penalties come from **missing a date** — late GST, late TDS, late returns. The cure is simple: a **compliance calendar** that reminds you what to do and when. This article gives you that framework, how to build and run it, and the month-end check — all in easy language.

## What is a compliance calendar

A compliance calendar is a simple list in which all the important dates of the year are written down in advance — which filing when, which payment when, which meeting when. It is not a complicated document; a sheet of paper, a wall chart, or a digital reminder is enough. Its only purpose is that no deadline depends on memory. Just as we remember the date of the electricity bill at home, every statutory date of the society should be written in one place — that is the compliance calendar.

## Why it matters
Late filing or payment attracts **interest + late fee**, and repeated misses show up in the audit. A calendar stops all of this.

It has three big benefits: **money is saved** (no penalty or interest), **reputation is saved** (timely compliance keeps the audit grade better), and **stress goes down** (no last-minute rush). On the other hand, a society without a calendar is often surprised only when a notice or late fee arrives — by then it is too late. So this small arrangement gives big protection.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** TDS dates have been matched with the Income Tax Rules, 2026 (Rules 218 and 219), and cooperative deadlines with the Haryana Cooperative Societies Act (including the 2013 amendment) — see "Sources" below. GST dates were not matched in this check, so they are not given here — get them from the GST portal or your CA. Dates can change; this article gives a framework, not the final authority.

## The usual quarterly/monthly framework
| Task | Usually when |
| --- | --- |
| GST (GSTR-1 / 3B) | **Every month** (by the due date) |
| TDS deposit | By the **7th of the next month** after deduction; deduction made in **March** by **30 April** (Income Tax Rules 2026, Rule 218) |
| Quarterly TDS statement (**Form 140**, earlier 26Q) | **31 July, 31 October, 31 January, 31 May** (Rule 219) |
| Advance tax (where applicable) | In instalments — dates from your CA |
| Audit, general body meeting, return to the Registrar (Haryana) | Within **6 months** of the end of the financial year — that is, by 30 September (Sections 95, 25(3), 48) |

> 💡 On the **1st** of every month, take 10 minutes and write down "what is due this month" — this is the cheapest "auto-reminder".

Remember that the table above is a **general framework** — not everything applies to every society. For example, a society with no GST registration has no GSTR to file; a society that does not deduct TDS has no Form 140 (earlier 26Q). So the first step is to know **which compliances apply to your society** — then put only their dates in the calendar.

## How to build your calendar — steps

1. **List the applicable compliances:** GST, TDS, annual return, audit, general body meeting — whichever apply to you.
2. **Write the due-date of each:** confirm current dates from your CA/portal and enter them month-wise.
3. **Fix responsibility:** whose task is each one (secretary/accountant) — so nobody says "I thought the other person would do it".
4. **Set reminders:** an alarm/note a few days before the due-date.
5. **Review every month:** at the start of the month, look at that month's tasks.

Once this calendar is made, you only have to follow it through the year — no need to think afresh every month.

## A sample annual calendar

Below is a **general** month-wise framework (only an example — confirm the exact dates that apply to your society from your CA/portal):

| Month | Main tasks (where applicable) |
| --- | --- |
| April | Start of the new financial year; budget finalised; March GST; TDS deducted in March deposited by 30 April |
| May | TDS statement (Form 140) for the January-March quarter by 31 May; monthly GST |
| June | First quarter review; monthly GST/TDS |
| July | TDS statement for April-June by 31 July; monthly compliance |
| August | Get the audit completed; prepare the general body meeting notice; monthly compliance |
| September | **In Haryana by 30 September:** audit (Section 95), general body meeting (Section 25(3)) and annual return to the Registrar (Section 48); monthly compliance |
| October | TDS statement for July-September by 31 October; monthly compliance |
| November | Audit compliance (settling objections); monthly compliance |
| December | Third quarter review; monthly compliance |
| January | TDS statement for October-December by 31 January; monthly compliance |
| February | Pre-year-end preparation; monthly compliance |
| March | Year-end closing; final reconciliation; monthly compliance |

If such a chart is on the wall or in a digital calendar, the picture of the whole year is in front of you at a glance. Apart from the above, also add the annual return, general body meeting and elections (when due) in their own months.

## Before the check — responsibility and records

A calendar works only when there is a **name** behind every task. So against every item, write whose responsibility it is — secretary, accountant, or someone else. Even where one person does everything, it must be written, so that accountability is clear. Also keep the **proof** of every filing and payment (challan, receipt, acknowledgement) in order in one folder. This record is very useful at audit time and becomes evidence of "it was done on time" in any dispute. A good habit is to put the proof in the folder on the same day, right after filing — if you put it off, it often gets lost.

## The 5-minute month-end check
1. GST matched + filed?
2. TDS deducted and deposited on time?
3. Bank reconciliation (BRS) up to date?
4. Bills attached to large vouchers?
5. Any due-date this month?

(For GST and TDS in depth, see the [GST guide](/blog/gst-for-cooperatives) and [TDS and Form 140](/blog/tds-and-26q-for-societies).)

This 5-minute check is the most powerful habit, because it catches small things every month, before they become a big mess at year-end. In a way, it is the "monthly health check" of compliance.

## What happens if you are late

If a filing or payment is missed, two things are usually charged — a **late fee** and **interest**. They look small, but when repeated they add up to a big amount and eat directly into the society's profit. An even bigger loss is **reputation** — repeated late compliance comes up as an objection in the audit report and lowers the society's grade. So the thinking "a little late, it's fine" turns out costly. Exact late-fee/interest rates change as per the rules — confirm with your CA/portal.

## Compliance is not only about tax

Note that the compliance calendar is not only for GST/TDS — also include **cooperative-specific compliance** in it: the annual audit, the annual return to the Registrar, the general body meeting on time, and elections (when due). All these also have fixed deadlines, and missing them can also bring Registrar objections and action. So a good calendar keeps tax compliance and cooperative compliance — both together. (Full picture of cooperative compliance: [10 essential tasks before year-end](/blog/year-end-readiness-checklist).)

## Main compliances — in brief

It is important to understand which compliances can come into the calendar. Main categories:

- **GST (where registered):** usually monthly/quarterly returns (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B) and an annual return. Not applicable to a society without GST registration. (See: [GST guide](/blog/gst-for-cooperatives).)
- **TDS (where deduction is made):** TDS deducted on salary, contracts, rent etc. deposited by the 7th of the next month (March's by 30 April), and the quarterly statements — Form 140 (earlier 26Q) for non-salary, Form 138 (earlier 24Q) for salary — on time. (See: [TDS and 26Q](/blog/tds-and-26q-for-societies).)
- **Cooperative annual return:** return to the Registrar as per the state Cooperative Act. In Haryana, as per Section 48 (2013), within 6 months of the end of the year — the annual report, audited accounts, plan for the surplus, list of bye-law amendments, and the declaration of the date of the general body meeting and elections.
- **Statutory audit:** in Haryana, as per Section 95 (2013), every year, within 6 months of the end of the year, by an auditor or auditing firm on the panel; if not done, the Registrar gets the audit done at the society's cost.
- **General body meeting (AGM):** in Haryana within 6 months of the end of the year (Section 25(3)); if not held, committee members risk 5 years' disqualification under Section 26(3).
- **Elections:** society elections, when due, at the fixed time.

The **exact dates, limits and rates** of every category change from time to time and by state/activity — so treat this list as a checklist of "what to keep in mind", and keep confirming the exact details that apply with your CA/RCS/portal. The aim here is that no category is missed, not to make you remember a date.

## Common mistakes

- **Not making a calendar at all** → everything on memory; misses are certain.
- **Not knowing the applicable compliances** → either unnecessary work, or something necessary gets missed.
- **Not fixing responsibility** → everyone thinks the other person will do it.
- **Leaving it to the last day** → missed due to a technical problem/rush; do it a few days earlier.
- **Not keeping records** → keep a copy of every filing/challan; it helps in the audit.

## How a digital system makes compliance easier

The most tiring part of compliance is not the filing but **preparing the figures** for it — matching sales and purchases for GST, the deduction details for TDS, the year-end figures for the return. Good cooperative accounting software keeps all this ready in advance: every voucher shows up directly in the reports, so the GST match, TDS summary and final accounts are available in one click at any time. This cuts filing preparation from hours to minutes, and also reduces the room for mistakes. Also, in digital records every challan and acknowledgement can be attached, which makes finding proof easy at audit time. So software does not replace the calendar — but it makes the work due on each date much lighter.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a compliance calendar?** — A list/reminder made in advance of all the important dates of the year (filings, payments, meetings).
**Why is it necessary?** — Late compliance attracts interest and late fee and lowers the audit grade; a calendar prevents this.
**Which compliances should it include?** — GST/TDS (where applicable), annual return, audit, general body meeting and elections.
**Where to get exact due-dates?** — Current dates keep changing; confirm them from the GST/Income Tax portal or your CA.
**Does everything apply to every society?** — No; first find out what applies to you, then put in only those dates.
**What is the loss if late?** — Late fee, interest and audit objection; if repeated, a big amount and falling reputation.
**Why the month-end check?** — To catch small misses in the same month, so they don't become big at year-end.
**How does a digital system help?** — It keeps reports and matching ready, so filing preparation is done in minutes.
**Should the calendar be on paper or digital?** — Both are fine; what matters is that reminders are set and everything is in one place. The auto-alarms of a digital calendar reduce the chance of a miss further.
**Who should be responsible?** — Fix one name (secretary/accountant) against every item; "everyone's responsibility" often becomes "no one's".
**What if a miss happens?** — Complete the filing/payment as soon as possible so the interest/late fee does not grow further; make the reminder firm for the future.

## Three things to remember
- 🗓️ Every due-date in the calendar — a miss = a penalty.
- 🔁 Build the habit of a monthly 5-minute check.
- 🧾 Keep a record of every filing/challan.

## In the end
Compliance is not hard — just "on time". Make a calendar that has both tax compliance (GST/TDS) and cooperative compliance (audit/return/general body meeting), fix the responsibility for every task, and make the 5-minute month-end check a habit. Once you do this, the society stays penalty-free and audit-ready — and you stay relaxed every month. Always keep confirming exact dates with your CA/portal, because they keep changing.

Remember, the whole game of compliance is "information + time" — knowing which task is due when, and getting it done on time. The calendar meets the first condition, and the month-end check and reminders the second. A society with these two habits rarely pays a penalty or gets a compliance objection in the audit. Start today with a plain sheet of paper or a digital calendar — this small investment of the year gives very big peace of mind.
