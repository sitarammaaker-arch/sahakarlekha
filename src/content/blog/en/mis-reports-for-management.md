# MIS Reports for Management: The Reports You Need to Run a Society

Audit and annual reports are made once a year — but a society **runs every day**. For day-to-day decisions the managing committee needs regular, short reports. These are called **MIS (Management Information System) reports**. This article explains what MIS is, which report to see when, what to put before a meeting, and what good MIS looks like.

## What is MIS
MIS means the **short, regular reports** that give the managing committee the right picture on time — so that a problem is caught when it happens, not at the end of the year. The aim is not the full accounts, but **a few figures useful for decisions**.

The difference is important to understand: final accounts and audit are for "compliance" (giving account to the law and to members), while MIS is for "management" — that is, for taking better decisions day after day. Audit tells you "what happened last year"; MIS tells you "what is happening now, and what should be done now". That is why MIS looks to the future, not the past — and this is what makes it the real tool of management.

## Which report, when
| Frequency | Report | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily | Cash/bank balance | Where the money stands |
| Weekly | Recovery/overdue list | Watch on recovery |
| Monthly | Income-expenditure summary, stock position | Profit/expenses and goods |
| Quarterly | Budget vs actual | Deviation from the plan |

> 💡 The rule of good MIS: **fewer but right.** Nobody reads a twenty-page report; a one-page summary is useful at every meeting.

### Purpose of each report

- **Cash/bank balance (daily):** The most basic — know how much money is in hand today, so that no payment is made without funds and any irregularity is caught early.
- **Recovery/overdues (weekly):** The lifeline of credit societies; if overdues are rising, act at once. (See: [Loan recovery](/blog/loan-recovery-before-year-end).)
- **Income-expenditure summary (monthly):** The month's profit/loss and big expenses — the main direction-finder for management.
- **Stock position (monthly):** For a society that sells goods — what is stuck, what is selling slowly.
- **Budget vs actual (quarterly):** How far you have moved from the plan, so that you can correct in time. (See: [Budget vs actual](/blog/budget-vs-actual-analysis).)

## What to look at in a managing committee meeting
At every meeting, at least: cash/bank balance, the month's income-expenditure, the overdue/recovery position, and big pending payments. With just this much, most decisions get taken on time. (See: [Roles of office-bearers](/blog/office-bearers-chairman-secretary-treasurer).)

A good habit is to make this one-page MIS summary the first item on every meeting's agenda — so that decisions are based on figures, not guesswork or memory. When the managing committee looks at these figures regularly, problems are caught while they are small, and the society runs by "planning" instead of "reaction".

## Qualities of good MIS

- **Timely:** A correct report that comes late is useless; it is needed at the time of the decision.
- **Short:** Understood at a glance; only as many figures as needed, not more.
- **Correct:** Figures must be reliable — wrong MIS leads to wrong decisions.
- **Comparative:** Compare with last month/budget, so the trend shows.
- **Actionable:** The report should make clear what needs to be done next.

MIS with these qualities becomes the managing committee's most reliable helper.

## What decisions come from MIS — examples

MIS is not just figures, it is the basis of decisions. Some examples:

- The cash report shows a lot of cash in the safe → decision to deposit it in the bank.
- The recovery report shows some members' overdues are rising → decision to run a recovery drive.
- Income-expenditure shows one expense head grew fast → decision to examine and control it.
- The stock report shows one item is selling slowly → decision to cut its order.
- Budget vs actual shows income is below estimate → check the cause and revise the plan.

That is, behind every report there is a possible action — this is what turns MIS from "information" into "management".

## One-page MIS — a sample

Suppose there is a credit-cum-consumer society. A one-page MIS for its monthly managing committee meeting could look like this:

| Item | This month | Last month | Budget/target | Remark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash balance | ₹42,000 | ₹38,000 | — | Within the safe limit |
| Bank balance | ₹6,10,000 | ₹5,80,000 | — | Reconciliation up to date |
| Income this month | ₹1,85,000 | ₹1,72,000 | ₹1,80,000 | Above target ✅ |
| Expenditure this month | ₹1,40,000 | ₹1,25,000 | ₹1,30,000 | Above target ⚠️ |
| Loans outstanding | ₹9,20,000 | ₹9,55,000 | Reduce | Falling ✅ |
| Recovery% | 88% | 84% | 90% | Improving |
| Slow stock | ₹22,000 | ₹18,000 | Reduce | Rising ⚠️ |

From this one table the managing committee sees at once what is fine (income, recovery) and what needs attention (expenditure rising, slow stock rising). This is the power of MIS — **one glance, the full picture.** Note that every item has a column for last month and target — a figure alone, without comparison, is incomplete. "Income this month ₹1,85,000" alone says nothing; but "above last month's ₹1,72,000 and the target of ₹1,80,000" says the direction is right.

## Which indicators (KPI) to watch

Every society has some basic indicators (Key Performance Indicators) that should be kept in the MIS regularly:

- **Recovery percentage:** How much of the total due was recovered — the most important indicator for a credit society.
- **NPA/overdue ratio:** How much of the loans is moving towards bad debt.
- **Income-expenditure ratio:** How much is spent compared to income — the health of profit.
- **Cash days:** How many days the society can run on its current cash.
- **Stock turnover:** How fast goods are selling (for a trading society).
- **Business per member:** A measure of the society's reach among members.

You do not need to see all of them together — pick the 3–4 most relevant to your type of society and look at them at every meeting. Over time, the trend of these tells the society's real story. (For depth, see: [Financial ratios](/blog/financial-ratios-for-cooperatives).)

## How to start MIS in a small society

If the society is small and has no MIS so far, keep the start simple — do not build the whole system in one day:

1. **Start with one register/sheet:** Writing the daily cash-bank balance in one place — this is your first MIS.
2. **Add a weekly overdue list:** Who owes how much each week, on one page.
3. **Make a monthly income-expenditure summary:** The month's total income and expenses, with the main heads.
4. **Put just this before the meeting:** Make a table like the sample above and make it the first item of every meeting.
5. **Grow slowly:** Once the habit forms, add comparison columns and KPIs.

With this step-by-step method the MIS habit forms without a burden. And when the society adopts a digital system, all these reports start being made automatically.

## Dashboard — the modern form of MIS
Now there is no need to make these reports on paper — the **dashboard** of cooperative-specific software shows cash, recovery, stock and income-expenditure on one screen, live. This is the easiest form of MIS.

The biggest advantage of a dashboard is that the figures are built **automatically, live** — nobody has to add up a report by hand, and the chance of error falls. When every voucher shows up directly in the reports, the managing committee can see the correct picture of the society on any day, at any moment — this is the biggest difference from the old "report made a month later".

## How to make MIS effective

Good MIS is built not just from correct figures but from correct habits:

- **Who makes it:** Usually the secretary/accountant, but the figures must come straight from the accounting records — not separately "made up" numbers, or trust breaks.
- **When to circulate:** It should reach members a few days before the meeting, so they come having read it — seeing it for the first time in the meeting keeps the discussion shallow.
- **Same format:** The same table, the same items every month — only then is month-to-month comparison possible. A new format each time spreads confusion.
- **Highlight the signals:** Highlight the item that needs attention with colour/marks (✅/⚠️), so the eye goes there at once.
- **Record action:** What was decided in the last meeting, its status should also be in this MIS — no "decided, then forgot".

With these habits MIS becomes not a formal paper but a living decision tool at every meeting.

## Common mistakes

- **Very long report** → nobody reads it; keep a one-page summary.
- **Late report** → it is needed at the time of the decision, not after.
- **Looking only at year-end** → the whole point of MIS is that it is regular (daily/weekly/monthly).
- **No comparison** → without comparison with last month/budget, the trend does not show.
- **Not acting after seeing the report** → take a decision on every signal.

## Reviewing the MIS from time to time

One thing is often missed — the MIS itself needs review. Once a year ask: are these reports still useful? Is there information we need that is not coming in the reports? Is there a column that nobody looks at any more? The society grows, its activities change — so the MIS must change with it. MIS that was right two years ago may be incomplete today. With this small yearly review the MIS always stays relevant and useful, and does not become a paper formality.

## Frequently asked questions
**What is an MIS report?** — Short, regular reports for management decisions (cash, recovery, income-expenditure etc.).
**How often should we look at them?** — Cash daily, recovery weekly, income-expenditure monthly, budget comparison quarterly.
**What is the difference between MIS and an audit report?** — MIS is regular and internal (for running the society, future-facing); audit is yearly and external (compliance, about the past).
**Does a small society need MIS?** — Yes; even a one-page weekly summary is very useful.
**What should good MIS be like?** — Timely, short, correct, comparative and actionable.
**Who is MIS made for?** — Mainly for the managing committee and office-bearers, for day-to-day decisions.
**What is a dashboard?** — The live, automatically built form of MIS — all figures on one screen.
**What is the benefit of MIS?** — Problems are caught early, decisions are based on figures, and the society runs by plan.

## Three things to remember
- 🗂️ MIS = short, regular reports useful for decisions (not the full accounts).
- ⏱️ Cash daily, recovery weekly, income-expenditure monthly, budget comparison quarterly.
- 📱 The dashboard is the easiest, live form of MIS.

## MIS and transparency

MIS is not only a tool for the managing committee — it is also a means of transparency towards members. When the society's main figures (total income, surplus, recovery position, progress of big schemes) also reach members regularly — for example on the notice board or in the general body — members' trust in the society grows. Openness is the core principle of cooperation itself; MIS is a simple way to put that principle into practice. Remember to keep the figures for members even simpler, in summary form — what is a detailed MIS for the managing committee can shrink to three or four key numbers for members.

## In the end
MIS reports save the society from "walking in the dark" — by giving correct, timely figures at every level they help the managing committee take better decisions. The rule is simple: fewer but right, on time, and with comparison. Keep a one-page summary at every meeting, act on every signal, and use a dashboard wherever possible. Do just this and the society starts running by plan instead of reaction — and that is the mark of a sound, growing society.
