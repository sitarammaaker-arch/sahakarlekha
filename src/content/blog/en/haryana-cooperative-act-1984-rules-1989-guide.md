In Haryana, every cooperative society — whether a PACS, a CM-PACS, a milk society or a big institution like HARCO Bank — is legally run under one basic law: the **Haryana Cooperative Societies Act, 1984** (Haryana Act No. 22 of 1984). From registration to elections, audit, disputes and winding up — every process is set by one chapter or another of this Act. But reading the Act alone is not enough — the practical framework for applying it comes from the **Haryana Cooperative Societies Rules, 1989**. This guide explains the full structure of both documents in simple language.

> ⚠️ **Information checked up to 18 October 2026.** For the Act, the basis is the government's text as amended up to October 2007 and the 2013 Amendment Act (Haryana Act 10 of 2013); for the Rules, the basis is the Haryana Cooperation Department's copy and the Indian Kanoon copy (see "Sources" below). We have not seen the text of any amendment after 2013. This is a general information guide, not legal advice — in a specific case, confirm with the original bare Act/Rules, the Haryana Cooperation Department or a qualified lawyer.

## The difference between the Act and the Rules

In simple words — the **Act** sets "what to do" (for example: every society must be audited), while the **Rules** mostly say "how to do it" (for example: forms, procedure, within how many months the Balance Sheet must be prepared). Made 5 years after the 1984 Act, these Rules give the practical procedure for each chapter of the Act — that is why both must be read together.

### Main provisions on registration (Chapter II)

To form a new cooperative society, at least **10 persons** (above 18 years) are needed; for a society formed under a government employment scheme this number can be 5 (section 5). If the Registrar refuses registration, the applicant must be informed with reasons within **one month** of the application, otherwise registration is deemed accepted (section 8(2)). An amendment of bye-laws is passed by a 2/3 majority of members present and voting in the general body, after 15 clear days' notice, and the Registrar has to register it within 3 months (section 10). The "sick society" provision (section 14-A) applies only to societies **in which the government holds the majority of shares**: if at the end of any one of the last three financial years the accumulated loss is equal to or more than the net worth, the Registrar can order its assets and liabilities to be handed over to another society, after which the society is dissolved.

### Rights of members (Chapter III)

Every member has the right to only **one vote** (section 20), however many shares they hold — this gives legal basis to the cooperative principle of "one member, one vote" (exceptions such as the chairman's casting vote, and no vote for nominal/associate members and defaulters, are also in this section). Voting is only in person; proxy is not allowed (section 21). Shares can be transferred only if they have been held for at least 2 years (section 22), and the liability of a former or deceased member continues for 2 years (section 24).

### Management, elections and Registrar's powers (Chapter IV)

In practice this chapter is the centre of the most disputes. The final authority of the society lies with the general body (section 25). If a committee resolution is against the Act/Rules/bye-laws or against the interest of the society, the Registrar can cancel it, and suspend it for up to 6 months until the decision (section 27). The committee must have one Scheduled Caste member and — after the 2013 amendment — **two women members**; where Backward Class members are 10% or more, one Backward Class member too (section 28). Members of a committee that does not hold elections on time can be barred from contesting elections for 5 years (section 28(3)), and since 2013 elections are held by a separate election authority (section 28(1A)). Where the government has given help of at least ₹1 lakh (share capital, loan or guarantee), it can nominate up to 3 members or 1/3 of the committee (whichever is less) — but **there is no government nominee on the committee of a primary agricultural credit society (PACS)** (section 29). After the 2013 amendment, an administrator can stay for up to 6 months (1 year in a banking society), and the committee of a society in which the government has no share, loan or guarantee cannot be superseded (sections 33-34, as changed in 2013).

### Audit, disputes, winding up and penalties (Chapters XIV, XV, XVI, XIX)

The main provision on audit is **section 95**. After the 2013 amendment the government makes a panel of auditors, the general body appoints the auditor from that panel (section 25), and the audit must be done within **6 months** of the end of the financial year; if the society fails, the Registrar can get the audit done at the society's cost. The provision for rectifying defects found in audit is in section 96. Disputes about the constitution, management or business of the society go not to a civil court but to the Registrar's arbitration (**section 102**, reference to an arbitrator in section 103); an employee's disciplinary/service matters are outside this (see: [Where to take a dispute in a society](/blog/cooperative-society-dispute-resolution-haryana)). Winding up is in **section 105**: after audit/inquiry/inspection, on the application of at least **three-fourths** of the members, when the number of members falls below the minimum of section 5, or when the society does not start work or stops working, the Registrar can pass an order, after which a liquidator is appointed. Offences and penalties are in section 117: after the 2013 amendment, most offences carry a fine of up to ₹10,000 and some also imprisonment; certain set offences can be "compounded" by the Registrar (section 117(20)).

### Some practical time limits to remember

| Provision | Time limit |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum families for registration | 5 (rule 4) |
| Preparing the Balance Sheet (from the end of the financial year) | Within 3 months (rule 79); audit in 6 months (section 95, 2013) |
| Arbitration award | Within 6 months, extension possible (rule 86) |
| Maximum limit of dividend | 10% per year (rule 72) |
| Contribution to education fund | Up to 2% of net profit (rule 73); section 87(1)(b) of the Act says up to 5% — check both |
| Permission to destroy records | 3 years after cancellation of registration, except pending cases (rule 101) |

One point to note is that the detailed procedure for committee elections — appointment of the returning officer, voter list, nomination, polling — is given not in the main Rules but in a separate **"Appendix A"**, which is directly referred to in rule 25. The framework for applying section 28 of the Act (the basic election provisions) in practice comes from here; since 2013 the election authority of section 28(1A) has also been added, so check its rules/instructions too.

> 💡 Members or society secretaries often assume that reading only the Act is enough, but real disputes (late audit, questions on election procedure, Balance Sheet not made on time) often arise from not knowing the exact time limits and forms given in the Rules.

## How to use this in practice

There is no need to panic on seeing so many chapters and sections — a society secretary or member does not have to memorise the whole Act, but has to know *which part to look at in which situation*. When the question is about registration → Chapter II; membership/voting → Chapter III; elections/management → Chapter IV; audit → Chapter XIV; disputes → Chapter XV. And every time, look at the related rule along with the Act — because the exact form, fee and time limit are found there. With this habit of "situation → chapter → rule", this huge law becomes a practical reference book, not a frightening document.

## Common practical situations

A rough map for the questions that come up most often day to day:

- **Want to form a new society?** → Minimum members/families, bye-laws, Registrar's decision (Chapter II + Rules).
- **Election/management dispute?** → Supremacy of the general body, suspension of resolutions, representation (Chapter IV) + the procedure in Appendix A.
- **Audit/accounts?** → Appointment of auditor and time limits (Chapter XIV, section 95 + rules 77-80).
- **Dispute with someone?** → Not a civil court, but the Registrar/arbitrator (Chapter XV, section 102).
- **Recovery of dues?** → Execution of awards/decrees (Chapter XVII) and attachment and sale of produce (Chapter X).

In every situation, treat the original bare Act/Rules and the Cooperation Department as the final word — this guide only shows the way.

## Why this matters

This law is from 1984, but even today every PACS, CM-PACS, milk society and marketing society in Haryana runs under it (see: [What is CM-PACS](/blog/cm-pacs-kya-hai)). For the sections with the most practical impact — 27 (cancellation/suspension of resolutions), 28 (elections) and 102 (disputes go to the Registrar; along with the bar on civil courts in section 128) — we have also published separate detailed articles.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between the Act and the Rules?** — The Act sets "what to do", the Rules say "how to do it" (forms, fees, time limits).
**How many members are needed to form a new society?** — At least 10 persons under section 5 of the Act (5 under a government employment scheme), and under rule 4 they must be from at least 5 families.
**Where is the detailed election procedure found?** — Not in the main Rules, but in a separate "Appendix A", which is referred to in rule 25.
**Which sections have the most practical impact?** — Usually section 27 (cancellation/suspension of resolutions), 28 (elections/representation) and dispute settlement (Chapter XV, section 102) — most everyday disputes revolve around these.
**Within how much time must the Balance Sheet be prepared?** — According to rule 79, within 3 months of the end of the financial year; and after the 2013 amendment, the audit within 6 months (section 95).
**The legal basis of "one member, one vote"?** — Section 20 of Chapter III — however many shares, every member has only one vote; proxy is not allowed (section 21).
**Where do disputes go?** — Not directly to a civil court, but to the Registrar/arbitrator (Chapter XV, sections 102-103); an employee's service/disciplinary matters are outside this. Appeal against the decision is in Chapter XVIII.
**Maximum limit of dividend?** — According to rule 72, 10% per year on paid-up share capital; to the education fund, up to 2% of net profit under rule 73 (up to 5% in section 87(1)(b) of the Act).
**When can a sick society be dissolved?** — Section 14-A applies only to societies in which the government holds the majority of shares: if at the end of any one of the last three financial years the accumulated loss is equal to or more than the net worth, the society is dissolved after transfer of assets.
**How to use this huge law?** — Don't memorise all of it; use it like a practical reference book with the habit of "situation → related chapter → its rule".
**Where to get final confirmation?** — In a specific case, from the original bare Act/Rules, the Haryana Cooperation Department or a qualified lawyer — this guide is general information, not legal advice.
