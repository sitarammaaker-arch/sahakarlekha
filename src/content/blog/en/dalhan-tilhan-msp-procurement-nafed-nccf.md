Government procurement of pulses and oilseeds at the minimum support price (MSP) happens under the Centre's **PM-AASHA** scheme, and its central nodal agencies are two cooperative federations — the National Agricultural Cooperative Marketing Federation of India (NAFED) and the National Cooperative Consumers' Federation of India (NCCF). This guide explains the whole framework in simple language — who buys, how a farmer sells, and how a society can join in.

The September 2024 Cabinet approval was up to 2025-26; we have not matched the position after that with any government text. ## The MSP procurement framework — why only cooperative federations

**PM-AASHA** (Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan) started in September 2018. On 18 September 2024 the Cabinet approved its continuation up to 2025-26 (total ₹35,000 crore). Its components are: the **Price Support Scheme (PSS)**, the Price Stabilisation Fund (PSF), the **Price Deficiency Payment Scheme (PDPS)** and the Market Intervention Scheme (MIS).

According to PIB (18 December 2024):

- PSS applies only in states that **make a request** and agree to **waive mandi tax** on procurement of notified pulses, oilseeds and copra.
- The Central Government gets procurement done from farmers at centres set up at different places, by engaging central nodal agencies like **NAFED and NCCF** together with **state-level agencies**.
- From 2024-25, PSS approval is given at the start for procurement of up to **25%** of the state's production (up to 25% of national production when needed).

> 💡 **A second route in oilseeds — PDPS:** If a state wishes, it can choose **PDPS** instead of PSS for a notified oilseed. Here the farmer sells in open auction in the mandi (that is, to traders), and the difference between MSP and the sale/model price — **up to 15% of MSP** — is paid by the Central Government directly to the registered farmer (up to 40% of production, for a period of 4 months). So the MSP benefit does not always come only from government purchase; sometimes it comes through a difference payment.

## What is MSP — in short

MSP (Minimum Support Price) is the minimum price the government guarantees — that is, even if the market price falls below it, the farmer will get this fixed price. It protects the farmer from the risk of a price fall and gives confidence in crop planning. It is especially important for crops like pulses and oilseeds, because their prices go up and down more. The government announces the MSP, but the work of putting it into practice on the ground — actually buying at that price — is done by agencies like NAFED/NCCF.

## What happened to private procurement (PPSS)

In 2018, along with PM-AASHA, a pilot for private procurement and stockists (PPSS) had also come. Among the PM-AASHA components continued in September 2024 (PSS, PSF, PDPS, MIS), **PPSS is not named**. We did not find the reason why PPSS was not kept in any government text.

> 💡 Under PSS, government purchase at MSP happens through the centres of NAFED/NCCF and state agencies — no private trader does "PSS MSP procurement". But in oilseeds, if the state has chosen PDPS, the farmer can sell to a trader in the mandi and still get the difference payment.

## Farmer registration is also through the cooperative route

According to PIB (18 September 2024), MSP procurement covers farmers **already registered** on NAFED's **eSamridhi** portal and NCCF's **eSamyukti** portal — that is, not just the purchase, but the start of the process also runs through these cooperative channels. (Some states also register farmers on their own portal — for example "Meri Fasal Mera Byora" in Haryana; check your state's system.) The farmer has to register on these portals in time; only then can they later sell their produce at MSP.

### Why pre-registration is necessary

Pre-registration is not just a formality — it is the backbone of the whole procurement system. It tells the agencies in advance how much produce is coming in which area, so they can prepare procurement centres, storage and payment. For the farmer it means — if they do not register in time, they may miss MSP procurement, even if their produce is eligible. So society secretaries have an important role — making their member-farmers aware and helping them register in time.

## Step by step for the farmer

If a farmer wants to sell pulses or oilseeds at MSP, the usual path is like this:

1. **Pre-registration:** Before the season, register on NAFED's eSamridhi or NCCF's eSamyukti portal (or through the system the state has set).
2. **Wait for the notice:** When you get notice of the procurement centre and date, keep the produce ready.
3. **Quality standards:** The produce must meet the set quality standards (moisture, cleanliness etc.) — only then does MSP procurement happen.
4. **Sale at the procurement centre:** Get the produce weighed and sell it at the fixed procurement centre (often a cooperative society/federation).
5. **Payment:** Payment usually comes directly into the farmer's bank account.

The most important of these is the first step — registering in time. A farmer who misses it often misses MSP procurement too. This is where society secretaries can help their members a lot — by reminding them to register on time, getting correct bank details filled in, and helping with the process.

## Which crops — a general picture

Under PSS, MSP procurement happens for **notified pulses, oilseeds and copra** — such as gram, moong, urad, arhar/tur and masoor among pulses, and mustard, groundnut and soybean among oilseeds. According to PIB (December 2024), in Rabi 2023-24, 6.41 lakh tonnes of pulses (from 2.75 lakh farmers) and 12.19 lakh tonnes of oilseeds were procured. For **tur, urad and masoor** the 25% limit was removed in 2024-25, and under the "Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses" started in October 2025, NAFED and NCCF will do **100% procurement** of these three pulses in participating states for the next **four years**. All this is mainly for these crops — that is, crops whose prices go up and down more and which need market protection the most. Exactly which crop is approved for MSP procurement, in which state, in which season — this depends on the government's announcement every year. So the farmer and the society should look at each season's official announcement, and not assume last year's position.

## An opportunity for cooperative societies — how to become a procurement centre

Since the whole procurement framework runs only through cooperative federations, state cooperative marketing federations and registered primary agricultural cooperative societies (including PACS) can link with NAFED-NCCF to work as procurement centres. This can give the society a double benefit — a reliable way for farmer members to sell directly at MSP rates, and a source of extra income for the society, such as procurement commission.

> ⚠️ The process of becoming a procurement centre, the documents needed and the structure of commission/service charges can change from state to state and season to season. Interested societies should confirm the current process directly with their state cooperation department or the regional office of NAFED/NCCF.

## Benefits of becoming a procurement centre — in depth

For a PACS or cooperative society, becoming a procurement centre is beneficial in many ways:

- **Service to members:** Farmer-members can sell at MSP in their own village/area — no need to go to a distant mandi.
- **Extra income:** Procurement commission/service charge is a new source of income for the society.
- **Trust in membership:** When the society gives members solid, monetary benefits, membership and activity grow.
- **Use of infrastructure:** Better use of the society's godown and staff.

So becoming a procurement centre is a win for both the society and the member, provided the society has enough capacity and clean accounts. (See: [PACS godown and storage scheme](/blog/pacs-godown-grain-storage-scheme-ami-aif-nabard).)

## NAFED and NCCF — the role of both

Both cooperative federations play a central role in MSP procurement, but their identities are slightly different. **NAFED (National Agricultural Cooperative Marketing Federation of India)** is an apex cooperative federation focused mainly on marketing and procurement of farm produce — it has been the main agency in MSP procurement of pulses and oilseeds. **NCCF (National Cooperative Consumers' Federation of India)** is the apex body of consumer cooperatives, which plays a role in distribution to consumers along with procurement. Under PSS, these two, together with state-level agencies, run the system of buying from farmers at MSP. From a farmer's/society's point of view — both are authorised, reliable procurement channels; which one is active in which area depends on the state and the season.

## An example

Suppose a village PACS decided to link with NAFED and become an MSP procurement centre for mustard. Before the season, it helped its member-farmers register on the portal. When procurement started, farmers brought their mustard to the society's centre, where it was bought at MSP after quality check and weighing, and payment went directly to the farmers' accounts. In return the society got procurement commission, and members were saved from going to a distant mandi and got a guaranteed price. The society kept accurate, farmer-wise accounts of this whole transaction — who gave how much produce, how much was paid — which also made the audit smooth. This is the ideal form of cooperative MSP procurement — benefit for all three: farmer, society and the system.

## Why accounts matter in procurement

In MSP procurement the society handles large quantities of produce and money — taking produce from farmers, paying for it, storing it, and handing it over to the agency. Accurate, transparent accounts are needed at every level: how much produce was taken from which farmer, of what quality, how much was paid, how much is due. Even one mistake hurts the farmer's trust and becomes an objection in audit. So for a society that becomes a procurement centre, keeping good member-wise and transaction-wise accounts is as important as doing the procurement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a private trader buy pulses and oilseeds at MSP?** — Government procurement under PSS happens through NAFED/NCCF and state agencies. But in oilseeds, if the state has chosen PDPS, the farmer can sell to a trader in the mandi and get the gap below MSP (up to 15% of MSP) from the government.
**What does a farmer have to do to sell at MSP?** — Pre-register before the season on NAFED's eSamridhi or NCCF's eSamyukti portal (or through the state's system).
**Can a cooperative society itself become a procurement centre?** — Yes; registered primary agricultural cooperative societies and state cooperative marketing federations can link with NAFED-NCCF and work as procurement centres.
**In which states does PSS apply?** — In states that make a request and waive mandi tax on procurement (PIB, December 2024).
**What is the farmer's first step to sell?** — Pre-registration on the NAFED/NCCF portal before the season; if not done in time, they may miss MSP procurement.
**What is MSP?** — The minimum guaranteed price; even if the market falls below it, the farmer gets this price — protection from the risk of a price fall.
**What happened to PPSS?** — PPSS is not among the PM-AASHA components continued in September 2024 (PSS, PSF, PDPS, MIS); we did not find the reason in any government text.
**What is special about tur, urad and masoor?** — Under the Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (October 2025), 100% procurement for four years in participating states, through NAFED and NCCF.
**What does the society get from becoming a procurement centre?** — MSP facility for members, commission income for the society, growing membership and better use of infrastructure.
**What is the difference between NAFED and NCCF?** — NAFED is an apex federation focused on farm marketing/procurement; NCCF is the apex federation of consumer cooperatives (also has a role in distribution) — both are authorised procurement channels.
**Which crops come under MSP?** — Mainly pulses (gram/moong/urad/arhar/masoor) and oilseeds (mustard/groundnut/soybean etc.); the exact list depends on each season's announcement.
**How is payment received?** — Usually directly into the farmer's bank account; so correct bank details are needed at registration.

## Three things to remember

- 🏛️ MSP procurement under PSS is done by NAFED/NCCF (with state agencies); in oilseeds the state can also choose PDPS (difference payment).
- 📝 To sell at MSP, prior registration on eSamridhi/eSamyukti (or the state's system) is necessary.
- 🏛️ Registered cooperative societies can become procurement centres themselves and benefit members — contact the state cooperation department/NAFED-NCCF for the process.
- 🧾 Procurement involves large produce and money — so accurate, transparent farmer-wise and transaction-wise accounts are needed.

## In the end

The whole MSP procurement framework for pulses and oilseeds rests on cooperative federations (NAFED/NCCF) — and this shows one big role of cooperation: getting the farmer a reliable, guaranteed price. For the farmer, the most important thing is pre-registration in time; and for cooperative societies there is a solid opportunity — becoming a procurement centre to serve members and increase their own income. But the success of this whole system depends on transparent, accurate accounts — because large quantities of produce, money and farmers' trust are at stake here. Always confirm the exact process, documents and fee structure with your state cooperation department or NAFED/NCCF, because these change by state and season.
